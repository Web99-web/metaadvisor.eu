A federal judge has ruled that **Google will not be forced to sell its Chrome browser**, despite the company being found guilty of maintaining a monopoly last year.  

The ruling bars Google from entering certain exclusive contracts with device makers and requires the firm to share search data with competitors.  

Judge Amit Mehta’s decision comes after months of speculation over potential penalties following his finding that Google violated antitrust laws by building what he called a search monopoly.  

Allowing Google to retain Chrome is a more lenient outcome than prosecutors sought, who requested the forced sale of Chrome and a five-year ban from the browser market. In his 230-page ruling, Mehta said prosecutors had “overreached” in demanding divestiture.  

Although Google escaped the harshest remedies, Mehta sided with prosecutors in blocking exclusive contracts related to the distribution of Chrome, Google Assistant, and the Gemini app. However, the ruling does not prohibit Google from making payments to distributors, arguing that a blanket ban could cause unintended harms.  

Following the ruling, Google’s shares rose in after-hours trading, a signal that investors viewed the outcome favorably.
