World Liberty Financial (WLFI), the Trump-family-affiliated DeFi project, is pitching a new tokenomics maneuver to restore confidence after a rocky trading debut: a buyback-and-burn program funded by protocol-owned liquidity fees.

The proposal comes as WLFI faces steep market headwinds after launching on major exchanges including Binance, OKX, Upbit, Coinbase, and Bithumb. WLFI trades around $0.23 – down 24% on the day – with a market cap near $6.39 billion, according to CoinGecko. At launch, the token briefly topped $40 billion in futures markets before heavy selling dragged prices lower.

According to a governance proposal released Tuesday, fees collected from WLFI’s liquidity on Ethereum, Binance Smart Chain, and Solana would be used to purchase WLFI on the open market and send it to a burn address. That mechanism would permanently remove tokens from circulation, reducing supply over time.

“This program removes tokens held by participants not committed to WLFI’s long-term growth,” the team wrote, arguing that the design aligns long-term holders with the protocol’s growth trajectory.

The measure applies only to WLFI’s own liquidity. Third-party or community LPs are not affected. Alternatives such as splitting fees between treasury and burns were rejected in favor of maximum impact.

For backers, the proposal seeks to shift the narrative from oversupply to engineered scarcity. More trading activity means more fees, which in turn would result in more WLFI being removed from circulation.
