Half of adults in the UK fear that artificial intelligence could threaten their jobs, either through job losses or changes to working conditions, according to a survey by the Trades Union Congress (TUC). Of the 2,600 respondents, 51% expressed such concerns.

The concern is particularly strong among workers aged 25 to 34: 62% said they were worried about AI’s impact. Large companies including BT, Amazon and Microsoft have indicated in recent months that advances in AI could lead to job cuts.

The UK labour market is currently weakening, with unemployment at a four-year high of 4.7%. Most economists, however, do not see a direct link to investment in AI.

The TUC stresses that AI can also benefit workers and public services, but calls on government and employers to actively involve unions and employees. It argues that this is necessary to protect jobs and ensure training for those affected.

Half of respondents, 50%, said they wanted a say in how AI is introduced in workplaces and the wider economy, while only 17% opposed this. The TUC also wants conditions attached to the billions in public funding for research and development to protect workers from displacement by new technologies.

The findings show that the public debate concerns more than technological efficiency. Trust and participation are central to acceptance of AI in the workplace.
