Indian e-commerce startup Citymall, which focuses on budget-focused grocery delivery for tier-2 and tier-3 towns, said today that it has raised $47 million in Series D funding led by Accel, with participation from existing investors including Waterbridge Ventures, Citius, General Catalyst, Elevation Capital, Norwest Venture Partners, and Jungle Ventures.

The Series D round comes three years after the company’s $75 million Series C round led by Norwest Venture Partners. The company’s valuation remains flat at $320 million despite revenue growth. According to sources familiar with the deal, investors applied nearly a 4x multiple of Citymall’s past year of revenue as a benchmark. The company has raised $165 million to date.

Investors said the stagnant valuation reflects the shift in market sentiment, but they remain optimistic about the company’s trajectory. “We’ve been investors since the Series A, and we wanted to double down because we believe online grocery shopping – especially in the value segment – is India’s largest consumer market,” said Pratik Agarwal of Accel.

Citymall’s funding comes at a time of a quick-commerce frenzy in India. Companies like BlinkIt, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, and Tata-owned BigBasket are racing to deliver groceries within 10 minutes. Citymall, by contrast, targets value-conscious customers who plan purchases ahead rather than ordering for immediate needs.
