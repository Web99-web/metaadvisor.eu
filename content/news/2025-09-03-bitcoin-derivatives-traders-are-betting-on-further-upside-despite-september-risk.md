Derivatives traders are expecting a moderately optimistic outlook for Bitcoin in September despite macroeconomic uncertainty and seasonal risks, with downside volatility appearing muted.

Bitcoin has risen 3% over the last two days, showing a slight bullish skew and currently trades around $110,000, according to CoinGecko data.

This uptick, however, comes amid flat cumulative volume deltas, with a noticeable increase in passive bids at a 10% order book depth, CoinGlass data shows. In other words, the move isn’t driven by aggressive buying, but rather by passive accumulation.

At the same time, open interest on perpetuals has surged 2.35% to $30 billion over the past two days, as traders prepare ahead of this week’s key U.S. employment data.

September’s historical bearish seasonality, meanwhile, is forcing U.S. investors to reassess positions as the fiscal year closes on September 30.

The Bitcoin options market, however, signals something different. Sean Dawson, head of research at on-chain options platform Dervie, told Decrypt that options traders are making bullish bets for the September 26 expiry, with open interest building at the $120,000, $130,000, and $140,000 strikes.

“Since market makers are net long gamma,” Dawson explained, “an increase in Bitcoin’s price will most likely be dampened by hedge selling. Similarly, price drops will also be cushioned as dealers buy to hedge their positions.”

Bitcoin’s implied volatility for the next 30 days is holding near 30%, underscoring the recent stretch of subdued price action.

**Our take:**  
Despite September’s historical headwinds, derivatives data suggest underlying bullish conviction. With options clustered at higher strikes, Bitcoin could grind upward, though hedging flows may smooth out sharp moves in either direction.
