**Anthropic** is making significant changes to how it handles user data: by **September 28**, all Claude users must decide whether they want their chats included in AI training.

### What’s changing?
Until now, Anthropic did **not use consumer chat data** for training its models. Going forward, it will incorporate **conversations and coding sessions** into training, and extend data retention to **five years** for those who don’t opt out.

Previously, prompts and outputs from Claude Free/Pro/Max users were deleted after 30 days (unless legally required or flagged, in which case data could be stored for up to two years).  

The new rules apply to **Claude Free, Pro, and Max**, including **Claude Code**. **Enterprise customers** (Claude Gov, Claude for Work, Claude for Education, API access) remain exempt – mirroring OpenAI’s approach.

### Why is this happening?
Anthropic frames it as a matter of **user choice**. By staying in, users will:  
- help improve **model safety** and reduce false positives,  
- support improvements in coding, analysis, and reasoning.

In other words: *help us help you.*

### Context:
The change comes amid growing debate over **AI companies’ use of user data**. While Anthropic stresses transparency and improved model safety, users are wary of **privacy risks** and the possibility of future misuse.
