# TAO news: Root Reborn could turn validators into capital managers for Bittensor AI subnets

Bittensor is one of the best-known crypto-AI projects, but it is also a project around which questions of governance, decentralization and the real distribution of power keep returning. The new proposal called Root Reborn brings those questions back into focus.

According to the available description of the proposal, the idea is to change how validators participate in supporting Bittensor subnets. Instead of being only technical participants in the network, validators could receive a more active role in choosing which subnets they want to support. That is why the proposal is being compared to a model in which validators become something similar to fund managers.

That does not mean the change has already been implemented or that its outcome is certain. It is a proposal that still needs to be tested, reviewed and discussed. But the idea itself matters because it shows the direction in which Bittensor is trying to develop: from a simpler AI crypto narrative toward a more complex ecosystem where capital, validators and subnets play an increasingly important role.

## Why is the proposal interesting?

Bittensor consists of multiple subnets, meaning smaller network areas connected to different AI tasks. If validators have a larger role in choosing which subnets they support in the future, a selection question appears: which subnets will receive support, which will be left behind and who actually decides what quality means?

In theory, such a model could help stronger subnets. If validators choose better projects, capital could move toward parts of the network that make more sense, show better development or have more real utility. That could make Bittensor more efficient.

But there is also another side. If validators receive more economic power, it will be important to watch whether that power is truly distributed or whether it becomes concentrated among a smaller number of large actors. In crypto projects, that is always a sensitive question, especially when a project already has a history of debates about decentralization.

## The history: decentralization and governance

Root Reborn does not appear in an empty space. Bittensor has already faced questions about real decentralization after the departure of the Covenant AI team, which we analyzed in an earlier article: <a href="https://metaadvisor.eu/hr/news/tao-bittensor-decentralizacija-kripto-ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TAO pod pritiskom: AI ‘Bitcoin’ i pitanje stvarne decentralizacije</a>.

At that time, the main question was whether TAO could preserve trust if doubts appeared inside the ecosystem around governance, centralized influence and the departure of important development teams. That is why this new proposal should also be viewed carefully. If validators receive a larger role in choosing subnets, then this is not only a technical change, but also a question of power.

That is especially important because Bittensor is not trying to be just another crypto project with one token. It is trying to position itself as a network for decentralized artificial intelligence. If such a network wants to be credible, it must show that decentralization is not only marketing, but a real mechanism for decision-making and the distribution of influence.

{{< support1 >}}

## Good or bad?

There is no simple answer to that question yet.

Root Reborn could be positive if it helps the network direct support toward higher-quality subnets and if it reduces pressures that harm the development of the ecosystem. In that case, validators could receive a more useful and more responsible role.

But the same proposal could become problematic if it increases the concentration of power, if a smaller number of validators receives too much influence or if the subnet selection process is not transparent enough. In that case, Bittensor could receive a new layer of centralized influence, only in a different form.

That is why this proposal is not simply “bullish” or “bearish”. It is more of a signal that Bittensor is in a phase where it is trying to change the internal economy of the network, but it is still not clear whether this will strengthen decentralization in the long run or open new governance risks.

## What does this mean for TAO?

For TAO, this topic matters because it shows that the ecosystem is trying to develop, while questions of trust continue to follow it. Investors and users should not look only at the token price, but also at how the network makes decisions, how power is distributed and who has real influence over future development.

If Bittensor succeeds in creating a more transparent and higher-quality model for supporting subnets, that could be a positive step. If, however, the new model only shifts power toward validators without enough oversight, then the old decentralization problem could reappear in a new form.

In other words, Root Reborn is not only a technical story for developers. It is also a governance story for anyone following TAO, AI crypto projects and attempts to build decentralized AI infrastructure.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

Root Reborn is an interesting proposal, but it should not be read as a finished change or as a guaranteed positive signal.

* The proposal shows that Bittensor is trying to change how subnets are supported.
* Validators could receive a more active role, which is why they are being compared to fund managers.
* This could help stronger subnets if the process is transparent and well executed.
* At the same time, a larger role for validators can open new questions about concentration of power.
* Bittensor already has a history of debates around decentralization and governance, so every new proposal needs to be viewed in that context.
* For TAO, this is not simply good or bad news, but a signal that the network is in a sensitive phase of development.
* The most important question is not only whether the proposal could help the token price, but whether it could help trust in the network.

The conclusion is simple: Root Reborn could be an interesting step for Bittensor, but it remains to be seen whether it will bring a better distribution of capital or only a new structure of power. In crypto-AI projects, technology is not the only question. It is just as important who governs the network, who decides and how decentralized the whole process really is.
