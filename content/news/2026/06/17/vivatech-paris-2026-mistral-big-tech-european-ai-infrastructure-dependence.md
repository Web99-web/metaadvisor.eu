# VivaTech Paris 2026: Mistral, Big Tech and Europe’s dependence on AI infrastructure

VivaTech Paris 2026 takes place from June 17 to 20 in Paris, at Paris Expo Porte de Versailles. It is one of Europe’s largest events for startups, technology, investors, innovation and major digital transformation.

This year’s edition is especially important because VivaTech is celebrating its 10th anniversary. The event was launched in 2016, and in ten years it has grown from a startup and innovation fair into a major European tech stage where artificial intelligence, infrastructure, chips, cloud, industry and technological independence are increasingly discussed.

## What is VivaTech?

VivaTech, fully Viva Technology, is an annual technology event in Paris. It brings together startups, large companies, investors, governments, media and technology leaders. For startups, it is an opportunity to present themselves to investors and partners. For large companies, it is a place to show new solutions and connect with innovators. For Europe, increasingly, it is a place to discuss who will control the next layer of the digital economy.

In the beginning, events like this were often mainly about apps, gadgets, robots and startup pitch presentations. Today, the focus is more serious. AI is no longer only a productivity tool. It is becoming infrastructure used in business, cybersecurity, the public sector, finance, healthcare, education and industry.

That is why VivaTech 2026 matters. It does not only show what is new in technology, but also where Europe is falling behind, where it has a chance and where it remains too dependent on American Big Tech.

{{< support1 >}}

## The main goal: less dependence on foreign AI infrastructure

The biggest theme of VivaTech 2026 is Europe’s dependence on American AI, cloud and chips. Europe has excellent researchers, strong regulation and several very interesting startups, but the largest AI models, GPU infrastructure, cloud platforms and capital are still mostly connected to the United States.

That creates a problem. If European companies, governments and institutions use American AI models, American cloud and American chips, then part of the digital power sits outside Europe. Europe can write rules, but if it does not have enough of its own products and infrastructure, it does not control the whole chain.

That does not mean Europe should end cooperation with the United States. That would be unrealistic. But it does mean Europe needs its own alternatives. Otherwise, European AI sovereignty remains more of a political wish than real technological strength.

## G7 context: Mythos and the “AI switch”

Anthropic and Mythos are not listed here as VivaTech exhibitors. They are important as a parallel G7 context that explains why Europe is talking so much about AI infrastructure in the first place.

At the G7 meeting, a debate opened about access to advanced American AI models, including Anthropic’s Mythos model, which has been linked with advanced cybersecurity capabilities. French President Emmanuel Macron commented on the question of expanding access to such models for trusted partners.

{{< support1 >}}

This is a concrete example of the “on/off switch” problem. If access to an important AI tool depends on a decision by a foreign company or on a security policy outside Europe, then Europe does not have full control over critical digital infrastructure. That is exactly why VivaTech and the discussions around the G7 increasingly focus on AI independence, partnerships and Europe’s own capacities.

## Who stood out at VivaTech and why?

**Mistral AI** is the most important European AI symbol in this story. The French startup is trying to become a European alternative to American players such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind. Mistral is not yet as globally dominant as its American competitors, but it matters because it shows that Europe can still build its own AI models and its own technological direction.

**Nvidia** is not a European company, but it is crucial for the entire AI infrastructure. Without GPU chips and computing power, there is no serious AI. Nvidia therefore shows Europe’s problem: even when Europe has a strong model or startup, it still needs chips, data centers and infrastructure that are largely connected to American technology chains.

**Publicis** stood out through Maurice Lévy’s call for a major European AI fund of 100 billion euros. Publicis is not an AI startup, but a global communications and advertising group, and that is exactly why it matters. It shows that AI is no longer only a developer topic. Marketing, media, advertising, brands and corporate communication increasingly depend on AI tools.

* **LVMH** is not an AI startup, but a VivaTech partner and an example of a large industrial group coming to VivaTech to look for technology for luxury, retail and brands. Through the **LVMH Innovation Award**, it connects with startups that can help with supply chain transparency, AI video production, employee training, e-commerce and how luxury brands appear in AI answers. That is why LVMH matters for this story: it shows that AI is no longer only a topic for tech companies, but is entering every serious industry.

## Startups that could become future stars

**Fairly Made** is a French startup focused on supply chain transparency, material traceability and measuring the environmental impact of products. This matters because brands increasingly need to prove where materials come from and what the real impact of production is. Fairly Made could become important in a world where sustainability is no longer only marketing, but also a business and regulatory obligation.

**Synthesia** is a British AI video company. It enables companies to produce video content, training materials and internal communication faster and in multiple languages. It competes with traditional video production, but also with new AI video tools. Its advantage is practical: large companies can reduce costs, speed up employee training and localize content more easily for different markets.

**Bluefish AI** is especially interesting because it deals with how brands appear in AI answers. This is the new version of an old SEO problem. In the past, companies asked how Google displayed them. Now they increasingly ask how ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or other AI systems display them. If users move from classic search engines toward AI answers, tools like this could become very important.

**SAP** is an example of major European enterprise software trying to position itself in the AI transformation of companies. SAP is not a small startup, but it is important because it shows that Europe still has large business technology companies that can integrate AI into real processes: finance, logistics, procurement, HR, production and data management. Compared with American cloud and SaaS giants, SAP is one of the few European players with global enterprise weight.

## The European paradox: regulation before growth

Europe’s problem is not only that it is behind American Big Tech. The problem is also that Europe often tries to regulate the market before allowing its own startups to become large enough. In theory, strict rules protect users, privacy, competition and the democratic space. In practice, too much early and expensive regulation can hit exactly those small European startups that do not yet have the capital, legal teams and compliance departments of American giants.

{{< support2 >}}

This creates the European paradox. Europe wants to protect users from the excessive power of platforms, but at the same time it creates an environment in which small European players find it harder to grow. While they struggle with rules, costs and bureaucracy, American companies have a larger market, more capital and fewer barriers in the early growth phase. Once they become large enough, Europe can no longer simply ignore them. It has to use them, regulate them and try to control them — but often from a weaker position.

This is already visible with social networks and digital platforms. Europe has introduced rules for **Big Tech**, but ordinary users and small entrepreneurs can still lose accounts, pages, contacts and digital businesses after automated decisions. The **Appeals Centre Europe** can issue a decision or opinion, but if that paper has no real enforcement power for a user who has lost a Facebook or Instagram account, then the question remains how effective the protection of people from Big Tech really is.

A similar problem can also appear with AI. If access to key AI models, security tools or advanced infrastructure depends on American companies and their rules, then Europe can have strategies, conferences and regulation, but not necessarily real control. If access is limited, changed or switched off, European users and companies once again depend on someone else’s “switch”.

{{< support2 >}}

## Why does this matter?

VivaTech 2026 shows that Europe can no longer talk only about regulation. If it wants to reduce dependence on Big Tech, it must also talk about growth, capital, infrastructure and the real use of European solutions.

Mistral can be a powerful symbol, but one startup is not enough. Fairly Made, Synthesia and Bluefish AI show that there are concrete AI and tech products that can compete in their own niches. SAP shows that Europe also has large enterprise software players. Publicis shows that big business wants serious European AI capital. LVMH shows that industry is already looking for practical solutions. Nvidia shows that without chips and computing power, there is no AI.

That is why the biggest question is whether Europe can create conditions in which such startups and companies can grow fast enough before they are overtaken by American or Chinese competitors.

VivaTech Paris 2026 matters because it shows that Europe’s tech debate is no longer only about innovation, but about infrastructure, capital and dependence on Big Tech.

* **Nvidia** shows that AI does not depend only on software, but also on chips, GPUs, data centers and computing power.

* **Bluefish AI** is especially interesting because it deals with a new question: how does a brand appear in AI answers, not only in Google results?
* **SAP** shows that Europe has large enterprise software companies that can integrate AI into real business processes.
* **Anthropic / Mythos** are not an example of VivaTech exhibitors, but G7 context that clearly shows the “AI switch” problem: access to advanced models can depend on decisions made outside Europe.
* Europe’s biggest problem is not only dependence on American AI, but also the fact that too much early regulation can slow small startups before they become large.
* AI sovereignty cannot be only a political message. It must include capital, infrastructure, space for startup growth and real enforcement power toward large platforms.

The conclusion is simple: VivaTech Paris 2026 shows that Europe has talent, startups, major industrial brands and ambition. But if it wants to depend less on Big Tech, it must stop being only a continent of regulation and become a continent that enables its startups to grow fast.
