Ethereum is under pressure again after an old large investor, often described as an Ethereum OG or ETH whale, sold a major amount of Ether during the recent price decline. These kinds of sales always attract attention because the market tries to read a bigger message into them: do early large holders know something smaller investors do not yet see, or is this simply one investor taking profit or reducing exposure?

According to data reported by Cointelegraph, an early Ethereum investor sold a total of around $136 million in ETH over the past week. The investor first sold 55,000 ETH worth about $112.25 million, followed by another 9,442 ETH worth roughly $24 million. The sale happened while Ether was moving closer to the psychologically important $2,000 level, which increased nervousness across the market.

## A large sale at a sensitive moment

According to blockchain tracker Lookonchain, the early Ethereum investor sold ETH at an average price of around $2,041 per token. That is large enough to draw attention, especially because it happened at a time when the market is already trying to identify a bottom for ETH and asking whether Ether could continue to move lower.

When an old wallet sells a large number of tokens, the market does not read it as just another transaction. Such a move is often interpreted as a possible signal that someone who has been in the project for a long time now wants to exit, reduce risk or move capital into other opportunities. That is why the immediate question appeared: are old Ethereum investors leaving the ship, or is this only one major seller?

## Onchain data does not yet show a mass exit

Although the headline about a $136 million ETH sale sounds dramatic, the broader onchain picture looks calmer for now. An analysis of Ethereum supply through so-called “HODL waves” shows that a significant part of ETH supply remains unmoved across different time frames. In other words, there is still no clear evidence that old Ethereum holders are selling en masse.

“HODL waves” are an onchain metric showing how long certain tokens have remained unmoved. They help distinguish who is selling: short-term traders, medium-term investors or truly old holders who have owned ETH for years. Based on the available data, most of the recently moving supply appears to come from shorter holding periods, not from the oldest Ethereum wallets.

## Short-term holders look more nervous

The data shows that the group of investors holding ETH for three to six months reduced its share of supply from 13.5% to 9% since May 19. The group holding ETH for one week to one month also dropped from 4.76% to 2.6% during the same period. This suggests that part of the selling pressure is probably coming from short- and medium-term investors.

That distinction matters. The market reads panic among short-term traders differently from an exit by long-term major players. If shorter-term holders sell after a decline, that can be a normal capitulation of one market segment. If old Ethereum wallets started selling in large numbers, that would be a much stronger psychological signal.

## Long-term ETH holders do not look panicked yet

Interestingly, the supply held by investors with a holding period of five to seven years has even increased slightly, from 8.59% to 9% since May 19. That does not mean Ethereum is safe from further downside, but it does mean that the available data does not confirm a broad exit by long-term holders.

In other words, one major Ethereum whale did sell, but that does not yet mean an entire generation of early ETH investors is leaving the market. For now, it is more accurate to say that there is one strong individual signal, but not yet a confirmed trend of mass selling by old Ethereum wallets.

## ETH under pressure as the market searches for a new narrative

Ethereum has been under pressure for some time as the market compares its momentum with Bitcoin, Solana and newer projects that seem to attract more speculative energy. If ETH continues to move toward the psychologically important $2,000 level or below it, every large sale can feel like another sign of weakness.

At the same time, the broader crypto market is showing signs of rotation. Bitcoin is under extra scrutiny because of Strategy’s Bitcoin sale, Ethereum is facing questions around large whale transactions, while newer tokens and narratives, including HYPE, are attracting more attention from traders. This does not necessarily mean that capital is permanently leaving BTC and ETH, but it may mean that the speculative part of the market is temporarily looking for new stories.

## Is this also “Sell in May and go away”?

Because the large ETH sale happened at the end of May, it is easy to connect this story with the old market saying “Sell in May and go away.” Of course, there is no proof that the old Ethereum investor sold because of a seasonal strategy. But at a moment when Strategy sells a small part of its Bitcoin, an old ETH wallet sells more than $136 million worth of Ether, and the market is loudly searching for new narratives, the phrase suddenly carries psychological weight again.

The most important question here is not whether someone literally followed an old stock market saying. What matters more is that the market seems to be showing signs of fatigue around the largest crypto assets. Bitcoin and Ethereum remain core assets, but part of the speculative capital appears to be searching for faster, newer and riskier stories.

## Hype around HYPE or real capital rotation?

In this environment, it is easy to connect several headlines into one larger story: Strategy sells a small part of its Bitcoin, an old Ethereum whale sells a large amount of ETH, and the market is talking more loudly about new tokens such as HYPE. That can look like the beginning of a capital rotation out of the large old crypto assets and into newer, faster narratives.

But this is where caution is necessary. The crypto market often turns individual events into big stories before the data actually confirms a real trend. HYPE may be an interesting new narrative, but it may also be another example of how the market searches for the next story when Bitcoin and Ethereum start to look tired. For smaller investors, the biggest risk is not only a falling price, but entering a story only after the hype has already become too loud.

For Metaadvisor, the key point is that this Ethereum whale sale should not be read in isolation. It sits inside a wider market mood: Bitcoin is being questioned after Strategy’s sale, Ether is being watched after a major whale exit, and traders are looking for new momentum elsewhere. That does not prove a structural shift, but it does show how quickly crypto sentiment can move from long-term conviction to narrative rotation.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

- The $136 million ETH sale looks dramatic, but it does not yet prove a mass exit by old Ethereum holders.
- Onchain data suggests that selling pressure is more likely coming from short- and medium-term holders than from the oldest investor groups.
- Ethereum remains psychologically vulnerable because its price is approaching important levels while the market searches for direction.
- Together with Strategy’s Bitcoin sale, this story strengthens the impression that part of the market is looking for new narratives.
- HYPE and similar stories can attract attention, but investors should distinguish real market rotation from short-term hype.
