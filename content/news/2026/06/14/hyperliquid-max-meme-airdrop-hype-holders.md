## What is MAX?

MAX is a new meme coin spreading through the Hyperliquid community. According to posts circulating on X, the token is linked to Hyperliquid, HYPE holders and the story that Max is the dog of Jeff Yan, one of Hyperliquid’s founders. For now, this should be treated as a community narrative, not as an officially confirmed fact.

But in crypto, the narrative often comes first. Dogecoin did not become famous because it had complicated technology. Pepe memecoin also did not begin as a serious financial instrument. Meme coins often start as a joke, a community symbol or a viral moment, and only later does the market decide whether to accept or forget that story.

## Did you hold at least 0.1 HYPE?

According to community posts, users who held at least 0.1 HYPE on HyperCore, including staked HYPE, or on HyperEVM were supposed to receive the MAX airdrop. Users report that the token has already been distributed automatically and is visible in their balances.

That is an important distinction. If the token arrived automatically, there is no reason to panic-click “claim” or “check” links. With crypto airdrops, phishing appears almost immediately: fake pages, fake checkers, copied profiles and links trying to make users connect a wallet or sign a transaction. If MAX is already in the balance, the user does not need to “hunt” for it online.

## HyperCore, HyperEVM and why users were confused

Part of the confusion comes from the structure of Hyperliquid itself. Hyperliquid has HyperCore and HyperEVM. Put simply, HyperCore is the main trading part of Hyperliquid, where spot and perpetual markets, order books and trading balances exist. HyperEVM is the EVM part of the ecosystem, used through wallets such as MetaMask and built for smart contracts.

For ordinary users, this can be confusing. Someone may say: “I have HYPE on Hyperliquid.” But the real question is where that HYPE was located: on HyperCore, in staking, on HyperEVM, as margin, in a spot balance or in another part of the system. With airdrops, exactly these details often decide who is eligible and who is not.

## MAX is not a typical pump.fun story

In recent memecoin cycles, a lot of attention has gone to pump.fun tokens on Solana, because pump.fun became one of the most active and visible ways to launch new meme coins quickly. Pepe is a different story: PEPE is an ERC-20 token on Ethereum. Dogecoin, however, is not an Ethereum token — Doge has its own blockchain and is much older than today’s wave of fast meme launch platforms.

MAX currently looks like a different case: a Hyperliquid-native community meme connected to HYPE holders and the Hyperliquid ecosystem. That does not make it less risky. A meme coin is still a meme coin. Its price can rise quickly because of hype, but it can also fall just as fast if interest fades.

## Why is this still interesting?

MAX is interesting because it shows how quickly a narrative can form in crypto. A known ecosystem, an active community, an airdrop and a simple meme story are enough. Within hours, people check balances, share screenshots, ask about eligibility and try to understand why some users received something and others did not.

Airdrops are no longer just technical token distributions. They are marketing, rewards, memes, community signals and trust tests. If users feel rewarded, the story spreads positively. If the criteria are unclear, the same story can create frustration.

## Be careful: do not click every “check” link

The most important practical message is simple: if you want to check whether you received MAX, first look at the official Hyperliquid app and your balance. If you use MetaMask or HyperEVM, check token balances in a read-only way or through an explorer, without signing transactions.

Do not connect your wallet to unknown pages, do not sign “claim” transactions and do not rush just because someone on X says they received a certain amount. Even when a link is shared by a profile with many followers, that is not a security guarantee.
