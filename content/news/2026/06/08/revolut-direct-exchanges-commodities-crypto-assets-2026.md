Revolut has informed users that from 15 July 2026 it will no longer be possible to directly exchange commodities assets for crypto assets, or crypto assets for commodities. In practice, this means users will no longer be able to make a one-step exchange from assets such as gold or silver into crypto, or from crypto back into commodities.

At first glance, this may look like a technical change inside the app. But for users who often move between different types of assets, the change may be very practical. Instead of a simple direct exchange from one asset class to another, the process will have to go through a fiat currency such as the euro, the US dollar or the British pound.

## What exactly is changing?

According to the notice shown by Revolut, the change does not affect existing commodities or crypto balances that users already hold in their account. In other words, current balances are not being changed. If a user already holds a crypto or commodities position, that position remains on the account.

What changes is the way future exchanges between these asset classes will work. After 15 July 2026, users will first have to sell commodities or crypto assets for fiat currency and then use that fiat currency to buy the desired asset. That turns what used to be a one-step process into a two-step process.

## For users, this may mean more steps and more fees

The most important practical part of this change is related to fees. Revolut says in the notice that both transactions may be subject to fees, which will be shown before each transaction is confirmed. That means users should still see the cost before confirming, but the fact that one direct exchange is being replaced by two steps raises the question of whether such exchanges may become more expensive for some users.

For example, if a user wants to move value from gold into Bitcoin, they will no longer be able to simply exchange gold directly for Bitcoin. First, they will have to sell gold for fiat currency, and then use that fiat currency to buy Bitcoin. The same applies in the opposite direction if a user wants to sell crypto and buy commodities.

## Direct auto-exchange instructions will also be cancelled

The change also affects automatic exchanges. Revolut states that all active auto-exchange instructions for direct exchanges between commodities and crypto assets will be cancelled after 15 July 2026. This is an important detail for users who may have set up automatic instructions and no longer check them regularly.

Those users should review their settings before the stated date. If they have active automatic exchanges between crypto and commodities, those instructions will no longer work in the same way. Instead, users will have to use the new two-step process through a fiat currency.

## Regulatory separation of crypto and commodities services

This change comes at a time when the European crypto asset market is increasingly adapting to the MiCA regulatory framework. In the notice, Revolut states that cryptocurrency services are provided by Revolut Digital Assets Europe Ltd, a company registered in Cyprus and authorised by CySEC under MiCA rules.

Commodities services, on the other hand, have a different regulatory treatment. The notice states that commodities services are provided by Revolut Ltd and that these services in the United Kingdom are not regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. That is an important signal because it shows that different types of assets are not treated the same way from a regulatory perspective, even if they may appear similar to users inside one app.

## One app, but different rules

For the average user, Revolut may look like one simple financial app where cards, currencies, stocks, crypto, commodities and other assets appear next to each other. Behind that simple interface, however, there are different types of services, different legal entities, different fees and different regulatory rules.

That is why this change is not just about one button inside an app. It shows a broader trend in which fintech platforms have to separate different asset classes more clearly. Crypto assets, fiat currencies and commodities are not the same thing, even when they appear in the same app and users experience them as parts of the same account.

## Less simplicity, but more regulatory clarity

For users who only hold existing assets and do not make frequent exchanges, the change may not be dramatic. Revolut makes clear that existing balances are not affected. But for users who actively move value between crypto and commodities, the change may matter.

Instead of one click, the process will include a sale into fiat and then a new purchase. That may mean more steps, more attention when confirming transactions and potentially higher costs. It also means users will have to pay closer attention to the difference between the asset price, spreads, fees and fiat conversion.

## What can users do?

Revolut tells users that if they do not agree with the changes, they can stop using commodities or crypto services before 15 July 2026. They can do this by selling remaining assets or, in the case of crypto, by transferring crypto assets to an external wallet.

This is especially important for users who want more control over their crypto assets. If crypto is held on a platform, the user depends on that platform’s rules. If crypto is moved to a personal external wallet, the user takes on more personal responsibility, but also gains more control over the assets.
