# Standard Chartered Sees Aave as Future DeFi Infrastructure

Aave is back in focus after Cointelegraph reported that Standard Chartered sees the DeFi lending protocol as one of the possible winners from the growth of tokenized assets. This is interesting not only because of Aave, but also because the story involves a major traditional bank.

Standard Chartered is a large international bank headquartered in London, with particularly strong business across Asia, Africa and the Middle East. When an institution like that talks about Aave and DeFi, it no longer sounds like a comment from a narrow crypto circle. It sounds like a signal that part of traditional finance is watching very closely what could develop on the blockchain.

## Why Would a Bank Care About DeFi?

Banks do not look at DeFi only as a “crypto playground”. They may also see it as a possible new layer of financial infrastructure. If bonds, funds, treasury bills, loans and other real-world assets are tokenized, the market will need systems for lending, collateral, interest, liquidity and risk management.

That is exactly what lending protocols such as Aave already do in the crypto world. This is why banks are interested in DeFi: not necessarily because they want to disappear, but because they want to understand where money, collateral and new financial products could move if part of the market goes onchain.

## Aave as a Bridge Between DeFi and Tokenized Assets

If so-called real-world assets, meaning tokenized real-world assets, move more deeply onto blockchain networks, Aave could play an important role. The logic is simple: if tokenized assets exist, the market also needs protocols that can work with collateral, liquidity and lending.

That is why Standard Chartered appears to be looking at Aave not only as another DeFi token, but as a possible part of future infrastructure. In other words, if tokenization grows seriously, Aave could be one of the protocols that benefits from it.

## But the Risk Has Not Disappeared

This is where the second important part of the story begins. Aave cannot be viewed only through a bullish narrative. If it wants to become infrastructure for more serious tokenized assets, then exploit risks, bridge risks, collateral risks and risks from connected protocols become even more important.

That matters because DeFi does not operate in isolation. A problem often does not come from one protocol alone, but from the entire connected system. That is why the story of Aave’s growth cannot only be a story about potential. It also has to be a story about resilience, security and trust.

## Why This Story Is Bigger Than Aave

This news is not important only for Aave. It shows something broader: traditional banks are taking tokenization and DeFi infrastructure more seriously. That does not mean banks will suddenly “move into DeFi” tomorrow. But it does mean they do not want to miss the next possible layer of the financial system.

If part of the market really moves onto blockchain, banks want to be close to that process — as partners, analysts, custodians, investors or at least as institutions that understand where new growth could emerge.
