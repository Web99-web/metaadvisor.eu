# Fake WhatsApp messages target hotel guests in Croatia

Tourists with reservations in Croatian hotels are being warned these days about dangerous WhatsApp messages. The messages ask them to confirm a reservation, re-enter card details or click a link so that the booking will not be cancelled. They are especially dangerous because they do not look like ordinary spam. In many cases, they contain real details: the guest’s name, the hotel name, the arrival or departure date and reservation information.

That is what makes this scam so convincing. If a guest receives a message that knows where and when they are travelling, it is natural to assume that it came from the hotel or an official booking platform. But this is the most important lesson of this case: the fact that a message knows your real details is no longer proof that it is safe. It may mean the opposite — that those details have already leaked somewhere or that part of the reservation chain has been compromised.

## Hotels warn: the messages do not come from them

According to information reported in Croatian media, several hotels in Croatia have warned guests that such messages do not come from them. It is especially important that hotels are stressing that WhatsApp is not the channel they use to request card confirmation, additional payment or clicks on unknown links. In other words, if you receive a message that looks as if it came from a hotel but sends you via WhatsApp to “confirm your reservation” or enter card details, it should be treated as a serious warning sign.

So far, it has not been officially confirmed where exactly the data leak happened. Public discussion first referred to the broader context of earlier Booking.com incidents and phishing campaigns connected with hotel reservations. In the Croatian case, PHOBS, or PHOBS – Virtual Sales Office, has also been mentioned as a Croatian platform for reservation management and hotel online sales. But at this point, the most accurate statement is: we do not know for certain where the problem originated.

## Why is this scam so convincing?

This matters because incidents like this do not always happen in one single place. Reservation data can pass through several systems: a hotel, booking platform, channel manager, online sales system, email accounts, employee user accounts and external providers. If only one part of that chain is compromised, scammers can obtain enough information to make a message look fully credible to the guest.

A similar pattern is not limited to Croatia. Security researchers and international media have already described so-called “reservation hijacking” scams, where real hotel reservation data is used for targeted phishing messages. Earlier investigations described campaigns that affected hotels, apartments and smaller accommodation providers in several countries, including major European tourist destinations. That does not automatically mean the Croatian case is part of the same campaign, but it shows that this is a broader pattern, not only an isolated local scam.

## The message creates panic: “confirm now or lose your reservation”

The most dangerous part of the scam is the feeling of urgency. The message usually suggests that the reservation will be cancelled unless the guest clicks immediately, confirms details or enters a card. This is a psychological trick. A guest who is travelling soon, especially during the season, may easily react out of fear of losing accommodation. That is exactly why scammers use words such as “urgent”, “confirmation”, “last step”, “verification” or “your reservation will be cancelled”.

{{< support1 >}}

For guests, the rule is very simple: do not click links from WhatsApp messages, SMS messages or suspicious emails that ask for card details, additional payments or reservation confirmation. Even if the message knows your name, hotel and arrival date, that is not enough reason to trust it. The reservation should be checked manually: through the official app used for the booking, the official hotel website or by contacting the hotel through a phone number or email address found independently on official channels.

For hotels, this situation is also a serious warning. Guests will not always know whether the problem started with the hotel, booking platform, external provider or some third system. For them, the experience is simple: they booked accommodation and then received a dangerous message with correct details. When that happens, trust can be damaged very quickly, even if the hotel was not technically the source of the problem.

## Digital tourism needs clearer security communication

That is why hotel communication is extremely important. If a hotel does not use WhatsApp for card confirmations or additional payments, it should state this clearly to guests. If fake messages appear, guests should be warned immediately through official websites, email, social media and reception contacts. In cases like this, a fast response can prevent financial damage and limit further panic.

This story shows how sophisticated today’s scams have become. These are no longer only poorly translated emails from unknown addresses. They are targeted messages that use real data and a real context. People do not click because they are “naive”, but because the message looks like a continuation of a real reservation.

The key message of this story is therefore simple: if a WhatsApp message knows your name, hotel and arrival date, that is no longer proof that it is safe. In today’s digital tourism, it may be exactly the sign to stop, avoid clicking and verify the reservation only through an official channel.
