Bitcoin is again holding above $63,000 this morning, after briefly slipping below $60,000 last week. At first glance, the market looks calmer. But data from the futures market and order books shows a more cautious picture: buyers have returned, but a large part of liquidity is waiting lower, in the area between $57,000 and $59,000.

That is an important detail because it suggests that the market does not yet see the recovery as a fully confirmed return to strong upside momentum. Bitcoin may move toward $64,000, but if new buyers do not step in with enough strength, the price could again test lower levels where stronger buying interest is now visible.

## The recovery is here, but the futures market has not exploded

Bitcoin has recovered after its move toward $59,000, but futures data shows that the market has not returned to the aggressive leverage environment that existed before the selloff. Open interest, meaning the total value of open futures positions, fell during the decline and remained below earlier highs.

That can be positive because part of the excessive leverage has been cleared out of the system. When leverage is lower, the market is less vulnerable to chain liquidations. At the same time, weaker open interest also means that a new large wave of aggressive long positions has not fully appeared yet.

## Buyers are waiting between $57,000 and $59,000

According to data cited by Cointelegraph, there are around $162 million in buy orders between $57,000 and $59,000. This is one of the larger visible liquidity zones below the current price and could become important if Bitcoin weakens again.

Those buy orders do not mean that Bitcoin must fall into that zone. But they show where part of the market believes the price may become interesting again for entry. In other words, buyers have not disappeared — some of them simply do not want to buy at the current price and are waiting for a possible move lower.

## Funding is slightly positive, but it does not show euphoria

The funding rate has turned slightly positive again after briefly falling below zero. This means futures traders are once again leaning slightly toward the long side, but leverage is not yet extreme. That is the difference between a recovery and real euphoria.

When funding becomes strongly positive, the market is often too heavily tilted in one direction. Now the picture looks more moderate. Traders are more willing to bet on upside than they were during the strongest panic, but it does not look as if a new wave of excessive risk is already building.

## Part of the move may be short covering

It is important to distinguish real new buying from the closing of short positions. If the price rises because short traders are closing positions, that can create a fast rebound. But it does not automatically mean that strong new capital has entered the market with the intention of pushing the price higher for longer.

That is why today’s recovery should be read carefully. Bitcoin has moved away from the lows, but for a stronger continuation higher, the market would need to see more stable spot buying, greater interest from new buyers and less dependence on a technical rebound after the selloff.

## ETF outflows are still the background to the whole story

Another factor is still weighing on sentiment: spot Bitcoin ETFs previously recorded around $1.7 billion in weekly outflows, already the fourth week in a row. That does not mean institutional investors are abandoning Bitcoin. But it does show that part of the larger money is still pulling back or at least reducing risk exposure.

That is why today’s picture is mixed. The Bitcoin price looks more stable, but ETF flows are not sending a message of euphoria. The futures market has reset, but a new aggressive long momentum has not been fully confirmed. Buyers are present, but an important part of them is waiting lower.

## Strategy and Saylor are again at the center of the debate

Additional nervousness comes from the debate around Strategy and Michael Saylor. After last week’s decline, Saylor linked the pressure on Bitcoin to massive capital flows into AI infrastructure, arguing that the AI buildout is temporarily absorbing capital from global markets.

But part of the market disagrees. According to CoinDesk, Arca rejected that explanation and argued that the pressure was more connected to the Strategy/MSTR story than to AI. In that view, the issue was not the amount of Bitcoin sold, but what a possible sale could mean for Strategy’s future obligations and financial structure.

## The market does not like unclear signals

In Bitcoin, the market often reacts not only to numbers, but also to narratives. If doubts appear that a large and well-known buyer could sell BTC, even a very small sale can raise a much bigger question: was this an exception, or the beginning of a change?

That is why discussions around Strategy matter even for people who do not follow the MSTR stock. Strategy has become a symbol of the corporate Bitcoin narrative. If the market starts questioning that narrative, pressure can spill over into broader crypto sentiment.

## USDT dominance shows defensive behavior

Another sign of caution can be seen in the rise of Tether dominance, or USDT dominance. When stablecoin dominance rises, it often means capital is moving out of riskier crypto positions and into dollar equivalents. That is typical defensive behavior when investors are not sure whether the market wants to continue higher.

But even here, the picture is not completely simple. If the market capitalization of stablecoins is falling at the same time, that may mean part of the capital is not merely parked in USDT waiting to return, but may have left the crypto market. That is why rising USDT dominance should be read together with other data, not automatically as a sign that “money is waiting on the sidelines.”

## The main question: defense of $57,000–$59,000 or a new attempt higher?

For now, the key question is whether Bitcoin can hold the area above $63,000 and attract new buyers, or whether the market will again test the zone where more liquidity is visible. If pressure returns, the area between $57,000 and $59,000 could become an important test.

If buyers react strongly there, the market could get confirmation that the decline was mostly a temporary leverage reset. If that zone does not hold, the question of a deeper correction and weaker sentiment would open again.
