# Anthropic Mythos as Claude Fable 5 goes public: can dangerous AI capability really be contained?

According to reports from Reuters and Axios, Anthropic has publicly introduced **Claude Fable 5**, the first broadly available model from the so-called **Mythos** class. Behind that name is not just another “stronger chatbot,” but a much more sensitive story: these are AI models that have shown highly advanced capabilities in cybersecurity testing, including code analysis, vulnerability discovery and potentially an understanding of how security flaws could be exploited.

That is why this launch matters not only to users who want a faster and smarter AI tool for writing, coding or data analysis. It opens a much bigger question: what happens when models that can help defend digital systems become powerful enough to also help attackers in the wrong hands?

## What is Anthropic Mythos?

Mythos is a name associated with an advanced class of Anthropic AI models, especially notable because of their cybersecurity capabilities. In an earlier preview phase, the model was available only to a limited number of organizations, security partners and government institutions through controlled access programs. Anthropic said at the time that Mythos could help discover software vulnerabilities — extremely useful for defending systems, but also highly sensitive.

In simple terms, such a model can behave like a very capable security analyst. It can inspect code, identify suspicious parts of a system, connect pieces of information and suggest where a vulnerability might be located. For companies, governments and security teams trying to patch weaknesses before someone exploits them, that can be a major advantage.

{{< support1 >}}

But that same capability has another side. If a model can find a vulnerability for defensive purposes, the obvious question is whether someone with bad intentions could use the same model for an attack. That is exactly why Mythos has drawn so much attention. The issue is not only how intelligent the model is, but what it can do in a very sensitive domain.

## Claude Fable 5: a public version with brakes

According to reports from Reuters and Axios, Anthropic is now releasing Claude Fable 5 as a Mythos-class model, but with protective mechanisms. In other words, the public is not getting the full “cyber Mythos” without restrictions, but a version that should be strong enough for general use while being limited in the riskiest areas.

That means Claude Fable 5 should be highly capable in programming, analysis, writing, business tasks and complex reasoning, but it should not freely assist users with high-risk cyber tasks. If a user tries to get help with dangerous security scenarios, the system is reportedly designed to redirect or restrict the response through a safer model or a safer level of response.

On paper, that sounds like a logical compromise. Anthropic wants to show that it can offer the public its most advanced class of models without fully opening the door to misuse. But the big question remains: can a dangerous capability really be “switched off” with rules, filters and guardrails?

## Why cybersecurity is so sensitive

Cybersecurity is an especially sensitive field because the line between defense and attack often depends on the user’s intent. The same request can be completely legitimate if it comes from a security expert testing their own system, but dangerous if it comes from an attacker looking for a weakness in someone else’s system.

For example, analyzing vulnerable code can be useful if a company wants to fix its software. But if the same analysis is used to find a way into a system before it is patched, the same capability becomes a threat. That is why models like Mythos are much more sensitive than ordinary AI tools for writing text or summarizing documents.

Speed is another issue. An AI model can review huge amounts of code, connect information and test hypotheses far faster than the average human. That can help defenders, but it can also accelerate attacks. And if the strongest tools are available only to large companies, governments and wealthy organizations, another question appears: who gets access to the best defense, and who remains exposed?

## Anthropic is trying to sell safety as part of the product

Anthropic has long tried to position itself as an AI company that puts safety at the center. That is part of its public identity: powerful models, but with an emphasis on control, risk assessment and responsible release. Claude Fable 5 is therefore not just a technical product. It is also a reputation test.

If the model proves genuinely useful while remaining difficult to misuse, Anthropic can show that frontier AI models can move toward public availability without a complete loss of control. But if guardrails can be bypassed, or if users can still extract dangerous cyber instructions, the story becomes much more serious.

That is why this release is interesting for Metaadvisor. It is not only news about a new model. It is a story about the future market for AI tools, where the competition will no longer be only about who has the smartest model, but also about who can prove that the model remains controllable in high-risk situations.

## What does this mean for ordinary users?

For ordinary users, Claude Fable 5 will probably look like another more advanced AI tool: better at coding, better at complex tasks, better at analysis and possibly more expensive than previous versions. But behind the user interface sits a much more serious debate.

If models like this are used in programming, business systems, finance, infrastructure and security, they gradually become part of the digital infrastructure. They are no longer just assistants for writing an email or an article. They become tools that can participate in technical decisions, risk analysis and the defense of systems.

That means the AI safety question is no longer only about whether a model might write a wrong answer. The question becomes: can a model help find real vulnerabilities? Can it explain those vulnerabilities in a way that is useful to an attacker? And can that capability be precisely limited only to “good” users?

## The biggest question: can guardrails be enough?

Guardrails are protective mechanisms designed to stop a model from answering dangerous or prohibited requests. The problem is that the history of AI models shows that users constantly look for ways to bypass restrictions. Sometimes they do it through rephrased prompts, sometimes through context, and sometimes through a series of smaller steps that do not look dangerous individually but together lead to a risky outcome.

For ordinary topics, that can be inconvenient. In cybersecurity, it can become a serious problem. If a model is not allowed to give direct attack instructions, but still helps the user reach the same goal through several indirect steps, the safety barrier has not really solved the problem.

That is why Fable 5 and the Mythos class will likely trigger two parallel debates. One will be commercial: how good is the model, how much does it cost and how much can it help companies? The other will be about safety: how real are the restrictions, and can they withstand pressure from users who deliberately try to bypass them?

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

- Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5 is not just another new AI model. It is a test of whether very advanced AI capability can be offered publicly while the riskiest functions remain controlled.

- The Mythos class is especially sensitive because it is associated with cybersecurity capabilities. This is a field where the same tool can serve both defense and attack.

- The most important question is not only how intelligent the model is, but who has access to the full capabilities and how reliably dangerous requests can really be limited.

- If the guardrails work well, this could be an important step toward safer public use of frontier AI models. If they do not work well enough, the risk can quickly move from the lab into the real world.

- For ordinary users, this is another sign that AI is no longer just a tool for writing and translation. We are entering a phase in which AI models increasingly affect the security of software, infrastructure and the digital economy.
