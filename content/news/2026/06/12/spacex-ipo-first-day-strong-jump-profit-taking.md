# SpaceX IPO first day: strong jump, fast profit-taking and a stock that is no longer just a Musk story

SpaceX went public on June 12, 2026 and immediately became one of the biggest market stories of the year. The stock debuted on Nasdaq under the ticker SPCX, with an IPO price of 135 dollars per share. Already at the open, it was clear how strong the interest was: trading began around 150 dollars, roughly 11% above the IPO price.

During the day, the enthusiasm increased further. At one point, the price jumped above 176 dollars, meaning the stock was more than 30% above the IPO price. But by the end of the trading session, part of that gain had faded. The stock closed around 161 dollars, or roughly 19% above the original IPO price.

## Enthusiasm at the open

The opening price of around 150 dollars showed that the market was willing to pay a significant premium to the IPO price. That is not surprising. SpaceX is not an ordinary technology company. For years, it was one of the most sought-after private companies in the world, while ordinary small investors had no simple way to access its shares.

That is why the first trading day also had a strong psychological element. Many investors were not just buying a stock. They were buying access to a company associated with rockets, Starlink, the space economy, possible defense contracts, satellite internet and the personality of Elon Musk.

{{< support1 >}}

With IPOs like this, the market often does not look only at current revenue and profit. It looks at the future, the story and expectations. SpaceX is exactly that kind of case: a company around which the feeling has been built for years that it represents the next phase of technological infrastructure.

## The jump above 176 dollars showed how strong demand was

The intraday high above 176 dollars showed that part of the market was willing to pay far more than the IPO price. At that point, the stock was more than 30% above the price at which shares were sold in the IPO.

For a company of this size, that is an exceptionally strong move. Large percentage jumps are not rare with smaller IPOs. But for a company that already begins with an enormous market valuation, every additional percentage point represents a major increase in total valuation.

{{< support1 >}}

That is why SpaceX’s first trading day was watched so closely. It was not only about whether the stock would rise or fall. It was about how much investors were willing to pay for a company that was treated as a symbol of the future even before it entered the public market.

## Closing around 161 dollars: a success, but not full euphoria

The closing price around 161 dollars and the gain of roughly 19% above the IPO price clearly represent a successful first day. Many IPOs would consider such a result a very strong outcome.

But the fact that the price fell from an intraday high above 176 dollars to around 161 dollars shows that the market was not simply buying blindly. Some investors clearly concluded that the fast jump was enough and decided to take profits. That is especially important for a high-profile IPO, because the first wave of buying often comes from a mix of enthusiasm, fear of missing out and short-term momentum trading.

In other words, SpaceX had a strong first day, but not without warning signals. The market showed interest, but it also showed that the stock price will be sensitive to expectations, valuation and short-term profit-taking.

## Was the price surprisingly high or lower than expected?

The answer depends on what was expected. For a casual observer, a gain of around 19% on the first day looks very strong. For the most optimistic investors, it may even have been disappointing that the stock did not close closer to the intraday high. For more cautious investors, such a move may mean that the market priced in very high expectations from the start.

The most realistic view is that the first day was strong, but not completely explosive. The stock rose enough to confirm enormous interest, but the decline from the intraday high showed that the market was not ready to push the price endlessly higher.

{{< support2 >}}

That may even be a healthier signal than complete euphoria. If the stock had closed near its highest level of the day, the story would have looked much more like classic IPO hype. This way, the message is more nuanced: SpaceX received a strong start, but the market wants to see how the company will justify its valuation.

## SpaceX is no longer just a private myth

The biggest change after the IPO is not only the stock price. The biggest change is that SpaceX is now a public company that the market will evaluate every day.

While SpaceX was a private company, much of the story could remain within a closed circle of private investors, internal valuations and occasional secondary sales. After going public, the price changes every second. Every piece of news about launches, Starlink, contracts, costs, regulation, accidents, delays or new projects can immediately affect the company’s market value.

That changes how the public looks at the company. SpaceX is no longer only a fascinating private story about space and Musk. It is now also a listed stock bought by funds, small investors, day traders and long-term shareholders. That brings a new level of pressure.

## The first day also revealed a wider rush for IPO exposure

Although this story is primarily about the stock price, it is impossible to completely ignore how large the interest was outside the stock exchange itself. Retail investors tried to gain exposure to SpaceX through different channels, from traditional brokers to new digital and tokenized products.

{{< support2 >}}

Some platforms had technical problems because of heavy traffic, while others had to return funds to users because they failed to secure the expected allocations. Those are separate stories, but the first day already showed that demand for SpaceX was larger than ordinary interest in just another IPO.

SpaceX did not attract only investors who follow balance sheets. It also attracted people who normally do not participate in IPOs, but wanted to be part of a major technological event. That explains why the pressure on infrastructure and allocations was so intense.

## Interesting: crypto did not collapse, it rose

What is especially interesting is that on the same day, some expected the SpaceX IPO to pull attention and liquidity away from the rest of the crypto market. But almost the opposite happened. Bitcoin climbed during the day toward the area around 64,000 dollars, while several altcoins also rose.

HYPE, the token connected to Hyperliquid, was also interesting, moving around 60 dollars on the same day that SpaceX was one of the most traded themes on that platform. This suggests that the SpaceX IPO did not necessarily suppress crypto interest. In some ways, it may even have increased it through pre-IPO and perpetual futures products that move traditional market stories into a 24/7 crypto environment.

{{< support2 >}}

That is an important signal for the future. Major IPOs no longer live only on Nasdaq or the New York Stock Exchange. They appear at the same time in broker apps, crypto derivatives, tokenized products and social networks. SpaceX showed on its first day that one market alone can no longer explain the full story.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* SpaceX’s first IPO day can be described as strong, but not without warnings. The IPO price was 135 dollars, the stock opened around 150 dollars, climbed above 176 dollars during the day and closed around 161 dollars. That means it ended roughly 19% above the IPO price, but clearly below the intraday high.
* This combination matters. On one side, the market showed great confidence and enormous interest. On the other side, fast profit-taking shows that investors did not completely forget valuation and risk.
* SpaceX proved on its first day that it is one of the most sought-after companies on the market. But from now on, a strong story will no longer be enough. As a public company, it will have to justify investor expectations regularly.
* For small investors, the main lesson is not only that SpaceX rose on the first day. The main lesson is that with major IPOs, it is important to look beyond the price jump itself. It matters where the stock opened, how high it went, where it closed and how quickly profit-taking appeared.
* SpaceX had a very successful first trading day. But the market already showed that this stock will be more than a simple story about rockets. It will be a story about expectations, valuation, patience and the company’s ability to grow from a private myth into a public market benchmark.
