# xStocks got stuck on the SpaceX IPO: Bybit, Binance Wallet, Bitget and Kraken refund users, Coinbase had only perps

The SpaceX IPO was not only a major event for Nasdaq and Wall Street. It was also a major test for the idea that access to the most sought-after stocks can be expanded through crypto infrastructure, tokens and wallet campaigns. That is exactly why the story around xStocks and the platforms offering tokenized access to SpaceX attracted so much attention.

In the end, it became clear that demand was much larger than the actually available backing. xStocks could not secure enough underlying SpaceX shares for all requests. The result was uncomfortable, but very instructive: Bybit, Binance Wallet and Bitget had to refund users, Kraken could not fulfill all requests, while Coinbase was a completely different case because it did not offer that kind of allocation, but only pre-IPO perpetual futures.

## Where did the problem start?

On paper, everything sounded very modern and very attractive. Users were offered something that looked like a new path into major IPOs: tokenized access, supposed exposure to SpaceX and the possibility for retail users to participate in a story that had been reserved for more closed investment circles for years.

But such a model depends on one very old thing: someone still has to secure real shares or a matching backing in the background. Blockchain can speed up distribution and make access easier, but it cannot create SpaceX shares out of thin air when there are not enough of them available.

{{< support1 >}}

The problem did not appear in a vacuum. Only about 5% of SpaceX was brought to the market in the IPO. That means even the traditional public offering was limited, while demand was enormous. Many investors could not get the quantities they wanted to buy, and in some retail channels users received only a small portion of the shares they requested. In such a situation, tokenized models automatically come under even greater pressure.

## Bybit: refund plus a small extra for users

Bybit said it did not receive a SpaceX allocation because its partner could not deliver the underlying assets. As a result, users who participated in the offering did not receive the expected allocation of tokenized SpaceX access.

The platform said it would automatically refund 100% of subscribed funds, with no further action required from users. In addition, Bybit announced extra compensation calculated at a 10% APR over a fixed four-day period. That is not a large compensation compared with the hype around the whole story, but it is still a signal that the platform had to acknowledge the problem and try to soften user disappointment.

{{< support1 >}}

## Binance Wallet: refund and airdrop

Binance Wallet also canceled its SPCXx IPO campaign. In its case, the message was clear: locked USDC would be fully refunded to users, and refunds were processed automatically.

Binance also announced a 1,000,000 dollar SPCXB token airdrop that was supposed to be distributed equally among all users who participated in the campaign. That is an interesting detail because it shows that platforms did not want to stop at a dry “refund” message. They also had to offer some kind of consolation compensation to soften the negative impression.

## Bitget: return of principal and the 5% fee

Bitget Wallet found itself in a similar situation. Interest in the tokenized SpaceX IPO was strong, but the tokens could not be distributed as planned. According to available information, users are being refunded, including the 5% handling fee that was part of the offering model.

{{< support2 >}}

That is an important detail because it shows that the problem was not ordinary trading or a market drop. The problem was that the desired allocation could not be delivered. Some reports also mention additional compensation such as whitelist priority for future tokenized IPOs and smaller gas bonuses, but the main message remains the same: users did not receive what they participated for.

## Kraken and xStocks: not all requests could be fulfilled

Kraken is important here because its IPO Access for SpaceX ran through xStocks. Kraken’s page clearly stated that users could submit interest in the SpaceX IPO through xStocks and, if allocated, receive tokenized equities under the ticker SPCXx. It was also clear that participation was at the offering price plus a 5% spread.

After the submission window closed, however, users were told to check their email for allocation information. In other words, there was no guarantee that everyone would receive what they had requested. Unfulfilled requests were not completed, and funds for such requests were returned to users. That means Kraken was not in the same category as Coinbase, nor did it simply “receive nothing” in the roughest interpretation of Bybit. But it remained inside the same problem of insufficient backing.

{{< support2 >}}

## Coinbase: no allocation, only perps

Coinbase can easily be placed into the same story by mistake, but that would not be correct. Coinbase did not offer a tokenized SpaceX allocation through xStocks. It offered pre-IPO perpetual futures. That means users received price exposure, not an attempt to obtain a tokenized share or a tokenized IPO allocation.

That is a major difference. With perps, the user does not buy a share that would have to be backed by real SpaceX shares in the background. They trade a derivative that reflects the expected market valuation. That is why Coinbase did not face the same question: “Did we get enough shares for all users?” Its product was different from the beginning.

## What did the whole story actually show?

SpaceX was the perfect first major test for tokenized IPO products because interest was huge and supply was limited. That is exactly why the limits of this model became visible very quickly. Tokenization can expand access, but it cannot remove the problem of scarcity. If there are not enough real shares, there are not enough tokenized representations that depend on those shares.

{{< support2 >}}

For small investors, the biggest lesson is not only that some platforms had to refund money. The main lesson is that “SpaceX exposure” does not always mean the same thing. Sometimes it is a real share, sometimes a tokenized representation, sometimes only a request for possible allocation, and sometimes a derivative such as a perpetual future.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

This was not only a refund story. It was a story about how hungry retail investors are for access to major IPOs — and how quickly the marketing of “democratization” reaches its limits when it collides with real scarcity.

Bybit, Binance Wallet and Bitget had to return money because there was not enough backing. Kraken could not fulfill all requests through the xStocks model. Coinbase was outside this problem because it offered only perps from the start. Behind everything remained the same fact: SpaceX brought only a small part of the company to the market, while demand was many times larger than the quantities users actually wanted to receive.

That is why the tokenized SpaceX IPO story matters for the future. If OpenAI, Anthropic or another major technology name comes next, the question will not only be whether a token can be created. The question will be whether enough underlying assets can really be secured for everyone who believes they are finally getting access.
