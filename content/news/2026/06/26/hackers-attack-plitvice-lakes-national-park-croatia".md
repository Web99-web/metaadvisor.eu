# Hackers Attack Plitvice Lakes National Park in Croatia

Plitvice Lakes National Park has announced that a cyber incident was detected within the public institution and required immediate action. According to the official notice, the incident was discovered two days earlier, the relevant state authorities were informed, security protocols were activated, and the investigation is being carried out in cooperation with cybersecurity experts.

So far, the goal of the attack is not known. It is not clear whether this was an extortion attempt, an attempt to disrupt operations, data theft, testing of vulnerabilities or another motive. The official notice states that, due to the ongoing investigation, there is currently no information about the amount of possible financial damage or any possible data compromise.

But in this story, the most important question may not be only who attacked the system. The key question is what happens when a digital system fails in a place where people depend on tickets, payments, invoices, food services and basic visitor services.

## A Cyberattack Is Not Just a Server Problem

When people hear about a cyber incident, many first think of servers, databases, backups and the IT department. But in a tourist destination such as Plitvice Lakes, digital systems are not separate from real life. They are connected to tickets, invoices, card payments, shops, restaurants and the entire organization of a visit.

Plitvice Lakes is not a square in a large city where a visitor can walk to another shop or café in five minutes. It is a large national park, a remote tourist destination and a place where people often arrive after a long journey, with children, elderly family members, organized groups or foreign tourists who do not know the local alternatives.

That is why the failure of a digital system in such an environment is not only a technical issue. It can very quickly become a problem of waiting, paying, issuing invoices and meeting basic needs.

## The Biggest Problem Is the Impact on Visitors

The official notice from the Park says that key business processes have continued, with possible operational difficulties for preventive reasons and to protect security. The Park remains open to visitors, and tickets can be purchased. It was also stated that free drinks and water were provided to guests at points near the lakes for refreshment.

That is an important detail, because it shows that the problem does not end with administration. If a visitor is inside the park, in the middle of the season, at temperatures that can rise above 30 degrees Celsius, the ability to buy water, food or basic refreshments is not a minor issue.

Even though Plitvice Lakes can often be cooler than other parts of Croatia, summer crowds, walking, waiting and a long tour can quickly become uncomfortable. If payment terminals, card payments or fiscal systems also have problems, a cyber incident becomes something an ordinary visitor immediately feels.

## Invoices Can Be Written Manually, But How Long Can a System Work That Way?

It is important to say that the failure of a digital system does not necessarily mean everything has to stop. In exceptional situations, invoices can be written manually, while respecting legal requirements and informing the relevant authorities. The Park’s official notice also states that a certain number of catering facilities are operating in accordance with prescribed legal regulations, with the competent authorities informed.

That is a useful example of emergency operation. At the same time, it shows how strongly modern tourism depends on digital infrastructure. Manual invoice issuing can help in a crisis, but it is not an ideal solution for prolonged and high-volume pressure, especially in a major tourist destination with many visitors.

If an attack lasts for several days, the question is no longer only: “Can an invoice somehow be issued?” The question becomes how long such a temporary mode can function without creating additional queues, pressure on staff and dissatisfaction among visitors.

## Cashless Tourism Needs a Real Fallback Plan

Plitvice Lakes has already been connected with discussions about card and cash payments in some hospitality facilities. Media reports have described situations in which visitors, including schoolchildren on excursions, could pay only by card in some facilities, while cash was not accepted.

That does not mean the entire Park is fully cashless, and the situation should not be oversimplified. But it does show why this topic matters. If part of the tourism infrastructure increasingly relies on cards, online systems, fiscal cash registers and digital terminals, then a cyberattack is not only an IT problem. It can become a problem of everyday operations.

A cashless system can be practical when everything works. It can speed up payments, reduce the need to handle cash and make business control easier. But if there is no sufficiently good fallback plan, that same system can become a vulnerability.

## What If a Visitor Cannot Even Buy a Bottle of Water?

This is the simplest, but perhaps the most important question. What happens if a visitor comes to a national park, far from other shops, after a long journey, in summer temperatures, and then cannot normally pay for even a bottle of water because the digital system is down?

That is exactly why the official information that free drinks and water were provided to guests near the lakes is especially important. It is a practical measure that shows people must be considered, not only systems.

Because visitors do not care whether the problem is in a server, terminal, fiscal system or security protocol. They see a queue, a closed facility, a rejected card, an impossible payment or a lack of information. In tourism, that can very quickly become a reputational problem.

## The Goal of the Attack Is Not Yet Known

At this moment, there should be no speculation about who is behind the attack or what the goal was. What is officially known is that the investigation is ongoing and that there is currently no confirmed information about financial damage or a possible compromise of data.

That matters because in incidents like this, people often jump too quickly to the conclusion that it must be ransomware, data theft or a politically motivated attack. All of those are possible scenarios, but without confirmation they remain assumptions.

For the public, the most important practical questions are now these: Are visitors’ personal data safe? How long will the difficult operating conditions last? Can people pay normally? Can invoices be issued? And is there a clear plan for situations when digital systems do not work?

## Plitvice Is a Warning for the Entire Tourism Sector

Plitvice Lakes is one of Croatia’s best-known tourist destinations and the country’s oldest national park. That is exactly why this incident has broader meaning.

If a similar problem can happen at such a famous destination, it can also happen to hotels, campsites, ferry systems, airports, museums, festivals, stadiums and other tourist locations.

The digitalization of tourism is useful and unavoidable. But digitalization without a fallback plan is not resilience. It is a new form of vulnerability.
