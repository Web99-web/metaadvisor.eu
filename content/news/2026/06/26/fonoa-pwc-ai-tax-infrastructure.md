# Fonoa, PwC and the race for AI tax infrastructure

Today, almost every company wants to say it is doing something with artificial intelligence. AI for marketing, AI for programming, AI for finance, AI for customer support. But when AI moves into tax, the story immediately becomes more serious. Tax is not an area where it is enough for a system to be “roughly right”. In large companies, one mistake can become a regulatory problem, a financial risk and a reputational issue. That is where Fonoa becomes interesting. Fonoa is a Croatian-Irish, or Croatian-founded, global tax-tech startup that automates indirect tax processes for large international companies. That includes VAT, GST, sales tax, tax ID checks, e-invoicing, filings, reporting and compliance processes behind global digital transactions.

At first glance, this sounds like dry tax infrastructure. But that is exactly where an important AI story begins. Fonoa announced two major pieces of news at the end of May: the company raised 110 million dollars in a Series C round and acquired PwC’s Indirect Tax Edge, a tax platform from one of the world’s largest consulting names.

The 110 million dollar figure sounds like the headline. But the second part of the story may be more important: PwC’s tax technology is now moving into the hands of the Fonoa team. This is not an ordinary software acquisition. PwC is one of the Big Four, and indirect tax is an area where trust, accuracy and audit trail cannot simply be claimed.

## Why PwC is an important part of the story

PwC’s Indirect Tax Edge is not a tool for small tax tasks, but an enterprise platform for global indirect tax compliance. Systems like this help large companies track tax obligations, prepare reports, manage filings, run analytics and keep data trails traceable across different markets.

This is becoming more important because tax is no longer only something processed after a transaction. The digital economy is pushing tax closer to real-time systems. More countries are introducing e-invoicing, digital reporting and stricter requirements for data proof and traceability. In other words, tax is becoming part of the business infrastructure itself. That is why the combination of Fonoa and PwC sounds interesting. One side brings startup speed and a technology platform, while the other brings global tax experience and enterprise credibility.

## AI in tax does not start with the model, but with data

The most important message of this story is not: “AI will solve tax.” That would be too simple. The real message is that AI in tax can only make sense if it works with high-quality, connected and verifiable data. Large companies often have tax data spread across multiple systems: ERP platforms, local tax tools, e-invoicing systems, compliance solutions and internal processes. If those systems are not connected, AI does not get the full picture. Then it does not solve the problem, but only processes incomplete information faster.

That is why Fonoa is interesting. The company’s story is not only about adding an AI layer on top of an existing tax process. The ambition is to connect data, transactions, filings, e-invoices and audit trail into a system that can support serious automation. That is the big difference between AI hype and real enterprise AI.

## The Croatian angle: why Fonoa matters

Fonoa is often described in international media as a Dublin-based startup. From a business perspective, that is correct. But for the Croatian audience, the other part also matters: Fonoa has strong Croatian roots, Croatian founders and a development presence in Zagreb. That is why this story is not just another foreign startup headline. It shows that global business infrastructure can be built from Croatia, and in a field that is neither simple, viral nor superficial.

Fonoa is not selling an app that millions of people will download to their phones. The company is building a system for large enterprises, in a segment where mistakes are expensive, rules are complex and trust is crucial. That may be less attractive than consumer AI apps, but from a business perspective it can be far more serious.

## Why this story goes beyond tax

This is not only a story about tax. It is an example of how AI will actually spread through large companies. Not through magical chatbots that “know everything”, but through specialized systems that understand data, processes, rules and responsibility. In tax, this becomes especially clear. If AI suggests a weak marketing slogan, the damage is small. If it makes a mistake in the tax treatment of thousands of transactions, the consequences can be serious.

That is why the winners in the enterprise AI market will probably not be only those offering a model, but those building the entire infrastructure layer around it: data, integrations, compliance, audit trail and domain expertise. That is exactly where Fonoa is trying to find its place.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

- Fonoa is not interesting only because of the 110 million dollar investment, but because of the acquisition of PwC’s Indirect Tax Edge.
- The real topic is not “AI for tax”, but tax infrastructure that allows AI to work with reliable data in the first place.
- The Croatian angle matters because Fonoa shows that a startup with Croatian roots can compete in the global enterprise league.
- The PwC part of the story adds extra weight because it connects Big Four tax knowledge with startup technology.
- Fonoa is also a reminder that Croatia does not only have talented developers, but also globally relevant digital products. Alongside Fonoa, that includes major Croatian app names such as Sofascore and Aircash, which show that domestic tech can reach far beyond the local market.
- This is a less glamorous, but much more serious part of the AI market: automation of processes where mistakes have real consequences.
