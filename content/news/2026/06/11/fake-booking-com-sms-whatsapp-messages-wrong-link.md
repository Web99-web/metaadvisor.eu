Fake Booking.com SMS and WhatsApp messages are becoming more convincing because they no longer always look like classic spam. Instead of poor translation and an unknown sender, a user may receive a message that mentions a hotel, a reservation, travel dates or the need to “urgently confirm” details. That is exactly why people click the wrong link: the message does not rely only on fear, but also on the trust users already have in a familiar platform.

The most dangerous part is not always the text itself, but the combination of details. If a WhatsApp profile shows a hotel name, “guest support”, “hotel manager”, a Booking.com email address or even a link to Booking.com, the user may assume that the message is really connected to the reservation. But those elements in a profile do not prove the sender’s identity. A WhatsApp profile can be edited manually by anyone, and scammers can add a known name, logo, description and even an official link in order to look trustworthy.

## Why do these messages look real?

With classic phishing, one quick look is often enough to notice that something is wrong. With travel bookings, the situation is different. People already expect communication from hotels, apartments, reception desks or booking platforms. If the trip is close and a message says that the reservation must be confirmed within a short time, the user may react quickly without checking further.

That is exactly what scammers rely on. The message creates pressure: “confirm your reservation”, “enter your details”, “your booking will be cancelled”, “card verification is required” or “click within a few hours”. When the familiar Booking.com name is added to that, the user may feel that they are simply completing a normal administrative step related to their trip.

## The profile may look convincing, but the link is what matters

It is important to understand one trick: a scammer can manually add a hotel name, a “Hotel manager” description, an email address that looks like a Booking.com address and even a real Booking.com link to a WhatsApp profile. That does not prove that the message really comes from Booking.com or from the hotel. The key issue is the link inside the message itself.

If the message asks the user to confirm a reservation, verify a card, confirm identity or enter personal data through a domain that is not the official Booking.com domain, the user should not click and should not enter any data. Even when the profile looks convincing, the real destination of the link may be completely different. That is where phishing often happens: the user trusts the profile and does not check where the link actually leads.

## Real booking details make users even more confused

Messages that contain correct or very convincing booking details are especially dangerous. If a message mentions the correct hotel, travel dates or guest name, the user is much more likely to believe that it is real communication. This does not automatically mean there has been a large public data breach, but it does mean that someone has obtained information that makes the message more credible.

In such cases, it is important not to look at just one message, but at the whole chain. A user may have booked accommodation, then expect a message from the hotel, then receive a WhatsApp or SMS message with a familiar context, then see Booking.com in the profile and only then click the link. Each of these steps increases the chance that the most important question is overlooked: does the link really lead to the official domain?

## How can you check whether a message is dangerous?

The first rule is simple: do not enter data through a link from an SMS or WhatsApp message if there is even the smallest doubt. Instead, the user should manually open the Booking.com app or the official website and check the reservation there. If there is a real problem, the notification should also be visible inside the official account or through official communication.

The second rule is to check the domain. Scammers often use domains that look similar at first glance but contain extra words, hyphens, unusual endings or names that refer to hotels, reservations or support. On a phone, a domain can look “convincing enough”, especially if the user is reading quickly and under pressure.

## Do not trust only the logo, name or profile picture

A logo, profile name and description are not proof of identity. This applies to WhatsApp, SMS, Telegram, email and social networks. A scammer can use a known brand, a hotel image or an official link in the profile description without having any real connection to the platform. In practice, the most important signal is not what appears in the profile, but what the message asks the user to do.

If a message asks for card details, a CVC code, login credentials, identity confirmation, a one-time code or an urgent payment, the user should stop. Booking, a hotel or a bank should never ask for sensitive data to be entered through a suspicious link from a message. It is especially dangerous when the message threatens that the reservation will be cancelled within a very short time.

## What should you do if you already clicked?

If the user only clicked the link but did not enter anything, the risk may be lower, but the page should still be closed and the process should not continue. If card details were entered, the user should immediately contact the bank or card issuer, block the card if necessary and monitor transactions. If login details were entered, the password on the official platform should be changed immediately and additional account protection should be enabled if available.

If the message is connected to a reservation, the user should contact the accommodation through the official channel inside the Booking.com app or by using a phone number listed on the official website, not the number from the suspicious message. It is also important to report the message to the platform, because such reports can help remove fake profiles and domains faster.

## Tourists are especially easy targets

Travellers are often tired, in a hurry or under stress. Some travel for the weekend, some are already at the airport, and some have a family reservation and do not want to risk losing accommodation. That is why hotel and travel scams can be especially effective. The attacker does not need to convince the user of a completely unknown story; it is enough to exploit a real situation the user is already in.

That is why education matters. People do not click such links because they are “naive”, but because the message is designed to look urgent, familiar and convincing. The scam succeeds when trust in a brand, a real travel context and short time pressure come together.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* Fake Booking.com SMS and WhatsApp messages can look very convincing because they use a familiar name, hotel context and sometimes booking details.
* Booking.com elements in a WhatsApp profile do not prove that the message really comes from Booking.com or the hotel.
* The key warning sign is often the link inside the message, especially if it leads to a domain that is not the official Booking.com domain.
* Users should not enter card, login or personal data through links from SMS or WhatsApp messages.
* If there is any doubt, the reservation should be checked manually through the official app or official website.
* Messages threatening quick cancellation should be checked especially carefully.
* If card details were entered, the bank or card issuer should be contacted immediately.
* If login details were entered, the password should be changed immediately and additional security should be enabled if possible.
* The most important rule: a familiar name in the profile is not proof of safety — always check the link domain.
