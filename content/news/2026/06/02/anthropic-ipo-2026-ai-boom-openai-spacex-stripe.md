Anthropic, the company behind the AI assistant Claude, has confidentially filed paperwork with the U.S. SEC for a possible initial public offering. That does not mean the IPO will happen immediately, and the share price, number of shares or ticker symbol are not yet known. But it does mean that Anthropic has taken an important first step toward the public market.

The news comes at a time when interest in artificial intelligence remains enormous. In recent years, Anthropic has positioned itself as one of OpenAI’s most important competitors, especially in enterprise use, model safety and more serious AI tools for companies. Claude is no longer just a chatbot that answers questions. For many businesses, it is becoming a work tool for writing, document analysis, coding, research and the automation of daily tasks.

## What does a confidential S-1 filing actually mean?

A confidential S-1 filing means that a company submits a draft of its public offering documentation to regulators, while the details are not yet publicly released. The U.S. SEC, or Securities and Exchange Commission, reviews the documents before the company approaches the broader market with full information about its business, risks, revenue and offering structure.

For Anthropic, this is especially important because the company is being valued at an extremely high level after a major private funding round. The public stock market, however, is not the same as a private investment round. While private investors often buy future potential, the public market asks much more concrete questions: how much does the company earn, how much does it spend, how fast is it growing and can that growth one day become profitable?

## Claude as a test for the entire AI economy

Anthropic is not coming to Wall Street simply as another technology company. It is arriving as a representative of a new generation of AI labs that spend enormous amounts on models, chips, cloud infrastructure and data centers. That is exactly why a potential IPO would not only be a test for Anthropic, but also a test for the entire AI boom.

If investors accept the story of Claude and its role in the business world, it would be a strong signal that the public market still believes in artificial intelligence as a new basic infrastructure of the digital economy. If investors become more cautious, it could mean that AI euphoria is beginning to collide with the question of real costs.

## OpenAI, SpaceX and Stripe in the same IPO year

The image also includes OpenAI, SpaceX and Stripe alongside Anthropic because 2026 is increasingly being described as a year of potentially historic technology IPOs. OpenAI remains the best-known name in generative artificial intelligence, SpaceX is being discussed as potentially one of the largest IPOs in history, while Stripe has for years been one of the most closely watched fintech candidates for a public listing.

Still, it is important to separate confirmed steps from market expectations. Anthropic has now officially submitted confidential paperwork. SpaceX is being mentioned in the media with more concrete IPO plans and possible major dates. OpenAI is seen as a logical candidate because of its enormous valuation and global recognition. Stripe is a different story: it has long been on the list of the most desired IPO candidates, but the actual timing of its listing depends on the company’s decision and market conditions.

## Small investors may not be able to buy at the IPO price

For small investors, the most important thing is to understand the difference between participating in an IPO and buying a stock after trading begins. Large funds and institutional investors often have an advantage in the initial allocation. Small investors can usually buy only after the stock appears on the exchange, for example through brokers that offer U.S. stocks.

After listing, the shares could appear at brokers that offer trading in U.S. stocks, such as Interactive Brokers, Trading 212, eToro, XTB or Revolut. However, investors must check whether they are buying a real share or a derivative/CFD, and whether the specific ticker is available in their country.

That means popular brokers such as eToro, Interactive Brokers, Trading 212, Revolut, XTB or local brokers may be useful only once the stock actually begins trading and if they add it to their offering. But that is not the same as buying at the IPO price. With very popular companies, the price after the market opens can rise sharply, but it can also fall quickly if the market decides that the initial excitement was excessive.

## AI boom or the beginning of a new bubble?

The biggest question is not only how much Anthropic is worth. The question is how much the entire AI boom is worth when it moves from private valuations to the public market. While companies are private, investors often look at the story, growth, technological advantage and the possibility of future dominance. Once they enter the stock market, a stricter test begins: revenue, margins, costs, competition and the real ability to generate profit.

That does not mean AI is a bubble. Artificial intelligence is already changing the way people work in programming, customer support, marketing, data analysis, law, education and many other sectors. But the question is whether valuations of hundreds of billions, or almost one trillion dollars, can justify the pace at which infrastructure is being built, experts are being hired and computing resources are being purchased.

## The year Wall Street tests the future

If Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX and perhaps Stripe really become part of the same major IPO wave, 2026 could be the year in which Wall Street tests the future of several industries at once: artificial intelligence, the space economy, digital payments and new technology infrastructure.

For investors, this could be a major opportunity, but also a major warning. Market history shows that the most famous companies are not always the best buys on the first trading day. Sometimes the public gains access only after the biggest private gains have already been made. That is why, with these IPOs, it will be important to look not only at the company name, but also at the price, revenue, costs and risks that will appear in the official documents.
