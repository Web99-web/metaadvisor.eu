# MEGA vs Google Drive: privacy or the Google ecosystem?

MEGA has attracted attention again by promoting a way to make the “de-Google” process easier, meaning the transfer of files from Google Drive to MEGA. At first glance, that sounds like an ordinary cloud feature. But it actually opens a much wider question: how used have we become to keeping our digital life inside one account, one email address and one ecosystem?

Google Drive did not succeed only because it is a good cloud service. It succeeded because it is directly connected to the Google account, Gmail, Android, Google Docs, Google Photos and the daily habits of billions of users. For many people, Google Drive is not something they consciously chose. It simply appeared together with email, smartphones, documents, photos, calendars and work routines.

## What is MEGA?

MEGA has existed since 2013 and emerged in the period after the shutdown of Megaupload, one of the best-known file-sharing services of its time. Unlike the old Megaupload, MEGA today presents itself as a privacy-cloud service for storage, backup and file sharing.

In simple terms, MEGA is used to store files in the cloud, create backups, share large files and synchronize content between computers and phones. It also offers chat and calling features. MEGA markets itself as encrypted cloud storage and emphasizes zero-knowledge / end-to-end encryption. The message is clear: your files belong to you, and the service should not be able to read them as ordinary open data.

## 20 GB vs 15 GB is not the whole story

For an ordinary user, the first comparison looks simple: MEGA offers 20 GB of free cloud storage, while a Google account offers up to 15 GB of storage shared between Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos. That is enough for people to immediately understand the basic difference.

But the real difference is not only in gigabytes. Google’s 15 GB comes with a huge advantage: everything is already connected. Gmail attachments, Google Docs, Sheets, Photos, Android backup, document sharing and Calendar are natural parts of the same account. The user does not have to learn a new service, search for a new app or change habits. Google is already there.

MEGA is trying to offer a different argument. It is not competing only by giving slightly more free storage, but by offering a more private backup, encryption and a second storage space outside the Google ecosystem. So maybe the real question is not “who has the better free plan”, but “where do you want to keep a copy of your most important files”.

## Google wins through habit

Google Drive has become a kind of digital cabinet for many people. In it we keep invoices, documents, contracts, photos, school materials, business presentations, spreadsheets, private notes and shared folders. Often we do not even think about the fact that we are using the cloud — we simply open Gmail, click Drive or share a Google Docs link.

That is Google’s biggest strength. It does not have to convince users to install a new tool. It is already built into email, smartphones, browsers, documents and work. If someone uses Android, Gmail and Google Docs, moving to another cloud is not only a technical file transfer. It is a change of routine.

That is why “de-Google” sounds simple, but in practice it is not simple. People are not moving only files from one folder to another. They are moving habits, documents, links, collaboration, family archives and business processes.

## MEGA tries to win with privacy

MEGA therefore does not necessarily have to beat Google Drive in order to be useful. It is enough to position itself as an alternative for people who do not want to keep everything only with Google. If a user has important documents, images, contracts, web projects or backups of business files, a second copy outside the Google ecosystem can be a smart decision.

This is especially important for small businesses, freelancers and people who work alone. If everything is tied to one Google account, a problem with that account can become a major risk. Losing access to email can mean losing communication. Losing access to Drive can mean losing documents. Losing access to Photos can mean losing years of private archives.

MEGA is trying to send a clear message here: you do not necessarily have to leave Google, but you can have a more private and separate copy of important files.

## De-Google does not have to mean fully leaving Google

When someone says “de-Google”, many people immediately imagine a radical move: closing a Google account, deleting Gmail, moving all documents and never coming back. For most people, that is not realistic. Google is too deeply built into everyday digital life.

But “de-Google” can mean something much more practical: not keeping absolutely everything in one place. Creating an additional backup. Copying important folders from Google Drive. Saving business documents and images somewhere else as well. Having a plan in case there is one day a problem with an account, password, access or platform rules.

In that sense, MEGA’s migration tool from Google Drive makes sense. It does not have to convince all users to leave Google. It is enough to remind them that a backup is not a real backup if it exists only in one account.

## Digital dependency is not only a social network

Recently, a lot has been said about dependence on social networks and what happens when Facebook, Instagram or an advertising account disappears overnight. But digital dependency is not only a social network. It is also email. It is cloud storage. It includes photos, documents, access credentials, passwords, AI tools, payment processors and business accounts.

If one large ecosystem controls too much, the user may not notice the problem while everything works. The problem becomes visible only when something goes wrong. Then the question is not only “which cloud is better”, but “do I have a second copy if I lose access to the first one”.

That is why the comparison between MEGA and Google Drive is interesting. It is not only a fight between two cloud services. It is a reminder that convenience often beats privacy — but convenience can also turn into dependency.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* Google Drive succeeded because it is not only cloud storage, but part of the Google account, Gmail, Android, Google Docs and users’ daily habits.

* MEGA is trying to offer a different value: privacy, encryption, 20 GB of free storage and an easier way out of Google Drive for those who want an additional copy.

* Google’s 15 GB and MEGA’s 20 GB are a good starting point for comparison, but the real difference is ecosystem versus privacy.

* For most people, fully leaving Google is not realistic, but it is very reasonable not to keep everything in one account.

* The smartest approach may be a combination: Google Drive for everyday work, MEGA or a similar service for an additional backup, and a local copy of the most important files.
