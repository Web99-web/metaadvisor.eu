# Mercuryo and Tangem: Apple Pay Makes Crypto Buying Simpler

Buying cryptocurrencies has been more complicated than it should be for many ordinary users. First, a person has to understand what an exchange is, then open an account, go through verification, add a card or bank account, buy crypto and only then think about where those digital assets should be stored. For experienced users, this may no longer feel like a major obstacle, but for new users, every extra step increases the chance that they will simply give up.

The news that Tangem users can now buy crypto through Apple Pay, with Mercuryo acting as the on-ramp partner, is therefore more interesting than it may first appear. This is not just another technical integration. It is an example of how crypto infrastructure is trying to move closer to the kind of payment experience that people already know from everyday digital transactions.

## Why is this integration important?

Tangem is known as a hardware wallet solution, meaning a product that allows users to store their digital assets themselves. This model is often described as self-custody. It means that the user does not keep crypto on a large exchange or centralized platform, but controls access to their own assets. The advantage is greater control, while the downside is that the initial learning curve can be more demanding for non-technical users.

This is where Mercuryo comes in. Mercuryo provides fiat-to-crypto infrastructure, or in simpler terms, a bridge between traditional money and cryptocurrencies. When people talk about an on-ramp, they mean this entry point: a user wants to buy crypto using money from a card, bank account or digital wallet. If that process is too long, unclear or feels unsafe, many users do not continue.

## Apple Pay reduces friction in the buying process

Apple Pay has a major psychological advantage in this context. People already use it to buy goods, apps, tickets, food and other services. The payment experience is fast, familiar and connected with biometric confirmation such as Face ID or Touch ID. When this kind of payment method appears inside a crypto wallet app, the user does not have to learn a completely new process from scratch.

The most important detail of this integration is that the purchase takes place inside the Tangem app. The user does not need to be redirected to an external website or an unfamiliar checkout page. These redirections are often a problem in crypto services, because users are not always sure whether they are still in a safe environment, whether they need to enter their details again and how they are supposed to return to the app.

## The Tangem card or ring may still be part of the process

However, it is important to explain precisely what Apple Pay changes here and what it does not change. Apple Pay simplifies the payment part and the confirmation of the card transaction, but the Tangem card or Tangem ring may still be needed for confirmation inside the wallet process, where applicable. In other words, it is not necessarily accurate to say that the user simply confirms with Face ID and the entire process is finished.

A more precise way to put it is that Apple Pay removes part of the friction around payment, while Tangem’s self-custody model may still include scanning a physical card or ring. This distinction matters, because Apple Pay does not change the logic of self-custody. It only makes the entry point into the buying process easier.

## Crypto becomes closer to ordinary digital payments

If the crypto industry wants to reach a wider audience, the user experience has to become simpler. People are used to modern apps working quickly and intuitively. When they order a ride, food or something online, payment happens in just a few taps. Crypto, however, often still feels like something that requires extra technical knowledge, patience and trust in several different platforms.

That is why this news matters, even though it is not as dramatic as a major Bitcoin rally or a new regulatory decision. It shows that crypto infrastructure is slowly moving away from the phase of being “only for technically advanced users” and toward an experience that resembles digital banking, fintech apps and everyday online payments. For users who already have an iPhone and use Apple Pay, buying crypto through a wallet app can feel much less intimidating.

## Easier buying does not mean lower risk

However, simpler payments do not mean lower risk. This may be the most important message to state clearly. If something becomes easier to buy, a user can make a decision faster — sometimes too fast. Cryptoassets can still fall sharply, the market remains volatile, and users need to understand that a simple checkout does not turn a risky asset into safe savings.

This integration therefore has two sides. On one hand, it is positive because it removes unnecessary barriers and makes the self-custody approach more accessible. On the other hand, precisely because buying becomes easier, user education becomes even more important. If someone can buy crypto in just a few taps, they also need to understand just as quickly and clearly that they can lose part or all of the money they put in.

## What does this mean for Mercuryo, Tangem and Apple Pay?

For Tangem, this is a step toward a better user experience. For Mercuryo, it confirms that on-ramp infrastructure is increasingly being embedded directly into apps and wallets. For Apple Pay, it is another example of how familiar payment systems are entering areas that were previously reserved for crypto exchanges and more technically advanced users.

The broader message is clear: crypto is trying less and less to present itself as a separate world, and more and more to become part of the digital financial experience that users already know. If someone can buy Bitcoin, Ethereum or stablecoins in a way that feels similar to paying for a subscription, an app or an online order, the border between the traditional fintech world and crypto infrastructure becomes thinner.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

News like this may not look spectacular, but it is very important for real adoption. Big changes do not come only through price movements, but also through easier use. When buying crypto becomes an experience that ordinary users can understand, the market moves closer to broader adoption.

Three things are especially important here:

* Apple Pay reduces payment friction and makes the first step easier.
* Mercuryo shows how important on-ramp solutions are for expanding the crypto market.
* Tangem keeps the self-custody model, while trying to remove part of the complicated user experience.

Still, simplicity must not replace caution. Apple Pay can make buying faster, Mercuryo can simplify the on-ramp, and Tangem can offer a self-custody experience. But the investment decision still remains with the user. Easier access does not mean a safer outcome.

The conclusion is simple: crypto adoption does not depend only on the price of Bitcoin or on major regulatory decisions. It also depends on whether ordinary users can understand what they are doing, how they are paying and where their digital assets are stored. Mercuryo, Tangem and Apple Pay point in exactly that direction — less friction, an easier entry point and a stronger focus on user experience.
