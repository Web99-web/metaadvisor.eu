Today’s trading day was not an ordinary correction. It looked more like a moment when investors suddenly started reassessing everything that had looked almost “safe” in recent months: AI stocks, chip companies, Bitcoin, Ethereum, altcoins and even gold. What yesterday still looked like the continuation of a major rally turned today into a broad risk-off day.

What makes the move especially interesting is that the pressure did not stop in one sector. Broadcom, Nvidia, Meta, Bitcoin, Ethereum, Zcash, NEAR and HYPE came under pressure almost at the same time. That is an important signal because it shows that the market was not only punishing one bad headline or one disappointing result. Investors started reducing risk in areas that had previously risen strongly or carried very high expectations.

## Arthur Hayes as the first sign of nervousness

One of the first signals of nervousness came already yesterday, when Arthur Hayes, co-founder of BitMEX, said he had sold his HYPE and NEAR positions. That was especially interesting because Hayes had previously held very optimistic views on both tokens. He had seen HYPE as a token with major upside potential, while for NEAR he had talked about the possibility of a strong rise in the coming years. When someone who had been publicly bullish suddenly says it is “time to take profit”, the market reads it as a psychological signal.

Hayes cited rising energy prices, geopolitical tensions and the possibility that large AI IPOs could drain liquidity from existing markets. That is an interesting angle because it connects crypto, AI and stocks into one broader story. If OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX or other major AI and future-tech companies move toward public listings, part of the capital that has so far been looking for opportunities in crypto tokens and risky tech stocks could be redirected toward new large IPO stories.

## AI stocks and chips under pressure

Today, that nervousness also received macro confirmation. US employment data came in stronger than expected, which sounds positive at first. But for Wall Street, it currently means something else: if the US labor market remains strong, the Federal Reserve has less reason to cut interest rates quickly. And when the market starts believing that rates may stay high for longer than hoped, the first assets to suffer are the stocks and tokens most dependent on future growth.

That is why AI and chip stocks were hit hardest. Broadcom was one of the most dramatic examples. AVGO fell in a broader range from around 507 USD toward 383 USD, a huge move for a company that had become one of the symbols of AI infrastructure. Nvidia fell from around 218 USD toward 205 USD. At first glance, that may not look as dramatic as the moves in smaller tokens, but for a company the size of Nvidia, even that kind of move erases enormous market value.

Meta also fell sharply, from around 629 USD toward 583 USD during the trading day, before partially recovering. In Meta’s case, another question also opened up: how much will AI infrastructure really cost, and will the company need to look for new ways to finance it? That matters because AI is no longer just a beautiful story about the future. It is now a story about chips, data centers, electricity, cooling, servers and billions of dollars in new investment.

Interestingly, Google, or Alphabet, did not move nearly as sharply. While some AI and chip stocks saw large declines, Alphabet moved roughly from 370 USD toward 365 USD, which was relatively calm compared with the rest of the market. That may mean the market does not currently see Alphabet as being as overheated as some other AI favorites, or that investors still see a more stable base in Alphabet’s advertising, cloud and search businesses.

## Crypto selloff: Bitcoin, Ethereum, ZEC, NEAR and HYPE

The crypto market was much more nervous. Bitcoin fell from around 64,000 USD toward 59,000 USD, again raising the question of how much leverage was sitting in the system. Ethereum fell over two days from around 1,850 USD toward the 1,528 USD area. Solana, NEAR, HYPE and other riskier tokens also came under pressure. When Bitcoin breaks important levels, altcoins often fall even harder because investors usually exit the riskiest positions first.

A particularly dramatic example was Zcash. ZEC fell from around 510 USD toward 260 USD, showing how quickly sentiment can change in tokens that previously had strong momentum. Moves like that are not only about normal selling. They are often a combination of panic, liquidations, weaker liquidity and automatic position closures.

On days like this, markets do not fall in a straight line. First, doubt appears. Then selling begins. Then stop-loss orders and liquidations are triggered, and finally the decline spreads to assets that may not even have had a direct negative headline. That is why it feels as if “everything is falling at once”. In reality, the market is simply reducing risk wherever it can.

## Why did gold fall too?

Interestingly, gold also fell. In normal times, many people expect gold to rise when stocks and crypto fall. But today was not a classic flight to safety. Gold fell from the area around 4,500 USD toward 4,300 USD per ounce because stronger US data increased expectations of higher interest rates.

When bond yields rise, gold becomes less attractive because it does not pay interest by itself. That is why gold also came under pressure today, despite nervousness in other markets. This is a very important detail. Today’s selloff was not just “fear”. It was fear of higher interest rates. When the market believes that rates will remain high, not only risky stocks and crypto can fall — gold can fall too.

## AI is no longer just a promise, but a bill

In this story, AI appears as the main theme, but no longer only as a promise. AI is now also a cost. A year or two ago, it was often enough for a company to say it had an AI strategy, and the market would react positively. Today, investors are asking: who pays for the chips, who pays for the data centers, who pays for the electricity and when will all of this come back as profit?

That is why today’s decline does not mean the AI story is over. But it does mean the market has entered a more serious phase. In that phase, not everything with the label AI, crypto, tokenization or future tech will rise. The winners will be those that can show real revenue, cost control, users, liquidity and a realistic return on investment.

Something similar applies to crypto. Bitcoin and Ethereum remain the main digital assets, but today’s decline shows how strongly leverage can accelerate every weakness. When markets rise, leverage looks like a smart strategy. When markets fall, leverage becomes a mechanism of forced selling. Then the decline no longer depends only on investor sentiment, but also on automatic liquidations.

The most important point is that today did not have only one story. It was not only Broadcom, not only Nvidia, not only Bitcoin, not only gold and not only Arthur Hayes. It was a combination of several signals: stronger US data, fear of higher interest rates, doubts about AI valuations, possible large AI IPOs, falling chip stocks, crypto liquidations and a broader exit from risky assets.

That is why today’s selloff should be seen as a warning. The market does not only punish bad news. It also punishes expectations that are too high when the question suddenly appears: how much will the whole story really cost?
