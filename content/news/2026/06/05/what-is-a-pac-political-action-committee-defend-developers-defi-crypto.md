In American politics, the abbreviation PAC appears often, but for many European readers the term is not always clear. PAC stands for Political Action Committee, a political committee used to finance campaigns. In the simplest terms, it is an organization that collects money and uses it to support political candidates, laws or political goals.

Money plays a major role in U.S. politics. Candidates need funding for campaigns, advertising, travel, advisers and media visibility. That is why different industries, associations and interest groups create PACs to support politicians who represent their interests. Banks do it, energy companies do it, technology firms do it, unions do it, the pharmaceutical industry does it — and now the crypto industry is doing it more and more.

## Is a PAC actually a form of lobbying?

Yes, in a broader sense, a PAC is part of the lobbying ecosystem, but it does not work in the same way as a traditional lobbyist who meets politicians and tries to convince them to support a certain law. A PAC primarily collects money and financially supports candidates, campaigns or advertisements that match its goal.

For European readers, this can feel unusual because politics and money are openly connected. But the difference is transparency: in the American system, this kind of campaign financing at least formally goes through registered committees, filings and public records. In countries with less transparent political cultures, the influence of interests is often not visible through official donations, but through informal arrangements, favors and money that never appears in public registers.

## What is the Defend Developers PAC?

**Defend Developers PAC** is a new crypto political committee that wants to support American politicians who understand the legal protection problem facing blockchain and DeFi developers. Its focus is not simply to promote crypto in general, but specifically to protect people who write software, smart contracts and open-source code.

According to the announcement, **Defend Developers PAC** plans to raise and donate more than six figures, meaning more than $100,000, across several important races in the U.S. midterm elections. That is not the scale of the largest crypto political funds, but it is enough to support targeted candidates who already understand DeFi, blockchain technology and the issue of developer liability.

## How is it different from larger crypto PACs?

**Defend Developers PAC** is not trying to be as large as **Fairshake**, the best-known and most powerful crypto super PAC in the United States. Nor is it expected to immediately reach the level of mid-sized crypto political funds such as **Fellowship PAC**, linked to **Tether**, or the **Digital Freedom Fund**, linked to **Tyler and Cameron Winklevoss** of **Gemini**.

It is also important that **Defend Developers PAC** is registered as a hybrid PAC. That means it can make limited direct donations to candidates under the rules of the U.S. Federal Election Commission, while also accepting larger corporate contributions for independent advertisements and political campaigns. A similar hybrid model is used by the **Blockchain Leadership Fund**, founded by **Anchorage Digital** and **Chainlink**.

## Why does this matter for DeFi?

DeFi, or decentralized finance, does not work like a traditional bank or exchange with a director, a counter and a central account. In many cases, it is built around open code, smart contracts and users who connect their own wallets. If regulation is written too broadly, there is a risk that developers could be punished for something they did not directly control.

This is where the issue of developer liability appears. If a user later abuses a protocol, launders money, bypasses rules or causes harm, should the developer who wrote part of the code be treated as a financial intermediary? For the DeFi industry, this is one of the most important questions because it could determine whether projects dare to build in the United States at all.

## The line between software and a financial intermediary

For ordinary readers, it can be explained like this: if someone builds a road, that person is not automatically responsible for every driver who later breaks a traffic rule. But if someone operates a toll booth, controls traffic and earns money from every transaction, their responsibility may look different.

Crypto regulation is now trying to determine where exactly the line is between neutral infrastructure and an active financial intermediary. If a developer only writes open-source software, that is not the same as operating a centralized exchange. But if someone controls a protocol, controls user funds or collects fees in a way that resembles a financial institution, regulators may look at it differently.

## Why is the crypto industry entering politics?

The crypto industry has understood that its future will not be decided only by the market, but also by laws. If Congress passes rules that are too broad, DeFi developers could end up being treated like banks, broker-dealers or financial institutions simply because they wrote code. That could push projects, capital and talent out of the United States.

On the other hand, a completely unregulated market is not a solution either. The crypto industry has already seen too many scams, hacks, failed platforms and false promises. That is why the key question is balance: how to protect users without destroying the open development of blockchain technology.

## A PAC is not an ideal system, but it is a visible system

For many people, PACs may look like proof that money has too much influence over politics. That is a legitimate criticism. But there is another side as well: it is better for the public to know who is financing which political idea than for influence to happen under the table, without receipts, without registers and without accountability.

In that sense, a PAC is not a perfect system, but it is at least a visible one. If the crypto industry wants to influence rules, it is better for that influence to be visible through registered committees and public records than through hidden arrangements. For DeFi developers, this may be a way to gain a political voice before laws are written without an understanding of the technology.

## Why does this matter outside the United States?

This topic is not just an American political technicality. The United States is one of the most important markets for finance, technology and digital asset regulation. If U.S. lawmakers set rules for DeFi developers, those rules can also affect other countries, exchanges, funds, investors and global projects.

If the rules are too strict, innovation may leave the United States. If they are too weak, users remain unprotected. If a good balance is found, DeFi could gain a clearer legal framework and more serious institutional development. That is why **Defend Developers PAC** is not just another American political committee, but a sign that the crypto industry is entering a new, more politically mature phase.
