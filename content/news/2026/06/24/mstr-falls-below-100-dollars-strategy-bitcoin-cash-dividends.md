# MSTR Falls Below $100: Strategy Is No Longer Just a Bitcoin Story

MSTR, the stock of Michael Saylor’s company Strategy, has fallen below $100 at a time when the broader crypto market is under pressure. As this article is being written, MSTR is trading around $95, after falling at one point during the day to around $92.17. Bitcoin also dropped toward the $59,000 area before partially recovering toward around $60,650. This adds further pressure to the market narrative around Strategy, because many investors see MSTR as a leveraged form of Bitcoin exposure.

This is an important moment because MSTR has been viewed for years almost as a market proxy for Bitcoin. When Bitcoin rises, Strategy’s aggressive Bitcoin accumulation model can look very powerful. But when Bitcoin weakens and MSTR breaks important psychological and technical levels, the story quickly expands beyond Bitcoin itself.

The market is no longer asking only how many Bitcoin Strategy holds. It is increasingly asking how much cash the company has, how much it must pay in dividends on preferred shares, how long it can continue at the same pace and whether common shareholders could face additional dilution.

## The Fall Below $100 Changes the Technical and Psychological Picture

The $100 mark is not only a technical level. It is also a psychological boundary that the market easily remembers. When a stock like MSTR falls below such a round level, investors begin to reassess not only the chart, but also the entire story behind the stock.

Cointelegraph warned about a potential head-and-shoulders pattern on MSTR’s monthly chart, with an important support zone around $100–$105. As this article is being written, MSTR is already below that zone and is trading around $95, after falling during the day to around $92.17.

That does not mean further downside is certain. Technical patterns are not prophecies. But the fact that the stock is already below the previously mentioned support zone means the market is no longer only talking about a possible technical risk. That risk is now already visible in the price.

## Strategy Is No Longer Just a Bitcoin Story

Strategy has built its identity around Bitcoin for years. Michael Saylor became one of the most visible public advocates of BTC, and the company became a symbol of corporate Bitcoin accumulation. For part of the market, MSTR was a way to buy Bitcoin exposure through a stock.

But MSTR is not Bitcoin. It is the stock of a company. That company has a balance sheet, obligations, a financing structure, preferred shares, dividends, possible new share issuance and market expectations.

In good times, that structure can amplify gains. In weaker times, it can amplify pressure. That is why MSTR falling below $100 is not only a chart detail. It is a moment when the market begins to reassess the entire model.

## CryptoQuant: Pause Bitcoin Buying and Rebuild Cash

According to a report cited by CoinDesk, on-chain analytics firm CryptoQuant believes Strategy should temporarily pause its Bitcoin purchases, rebuild its cash reserve and be more disciplined about when it buys.

That is significant because the market is now paying more attention to the financing structure. Strategy has used different instruments to continue its Bitcoin strategy, including preferred shares such as STRC. Such instruments carry dividend obligations, and the market is now watching whether the company can comfortably maintain that structure.

According to the report, Strategy’s U.S. dollar reserve has fallen by 38% since the start of 2026, while annual dividend obligations have nearly quadrupled to around $1.2 billion. Dividend coverage has reportedly fallen from more than seven years to about 14 months.

That does not mean a forced sale of Bitcoin is immediately ahead. But it does mean the financial room for error is smaller than before.

## STRC Matters Because It Shows Trust in the Structure

STRC is a preferred share issued by Strategy that is supposed to trade around its $100 face value. When such an instrument falls significantly below that level, the market is sending a message that it wants a higher yield to take on that risk.

That matters because preferred shares are not a side issue. They are part of the financing mechanism through which Strategy tries to continue its Bitcoin strategy without directly selling Bitcoin. If that mechanism becomes more expensive, weaker or less convincing, the pressure can spill over to ordinary MSTR shareholders.

In other words, the question is not only how much BTC Strategy owns. The question is also how much it costs to maintain that strategy — and who bears the burden if the market loses confidence.

## Shareholder Dilution Becomes an Important Topic

If Strategy wants to continue paying dividends, rebuild its cash reserve and keep its Bitcoin strategy at the same time, one option is to issue new shares. That can bring in fresh cash, but it also creates dilution risk for existing shareholders.

This is a sensitive point. A shareholder who buys MSTR may think they are buying a “Bitcoin story”, but they are also buying the entire corporate structure around that story. If the company has to issue new shares to maintain the model, existing shareholders may own a smaller share of the same story.

In a bull market, that risk often stays in the background. In a falling market, it comes to the foreground much faster.

## Small Investors Often Feel These Drops the Most

That is why such drops often hit small investors hardest, especially those who enter late, while professional market participants wait for moments of weakness, forced selling or panic to buy cheaper or close their positions.

With MSTR, this is especially sensitive because the stock is not seen only as an ordinary stock, but as amplified Bitcoin exposure. When Bitcoin falls and MSTR simultaneously breaks psychological levels such as $100, some small investors may react emotionally, while professional participants usually watch levels, liquidity, volatility and risk structure.

## What Should Be Watched Now?

After the fall below $100, it is important to watch whether MSTR can quickly return above the previously mentioned support zone of $100–$105, or whether that zone becomes a new resistance area. This matters not because one number alone decides the fate of the company, but because markets often use such levels as signals of changing sentiment.

The second important question is Bitcoin. If BTC stabilizes and starts moving higher again, some of the pressure on MSTR could ease. But if Bitcoin remains weak, the market will likely focus even more on Strategy’s financing structure and not only on the size of its Bitcoin holdings.

The third question is cash. If the discussion around cash reserves, dividend obligations and STRC continues, MSTR could increasingly be viewed not only as a Bitcoin stock, but as a complex financial instrument with additional layers of risk.

## Bitcoin and MSTR Are Not the Same Thing

This may be the most important lesson. Bitcoin can have its own long-term thesis. Someone may believe in Bitcoin as a digital asset, a hedge against the monetary system or a new financial infrastructure. But MSTR is not the same as holding Bitcoin directly.

MSTR is the stock of a company that uses financial instruments to buy Bitcoin. That means Bitcoin risk comes with corporate risk: obligations, dividends, market confidence, cost of capital and the possibility of dilution.

That is why MSTR can move differently from Bitcoin. In some periods, it can rise faster. In others, it can fall harder.
