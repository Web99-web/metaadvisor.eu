# Proton Mail Now Offers Gmail Connection: Privacy, Convenience or a New Risk?

Proton Mail is a Swiss email service that has positioned itself for years as a more private alternative to Gmail. Its message is simple: email should not become just another channel for tracking users, serving ads and collecting behavioral data. Unlike Gmail, which is deeply connected to Google’s wider ecosystem of search, YouTube, Android, Google Drive and advertising, Proton emphasizes privacy, encryption and a business model less dependent on large advertising platforms.

That is why Proton’s new message, “Use Gmail securely in Proton Mail”, may look confusing at first. If Proton is supposed to be an alternative to Gmail, why is it now offering a Gmail connection? The likely answer is very practical: most people cannot abandon an email address they have used for years overnight. Proton is trying to build a bridge between the old email world and a more privacy-focused one.

## What is Proton actually offering?

According to Proton’s own explanation, users can connect a Gmail account to Proton Mail. This may include importing existing Gmail messages, forwarding new Gmail messages into Proton Mail and sending messages from a Gmail address directly through the Proton interface.

That does not mean Proton and Gmail become the same service. Gmail remains a Google account, and Proton remains a Proton account. The difference is that users can access part of their Gmail functionality inside Proton Mail, without constantly switching between two apps or inboxes.

For people who are thinking about moving from Gmail to a more private email provider, this can be useful. They do not have to immediately change their email address across every account, inform every contact or risk missing important messages. They can test Proton gradually while Gmail remains available as their old address.

## Privacy: what is the main difference between Proton and Gmail?

Gmail is convenient, fast and deeply integrated with other Google services. That is exactly why so many people use it. But the price of that convenience is dependence on a large digital ecosystem where user data, behavior and habits are an important part of the broader business model.

Proton positions itself differently. Its message is that email should be more private, more secure and less connected to advertising-based tracking. Proton emphasizes encryption, content protection and a business model where revenue comes from users who pay for additional features, rather than from advertising built around user data.

This does not mean every Gmail user is “unsafe”, and it does not mean Proton is a magic solution for everything. But the philosophy is clearly different. Gmail is part of the Google system. Proton wants to present itself as a more private way out of that system.

## The key practical question: what about free storage limits?

This may be the most practical question an ordinary user should ask before clicking “Connect your email”.

Both Gmail and Proton have their own free storage limits. If someone has used Gmail for many years, with thousands of messages, invoices, PDF files, photos and attachments, they need to understand what happens when the account is connected. Are Gmail messages only displayed through Proton? Are they copied into Proton? Is the full old archive imported, or only part of it? Are only new messages forwarded from that moment on?

That difference matters. If messages are only displayed or forwarded from a certain point forward, the risk for Proton storage may be smaller. But if a larger part of the Gmail archive is copied into Proton Mail, the user could quickly use up the free Proton Mail storage. That could lead to a situation where they have to delete old messages, stop the import or move to a paid plan.

That is why this option should not be clicked automatically, especially if the Gmail account is many years old and full of large attachments. Before connecting the accounts, users should carefully check which options are offered and what Proton is actually importing.

## Can Google see the Proton inbox?

According to Proton’s communication, connecting Gmail with Proton Mail does not mean Google receives access to the Proton inbox. Google may see that an account has been connected to the Proton app, but that is not the same as being able to read Proton messages.

Still, every connection between two accounts should be treated carefully. When Gmail is connected to any external app, the user must approve certain permissions. Before confirming, it is worth reading what is being requested: access to messages, permission to send messages, contact import, calendar access or something else.

Extra caution is especially important with business Gmail accounts, recovery accounts for other services, Google accounts connected to domains, advertising, financial tools or important documents. Such accounts should not be connected out of habit or curiosity, but only after the user understands exactly what is being allowed.

## Who is this feature useful for?

This feature is most useful for people who want to move gradually from Gmail to Proton Mail. If someone already wants a more private email service but is worried about making a sudden switch, Gmail connection can be a practical transition step.

It can also be useful for users who want to test Proton without fully giving up Gmail. They can see whether they like Proton’s interface, inbox organization, sending experience and privacy approach before making a bigger change.

But for a user who has no clear need to connect accounts, the best decision may be simple: close the notification and keep using the accounts separately. The new feature is not a security requirement and it is not something that must be activated.

## What should a normal user check before connecting?

Before connecting Gmail with Proton, the user should check several things. First, how large is the Gmail account and how many large attachments does it contain? Second, how much free space is available in Proton Mail? Third, does Proton import the entire old archive or only part of the messages? Fourth, can the import be limited or stopped later? And fifth, what Google permissions are requested during the connection process?

That may sound like a technical detail, but for a free-plan user it can be decisive. One careless import of an old Gmail account could turn a private and tidy inbox into a full account that immediately requires cleanup or an upgrade.

Metaadvisor sees this as the most important practical point in the story. Privacy is the headline, but storage limits may be the first problem ordinary users actually feel.

## Our View (Metaadvisor)

- Proton’s new Gmail option can be a useful bridge for users who want to leave Gmail but cannot change their old email address overnight.
- The biggest value of Proton is not that it can “replace Gmail with one click”, but that it offers a different email philosophy: more privacy, less advertising-based tracking and a stronger focus on security.
- Still, account connection should not be treated lightly. Gmail often contains years of archived messages, large attachments, business communication and recovery links for other services.
- The storage question is especially important. If Gmail messages are imported into Proton, the user must understand how much Proton Mail storage will be used and whether the free limit could be reached quickly.
- That is why this function should be seen as a practical tool, not as something to activate automatically without thinking.
- For users who seriously want a more private email service, Proton Mail may be an interesting alternative. But the first step should not be a quick click. It should be a simple check: what is being connected, what is being imported, which permissions are being granted and how much storage remains?
