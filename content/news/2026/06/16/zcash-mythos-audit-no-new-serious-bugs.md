# Zcash after the Orchard bug: Mythos audit found no new serious vulnerabilities

Zcash is once again in the focus of the crypto public, but this time the story is not only about the price of ZEC. After the recently discovered serious bug in the Orchard shielded pool, Zcash founder Zooko Wilcox said that an additional security audit using Anthropic’s Claude Mythos AI model found no new serious vulnerabilities in the Zcash protocol.

That is an important message for a project built on privacy, cryptography and trust in mathematical design. But it is equally important to understand what this message means — and what it does not mean. An audit that finds no new serious bugs is not the same as proof that there was never any risk. Especially not in a privacy coin, where part of the system is intentionally designed to hide transaction data.

## What happened with Zcash?

In early June, Zcash temporarily suspended Orchard transactions after a vulnerability was discovered in the shielded pool. Orchard is the part of the Zcash protocol that enables more private transactions, meaning transactions where not all details are publicly visible as they are in transparent blockchain transfers.

The issue was especially sensitive because it was a so-called forgery bug. According to available information, the vulnerability could theoretically have allowed the creation of unauthorized value, or fake ZEC, inside the private part of the system. That is one of the most sensitive things that can happen to a privacy coin: if there is a possibility of silently creating new coins, the question of trust becomes much bigger than an ordinary technical patch.

The bug was discovered by security researcher Taylor Hornby, with help from Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.8 model. After the report, Zcash developers launched an emergency response, Orchard functionality was temporarily paused, and then restored through an emergency upgrade.

## Mythos audit after the patch

After that event, Shielded Labs requested an additional AI security audit of the Zcash protocol using Anthropic’s Claude Mythos model. According to Zooko Wilcox, that audit found no “more serious bugs” in Zcash.

That is positive news for the Zcash community. After panic, a price drop and questions about trust in the shielded pool, an additional review that does not find new serious vulnerabilities can calm part of the market. But precision matters: it does not mean that Zcash is now “proven perfect” or that no bug can exist. It means that this audit did not find new serious vulnerabilities.

In security, that difference matters a lot. An audit can reduce risk, but it cannot magically remove all uncertainty. Especially when it involves a complex cryptographic protocol and private transactions.

## AI found a problem that humans had missed

This story is also interesting because of the role of artificial intelligence. Claude Opus 4.8 reportedly helped Taylor Hornby find the Orchard bug that had existed for years. That raises a major question: are we entering a period in which frontier AI models become key tools for auditing complex software?

On one side, that is good news. If AI can help experts find vulnerabilities that earlier audits missed, it can improve the security of the entire crypto ecosystem. Blockchain, DeFi, privacy coins, wallets and smart contracts are becoming increasingly complex. Human auditors remain essential, but AI can become an additional layer of review.

On the other side, there is also a problem. The same models that can help good researchers find vulnerabilities can, in the wrong hands, help attackers too. That is why there is already a broader debate around Anthropic Mythos / Claude Fable 5: how powerful should a public AI security tool be, and under what restrictions?

## “No new serious bugs” is not the same as “no risk”

For the market, the statement that the Mythos audit found no new serious bugs sounded reassuring. But users should understand what such a statement really means. It means that an additional analysis did not find new major problems in the reviewed part of the protocol. It does not mean that every possible risk has been removed forever.

With Zcash, this is especially important because privacy is the project’s core value. Zcash uses shielded transactions and zero-knowledge technology to hide transaction details. That is exactly why Zcash is interesting to users who want greater financial privacy. But that same privacy makes it harder to publicly and simply verify everything that happened inside the shielded pool.

The Zcash Foundation previously said there was no evidence that the vulnerability had been exploited and that no unauthorized value creation had been detected. That matters. But in the world of privacy coins, trust does not return through one sentence. It returns through time, transparent communication, additional audits and technical explanations that the community can examine.

## Why this matters for the entire crypto sector

This is not only a Zcash story. It is a story about the entire crypto sector, which increasingly depends on complex code, cryptography and tools that the average user cannot verify alone. A user can see the token price. They can see the chart. They can read a team announcement. But very rarely can they personally understand whether a cryptographic protocol is truly secure.

That is why security audits matter so much. But they are not perfect either. According to available information, the Orchard bug existed for years and survived earlier reviews. That shows that neither large projects, nor privacy protocols, nor previous audits are an absolute guarantee.

An AI audit can be an important new layer of security. But it must not become a new form of blind trust. If the community simply replaces “we trust the developers” with “we trust the AI model”, the problem is not solved. AI can help, but responsibility still remains with people, organizations and open review.

## Trust is hard to rebuild

ZEC recovered after the initial panic, but price is not the only measure of trust. With a privacy coin, trust is a deeper question. Users do not only ask whether the token will rise. They ask whether they can trust that the system was not silently compromised.

That is the hardest part of the story. If AI finds a bug and another AI audit does not find new serious problems, that is positive. But the market and users still have to process the fact that a critical vulnerability could exist for a long time in a part of the system that was supposed to be the foundation of privacy.

That means Zcash now has two battles. The first is technical: to show that the protocol is safer after the patch and that additional reviews continue. The second is communication: to explain to users what happened, what was fixed and why they should trust the system again.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* Zcash received an additional AI audit using Anthropic’s Claude Mythos model after the serious Orchard bug.
* According to Zooko Wilcox, that audit found no new serious vulnerabilities in the Zcash protocol.
* The earlier Orchard bug was discovered by Taylor Hornby with help from Claude Opus 4.8, showing how important AI may become for security reviews.
* Still, “no new serious bug found” is not the same as “there is no risk anymore”. With privacy coins, trust has to be rebuilt especially carefully.
* The most important lesson is not only that AI can find vulnerabilities. The lesson is that AI can support security, but it cannot restore trust by itself if a system has already shown that a vulnerability could exist for a long time.
