# BTC rose on SpaceX IPO day: crypto did not crash

Bitcoin rose after the SpaceX IPO: the crypto market did not experience the expected crash. After the SpaceX IPO, many expected the crypto market to come under pressure. The logic was simple: the biggest IPO of the year could have pulled attention, liquidity and speculative capital away from Bitcoin, altcoins and DeFi tokens. If the whole world was looking at SpaceX, why would money stay in crypto?

But the market did not show a broad crash. Bitcoin did not fall; it rose from intraday lows into higher trading zones. HYPE remained one of the most important crypto stories of the day because Hyperliquid was tied to the SpaceX pre-IPO/perps narrative, even though the token later cooled from around 61–62 dollars toward the 58–59 dollar area. Ethereum and Solana were weaker, but the overall picture was not panicked.

## Bitcoin did not fall — it rose from intraday lows

Bitcoin moved during the day from the area around 62,800 dollars toward an intraday high above 64,000 dollars. It was not an explosive rally, but it was the opposite of what many expected: there was no flight out of Bitcoin because of the SpaceX IPO.

That is an important detail. If the market had really reacted with panic, Bitcoin would probably have tested lower levels instead of moving higher. Instead, BTC showed that the largest crypto asset still has its own rhythm, even on a day when SpaceX was the biggest financial story.

That is exactly why the title of this story is not “crypto stayed stable”, but “Bitcoin rose”. Stability would be too weak a word for a day when many expected a drop, while BTC moved up from intraday lows.

## Ethereum and Solana were not strong, but there was no breakdown

Ethereum and Solana did not look as strong as Bitcoin. ETH traded slightly weaker, while SOL was also in the red or close to neutral territory, depending on the moment of measurement. A similar picture appeared in some larger altcoins such as XRP, ADA and Dogecoin.

But that is not the same as a market breakdown. Weaker altcoins do not automatically mean a crypto crash. If the SpaceX IPO had triggered a serious sell-off, we would have expected broader and more aggressive losses, stronger pressure on major altcoins and a clearer flight from risk assets.

That did not happen. The market was mixed: Bitcoin stronger, some altcoins weaker, and HYPE especially interesting because of its own narrative.

## HYPE had its own SpaceX story

HYPE was not an ordinary altcoin that day. Hyperliquid stood at the center of the SpaceX narrative because crypto infrastructures like this were used to trade the pre-IPO/perps story linked to SpaceX. These were not real SpaceX shares, but derivative price exposure. Still, for the crypto market, that was enough to give Hyperliquid major attention.

HYPE traded during the day around 61–62 dollars and later moved back toward the 58–59 dollar area. That is why it would not be accurate to say HYPE simply “rose all day”. A more precise description is that HYPE had a strong daily narrative, high visibility and volatile price action, while Hyperliquid became one of the main crypto channels through which the SpaceX pre-IPO story was read.

{{< support1 >}}

This may be the most important part of the whole day. The SpaceX IPO was not only competition for crypto. In some parts of the market, SpaceX itself became a crypto story.

## Crypto was not just standing aside

The SpaceX IPO showed that crypto is no longer just a separate world reacting to stocks from a distance. Crypto infrastructure tried to participate in the IPO story itself.

Hyperliquid had pre-IPO perpetual futures. xStocks tried to offer tokenized access to SpaceX shares. Bybit, Binance Wallet, Bitget and Kraken ran into allocation problems because there were not enough underlying shares. Coinbase offered pre-IPO perps, meaning price exposure, but not a real share.

In other words, crypto was not just watching SpaceX. Crypto tried to build a parallel market around SpaceX.

## Why did the expected crash not happen?

The first reason is that the SpaceX IPO was expected. The market had been talking for days and weeks about valuation, price, allocation, tokenized shares and pre-IPO perps. When an event comes after so much preparation, it does not necessarily create a shock.

The second reason is Bitcoin’s position. BTC looked more like an asset recovering from lower intraday levels than an asset everyone was fleeing. That does not mean the entire crypto sector was strong, but it does mean that the main crypto asset did not show panic.

The third reason is that the SpaceX IPO opened a new crypto theme: pre-IPO price discovery, tokenized shares and 24/7 markets. Part of the attention did not move from crypto to SpaceX; it moved through crypto products connected to SpaceX.

## Major IPOs now have a crypto shadow

If future IPOs involve companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic or other major technology names, the crypto market will probably not be just a spectator. A model is already visible: pre-IPO perps, tokenized shares, wallet campaigns, allocation experiments and synthetic 24/7 markets.

SpaceX showed that this model can generate enormous interest, but also major problems. Tokenized allocations can get stuck if there are not enough real shares. Perps can offer price exposure, but not ownership. Retail can get access, but not necessarily the quantity it wants.

That is why SpaceX was more than an IPO. It was a test of the relationship between Wall Street and the crypto market.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

The SpaceX IPO did not trigger a crypto crash. Bitcoin rose from intraday lows, HYPE had a strong SpaceX/Hyperliquid narrative, and the broader market was mixed, but not panicked.

Yes, Ethereum, Solana and some altcoins did not look strong. Yes, HYPE later cooled from higher intraday levels. But the expected broad sell-off did not happen.

The most interesting part is that SpaceX did not only pull attention away from crypto. In one part of the market, SpaceX became a crypto theme. Perps, tokenized shares, xStocks, Hyperliquid and Coinbase showed that future major IPOs will no longer be followed only on the stock market.

They will also be followed on crypto markets that trade 24 hours a day.
