# NFTs Between Collectibles and Investment

The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has further clarified its stance on NFTs, emphasizing that in most cases they are not considered securities. According to SEC Chair Paul Atkins, nonfungible tokens are generally treated as digital collectibles rather than investment contracts — placing them outside stricter financial regulation.

This clarification comes at a time when regulators are trying to bring more structure to a rapidly evolving digital asset landscape that often stretches beyond existing legal frameworks. NFTs have gone through several phases in recent years — from experimental projects and digital art to more complex ecosystems involving gaming, memberships and financial models.

## What Determines the Legal Status of an NFT?

Although NFTs are typically not classified as securities, the SEC stresses that there is no universal rule. The key factor lies in how an NFT is used and how it is presented to users.

If an NFT is purchased as an item to own — such as digital art, a collectible token or a unique digital record — it generally does not qualify as an investment contract. However, the situation changes if there is an expectation of profit, especially when that profit depends on the efforts of others, such as developers, teams or platforms.

Metaadvisor reports that the SEC continues to rely on long-established legal criteria, particularly the concept of an “investment contract.” This concept represents the critical dividing line between a collectible product and a financial instrument.

## Digital Collectibles in a New Context

The SEC compares NFTs to traditional collectibles such as sports cards, artworks or memorabilia. In this context, value is driven by scarcity, community interest and subjective perception — not by guaranteed financial returns.

This comparison helps explain why most NFTs are not considered securities. Buying an NFT in this sense is closer to purchasing a piece of art than investing in a stock. However, the digital environment introduces new use cases that increasingly blur this boundary.

## Where Does Regulation Begin?

The line becomes unclear when NFT projects start incorporating elements that resemble financial products. This includes cases where there are implicit or explicit promises of returns, revenue-sharing mechanisms, staking features or other models where users participate in potential profits.

In such cases, regulators may determine that the NFT is not merely a collectible but an investment contract. This is why the SEC emphasizes that each case must be assessed individually, based on its specific characteristics.

Metaadvisor observes that this flexible interpretation allows regulators to adapt, but also creates uncertainty for the market. Projects aiming to avoid regulation must carefully design both their structure and their communication toward users.

## The Evolution of the NFT Market

The NFT market today looks very different from a few years ago. While earlier phases were dominated by speculation and hype, there is now a stronger focus on real utility. NFTs are increasingly used in gaming, digital identity systems, exclusive content access and community-based ecosystems.

This shift toward functionality could contribute to long-term stability. Instead of focusing purely on price, the market is gradually moving toward real-world use and practical applications.

Metaadvisor reports that this transition may play a crucial role in future regulatory treatment. Projects that offer clear utility without promising financial returns are more likely to remain outside stricter regulatory frameworks.

## What Does This Mean for Users?

For users, this clarification brings both reassurance and responsibility. Understanding the difference between a collectible and an investment becomes essential for making informed decisions.

Buying an NFT is no longer just a technical action, but also a legal and financial consideration. Users need to pay attention to how a project is structured, what benefits it offers and whether there is any implicit expectation of profit.

Metaadvisor notes that this is where misunderstandings often arise — many users enter NFT projects expecting price appreciation, even when that is not the core purpose.

## Global Interest and Market Development

The regulatory evolution of NFTs is not only being followed in the United States. Metaadvisor observes strong global interest in this topic, particularly among readers from technologically advanced regions and markets actively engaged in digital innovation.

This confirms that NFTs are no longer a niche technology, but part of a broader discussion about digital ownership, value and the future of the internet.

## A New Phase of Digital Assets

NFTs are entering a phase between experimentation and maturity. While they currently remain largely outside the definition of securities, their future will depend on how the market evolves and how they are used.

Regulatory clarity may help stabilize the ecosystem, but it will not eliminate complexity. Digital assets often transcend traditional categories — and that is both their strength and their challenge.
