# Bitcoin after $74,000: market divided between accumulation and selling

Bitcoin once again attracted strong attention at the beginning of March after the price briefly reached **$74,000**. Shortly afterwards, however, the market lost momentum and the price returned to the **$69,000–$70,000 range**. At first glance this might look like a normal correction following a strong rally, but market data and blockchain analytics reveal a much more complex picture.

Traders, analysts and institutional investors are currently **deeply divided** about what is actually happening in the market. While some warn that **$74,000 may have been a local top**, others believe this is merely a temporary pause before the next upward move.

At the same time, contradictory signals are emerging: **short-term investors are selling**, while larger market participants according to on-chain data appear to be **accumulating bitcoin**.

## The market cools after the rally

During the week Bitcoin broke above the psychological **$70,000 level** and briefly climbed to **$74,000**, the highest level seen in several weeks. However, the market failed to sustain this breakout. The price quickly pulled back, and some traders interpret this as a possible **“bull trap” scenario**.

A bull trap describes a situation where the price breaks above a key level, triggers optimism among buyers and then quickly reverses, causing a wave of selling.

Some analysts warn that the current market structure resembles phases seen during the **2022 bear market cycle**, when similar short-lived rallies were followed by deeper corrections. According to that interpretation, it would not be surprising if bitcoin were to revisit the area below **$60,000**.

## Macroeconomics again pressures the crypto market

Bitcoin’s price movement is currently influenced not only by the crypto market itself but also by global economic developments.

Weak labor-market data from the United States added further uncertainty. According to official figures from the **Bureau of Labor Statistics**, the U.S. economy lost **92,000 jobs in February**, while analysts had expected employment growth. The unemployment rate also increased to **4.4 percent**.

Such data would normally increase expectations that the **Federal Reserve (the central bank of the United States)** might lower interest rates to support the economy. However, inflation and rising energy prices complicate the situation. Markets currently expect **only one interest-rate cut during 2026**, which dampens optimism across risk assets.

At the same time, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have pushed oil prices closer to **$90 per barrel**, adding inflationary pressure and weighing further on financial markets.

In such an environment investors tend to become more cautious, and risk assets such as cryptocurrencies often lose some momentum temporarily.

## Short-term investors take profits

One of the reasons behind the recent decline in bitcoin’s price is **profit-taking among short-term investors**.

According to data from the analytics platform **CryptoQuant**, short-term bitcoin holders transferred more than **27,000 BTC** to exchanges within a 24-hour period. The value of those transfers is estimated at roughly **$1.8 billion**.

Such behavior is not unusual after a strong rally. Investors who bought bitcoin several weeks ago are now sitting on profits and may decide to close part of their positions.

Short-term investors typically react faster than long-term holders, which means their activity can amplify short-term volatility in the market.

## Large bitcoin outflows from exchanges

At the same time, blockchain data shows a completely opposite trend among larger investors.

In a single day almost **32,000 BTC** were withdrawn from exchanges, while total weekly outflows reached approximately **47,700 BTC**. A large portion of this movement occurred on the exchange **Bitfinex**.

It is important to understand what such an outflow means. When bitcoin is **withdrawn from exchanges**, it is typically **moved into private or institutional wallets**, where it is stored for longer periods of time. These transfers usually indicate that investors **do not intend to sell their bitcoin immediately**, but instead plan to hold it outside exchanges.

Because of this, analysts often interpret large exchange outflows as a **sign of accumulation**. A smaller amount of bitcoin held on exchanges also means **less potential selling pressure on the spot market**.

Interestingly, **stablecoins have been flowing into exchanges at the same time**, which could signal that new capital is preparing to buy bitcoin at lower price levels.

## The clash between two market forces

The current situation in the bitcoin market essentially represents a **conflict between two different groups of investors**.

On one side are **short-term traders**, who are taking profits and reacting quickly to price movements.

On the other side are **larger and longer-term investors**, who according to on-chain data are withdrawing bitcoin from exchanges and moving it into private wallets.

This type of dynamic often appears during transitional market phases when capital gradually moves from short-term traders into the hands of long-term investors.

## What comes next for Bitcoin?

At the moment it is difficult to give a clear answer regarding the future direction of the market. Technical indicators show **increased volatility**, while macroeconomic conditions add additional uncertainty.

If institutional demand and accumulation continue to increase, the market could experience another **short squeeze**, a situation where traders holding short positions are forced to close them, pushing the price sharply higher.

On the other hand, further deterioration in the global economic environment or escalation of geopolitical conflicts could encourage investors to temporarily reduce their exposure to risk assets.

One thing is clear: the **$74,000 level has become a key psychological threshold** that will likely play an important role in determining the next direction of the bitcoin market in the coming weeks.
