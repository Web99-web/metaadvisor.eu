# Sony raises PS5 prices by $100 as AI demand and Iran conflict push chip costs higher

## When gaming starts to feel global shifts

Sony has announced a new global price increase for the PlayStation 5, including a $100 rise in the United States. It marks the second price hike in less than a year, highlighting how global economic and technological trends are increasingly affecting everyday consumer products.

The new pricing will take effect on April 2, with the standard PS5 now costing $649.99, the digital edition priced at $599.99, and the PS5 Pro reaching $899.99. The increase also extends to related devices such as the PlayStation Portal, reinforcing that the pressure is not limited to a single product but affects the entire ecosystem.

## AI demand reshaping the chip industry

One of the main drivers behind the price increase comes from an unexpected direction — artificial intelligence. The global race to build AI infrastructure has significantly increased demand for memory chips, which are essential not only for data centers but also for gaming hardware.

Chip manufacturers are increasingly prioritizing higher-margin components for AI systems and servers, reducing the availability of chips for consumer electronics like gaming consoles. As Metaadvisor.eu has observed in multiple analyses, this shift in priorities is beginning to reshape the entire semiconductor market.

## Geopolitics: how helium affects technology prices

The situation is further complicated by geopolitical developments. A recent Iranian attack on gas infrastructure in Qatar forced the shutdown of a key export facility, reducing global helium supply — a critical resource used in semiconductor manufacturing.

Qatar accounts for roughly one-third of global helium production, and a reported 14% drop in exports has direct implications for pricing. While helium is widely known for inflating balloons, its industrial role in chip production makes it a vital, yet often overlooked, component of modern technology.

## Supply chain ripple effects

The combination of rising demand for chips and reduced availability of key resources is creating a ripple effect across global supply chains. When essential components become more expensive or harder to source, manufacturers like Sony have little choice but to pass those costs on to consumers.

This effect is not limited to gaming. Similar pressures are already being observed in smartphones, computers, and other electronic devices, suggesting that this may become a longer-term structural trend rather than a short-term disruption.

## What this means for consumers and the market

Analysts warn that such price increases could slow down growth in the gaming industry this year. Higher prices reduce accessibility, especially for younger consumers and price-sensitive markets.

At the same time, this case illustrates how interconnected global factors have become. AI development, geopolitical tensions, and supply chains are no longer separate issues — they are converging forces shaping the cost of everyday technology.

## Conclusion: a new reality for tech markets

This story is not just about PlayStation. It reflects a broader shift in how technology, economics, and geopolitics intersect.

As AI infrastructure continues to expand and global tensions persist, similar price increases may become more common. Consumers are likely to feel these changes directly through rising costs across a wide range of products.
