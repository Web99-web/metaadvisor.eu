# Big Tech vs global scams

Several of the world’s largest technology companies have decided to jointly respond to one of the biggest problems of the modern internet — **global online scams**.

Companies including **Google, Microsoft, LinkedIn, Meta, Amazon, OpenAI and Adobe** have signed a voluntary agreement committing to **cooperation in detecting and combating digital fraud networks** operating across their platforms.

The initiative aims to enable **faster information sharing about new scam tactics, coordinated removal of fraudulent networks and better identification of organized criminal groups** using digital platforms to deceive users.

This move comes at a time when global losses from online scams are estimated to reach **tens of billions of dollars annually**, while many attacks increasingly rely on **artificial intelligence, deepfake technology and automated bots**.

## Why online scams have become a global problem

Internet scams are not new, but in recent years their **scale and sophistication have increased dramatically**.

Criminal networks now use a wide range of tactics to deceive users, including:

- fake investment platforms  
- phishing emails  
- deepfake video calls  
- fraudulent social media advertisements  
- romance scams and fake profiles  

Many of these scams spread through large digital platforms, which is one of the main reasons why major tech companies decided to **combine resources and share information**.

According to security organizations, **millions of users become victims of online scams every year**, and many of these operations cross national borders.

## What the agreement actually includes

According to available information, the agreement between the technology companies focuses on several key principles.

First, companies will **share data about new scam methods and criminal tactics** detected on their platforms.

For example, if one platform identifies a new type of fraud, that information can be **quickly shared with other companies**, allowing them to block similar attacks much faster.

In addition, the companies plan to **develop joint technologies for scam detection**, including systems that use **artificial intelligence to identify suspicious behavioral patterns**.

Such cooperation could significantly increase the chances of **identifying organized scam networks before they reach large numbers of victims**.

## The role of artificial intelligence in fighting scams

Ironically, the same technology often used by scammers — **artificial intelligence** — is also becoming a powerful tool in the fight against digital fraud.

Major technology companies are developing systems capable of **analyzing massive amounts of data in real time** to detect suspicious activities.

For example, AI systems can identify:

- unusual messaging patterns  
- sudden spikes in newly created fake accounts  
- coordinated distribution of scam advertisements  
- suspicious financial transactions  

These analyses can help platforms **detect fraud earlier and stop scams before they spread to millions of users**.

## Why cooperation between companies matters

One of the biggest challenges in fighting digital scams is that **criminal networks operate across multiple platforms simultaneously**.

A scam might start on a social network, continue through an email service and end on a fraudulent investment website or application.

If each platform operates independently, scammers can **simply move to another platform once they are detected**.

This is why many experts believe that **cooperation between major technology companies is essential to combat global scam networks more effectively**.

The new agreement aims to address exactly this issue through **shared intelligence and coordinated security measures**.

## Will this initiative really help

While many experts welcome this initiative, others warn that **the agreement alone will not automatically solve the problem of online scams**.

Criminal networks often adapt quickly to new security measures, and the global nature of the internet makes complete elimination of these activities extremely difficult.

However, most cybersecurity specialists agree that **collaboration between large technology companies is an important step toward reducing global online scams**.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

The decision of major tech companies to cooperate shows that digital scams have become **too large and complex to be tackled by individual platforms alone**.

The internet is a global infrastructure, and criminal networks exploit that global reach to hide their operations and quickly shift between platforms.

If cooperation between companies such as **Google, Microsoft, Meta and Amazon** becomes truly operational and effective, it could significantly reduce the space in which large scam networks operate.

However, users must remain cautious, because even the most advanced security systems cannot completely eliminate scams based on **psychological manipulation and social engineering**.

In other words, technology can help — but **critical thinking by users remains one of the most important defenses against online fraud**.
