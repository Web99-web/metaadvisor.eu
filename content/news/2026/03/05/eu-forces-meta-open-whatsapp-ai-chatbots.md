# EU forces Meta to open WhatsApp to rival AI chatbots

The European Union once again demonstrates how strong its regulatory influence over global technology companies can be. Following pressure from the European Commission, Meta announced that competing AI companies will be allowed to offer their chatbots through WhatsApp in the European market over the next 12 months. The decision comes after regulators in Brussels warned that Meta could face interim measures due to policies that previously restricted third-party AI systems from accessing the WhatsApp Business API. As a result, WhatsApp — one of the largest communication platforms in the world — could become a new battleground for competition among AI systems.

## Regulatory pressure from the European Commission

According to a report by the technology outlet TechCrunch, the European Commission had earlier signaled that it was considering interim regulatory measures against Meta. The issue arose when the company blocked rival AI chatbots from accessing WhatsApp, potentially giving its own system **Meta AI** an unfair advantage on the platform. The Commission believes such practices could restrict competition, especially at a time when artificial intelligence is becoming one of the most important technology markets globally.

To avoid potential regulatory sanctions, Meta decided to revise its policy and temporarily allow other AI companies to access WhatsApp. The new policy will apply for **12 months**, during which the European Commission will continue evaluating the market impact of Meta’s AI strategy.

## AI chatbots on WhatsApp – but for a fee

Although Meta is formally opening the platform to competition, access to the WhatsApp infrastructure will not be free. The company announced that it will charge between **€0.0490 and €0.1323 per message** for so-called “non-template” messages, depending on the country. For AI companies developing chatbot services, this could represent significant costs because conversations with AI assistants often involve dozens of messages.

In other words, Meta is allowing competition while still maintaining strong control over the infrastructure and monetization of communication on its platform. The model resembles how mobile platforms like Apple’s App Store or Google Play allow applications to reach users — but under specific rules and fees.

## Why WhatsApp matters for the AI market

WhatsApp currently has more than **two billion users worldwide**. For AI companies, this represents an enormous market opportunity. Integrating AI assistants directly into an already widely used messaging app allows developers to reach a large user base without requiring people to install additional applications. In practice, this could allow users to interact with different AI assistants within the same app — for searching information, shopping, making reservations, or customer support.

This is why control over platforms like WhatsApp has become strategically important for companies developing artificial intelligence technologies.

## Meta AI and platform strategy

Meta has been heavily investing in its own AI system known as **Meta AI**, which is gradually being integrated across Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. Opening the platform to competing chatbots might therefore appear surprising at first glance. However, many experts believe the move represents a regulatory compromise.

In recent years, the European Union has introduced stricter legislation for digital platforms, including the **Digital Markets Act (DMA)** and the **Digital Services Act (DSA)**. These laws aim to prevent large technology companies from abusing their dominant market positions. If Meta were to completely block rival AI assistants on WhatsApp, regulators could interpret such behavior as anti-competitive.

## A new model for AI platforms

Opening WhatsApp to multiple AI systems could mark the beginning of a new model for digital platforms. Instead of relying on a single AI assistant, users may eventually have access to an entire ecosystem of specialized chatbots. One system could focus on information retrieval, another on shopping or financial services, and a third on customer support.

In such a scenario, WhatsApp could evolve into a **distribution platform for artificial intelligence**, similar to how mobile operating systems became platforms for apps. For Meta, this model could also open new revenue streams, as every AI interaction would run through its infrastructure.

## Europe as a regulatory laboratory

It is notable that this change initially applies primarily to the European market. The European Union has increasingly acted as a global regulator of digital platforms, particularly in areas such as privacy, competition, and artificial intelligence. Many technology companies therefore implement regulatory adjustments in Europe first before potentially expanding them to other markets.

In this context, opening WhatsApp to AI chatbots can be seen as an experiment that may shape future global platform policies.

## What this means for the AI industry

If the model proves successful, WhatsApp could become one of the largest marketplaces for AI assistants in the world. AI companies would gain access to billions of users, while Meta would still control the infrastructure and monetization of communication. At the same time, regulatory pressure from Europe could accelerate the development of more open digital ecosystems where multiple AI systems compete on the same platform.
