# Cardano ADA payments reach Spar: 137 Swiss stores test crypto at checkout

For years, cryptocurrencies have been trying to move beyond investment speculation and into everyday economic activity. One of the newest real-world experiments comes from Switzerland, where shoppers can now pay for groceries using the Cardano (ADA) cryptocurrency in 137 Spar supermarkets across the country.

The initiative represents one of the largest real-world tests of crypto payments in European retail. While cryptocurrencies have already gained traction in online commerce and financial services, their use at physical supermarket checkouts remains relatively rare.

The integration is made possible through **Open Crypto Pay**, a payment infrastructure developed by Swiss fintech company **DFX.swiss** in cooperation with the **Cardano Foundation**. The system processes transactions in real time and connects ADA wallets directly to merchant payment systems.

## How ADA payments actually work in Spar stores

Although it may appear that Spar is directly accepting cryptocurrency, the payment process works slightly differently behind the scenes. When a customer chooses to pay with ADA, they scan a QR code at the checkout using their crypto wallet. The transaction is then processed through the Open Crypto Pay infrastructure.

A key element of the system is instant conversion. The cryptocurrency sent by the customer is automatically converted into Swiss francs (CHF), meaning the retailer ultimately receives fiat currency. This mechanism protects Spar from crypto price volatility.

For merchants, this approach makes it possible to accept cryptocurrency payments without managing crypto assets themselves. In many ways, the system operates similarly to modern digital payment tools such as Apple Pay or traditional card processing networks.

According to project partners, Open Crypto Pay could reduce merchant transaction costs by as much as two thirds compared with conventional card payment systems.

## Why Cardano instead of Bitcoin or Ethereum

One of the most interesting aspects of the project is why Spar opted for Cardano rather than more widely known cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin or Ethereum.

One major reason is transaction fees. Both Bitcoin and Ethereum networks can experience significant fee spikes during periods of heavy network activity. Such fluctuations can be problematic for retail environments, where purchases are typically small and transactions must remain fast and predictable.

Cardano’s blockchain, by contrast, offers relatively stable and lower transaction fees, making it more suitable for everyday payments.

Another factor is geographic context. The **Cardano Foundation is headquartered in Switzerland**, which simplifies collaboration with local fintech firms and retailers. In many ways, Cardano is already deeply integrated into the Swiss blockchain ecosystem.

Infrastructure also plays a role. DFX.swiss developed the Open Crypto Pay system with Cardano compatibility and merchant POS integration in mind, allowing retailers to implement crypto payments with minimal technical changes.

## Spar has already been experimenting with crypto payments

This is not the first time Spar has explored cryptocurrency payments in Switzerland. In 2025 the retailer launched a pilot program allowing crypto and stablecoin payments in selected stores.

That initial project involved **Binance Pay** and **DFX.swiss**, with plans to expand the infrastructure over time. The current Cardano integration represents the next phase of that experiment.

With 137 participating supermarkets, the initiative now stands as one of the largest retail crypto payment pilots in Europe.

## Switzerland as a global crypto hub

Projects like this are not happening by chance. Switzerland has spent years building a reputation as one of the most blockchain-friendly jurisdictions in the world.

The region of Zug, widely known as **Crypto Valley**, hosts a large concentration of blockchain companies, venture funds and fintech startups focused on digital assets.

Meanwhile, the city of Lugano has launched the **Plan B initiative**, an ambitious strategy designed to position the city as a global hub for digital asset infrastructure. Residents there can already pay certain municipal fees using Bitcoin and the stablecoin USDT.

Such an environment makes Switzerland an ideal testing ground for real-world blockchain applications.

## What this means for the crypto industry

For the crypto industry, a central question has always been whether digital assets can become practical everyday payment tools rather than purely speculative investments.

Retail experiments like the Spar integration offer a chance to test exactly that. If customers begin using cryptocurrency regularly for grocery shopping and other everyday purchases, it could open the door to broader adoption of blockchain payments.

Even if adoption remains limited, the fact that a major supermarket chain is testing crypto payments across more than one hundred locations demonstrates how quickly the boundaries between traditional finance and digital assets are evolving.
