# Professors vs ChatGPT: can education survive in the age of artificial intelligence?

The rise of **artificial intelligence (AI)** in recent years has dramatically changed how students learn, write and think. Tools such as ChatGPT have become everyday assistants for writing essays, preparing academic papers and solving assignments. While some see this as technological progress that could improve education, a growing number of professors warn that the consequences for **critical thinking and academic learning** could be serious.

Some professors openly admit their frustration. One of them even said during a discussion about AI tools that he wished he could **“push ChatGPT off a cliff,”** expressing how difficult it has become to preserve traditional learning methods.

## Students increasingly rely on AI

Across universities worldwide, students are increasingly turning to AI tools to assist with writing and studying. In many cases artificial intelligence can generate text that looks convincing, grammatically correct and structured like an academic paper.

The problem, however, is that such texts often **look impressive but contain very little real thinking**.

Literature professor **Lea Pao from Stanford University** has been experimenting with ways to reconnect students with learning experiences that do not depend on artificial intelligence. In some of her courses she asks students to memorize poems, perform recitations, analyze artworks in museums and write personal reflections based on real-world experiences.

The idea is to bring students back to **authentic learning experiences** involving observation, reflection and personal interpretation.

## When even personal assignments fail

However, even these types of assignments do not always work.

In one case, the professor asked students to visit a museum, spend ten minutes observing a painting and then write a few paragraphs describing their experience. The assignment was intentionally designed as a personal experience that would be difficult for AI to generate.

Yet one student submitted a perfectly written text that lacked real substance. It later turned out that the student had attempted to visit the museum on a Monday – when it was closed – and eventually asked an AI tool to complete the assignment instead.

Examples like this illustrate how quickly **AI has become integrated into students’ learning habits**.

## How professors detect AI usage

Interestingly, professors increasingly rely on a simple method to check whether students actually wrote their assignments themselves. After receiving an essay, they ask **short follow-up questions about the text**.

For example:

- Why did you choose this argument?  
- How did you arrive at this conclusion?  
- Can you explain this sentence in your own words?  

Students who wrote the essay themselves can usually explain their reasoning process. Students who relied on AI without understanding the content often struggle to explain their own text.

Professors say the difference becomes obvious very quickly because **AI can generate text, but it cannot explain the real process of learning**.

## AI as a tool or a threat?

In fields such as mathematics, computer science or medicine, many researchers believe artificial intelligence can significantly accelerate research. AI can analyze massive datasets, support medical discoveries and speed up technological development.

For that reason, many scientists describe AI as one of the most important technological tools of the 21st century.

However, in disciplines such as philosophy, literature and the social sciences, many academics fear that artificial intelligence could weaken exactly what education is supposed to develop – **independent thinking**.

## A crisis for the humanities?

Humanities fields traditionally rely on analysis, interpretation and critical reasoning. If students increasingly use AI to generate essays and analyze texts, professors worry that students may never develop the skills required for this type of work.

Some academics warn that this could lead to a **long-term decline in educational quality**.

If students lose the ability to analyze arguments and think critically, the consequences could eventually extend beyond universities into media, politics and society at large.

## Three types of students in the AI era

Interestingly, professors are already beginning to identify **three types of students in the age of artificial intelligence**.

The first group uses AI as a tool for ideas and support but still thinks independently and verifies information. The second group mainly uses AI to speed up writing and structure their work. The third group relies almost entirely on artificial intelligence to complete assignments.

In an experiment conducted at a large university, researchers found that **students who first attempt to solve a problem themselves and only later use AI as support make the greatest progress**. Students who immediately rely on AI often develop a weaker understanding of the subject.

## A new reality for education

Despite criticism, artificial intelligence is unlikely to disappear from education. AI tools have already become part of everyday life for many students, and universities are increasingly trying to find ways to integrate them into teaching rather than banning them entirely.

This suggests that the future of education will likely involve **a combination of human thinking and AI tools**.
