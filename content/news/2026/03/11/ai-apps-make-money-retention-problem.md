# AI apps can generate revenue, but keeping users is harder

The global app ecosystem has been rapidly transformed by the rise of **artificial intelligence (AI)**. From chatbots and image generators to productivity tools and creative software, a growing number of applications now promote themselves as **AI-powered products**.

For many developers, integrating AI features appears to be a fast path to monetization. However, new data suggests that the business model behind many AI apps may be more fragile than expected.

A new industry report indicates that while AI apps can quickly attract paying users, **they often struggle to retain subscribers over the long term**.

## AI hype does not guarantee loyal users

The findings come from the **2026 State of Subscription Apps Report** published by RevenueCat, a company that provides subscription management tools for mobile apps.

More than **75,000 developers worldwide** use RevenueCat’s infrastructure to manage their subscription models. The company’s platform processes over **one billion in-app transactions each year**, generating more than **$11 billion in annual developer revenue**.

This large dataset allows analysts to identify meaningful patterns across the subscription-app economy.

One of the report’s key conclusions is that **AI integration alone does not guarantee long-term retention**. While AI apps often attract strong initial interest, many users quickly cancel their subscriptions after the novelty wears off.

According to the report, users cancel **annual subscriptions for AI apps about 30% faster** than for apps that do not rely on artificial intelligence.

In the subscription economy this metric is known as **churn** – the rate at which customers stop using a service.

## AI apps are growing but still a minority

Despite the enormous attention around AI, these apps still represent **a minority within the broader subscription ecosystem**.

The report estimates that **27.1% of subscription apps include AI features**, while **72.9% are still non-AI applications**.

In practical terms, roughly **one in four apps today markets itself as AI-powered**.

Importantly, the category does not only include major AI platforms such as **ChatGPT or Gemini**, but any application that promotes itself as using artificial intelligence in some way.

Because of this, the label **“AI-powered” has increasingly become a marketing term** rather than a precise technical classification.

## Where AI adoption is strongest

The study also shows that AI adoption varies significantly across app categories.

The category with the highest concentration of AI apps is:

**Photo & Video applications — 61.4%**

These include tools used for:

- AI image generation  
- photo editing  
- video effects  
- AI filters  

Generative AI technologies were adopted early in these creative fields, which explains the high adoption rate.

Other sectors have integrated AI more slowly:

- **Gaming — 6.2%**  
- **Travel apps — 12.3%**  
- **Business apps — 19.1%**

This uneven distribution suggests that AI transformation across the digital economy is still in its early stages.

## Why users abandon AI apps

One of the most interesting parts of the report focuses on **why AI apps struggle with long-term retention**.

A major factor is the **novelty effect**. Many users install AI apps out of curiosity and experiment with the technology. Once that initial curiosity fades, engagement often drops significantly.

Another reason is **subscription pricing**. Many AI apps charge between **$10 and $30 per month**, which can feel expensive for tools used only occasionally.

Competition also plays a role. In many categories there are now **dozens of similar AI tools offering comparable features**, making it easy for users to switch between products.

## The AI startup boom meets market reality

The explosion of interest in artificial intelligence has triggered a massive **startup boom**. Thousands of AI-based applications have launched in recent years as venture capital poured billions of dollars into the sector.

But as the market matures, analysts increasingly emphasize that **not every AI product will succeed long term**.

Many apps achieve rapid early growth thanks to the AI trend, but building sustainable user engagement remains a significant challenge.

## What this means for the future of AI apps

The report does not suggest that AI apps lack long-term potential. On the contrary, artificial intelligence is already reshaping how people interact with software.

However, the data suggests that long-term success will depend on **delivering real value**, not simply adding AI features as a marketing strategy.

Developers may increasingly need to build AI tools that solve **specific and practical problems** rather than relying on the hype surrounding artificial intelligence.
