# Musk’s xAI receives approval to expand energy supply for AI infrastructure

The company **xAI**, founded by Elon Musk, has officially received approval to expand the power supply of its large AI datacenter **Colossus 2** in the U.S. state of Mississippi. According to a decision by the **Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ)**, the company is allowed to operate **41 methane-powered gas turbines** to supply electricity to its massive computing complex.

The decision comes at a time when the global race in **artificial intelligence (AI)** increasingly demands enormous amounts of electricity. Modern AI models are trained on thousands of specialized chips, and the datacenters powering these systems often consume energy comparable to that of small cities.

In the case of xAI, the turbines will power the infrastructure behind the company’s **AI supercomputer clusters** — massive arrays of GPUs and AI chips used to train models such as **Grok**, the AI assistant Musk introduced as a competitor to systems like ChatGPT.

## Datacenters as the backbone of the AI revolution

The development of artificial intelligence in recent years has dramatically increased the demand for **computing infrastructure**. AI models require massive processing power and memory in order to analyze large datasets and generate responses.

For that reason, technology companies around the world are rapidly building new **AI datacenters**. These facilities contain thousands of specialized chips and operate around the clock, often consuming enormous amounts of electricity.

The **Colossus 2** datacenter developed by xAI is part of that broader trend. The project is designed to provide the infrastructure necessary to run large AI models and support the development of next-generation artificial intelligence systems.

However, as these projects expand, questions about **energy sustainability** and the impact on local communities are increasingly being raised.

## Local community raises environmental concerns

The decision to approve the operation of **41 gas turbines** has triggered strong reactions from local residents and environmental organizations. Residents around **Southaven, Mississippi**, claim that the project has already created problems related to noise and air quality.

During a public hearing organized by MDEQ, **hundreds of citizens attended to express their concerns** about the project. According to media reports, almost no one present spoke in favor of granting the permit.

Environmental groups warn that the concentration of gas generators could increase **air pollution**, particularly in populated areas near the datacenter.

One of the most vocal critics has been the **NAACP**, an organization focused on social and climate justice. Representatives argue that the decision was made without sufficiently considering the potential impact on local residents.

## Legal battle over AI infrastructure

The dispute surrounding the Colossus 2 datacenter has already evolved into a **legal conflict**. The NAACP has filed a lawsuit against xAI, claiming that the company failed to properly assess the environmental impact of the project.

According to critics, some of the turbines had previously been installed and activated **without proper permits**, which further fueled concerns among residents.

Opponents argue that the development of the infrastructure is happening **too quickly and without a long-term sustainability plan**.

Technology companies, on the other hand, warn that slowing the expansion of AI infrastructure could harm **global competitiveness in artificial intelligence development**.

## The energy challenge of the AI industry

The debate around the xAI datacenter raises a broader question: **how much energy artificial intelligence actually consumes**.

Large AI models require enormous amounts of electricity for both training and operation. Estimates suggest that individual AI computing clusters can consume **tens of megawatts of electricity**, and the demand for energy in the AI industry continues to grow each year.

As a result, many technology companies are exploring various energy sources:

- natural gas  
- nuclear energy  
- renewable sources such as solar and wind  

In practice, a **combination of multiple energy sources** is often used, since large datacenters require stable and continuous power supply.

## AI race among tech giants

The xAI project is also part of a broader technological race between companies such as **OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Meta**, all of which are investing billions into infrastructure capable of supporting the next generation of AI models.

With xAI, Elon Musk aims to build an **alternative to existing AI platforms**, and his model Grok is already integrated into the social platform **X (formerly Twitter)**.

As AI models continue to grow in scale and complexity, infrastructure such as Colossus 2 becomes a crucial factor in global competitiveness in artificial intelligence.

For analytical platforms like **Metaadvisor.eu**, projects like this illustrate that the future of artificial intelligence will depend not only on algorithms and software but also on **physical infrastructure, energy supply and regulatory decisions**.

## Technology versus environment

The conflict between technological progress and environmental protection is becoming increasingly visible in the AI industry. While companies argue that new datacenters are essential for innovation, local communities often bear the burden of increased energy consumption and industrial infrastructure.

The decision to expand xAI’s power infrastructure is therefore viewed not only as a technical issue but also as a **broader social and political debate**.

As the AI industry continues to grow, similar controversies are likely to emerge in many other parts of the world.
