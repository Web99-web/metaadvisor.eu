# A small country that built thousands of BTC mostly through mining is now becoming one of the biggest sellers

## Quiet accumulation turns into large-scale selling

In the world of crypto, attention is usually focused on large funds, exchanges, and institutional investors. However, one of the biggest Bitcoin sellers this year is neither a hedge fund nor a tech giant — but a small Asian country: Bhutan.

What makes this story particularly interesting is how Bhutan accumulated its Bitcoin. Most of its holdings were not purchased on the market, but generated over years through mining, using domestic energy resources. Now, the country is increasingly selling BTC and converting it into liquid capital.

According to available data, outflows in 2026 alone have already exceeded $150 million. The most recent transfer included more than 500 BTC, confirming that this is not a one-off move, but part of a consistent strategy.

## From controlled transactions to rapid acceleration

At the beginning of the year, the selling activity was relatively small and gradual. In January and February, transactions ranged between $5 million and $15 million, suggesting careful portfolio management.

However, in March, the pace changed significantly. Individual transfers jumped to $35–45 million, and in a single week, total outflows reached approximately $72 million. This shift clearly indicates a move from cautious selling to a more aggressive approach.

The latest transfer reinforces this trend. Over 500 BTC were sent to external addresses, typically interpreted as preparation for sales or OTC transactions.

## How are they selling without crashing the price?

Despite the large volumes, the market is not reacting with sharp price drops. The reason lies in how these sales are executed.

Instead of selling directly on public exchanges, a significant portion of Bitcoin is transferred to institutional counterparties, including firms such as QCP Capital in Singapore. This suggests the use of OTC (over-the-counter) trading, where large volumes are traded privately.

This approach allows major sales to happen without triggering panic or sudden price shocks. In other words — these are structured, professional transactions.

## Energy as the foundation of the strategy

The key to this entire strategy is energy. Bhutan uses hydropower and surplus electricity to mine Bitcoin, which keeps production costs extremely low.

Unlike investors who buy Bitcoin at market prices and depend on price appreciation, Bhutan operates from a completely different position. Its Bitcoin is largely the result of converting energy into digital assets.

This gives the country flexibility — it can sell without pressure or fear of losses, since the initial cost base is minimal.

## How much Bitcoin does Bhutan actually hold?

Estimates suggest that Bhutan held around 13,000 BTC at its peak, placing it among more significant — yet often overlooked — players in the crypto market.

These “quiet” actors can have a substantial impact, precisely because their moves often go unnoticed by the broader public.

## What does this mean for the market?

This situation raises an important question — how resilient is the market really?

The fact that the market can absorb hundreds of millions of dollars in Bitcoin sales without major disruption may signal growing maturity. At the same time, it raises concerns about how many similar players might exist.

If more countries or institutions follow a similar strategy, a long-term supply pressure could gradually build.
