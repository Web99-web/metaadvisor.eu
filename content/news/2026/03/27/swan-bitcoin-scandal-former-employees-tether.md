# Swan Bitcoin at the center of a scandal: former employees, Tether and alleged data theft

## When the internal team becomes the biggest risk

The Bitcoin industry is not only watching price movements these days, but also one of the more serious business conflicts that could have wider implications for the entire sector. At the center of the story is Swan Bitcoin, a U.S.-based company focused exclusively on Bitcoin, specializing in long-term investing, education, and infrastructure development around BTC. As Metaadvisor.eu has previously noted, Bitcoin-focused companies often build deep relationships with partners, making internal disruptions particularly sensitive.

According to court filings, the company alleges that a group of former employees coordinated their departure, took confidential documents, and shortly afterward launched their own project. What makes this situation especially serious is that these were not ordinary employees, but individuals involved in key operations, including Bitcoin mining initiatives.

## The “rain and hellfire” plan and the battle for Tether

According to Swan Bitcoin, the former employees internally referred to their plan as “rain and hellfire,” a phrase that in a business context suggests an aggressive and coordinated strategy to take over projects and partners. This goes beyond a simple job change — it points to an attempt to capture an entire segment of the business.

At the center of this conflict is Tether, one of the most influential players in the crypto industry. Swan claims that the former employees managed to persuade Tether to cut ties with Swan and move to a new structure, which, if confirmed, would represent a significant blow to the original company.

## Who the competitor really is — and who it is not

The new company is allegedly named Proton Management, according to Swan Bitcoin’s claims. It is important to emphasize that this is an allegation presented in a legal dispute, and the court has yet to determine the full facts.

Due to the similarity in name, it is also important to clarify that Proton Management has no connection to Proton Mail. Proton Mail is developed by Proton AG, a Swiss technology company known globally for secure, encrypted communication services focused on privacy.

## The role of Wall Street and political implications

The case gains additional weight due to the involvement of major financial and political figures. Swan Bitcoin is seeking a subpoena to obtain documents that may be held by Cantor Fitzgerald and its former CEO, Howard Lutnick, who currently serves as U.S. Secretary of Commerce.

Cantor Fitzgerald has long-standing ties with Tether as an investment advisor, raising questions about who knew what — and when — regarding potential changes in partnerships. If it turns out that key information was known in advance, the case could expand far beyond a standard corporate dispute.

## How many employees were involved — and why it matters

The exact number of employees who left Swan Bitcoin has not been publicly confirmed, but available information suggests that multiple individuals with access to critical data and strategic projects were involved. This makes the situation significantly more serious than a typical business dispute.

In business, the greatest risk often comes from within. When people who understand a company’s structure, clients, and strategy leave in a coordinated way, the consequences can be substantial — even for otherwise stable organizations.

## What this case reveals about the crypto industry

This case highlights how much the crypto industry has evolved. What was once primarily technology-driven now increasingly resembles traditional financial sectors, where disputes over capital, partnerships, and information are common.

As institutional players and larger capital flows enter the space, conflicts like this are becoming inevitable. The question is no longer whether they will happen, but how often — and how far they will go.
