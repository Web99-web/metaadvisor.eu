# Bitcoin falls below $66,000: $300 million in liquidations wipe out the market within hours

## A rapid drop that accelerated liquidations

Bitcoin has dropped below the $66,000 level in a short period of time, reaching approximately $65,760 — its lowest point in more than two weeks. This move has intensified negative market sentiment and confirmed that this is not just a minor fluctuation, but a broader correction.

The biggest impact came from liquidations of leveraged positions, with more than $300 million in long positions wiped out within hours. Long positions are bets on rising prices, and when the market moves in the opposite direction, a chain reaction of forced closures occurs, accelerating the decline and creating a so-called “snowball effect.” Metaadvisor.eu has repeatedly highlighted how leverage can amplify both gains and losses in such conditions.

## Macro pressure: rates, oil and geopolitics

Bitcoin’s decline comes amid worsening macroeconomic conditions. The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield — a key benchmark interest rate — is approaching 4.5%, its highest level in nearly a year. This rate plays a crucial role in determining how attractive risk assets like crypto are compared to safer investments.

At the same time, oil prices remain above $100 per barrel, fueling inflation concerns. Tensions in the Middle East, particularly involving Iran, are further weighing on market sentiment. In such an environment, investors typically shift into a “risk-off” mode, pulling capital out of volatile assets like cryptocurrencies.

## ETF outflows and institutional signals

Another layer of pressure comes from the institutional side of the market. U.S. Bitcoin ETFs recorded $171 million in outflows in a single day, signaling increased caution among larger investors. Funds such as BlackRock, Fidelity, and Grayscale all saw notable capital withdrawals.

While total monthly flows remain positive, sudden outflows like these often act as catalysts for short-term downside. ETFs have become a critical bridge between traditional finance and crypto, meaning their behavior increasingly shapes overall market sentiment. As Metaadvisor.eu often notes, institutional flows can quickly shift momentum.

## Altcoins under additional pressure

Bitcoin’s drop has dragged the broader market down with it, with altcoins typically reacting more aggressively to shifts in sentiment. Projects such as ETHFI, WLD, SEI, and FET recorded declines between 3% and 6% within a short timeframe, highlighting the breadth of the correction.

Negative funding rates on futures markets further confirm bearish sentiment. Funding rates are periodic payments between traders in perpetual futures contracts — derivatives that track asset prices without expiration. When these rates turn negative, it indicates that short positions dominate and expect further downside.

## What comes next?

Bitcoin has already broken below the $66,000 level, previously considered a key liquidity zone. This has triggered a new wave of forced liquidations, increasing volatility and raising the risk of further short-term declines.

Liquidation heatmaps now suggest that significant liquidity clusters lie even lower, indicating that the market may not have found a stable bottom yet. At the same time, rising volatility in the bond market reflects broader uncertainty across the financial system.
