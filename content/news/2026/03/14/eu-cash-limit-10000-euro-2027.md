# EU introduces €10,000 cash payment limit — what it means and when it starts

The European Union has adopted a new regulation introducing a **€10,000 limit for cash payments**. The measure is part of a broader anti-money laundering legislative package adopted in 2024 after years of discussions between EU member states, the European Parliament and the European Commission.

The rule does not ban cash, but it creates **a unified European framework for large cash transactions**.

## When the new rule will apply

According to the regulation, the **€10,000 cash payment limit will start applying across the European Union in 2027**. Until then, member states have a transition period to adapt their national legislation.

This means that **cash payments above that amount will have to be made through bank transfers or other electronic payment methods**.

Importantly, the limit applies to **a single transaction**, not to a weekly, monthly or yearly spending amount.

## Member states can impose stricter limits

Although the EU sets the limit at €10,000, member states are allowed to **maintain stricter national limits**.

Some countries already have lower thresholds. **Spain and France, for example, apply limits of around €1,000 in certain transactions between consumers and businesses**, while **Italy has a limit of around €5,000**.

As a result, the EU rule effectively establishes **a maximum threshold**, not an obligation for all countries to adopt the same level.

## How the law was adopted

The regulation was adopted through the EU’s ordinary legislative procedure. This means it was approved by both the **European Parliament and the Council of the European Union**, where national governments are represented.

Such legislation does not usually require unanimous agreement from all member states, but rather a **qualified majority vote**.

## Why the EU wants to limit large cash payments

According to European institutions, large cash transactions are often associated with activities that are difficult to track, including tax evasion, organized crime and money laundering.

Bank transfers and digital payments leave **a traceable record in the financial system**, while cash transactions are harder to monitor.

## Will cash disappear

Despite claims circulating on social media, the new regulation **does not ban cash**.

Citizens will still be able to withdraw and use cash for everyday purchases. However, larger transactions will increasingly move into the banking system.
