# Meta plans to lay off around 16,000 people — AI is taking over, but the problem has already started

Meta is according to reports from Reuters and Business Insider, with estimates by JP Morgan, reportedly planning to lay off around 20% of its workforce — roughly 16,000 employees — with estimated savings of $5 to $6 billion. This move comes at a time when the company is aggressively investing in artificial intelligence and operational optimization.

20% of employees might not sound dramatic on paper, but when translated into real people — around 16,000 — it takes on a completely different weight. That is the size of a small city simply disappearing from the system. This is not a typical cost-cutting move driven by crisis, but a deliberate reallocation of resources: money is flowing into artificial intelligence, while people are becoming collateral change.

While Meta publicly talks about “efficiency” and “restructuring,” more and more users are noticing something else — a sharp increase in problems with automated content moderation. Meta is now investing billions into AI models, infrastructure and automation, aiming to do the same work with fewer people, faster and cheaper. Investors reward this, the market understands it, but what is being lost does not appear in financial statements. The human layer between the platform and the user is disappearing — precisely the layer that becomes critical when something goes wrong.

## AI moderation on Facebook: expectations vs reality

AI moderation has been presented for years as the solution to internet problems. The idea is simple: algorithms will remove spam, scams, fake ads and harmful content, making platforms safer. But what is actually happening on Facebook during 2025 and 2026 tells a completely different story. A growing number of ordinary users are reporting permanent bans without explanation — and not just any users, but people who have been using Facebook for 10 or 15 years without issues. These are not anonymous profiles or suspicious accounts, but real people with real networks, jobs, families and social roles built over years on the platform.

For these users, a permanent ban means losing not only Facebook access but also Messenger. With a single click, they lose all private and business contacts.

### A case that raises the question: what kind of “social” network is this?

One example that stands out is the case of a community officer from Canada — a person whose job was to work with people and communities, someone who should represent exactly what a “social network” is supposed to be. This person was removed from the system without a clear reason, without explanation and without any possibility of communication. If a social network removes people whose role is to build communities, the question becomes unavoidable: what kind of society remains?

These are no longer isolated incidents. Increasing user reports and estimates suggest that the wave of AI-driven removals intensified further in 2026, with discussions pointing to the possibility that millions of users may have been affected. There are no official numbers, but the patterns are clear and repeatable. People are disappearing from the system without warning and without a way back. If AI is removing real users — who will remain? AI bots?

## Facebook system completely ignores removed users: ban and total silence

The problem does not end with the ban — it begins there. When a user is removed from Facebook, the communication channel between the user and the platform effectively disappears. There is technically an option to appeal within 180 days, but the paradox is that there is no officially published email address to contact — or it is so deeply hidden that it cannot be found. Once removed, users lose access to everything, because all communication is locked inside a system they can no longer enter. There is no person reviewing the case. There is no transparent appeal process. What remains is an automated system that has already made a decision and has no mechanism to review it at a human level.

The European Union formally provides certain protection mechanisms, such as the ability to submit complaints through platforms like AppealsCentre.eu, but in practice this is not a real solution. Users report that responses can take weeks or never arrive at all, meaning that even regulatory frameworks fail to ensure timely action. In such a system, the user loses not only an account, but the possibility of being heard at all.

## 16,000 layoffs and the question no one is asking

In this context, the planned layoffs take on an entirely new dimension. If Meta is already not responding to users, if communication channels are already effectively shut down, what does the additional removal of 16,000 people mean? It is highly likely that a significant portion of these employees comes from operational functions — particularly customer support and moderation — because these are the easiest areas to automate and the most common targets for cost reduction. This means that a system that already does not respond will become even more closed.

## Internet without people: where is the limit?

We are moving toward a structure where artificial intelligence makes decisions, people are removed from the process, and users have no one to turn to. This is no longer just a problem of a single company, but a model spreading across the entire industry. The internet is gradually becoming a space where algorithms control content, visibility and access, while human communication is reduced to a minimum. But there is a threshold beyond which such a system ceases to be a social network and becomes a closed system without accountability.

If a platform can remove a user without explanation, without contact and without the possibility of appeal, then the question is no longer technical, but fundamental: does accountability even exist? And more importantly — if real people are being removed from the system, leaving only algorithms and automated content behind, what remains of the “social” internet?

No one is telling Mark Zuckerberg: “You did an incredible job building Facebook, but don’t sacrifice users for AI.” If the trend continues in which more and more ordinary people are permanently removed without explanation, users will begin to withdraw from the network themselves — because who wants to be part of a system where they constantly live in fear of being randomly flagged and removed by AI?
