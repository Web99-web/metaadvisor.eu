# Oracle fired 20,000 people in a 6 AM email?

At 6 in the morning, while most people hadn’t even had their first coffee, thousands of employees around the world opened their inbox and were confronted with a message that changed their lives in seconds. Their position no longer exists. The termination is effective immediately. System access was revoked almost at the same time.

No warning. No transition period. No conversation.

Although Oracle Corporation has not officially confirmed the number of affected employees, estimates circulating across business media, internal channels, and analytical reports suggest as many as 20,000 people. The number is not confirmed — but it has not been denied either — and that silence makes the situation even heavier.

## Numbers that change the perception of security

If these estimates prove accurate, this would be one of the largest and fastest layoffs in modern tech history. But what makes it even more disturbing is not just the number — it’s the way it was done.

One email. One click. One moment.

For many employees, this is not just the end of a job — it is the loss of financial stability on which they built their life decisions. Someone may have paid for a new car yesterday. Someone signed a mortgage. Someone planned a trip they had been waiting for years. And none of them knew that by the next morning, they would be out of a job.

## Not a classic crisis — this is transformation

At first glance, moves like this could suggest a company in serious trouble. But the reality is different. Oracle is not disappearing — it is transforming.

At the center of that transformation is artificial intelligence.

Over the past year, the tech industry has entered an unprecedented race to invest in AI infrastructure. Data centers, cloud platforms, and advanced models require tens — even hundreds — of billions of dollars. Oracle is deeply involved in that race, trying to secure its position among the key players.

## People as cost, infrastructure as priority

In such an environment, business logic changes. Costs are no longer viewed the same way. Employees who spent years building the products and systems the company profits from today are now becoming a line item that can be reduced to free up capital.

On the other side, infrastructure becomes an investment. This is not accidental. This is strategy. Companies are no longer cutting jobs just to survive. Increasingly, they are doing it to redirect resources into technologies that promise efficiency, automation, and long-term growth.

{{< support2 >}}

## A new reality arriving faster than expected

This event is not isolated. Similar patterns are already appearing across the industry. AI filters CVs before a human ever sees them. Algorithms decide which content stays online and which disappears. Systems are making decisions that were once reserved for people.

The difference now is that the consequences are becoming visible — and at scale.

For many, this is the first encounter with that reality. For others, it has already begun.

# This is the NewTech-AI world — one click and you’re gone

For some, this is just another tech headline. But for those who have already experienced what it means when a system suddenly “switches off” their profile, job, or access, this is not an abstract story. It is a reality where a single click can erase years of work, contacts, income, and plans — without warning and without a real way to appeal.

Similar things have already happened to thousands, and likely millions, of small businesses that disappeared overnight from digital platforms. Their profiles were simply shut down. After years of work and effort. The only difference is that nobody hears about them, because they are not one big story, but thousands of individual shutdowns happening below the radar.

## Conclusion

Perhaps the most important question this story raises is not how many people lost their jobs, but what it says about the direction the world is moving in.

Companies are no longer cutting jobs because they are failing.
They are cutting jobs because they are transforming.

And for those who opened that email at 6 in the morning — that transformation has already begun.
