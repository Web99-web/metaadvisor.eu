# Bitcoin near $70,000 as institutional flows return

Bitcoin is once again approaching the psychologically important **$70,000 level**, but the seemingly stable price hides a complex market dynamic. While **U.S. Bitcoin ETFs have started to attract capital again**, altcoin-related funds are experiencing multi-day outflows. At the same time, large institutional buyers and OTC trading desks stepped in during volatility caused by rising oil prices and geopolitical tensions.

This combination places the Bitcoin market **between renewed institutional optimism and technical caution**.

## Bitcoin ETFs attract fresh capital

After a brief period of withdrawals, **U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded fresh inflows**. According to market data, about **$167 million flowed into these funds in a single trading session**, ending a short streak of withdrawals that had totaled roughly **$577 million**.

The return of capital coincided with Bitcoin trading close to **$70,000**, a level widely considered a key psychological and technical threshold for the market.

However, the picture is different across other crypto investment products. **Funds tied to Ether (ETH), XRP and Solana (SOL)** have seen continued outflows even as the underlying assets posted modest gains.

Single-day withdrawals included approximately:

- **$51 million from Ether funds**
- **$18 million from XRP funds**
- **$2.5 million from Solana funds**

In total, Ether-related funds saw about **$225 million in outflows over three days**.

Such behavior is often interpreted as **capital rotation**, where investors reduce exposure to riskier altcoins and move funds back into Bitcoin.

## Oil shock shakes global markets

Bitcoin’s stability has also been tested by developments in global energy markets. **Oil prices have surged roughly 30% in recent weeks**, with major benchmarks such as Brent and WTI briefly trading **above $100 per barrel**.

Rising energy prices typically pressure equity markets because they increase production costs and inflation risks. Asian and European equities experienced significant volatility during the same period.

Bitcoin, however, reacted differently. Instead of falling sharply, the cryptocurrency remained relatively stable and even **gained around 4% over the month**.

## OTC demand from large buyers

Analysts believe that part of the demand came from **OTC (over-the-counter) trading desks**. These private trading venues allow large transactions to be negotiated directly between buyers and sellers rather than through public exchange orderbooks.

OTC markets are widely used by **institutional investors, hedge funds and large asset managers**, enabling them to accumulate large positions without causing sudden price swings.

Some analysts also pointed to ongoing corporate accumulation strategies, where public companies continue adding Bitcoin to their balance sheets.

## Carry trade strategies involving Bitcoin

Market observers have also highlighted the growing use of **carry trade strategies** involving Bitcoin and MicroStrategy (MSTR) stock.

In this strategy traders simultaneously **short MicroStrategy shares** while **buying Bitcoin or Bitcoin ETFs**. The trade can generate profits if Bitcoin rises faster than the stock declines.

Such strategies illustrate how deeply the crypto market has become connected to **traditional financial markets (TradFi)**.

## Orderbook signals possible correction

Despite positive signals from ETF inflows, technical indicators suggest that the market could face resistance at higher levels.

According to market data, **sell-side liquidity in Bitcoin orderbooks has reached its highest level in two months**. Within a band of roughly **5% above the current market price**, sell orders exceed buy orders by about **40%**.

This indicates a relatively heavy supply layer above the current price.

Such setups often appear when investors prepare to **take profits during rallies**.

## Where major Bitcoin supply sits

Further insight comes from the distribution of Bitcoin holdings. Analysts have identified a large cluster of coins accumulated between **$86,000 and $99,000** during late 2025 and early 2026.

At the same time, the average entry price for **short-term holders (STH)** — investors who have held Bitcoin for less than six months — is estimated at around **$88,900**.

This means many newer investors remain underwater and could become sellers if prices recover toward their entry levels.

## A market between optimism and caution

Taken together, the return of ETF inflows, institutional demand and elevated sell liquidity creates a market that sits **between optimism and caution**.

Institutional capital continues to provide support, but technical indicators suggest that some investors may be preparing to realize profits if Bitcoin rallies further.

Periods like this often result in **sideways price action**, where the market consolidates before its next major move.
