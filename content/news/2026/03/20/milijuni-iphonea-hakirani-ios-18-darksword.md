# Millions of iPhones can be hacked — iOS 18 is at the center of the attack

## Attacks that are no longer rare

For years, serious attacks on iPhones were considered rare and reserved for very specific targets such as journalists, politicians, or activists. For most users, there was an unspoken assumption that such scenarios were far removed from everyday life.

But recent findings from security researchers show that this perception is rapidly changing.

A new attack technique called DarkSword demonstrates that compromising iPhones is no longer limited to targeted operations. Instead, a model is emerging that allows for mass exploitation — potentially affecting thousands of users at once.

## Visiting a website is enough

Unlike traditional attacks that require clicking a malicious link or installing an application, DarkSword uses a so-called “drive-by” approach.

In practice, this means the user does not need to do anything specific.

It is enough to:

- open a compromised website  
- and the attack can begin automatically  

Without downloads, without warnings, and without any clear indication that something is happening.

These types of attacks are particularly dangerous because they bypass the basic security habits users rely on.

## What attackers can access

According to analyses by cybersecurity firms, DarkSword enables access to a wide range of data within the device.

This includes:

- passwords and stored data  
- photos and personal files  
- messages from apps such as WhatsApp and Telegram  
- browsing history  
- notes and calendar  

In some cases, attackers may even access data from health-related applications.

It is particularly significant that the attack targets cryptocurrency wallet data, suggesting a potential financial motivation behind these operations.

## Why iOS 18 is key

While the latest versions of iOS include security patches that block this attack, the problem lies in the fact that many users are still running older versions of the system.

Estimates suggest that around a quarter of iPhone users are still using iOS 18.

This means we are not dealing with outdated devices, but with millions of active users who rely on their phones daily for communication, financial activities, and access to sensitive data.

That is exactly why iOS 18 is at the center of the attack. At this point, many users are already making a small but important mistake — and that mistake further adds to the confusion.

## Important note: iPhone 18 ≠ iOS 18

This is where a common misunderstanding comes into play — iPhone 18 is not the same as iOS 18.

At first glance, the terms may sound similar, but they refer to two completely different things.

The iPhone is the device, while iOS is the operating system that runs on that device.

This means that different models — such as iPhone 14, 15, or 16 — can all run the same system version, including iOS 18. It is precisely this version that is currently targeted.

In other words, the issue is not tied to a specific phone model, but to the software version running on it.

## Attacks without traces

One of the key characteristics of DarkSword is how it operates.

Unlike traditional malware, this attack does not install persistent spyware on the device.

Instead, it uses a so-called “fileless” approach:

- it leverages existing system processes  
- leaves no traditional traces  
- disappears after the device is restarted  

This makes it significantly harder to detect and analyze.

The attack functions as a quick operation — it collects data in a short time frame and then disappears.

## From espionage to a marketplace

What makes this story particularly important is not only the technology itself, but also how such tools are now spreading.

Similar tools were previously limited to state actors and highly specialized teams.

Today, there is increasing discussion about an exploit marketplace — a space where vulnerabilities are bought, sold, and redistributed.

In the case of DarkSword, researchers observed that parts of the code were left publicly accessible, significantly increasing the risk of further spread.

This means multiple groups can use the same methods — without developing them themselves.

## What users can do

Although the attack appears complex, the core protective measures remain relatively simple.

The most important one is:

- keeping the operating system up to date  

Additionally, it is recommended to:

- avoid suspicious websites  
- be cautious with unknown links  
- use additional security features available on the device  

Security today is not something that can be assumed — it is something that must be actively maintained.

## Our view

DarkSword clearly shows a shift in how digital attacks are carried out.

What was once rare and targeted is now becoming accessible and scalable.

The line between “targeted individuals” and “regular users” is becoming increasingly blurred.

Perhaps the most important change is not technical, but perceptual.

It is no longer a question of who the target is.

The real question is how many users are still running vulnerable versions — and how many still believe that such attacks only happen to someone else.
