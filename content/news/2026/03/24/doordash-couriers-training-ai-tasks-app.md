# DoorDash looks like delivery — but couriers are now training AI  

Couriers paid to film themselves washing dishes — for AI.

At first glance, DoorDash looks like just another food delivery app. But behind this simple model lies a new phase of technological development, where everyday work is being transformed into data for artificial intelligence. The new “Tasks” app allows couriers to earn money by completing small assignments — from recording videos to taking photos and providing voice samples — all aimed at training AI systems.

According to available information, including Bloomberg reports, the collected audio and video material is used to develop and test AI models — not only for DoorDash itself, but also for its partners across retail, insurance, hospitality and technology sectors. What once seemed like a regular job is now becoming part of a global infrastructure for building artificial intelligence.

## AI is not learning to wash dishes — it’s learning how the world works  

One of the most interesting examples of a task involves filming dishwashing using a body camera. At first glance, this may seem trivial. But in reality, such tasks represent one of the biggest challenges for AI systems.

AI is not trying to learn how to wash dishes — it is trying to understand how the physical world works. As the camera captures hands in motion, the system learns how humans grasp objects, rotate them and adjust movements to different situations. The difference between dirty and clean, between a plate and a glass, between water and foam — all of these are complex problems for artificial intelligence.

Even more importantly, AI learns sequences of actions. A process that humans perform automatically — pick up, wash, rinse, place — becomes a structured chain of decisions that a machine must learn in the correct order. This ability to understand real-world processes is critical for the development of robots and autonomous systems.

## From kitchen to robots: where the data goes  

The data collected by DoorDash does not remain within a single app. It becomes the foundation for systems that will operate in the real world — from household robots to logistics and automation systems.

DoorDash has made it clear that these materials are also used by its partners, meaning this network of couriers is evolving into a broader infrastructure for AI training. What appears today as a simple task could tomorrow become a key component of systems that automate physical work.

## How much they get paid — and why it’s not a traditional job  

Unlike traditional employment, the Tasks model is based on micro-tasks. Pay varies depending on the complexity of the assignment — from a few cents to around ten dollars per task. Simpler tasks often pay less than five dollars, while more complex ones can reach ten or more.

This means workers are not paid for time, but for output. They don’t earn hourly wages — they sell data that AI systems can use. The model increasingly resembles digital piecework — fragmented labor without stability.

## Where DoorDash operates  

DoorDash is primarily focused on the United States, where it is one of the leading food delivery platforms. It also operates in Canada, Australia and parts of Japan. Europe is not currently a core market, but the model is highly transferable.

This suggests that similar systems could eventually appear on European platforms as well.

## A new economy: humans as AI workers  

DoorDash is not alone. Uber has already announced similar initiatives, allowing drivers to earn additional income by collecting data for AI systems.

This signals the emergence of a new model — the AI gig economy. People are no longer just performing physical labor; they are becoming sources of data that power artificial intelligence.

## The dark side: privacy and data value  

This model also raises serious concerns. Workers are recording their own lives — their hands, voices and surroundings. The boundary between work and personal life is becoming increasingly blurred.

At the same time, compensation remains relatively low, while companies use this data to build systems worth billions. This raises a critical question: who truly benefits from this new economy?

## Conclusion  

DoorDash is no longer just a delivery platform. It is becoming infrastructure for training artificial intelligence.

The greatest irony is that the people who may one day be replaced by AI are now actively helping to build it.
