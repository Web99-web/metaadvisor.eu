# Wherever you go — fiat, crypto or digital: Visa and Mastercard are everywhere

While the crypto industry spent years promising a revolution that would remove intermediaries from the financial system, two companies from the “old world” did the exact opposite — **they adapted and ensured they remained at the center of everything**. Instead of being replaced, Visa and Mastercard are once again a core layer through which a massive share of global payments flows — including crypto.

At first glance, crypto was designed as a system without intermediaries: users send funds directly to one another without banks or card networks. But reality tells a different story — most people want simplicity. They want to pay with a card, without thinking about private keys, blockchain mechanics or currency conversions.

This is exactly where Visa and Mastercard found their biggest opportunity.

## 2017–2018: chaos, hype and early failures

In the early stages of crypto development, the first attempts to connect cards and cryptocurrencies began to appear. Projects like **Centra Tech** promised cards that would enable global crypto payments through Visa and Mastercard networks.

The reality was different. Many of these projects ended as scams or failed completely. Infrastructure, regulation and sustainable business models were missing. That period was driven by hype — but lacked real substance.

## 2019–2020: the first real bridge

The real shift came with companies like **Crypto.com**, which introduced the first working solutions. The key was not to replace the existing system, but to use it.

Users could pay with crypto, which was automatically converted into fiat behind the scenes, while the transaction still ran through Visa or Mastercard networks. Forbes described this as the **first sustainable bridge between crypto and real-world spending**.

## Stablecoins: the moment they took control

The major turning point came in 2021 with the rise of stablecoins. Visa partnered with Circle and enabled **USDC for transaction settlement**, turning crypto from an experiment into infrastructure.

Yahoo Finance and PaymentsDive highlighted that Visa no longer saw crypto as a test case, but as part of its core system. Transactions became faster and more global — yet still flowed through existing networks.

Mastercard simultaneously built its own ecosystem of crypto partners. According to WhiteSight and Fintech Magazine, partnerships grew into dozens and eventually over a hundred programs.

In other words: **they didn’t lose control — they redefined it**.

## How they actually make money

One key point often overlooked: Visa and Mastercard **do not make money from crypto itself**. They don’t invest, trade or take on volatility risk.

They make money from infrastructure. When a user pays with crypto, it is converted into fiat, the merchant receives payment, and the transaction runs through their network.

That’s where revenue is generated — through transaction fees.

## The next phase: infrastructure of the future

Since 2023, the focus has shifted even further. It’s no longer just about cards. Visa and Mastercard are actively working on blockchain settlement, stablecoin systems and tokenization.

Reuters reports on investments and acquisitions showing Mastercard’s ambition to play a central role in the stablecoin economy. Visa is developing solutions for **direct blockchain settlement**, preparing for a future where traditional intermediaries may disappear — but the infrastructure remains.

## The ECB enters the game  

In this context, the digital euro is not just a technological project, but a **strategic tool to reduce Europe’s dependence on global private payment networks such as Visa, Mastercard and PayPal**, as well as on the broader ecosystem increasingly tied to the **digital dollar through stablecoins**. That is why the ECB is moving from concept to real implementation — from ATMs to card payments across the eurozone.

👉 [Digital euro enters the real world: ECB prepares ATMs, cards and payments](/news/digital-euro-ecb-atms-cards-payments-eu/)

This introduces a new dimension — it is no longer just about technology, but about **control over financial infrastructure in Europe**. The ECB is not trying to replace existing systems overnight, but clearly signals the intention to build an alternative that does not rely on external players.

## Three systems, one battlefield

Today, three parallel systems are emerging:

- **Crypto (wallet → wallet)**  
- **Visa/Mastercard (intermediary model)**  
- **Digital euro / CBDC (state model)**  

All competing for the same thing: **control over how money moves**.

## Conclusion

Visa and Mastercard didn’t lose the battle to crypto — they changed their strategy. Instead of resisting change, they absorbed it.

The biggest irony is that the companies that were supposed to disappear are now stronger than ever.

No matter what form money takes, one thing remains the same:  
**it still flows through their networks.**

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

- Visa and Mastercard adapted instead of resisting crypto  
- Stablecoins are the key turning point in their transformation  
- The digital euro represents the first serious challenge to their dominance  
- Control over infrastructure matters more than the currency itself  
- The future will be a mix of private and state systems
