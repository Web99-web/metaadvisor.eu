# Is Crypto a Ponzi? Hollywood Enters the Debate

Cryptocurrencies have long been perceived as a technological innovation and a financial alternative to the traditional system, but in recent years they have increasingly become the subject of broader public debate. The latest signal comes from Hollywood, where actor Ben McKenzie, through his documentary *“Everyone Is Lying to You for Money”*, raises a question that is being asked more and more loudly outside the crypto community — is part of the industry built on real value, or on perception and expectations.

Unlike earlier media portrayals that often presented crypto either as a revolution or a scam, this approach attempts to connect concrete events, personal experiences, and the broader market context. The film features key figures such as **Sam Bankman-Fried**, founder of FTX, whose political donations and influence were part of a wider financial and regulatory narrative for years, as well as **Alex Mashinsky**, founder of the Celsius platform, which promised stable returns in an unstable market. At one point, actor **Gerard Butler** also appears, openly admitting that he made money in crypto without truly understanding the system he was investing in — further highlighting the gap between perception and reality.

## FTX (2022): the moment trust broke

The collapse of FTX in 2022 marked one of the biggest turning points in the history of crypto. A platform with global reach, political connections, and strong investor backing collapsed in a very short period of time, leaving behind billions of dollars in losses and severely damaged user trust. FTX was not an isolated case, but a signal of deeper structural issues within the industry. Questions about transparency, handling of user funds, and political influence opened a debate that continues to this day. After that, further collapses followed in waves, with the most notable being the bankruptcy of Celsius.

## Celsius (2022): when crypto becomes USD

The bankruptcy of the Celsius platform in the same year, 2022, further complicated the situation. Although often mentioned together with FTX, it is important to note that Celsius was part of a broader wave of collapses that FTX further accelerated, creating a domino effect within the market. We also wrote about the Celsius paradox and the way accounting was handled:

👉 **Celsius paradox: company mines BTC, but creditors get USD accounting in bankruptcy**
https://metaadvisor.eu/news/celsius-paradox-company-mines-btc-but-creditors-get-usd-in-bankruptcy/

In the case of Celsius, the issue was not only the loss of funds, but also how those funds were treated in legal and financial processes. Many users were confused — there was talk of a 70% recovery, and everyone expected approximately **70% of the value of their BTC**, but the calculation was made in **US dollars at the time of bankruptcy**. At that moment, crypto effectively stopped being crypto and was converted into fiat value.

Payouts came only after a significant delay, often one or two years later. By the time funds were returned, their real value relative to BTC was significantly lower — in some cases not 70%, but only around **20% of the value users had expected**. During that period, users received numerous emails and legal documents, often extensive and written in complex language, making the situation even harder to understand.

**This case raised a key question: what actually happens to cryptocurrencies when they enter legal systems and bankruptcy processes — they cease to be crypto and become fiat (traditional money), in this case USD.**

## The Great Crash of 10.10.2025: a market without clear answers

After the events of 2022, the market gradually stabilized, but a new shock followed with an event remembered by investors as the **Great Crash of 10.10.2025**. Within a very short time, a sharp drop in prices occurred. **Estimates suggest that between 500 billion and nearly 1 trillion dollars in value were wiped from the market during the Great Crash of 10.10.2025, in just a few hours.** In a single hour, the market lost more value than the annual GDP of many countries. Events like these raise the question of how truly decentralized the market is and whether larger players have the ability to trigger chain reactions.

## Understanding as the key problem

One of the most important messages running through the entire story — both the documentary and real-world events — is the difference between the availability of information and its actual understanding. The crypto industry has developed its own language, a mix of technology, finance, and legal frameworks, which often remains difficult for the average user to fully grasp.

This is where the core challenge emerges: decisions are made within a system that many users do not fully understand, while the consequences of those decisions can be significant.
