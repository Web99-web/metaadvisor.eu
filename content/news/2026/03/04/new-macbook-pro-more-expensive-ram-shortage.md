Apple has unveiled its latest MacBook Pro models powered by the new M5 Pro and M5 Max chips, highlighting major performance gains — especially for artificial intelligence workloads. Yet alongside the improved specs comes a noticeable price increase: the new devices are up to $400 more expensive than their predecessors.

According to TechCrunch, the 14-inch MacBook Pro with the M5 Pro chip starts at $2,199, while the 16-inch model begins at $2,699. The more powerful M5 Max variants start at $3,599 and $3,899 respectively. By comparison, the 14-inch MacBook Pro with the M4 Pro launched at $1,999.

## M5 Pro and M5 Max: Built for the AI Era

Apple says the M5 Pro features an 18-core CPU and significantly enhanced GPU performance. The company claims peak GPU compute for AI workloads is more than four times higher compared to the previous generation.

The M5 Max is aimed at professionals working with large AI models, 3D graphics, video production, and advanced software development. As local AI processing becomes increasingly important, Apple positions the MacBook Pro as a premium tool for developers and creators.

But higher performance comes with higher costs.

## RAM Shortage Driving Prices Higher

One of the key factors behind the price increase is the global shortage of RAM (Random Access Memory). The semiconductor market has been under pressure as demand for memory surges due to AI infrastructure expansion and data center growth.

Large language models and AI systems require massive amounts of high-performance memory. Meanwhile, memory chip production depends on a limited number of highly specialized manufacturing facilities. When demand outpaces supply, prices rise.

Analysts warn that not only laptops but also smartphones, servers, and gaming hardware may be affected.

## Is Apple Just the Beginning?

Apple often sets the tone in the premium hardware segment. When it adjusts pricing, competitors and investors take notice.

If memory constraints persist, other manufacturers may follow with price increases. At the same time, consumers must decide whether AI-optimized performance justifies higher costs — or whether upgrades can wait.

## The AI Boom Reshaping Cost Structures

The technology industry is undergoing a transformation driven by artificial intelligence. More powerful chips, greater memory capacity, and higher production demands are reshaping hardware economics.

The new MacBook Pro pricing may not be an isolated case, but rather an early indicator of a broader structural shift in the tech hardware market.
