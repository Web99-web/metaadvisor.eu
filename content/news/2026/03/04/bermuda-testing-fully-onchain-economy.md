When Bermuda announced its ambition to become the world’s first fully onchain national economy, the move sounded revolutionary. Yet the reality is far more measured. The island is not banning cash, not imposing crypto mandates, and not forcing merchants to accept digital assets overnight. Instead, Bermuda is pursuing a cautious, regulator-led transition toward blockchain-based infrastructure.

## What Does “Onchain Economy” Really Mean?

“Onchain” refers to systems and transactions that occur directly on blockchain infrastructure — a decentralized digital ledger secured by cryptography.

In a national context, this could include:
- digital government payments  
- tax settlements  
- asset registries  
- subsidies and grants  
- cross-border settlements  

However, this does not automatically mean eliminating physical cash or digitizing every aspect of daily life.

## Pilot Programs Instead of Revolution

Bermuda plans to begin with carefully designed pilot programs operated through licensed and supervised institutions. Transparency and evaluation are central components.

Expansion will only occur once systems demonstrate reliability and efficiency. The goal is to establish onchain as dependable public infrastructure rather than a political statement.

## The Role of Circle and Coinbase

The initiative is supported by Circle, issuer of the USDC stablecoin, and Coinbase, one of the largest regulated crypto exchanges.

Stablecoins provide:
- price stability  
- digital portability  
- blockchain transparency  

Unlike volatile cryptocurrencies, stablecoins are better suited for institutional and everyday economic functions.

## Regulation as the Foundation

Bermuda has positioned itself as innovation-friendly yet regulatory disciplined. Digital assets operate within defined legal frameworks, not outside them.

This means onchain infrastructure is meant to integrate with — not replace — the existing financial system.

## Will Cash Disappear?

A key concern is whether an onchain economy implies the end of cash.

Based on current information: no. There is no announced plan to eliminate physical currency. The digital layer is intended to complement existing systems.

## Global Context

As central banks explore CBDCs and stablecoins exceed $300 billion in market capitalization, Bermuda’s experiment arrives at a critical global moment.

If successful, the model could serve as a blueprint for smaller economies seeking innovation without destabilization.

## Evolution, Not Ideology

Perhaps the most striking aspect is the restraint. Instead of bold disruption, Bermuda is opting for regulated experimentation and gradual scaling.

In a crypto landscape often associated with volatility, the island aims to position blockchain as reliable state infrastructure.
