Ethereum (ETH), the second-largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization, is once again at a critical crossroads. After attempting to break above the psychological $2,000 level, price action stalled under heavy resistance, pushing ETH back toward the key $1,800 support zone.

According to analysis referenced by Cointelegraph, this level could determine whether March brings stabilization — or another wave of downside pressure.

## Why $1,800 Matters

On the daily chart, ETH/USD has formed a symmetrical triangle — a classic technical pattern that often precedes a decisive breakout. The lower trendline of this formation currently aligns with the $1,800–$1,900 range.

As long as ETH remains above this boundary, the consolidation structure remains intact, leaving room for a renewed push toward $2,000. However, a decisive break below $1,800 could accelerate bearish momentum and expose $1,750 — a level last seen in early February.

Below that, the measured move of the triangle projects a potential target near $1,400, representing roughly a 25–30% decline from current levels.

## On-Chain Data Signals Strong Interest Around $1,890

Cost-basis distribution data shows that approximately 1.23 million ETH were acquired over the past 30 days at an average price close to $1,890. This suggests that a significant number of holders are positioned just above current levels.

Such clusters often act as defensive zones, as investors are reluctant to realize losses. But if price breaks below this area, selling pressure could intensify.

Additional data indicates that around 67,000 ETH — worth roughly $130 million — sit just below spot price, reinforcing the technical importance of this support region.

## Liquidations and Market Structure

Derivatives data reveals that over $120 million in short positions have been liquidated over the past two days, reducing overhead selling pressure.

However, roughly $624 million in cumulative long liquidation exposure remains positioned above $1,800. If price moves sharply downward, forced liquidations could amplify volatility and trigger a cascade effect.

Crypto markets are particularly sensitive to leverage-driven moves, and Ethereum remains one of the most actively traded assets in this environment.

## MVRV and the Broader Picture

The MVRV (Market Value to Realized Value) ratio — a metric comparing market capitalization to realized capitalization — suggests Ethereum is approaching historically undervalued territory.

In previous cycles, similar readings preceded longer-term recoveries. That said, undervaluation alone does not prevent further short-term declines.

Ethereum remains approximately 60% below its 2025 high. At the same time, institutional interest from traditional finance (TradFi — traditional financial institutions) in staking infrastructure, ETFs, and Ethereum-based products continues to provide a structural foundation.

At Metaadvisor.eu, we observe that this tension between short-term technical weakness and long-term structural adoption defines the current phase of the market.

## A Pivotal Moment for ETH

If buyers successfully defend $1,800, Ethereum could enter a stabilization phase and attempt to reclaim $2,000. A breakdown below support, however, would significantly increase the probability of a deeper correction.

March may therefore serve as a critical test of confidence — not only for traders, but for the broader Ethereum ecosystem.

As Metaadvisor.eu continues to monitor on-chain signals and market structure, one thing remains clear: the $1,800 level is not just technical — it is psychological.
