Stablecoins have long been presented as a bridge between traditional finance and the crypto ecosystem. However, a new working paper from the European Central Bank (ECB — European Central Bank, the eurozone’s monetary authority) suggests that this bridge may also introduce structural risks to the banking system.

According to the report titled “Stablecoins and Monetary Policy Transmission,” increasing stablecoin adoption could lead to measurable declines in retail bank deposits and reduced lending to firms. Funds held in digital tokens pegged to the US dollar or euro are funds no longer sitting on bank balance sheets — and deposits remain a core, low-cost funding source for bank lending.

## How Stablecoins Impact Banks

Stablecoins are digital assets typically pegged to fiat currencies such as the US dollar or euro (e.g., USDT or USDC). Their relative price stability makes them attractive compared to more volatile cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin or Ethereum.

Yet this same stability may encourage users to shift savings from traditional bank accounts into stablecoins. The ECB notes that growing interest in stablecoins is associated with declining retail deposits and a reduction in bank lending to the real economy.

The impact is described as nonlinear, depending on the scale of adoption, token design, and regulatory oversight.

## Pressure on Monetary Policy Transmission

A central element of monetary policy is the transmission mechanism: when central banks adjust interest rates, banks are expected to modify lending conditions accordingly.

If a substantial share of liquidity migrates into stablecoins, this transmission channel could weaken. Such a scenario raises concerns for European policymakers, particularly amid discussions about a potential digital euro.

Stablecoin market capitalization has more than doubled over the past three years to approximately $312 billion, with projections suggesting it could reach $2 trillion by 2028.

## FATF: Stablecoins and Illicit Activity

Alongside the ECB’s systemic concerns, the FATF (Financial Action Task Force — the global anti-money laundering watchdog) has warned that stablecoins now account for the majority of illicit crypto transactions.

Estimates indicate that in 2024 alone, approximately $51 billion in illicit activity involved stablecoins. By 2025, 84% of total illicit virtual asset transaction volume was linked to stablecoins.

The report highlights cases involving sanctioned actors in Iran and North Korea who allegedly used dollar-pegged tokens such as USDT for cross-border payments and sanctions evasion.

FATF also identifies peer-to-peer transfers via unhosted wallets as a “key vulnerability,” since such transactions can occur without standard AML (Anti-Money Laundering) controls.

## Tighter Regulation on the Horizon?

While stopping short of calling for outright bans, FATF urges countries to impose stricter AML obligations on stablecoin issuers and consider technical measures such as wallet freezing capabilities.

Within the European Union, the MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets) framework is already in force, aiming to provide regulatory clarity. However, whether it will be sufficient amid exponential market growth remains an open question.

The ECB and FATF warnings are not contradictory — rather, they address different dimensions of risk: systemic banking stability and illicit financial activity.

## What This Means for Markets

Stablecoins form the liquidity backbone of the crypto ecosystem. Without them, exchange trading, DeFi protocols, and cross-border transfers would be far more complex.

Yet their rapid growth places them squarely in the regulatory spotlight. Stricter rules could reshape reserve requirements, operational design, and the broader structure of stablecoin markets.

For investors, this signals that stablecoins are no longer a neutral infrastructure layer — they are now central to debates about the future of digital finance.
