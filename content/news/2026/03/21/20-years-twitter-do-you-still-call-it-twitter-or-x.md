## 20 years of Twitter — do you still call it Twitter or X?

On this day, March 21, 2026, exactly 20 years ago, Jack Dorsey posted a short message: *“just setting up my twttr”*. There was no announcement, no major launch, and no sense that something important was happening. It was an experiment inside a small startup trying to find a new direction after losing its original business opportunity. Yet it is precisely from such moments that things emerge which later define entire industries.

Twitter did not start as a global vision, but as a byproduct of a problem. The company Odeo, where the idea originated, lost its purpose after Apple entered the podcast market. The team had to invent something new, and the solution they came up with was almost trivial — a system for sharing short status messages. Yet that very simplicity became its greatest strength.

At the beginning, messages were limited to 140 characters. The reason was not aesthetic but technical: SMS messages had a limit of 160 characters, and after reserving space for the username, exactly that much remained. This limitation, born out of a technical compromise, turned into a communication style that would later define the internet — fast, concise, and often powerful.

## Who actually created Twitter — and what was happening behind the scenes

Twitter is often associated with a single name — **Jack Dorsey** — but the real story of its creation is far more complex and far less “clean” than it is usually presented. Behind it stood **Biz Stone**, **Evan Williams**, and **Noah Glass**, a team working within the startup Odeo at a time when the company had effectively lost its core idea and had to start over. It was in this environment, between uncertainty and the need to survive, that the concept of Twitter was born.

**Noah Glass** was one of the strongest advocates of the project and the person who proposed the name “Twitter,” while **Evan Williams** brought experience, structure, and financial stability after his success with Blogger. **Biz Stone** shaped the tone and communication style of the platform, which later became its signature. However, as with many startups, development was not linear. There were disagreements and shifts within the team, and **Noah Glass** was eventually pushed out of the company despite his key role in its early days.

That decision remained in the background for years, but today it is often cited as one of the more controversial moments in the platform’s history. The story of Twitter’s founders is therefore not just a story of innovation, but also of risk, uncertainty, and difficult decisions that shaped a product now used by hundreds of millions of people.

## What role did the South by Southwest (SXSW) festival play in Twitter’s breakout?

Twitter’s early growth was slow and organic. The first 10 users were people from the same office. The first 100 came from the broader startup community. Reaching 1,000 users already signaled that something interesting was happening. But the real turning point came in 2007 at the South by Southwest (SXSW) festival, where large screens displayed tweets in real time. People literally stood and watched messages appear live, realizing for the first time how fast information could travel.

At that moment, Twitter stopped being just a tool and became a phenomenon. User numbers began to grow exponentially, and the concept of a “tweet” entered everyday language. The word “tweet” became a global verb — a rare achievement for any digital product.

## 2010 and the Arab Spring — when Twitter moved from the digital world into reality

By 2010, Twitter crossed the boundary of the digital space and began influencing real-world events. During the Arab Spring, the platform was used to organize protests and spread information from countries where traditional media were restricted. Information from the ground reached the public in real time, often before traditional media could pick it up, and also outside their editorial and political limitations. This direct communication allowed users to bypass censorship and information control, giving Twitter an entirely new role in global information flow. This was the moment when the platform became more than a social network — a real-time information tool.

## From messages to media

As the number of users grew, so did the role of the platform. What began as an exchange of short messages became a system through which politicians communicate with the public, journalists follow breaking news, and users participate in real-time discussions. Twitter became a place where news is created, not just distributed.

In 2017, a major change took place — the character limit was increased to 280. This opened space for more detailed expression while preserving the core idea of speed and directness.

## Turning point: Musk and the transformation into X

A major shift came on April 14, 2022, when Elon Musk tweeted *“I made an offer”*. A few months later, the deal worth around $44 billion was completed, and Twitter entered a new phase. In 2023, the platform was rebranded as X, marking the beginning of a redefinition of its identity.

Musk does not see X as a social network, but as infrastructure for a much broader digital system. His vision clearly points toward an “everything app,” similar to China’s WeChat, where communication, payments, shopping, and digital identity become part of a unified ecosystem. In this context, the X Money project is being developed — a payment system integrated directly into the platform that could reshape how users experience social networks.

We have already covered this transformation in the article: [**Elon Musk is building X Money — a digital payment system inside the X platform**](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/x-money-musk-digital-payments-x-platform/)

## Who uses the platform today

Today, the platform has hundreds of millions of users — an estimated 550–600 million monthly active users, with around 250 million using it daily. There is still a predominantly male user base, with estimates suggesting that around 64% of users are men, which influences both content and dominant topics.

Regional differences are also significant. In Nigeria, the platform has very high adoption and is used by a large portion of the online population, while in countries such as Germany or France, its presence is significantly lower. This shows that X is not the same product everywhere, but a platform used differently depending on the market.

## A new phase: AI moderation and the question of the future

Twenty years after the first tweet, it is clear that Twitter — now X — is no longer just a social network, but infrastructure for real-time communication. The platform has changed how information is created, distributed, and how it influences society. Today, it is entering a new phase where communication, finance, and digital identity are being combined into a unified system.

However, as platforms grow, so does the need for control.

This is where one of the biggest shifts in today’s internet emerges — the introduction of AI moderation. Systems that analyze content in real time are becoming essential for managing millions of posts, but they also raise important questions. AI often fails to understand context, humor, irony, or sarcasm, which can lead to incorrect decisions.

On Meta platforms, numerous issues with AI moderation have already been documented, where long-time users — with accounts spanning 15 to 20 years — have reported suspensions and permanent removals without clear explanation. After being removed, they often have no way to contact support.

These situations raise a broader question about the future of digital communities: what happens when systems begin removing the very people who have spent years building the platform? If long-time users disappear, the question becomes — who remains: real people with history and identity, or increasingly content that is generated, filtered, and controlled by algorithms?

In this context, the future of platforms like X depends not only on technology and innovation, but also on the balance between control and freedom of communication.

## Our view

Twenty years after the first tweet, it is clear that Twitter — now X — is one of the most influential communication platforms of the modern internet. It has changed how information is created, spread, and reaches audiences — often faster than ever before in history. What began as a simple idea has become a global system through which millions of people exchange information every day in real time.

Today, the platform is entering a new phase, aiming to become far more than a communication tool — a digital ecosystem connecting content, finance, and everyday online activity. This ability to continuously evolve is one of the key reasons it has endured for two decades.

Twenty years later, one thing is certain — regardless of its name, the platform’s impact remains enormous. If current trends continue, we will be using it even more. Whether X becomes an “everything app” or remains primarily a communication platform will depend on how users respond to these changes — because technology can set the direction, but users decide whether to follow.

One thing has not changed in 20 years — users want freedom of expression, because it is precisely in that unfiltered exchange of information that the platform’s greatest strength lies.

Happy birthday to the platform that changed the internet.
