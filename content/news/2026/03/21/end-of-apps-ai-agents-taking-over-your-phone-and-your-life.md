# The End of Apps? AI Agents Are Taking Over Your Phone — and Your Daily Life

Smartphones as we know them today may soon undergo one of the biggest shifts since their creation. Can you imagine life without the App Store? Instead of dozens of apps we open, search through, and compare every day, the next generation of devices could rely on artificial intelligence that does all of that for us.

According to the vision of Carl Pei, founder and CEO of Nothing, apps as the core interface of technology could gradually disappear. In their place, AI agents would take over — systems that not only execute tasks, but learn user habits, understand context, and increasingly make decisions on our behalf.

But while this vision sounds like a natural evolution of technology, it also raises a number of concerns that are already very real today — especially when it comes to reliability, safety, and truly understanding the user.

## From apps to an AI interface

Today, apps are the primary interface between users and the digital world. Every task has its own app — communication, shopping, travel, entertainment. The user actively navigates the process, compares options, and makes final decisions.

In the emerging model, that relationship changes. The AI agent becomes the central interface connecting all services. Want to travel? Buy a house? Invest money? Instead of searching and comparing, you simply express your intent — and the system handles execution.

At first glance, this means simplicity. In reality, it also means shifting control from the user to the system. How reliable are these systems, really? Even if they are correct 99% of the time, that remaining 1% could send you to the wrong destination or invest your money into something that fails. Who takes responsibility then?

## AI makes decisions — but doesn’t always understand

The core assumption behind this vision is that AI can understand users well enough to act on their behalf. However, current experience shows that this is not yet the case.

AI systems can hallucinate — meaning they generate incorrect or entirely fabricated information while presenting it as accurate. In real-world situations, this could lead to wrong bookings, poor recommendations, or flawed financial decisions.

Additionally, AI often struggles with context in ways humans do not. Humor, sarcasm, and user intent can be misinterpreted. This is not theoretical — it is already happening.

For example, AI moderation systems on social platforms have recently flagged, restricted, or even removed Facebook long-time users without clear explanations. Possibly, millions of users may have been affected by incorrect interpretations of content.

If AI already struggles with basic interactions, it raises serious questions about its readiness to handle complex, real-life decision-making.

## Trust and safety as the key challenge

For AI agents to replace apps, users must fully trust them. That means trusting that the system makes correct decisions, avoids critical errors, and truly understands user needs.

Right now, that level of trust does not exist.

Another major issue is transparency. With apps, users can see the process — what they choose and why. With AI systems, decisions often happen inside a “black box.” Users see the outcome, but not how it was reached or what alternatives were considered.

In practice, this could mean the system keeps recommending the same choices based on past behavior, while better options go unnoticed simply because the user never sees them.

This lack of visibility creates an additional layer of uncertainty — especially as AI becomes more autonomous.

## Whoever controls AI controls everything

If AI becomes the main interface, the entire digital economy shifts. It’s no longer about which app is best — it’s about who controls the AI layer making decisions for the user.

This raises concerns about concentration of power. Companies controlling operating systems and AI infrastructure could gain unprecedented influence over everyday decisions.

In such a model, users no longer actively choose — the system chooses for them.

## Conclusion: between vision and reality

A world without apps is not impossible — but it is still far from reality. The technology is advancing quickly, but challenges like hallucinations, safety, and user understanding remain significant.

Apps may not disappear overnight, but the way we use them is already changing. AI is becoming the intermediary between users and the digital world — gradually taking on more responsibility.

## Our view

This vision represents a logical direction of technological evolution, but also raises critical questions about control and trust. If AI can make mistakes, misinterpret users, or even exclude them from systems — as we are already seeing — then full reliance on it becomes risky.

The future may not be fully app-free, but rather a balance between automation and control.

Because once technology starts making decisions for us, the question is no longer what we can do — but who is actually deciding.
