# Opera and Celo redefine payment models

Opera, a Norwegian technology company best known for its web browser, is proposing a major shift in how it gets compensated by the Celo blockchain ecosystem. Instead of receiving quarterly payments in US dollars, the company wants to be paid in native CELO tokens, aligning its business model more directly with the performance of the network.

This move comes as Opera continues to reposition its browser as a financial platform rather than just a tool for accessing the web. The integration of crypto wallets and the development of the MiniPay app clearly indicate a strategic focus on markets outside Europe, particularly regions where traditional banking services are limited or inefficient.

## MiniPay and the expansion of crypto payments

The collaboration between Opera and the Celo ecosystem dates back to 2021, when stablecoins were first integrated into Opera’s built-in wallet. Today, the partnership revolves largely around MiniPay — a self-custodial crypto wallet that allows users to send and receive money via mobile devices without relying on traditional banks.

MiniPay has grown to more than 14 million users and is increasingly used in regions such as Latin America and Africa. Integrations with local payment systems like Brazil’s PIX and Mercado Pago have further enhanced its usability, especially in economies where banking services are expensive or difficult to access.

## Why Opera is choosing tokens over dollars

The proposal includes an allocation of approximately 160 million CELO tokens, which would make Opera one of the largest institutional holders within the ecosystem. However, this shift is not without risk, as token values can fluctuate significantly compared to stable dollar payments.

On the other hand, this model allows Opera to directly participate in the network’s growth. If the Celo ecosystem expands, the value of the tokens could significantly exceed traditional revenue streams. In that sense, Opera is shifting its role from a service provider to a stakeholder with a direct financial interest in the success of the network.

## What this means in a broader context

This move reflects a broader trend in which technology companies are increasingly experimenting with token-based models and decentralized finance. Instead of fixed contracts and predictable revenue, companies are aligning incentives with the growth and adoption of blockchain ecosystems.

At the same time, these models raise important questions about regulation, stability, and long-term sustainability. While such systems can thrive in emerging markets where financial infrastructure is lacking, traditional systems still dominate in developed economies.

## Conclusion

The partnership between Opera and Celo highlights how the boundaries between technology, finance, and the internet are becoming increasingly blurred. A browser is no longer just a tool — it is evolving into a platform for payments, transactions, and financial services.

Whether this model will gain traction in Europe and other developed markets remains uncertain. What is clear, however, is that the global financial landscape is already beginning to shift.

- The shift to tokens signals strong confidence in the Celo ecosystem  
- MiniPay has the greatest potential in regions with limited banking infrastructure  
- These models represent a parallel financial system outside traditional institutions  
- The main risk remains token volatility and dependence on network performance
