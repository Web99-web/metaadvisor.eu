The **US bank Capital One** has announced it will acquire fintech company **Brex** in a deal valued at **$5.15 billion**, marking one of the clearest moves yet by a major American bank into **crypto payments** and **stablecoin-based infrastructure**.

This is one of the largest fintech acquisitions in recent years and comes only months after Brex launched native support for stablecoins — digital tokens pegged to the US dollar.

The transaction will be completed through a mix of cash and stock and is expected to close in **mid-2026**, subject to regulatory approval.

## Why are banks allowed to buy other companies?

A common question raised by this deal is simple: **Are banks actually allowed to buy other firms?**  
The short answer is **yes — but under strict regulatory oversight**.

Large banking groups such as Capital One are permitted to acquire other companies as long as:
- the deal is approved by financial regulators,
- it does not threaten the bank’s stability,
- and the acquired company fits within payments, finance, or financial infrastructure.

In this case, Capital One is **not buying a crypto exchange**, but a **payments-focused fintech** that uses stablecoins as part of its technology stack. Such acquisitions are well within the boundaries of existing banking regulations.

## Why this acquisition matters

Capital One is widely known in the US for credit cards and consumer banking. By acquiring Brex, the bank is sending a clear signal that **traditional financial institutions are no longer ignoring crypto-related technology**, but are instead integrating it into their core services.

Capital One founder and CEO **Richard Fairbank** said the acquisition accelerates the bank’s long-term strategy, particularly in the area of **business and corporate payments**.

## What is Brex and how does crypto fit in?

Brex is a fintech company best known for **corporate cards and financial tools for startups and technology companies**. In October, it became the **first global corporate card provider** to enable **native stablecoin payments**, starting with **USDC**.

Stablecoins are increasingly seen as a bridge between traditional banking and blockchain technology, enabling faster digital payments without the volatility associated with many cryptocurrencies.

Brex CEO **Pedro Franceschi** confirmed he will continue to lead the company after the acquisition, stating that the combined organization will be able to move faster and invest more deeply in modern financial infrastructure.

## Stablecoins and banks: a growing trend

The acquisition comes at a time when stablecoins are gaining rapid acceptance within traditional finance, especially following the introduction of clearer regulatory frameworks in the United States.

The total market capitalization of stablecoins has risen by **18.6%**, reaching approximately **$314 billion**, making the sector too large for major banks to ignore.
