The U.S. **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** has agreed to **withdraw its lawsuit against crypto exchange Gemini** related to the now-defunct **Gemini Earn** product, effectively closing one of the most significant regulatory cases to emerge from the **FTX collapse** and the broader crypto crisis of 2022.

In **2023**, the SEC sued **Gemini** and **Genesis Global Capital**, alleging that the companies had offered **unregistered securities** through the Earn program. Under the structure, users deposited their crypto assets with Gemini, which then lent those funds to Genesis. After FTX collapsed and market conditions deteriorated sharply, Genesis **halted withdrawals**, leaving many users unable to access their assets.

## Why the SEC is withdrawing the case now

The case was effectively **paused in April last year** following a leadership change at the SEC. In a recent joint court filing, the SEC and Gemini stated that the **full repayment of customer funds** was the decisive factor.

Through Genesis’ bankruptcy process, **Gemini Earn users have now recovered 100% of their crypto assets**. On that basis, the SEC concluded that continuing the litigation was no longer warranted. While a federal judge must still formally approve the dismissal, the case is widely viewed as effectively closed.

## Genesis and the regulatory resolution

**Genesis** had already resolved its regulatory exposure earlier, reaching a settlement with the SEC that included a **$21 million penalty**. **Gemini** had also previously settled with New York state regulators, bringing its outstanding Earn-related disputes to an end.

Notably, the lawsuit had previously **survived a motion to dismiss**, after a judge ruled that the SEC had plausibly alleged securities law violations. However, the changed legal and economic context ultimately led to a different outcome.

## A broader signal to the crypto industry

The **Gemini–Genesis** case is part of a wider trend in which the SEC has **dropped more than a dozen crypto-related enforcement actions** over the past year, many of which were initiated in the immediate aftermath of the FTX collapse.

For Gemini, which has long positioned itself as a **compliance-focused and institutionally oriented platform**, the decision represents a significant **reputational relief**. At the same time, the Earn saga remains a reminder that **yield-bearing crypto products** continue to sit at the most sensitive intersection of market risk and regulatory scrutiny.

- **Gemini exits the case reputationally stronger, while Genesis had already closed its obligations.**  
- **Earn and yield products remain the most fragile segment of crypto regulation.**
