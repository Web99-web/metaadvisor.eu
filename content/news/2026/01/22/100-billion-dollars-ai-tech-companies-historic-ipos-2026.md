# 100+ Billion Dollars: AI and Tech Companies Prepare Historic IPOs in 2026

The year 2026 could mark a historic moment for global capital markets. For the first time, several private technology companies are approaching potential public listings with valuations exceeding $100 billion — a scale previously reserved for only the largest, long-established corporations.

At the heart of this shift are artificial intelligence, space technology, and digital payment infrastructure — sectors that have attracted unprecedented investment while simultaneously raising concerns about valuation sustainability.

## Concrete IPO candidates above $100 billion

Among the most frequently cited IPO candidates for 2026 are **OpenAI**, **Anthropic**, **SpaceX**, and **Stripe**. These companies are often described as *hectocorns* — firms with potential valuations well above the $100 billion threshold.

**OpenAI** has become the defining name of the AI boom. Since the global adoption of generative AI models, the company has positioned itself as a central pillar of the AI ecosystem, supported by major strategic investments and partnerships.

**Anthropic** is widely viewed as one of the most serious competitors in advanced AI development, with a strong emphasis on safety, alignment, and regulatory compliance — themes that are increasingly important for institutional investors.

**SpaceX**, the privately held space technology giant, dominates commercial satellite launches and space infrastructure. An IPO would have far-reaching implications not only for the tech sector, but also for aerospace, defense, and government-linked industries.

**Stripe** remains one of the most anticipated fintech IPOs globally. As a backbone of online payments and digital commerce, the company plays a critical role in global financial infrastructure and has long been rumored to be preparing for a public listing.

## Artificial intelligence as the valuation engine

What unites these companies is their direct or indirect exposure to artificial intelligence and scalable digital infrastructure. Investors increasingly view AI as a long-term productivity engine capable of reshaping entire industries — a narrative used to justify extraordinary valuations ahead of profitability.

Still, market observers warn that expectations may be running ahead of fundamentals, echoing concerns seen in previous technology cycles.

## IPOs under geopolitical and regulatory pressure

These potential IPOs come amid heightened geopolitical tension, trade disputes, and expanding regulatory scrutiny of large technology firms. Several public offerings were delayed in recent years due to political gridlock and institutional disruption.

Despite these risks, markets remain resilient. Fueled by AI optimism, technology stocks continue to trade near record highs, and investor risk appetite remains elevated.

## Will 2026 confirm — or puncture — the AI euphoria?

If **OpenAI**, **Anthropic**, **SpaceX**, or **Stripe** proceed with IPOs, 2026 may become the year that determines whether the AI boom represents a durable economic shift or another cycle of inflated expectations.
