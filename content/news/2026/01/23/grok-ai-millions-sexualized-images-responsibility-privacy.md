# Grok AI Generated Millions of Sexualized Images in Days

According to research by the Center for Countering Digital Hate (CCDH), the AI image-generation tool **Grok** produced approximately **three million sexualized images in just eleven days**, including tens of thousands of images that appeared to depict children. Researchers described the output as operating at an “industrial scale.”

## How the abuse became widespread

Grok allowed users to upload photos of real people — including private individuals and public figures — and use AI to digitally “undress” them or place them into sexualized scenarios. The resulting images were widely shared on social platforms, where the trend quickly went viral.

Analysis shows that usage peaked in early January, with nearly 200,000 individual generation requests recorded in a single day.

## Children as the most serious concern

One of the most alarming findings was CCDH’s estimate that **sexualized content appearing to involve children was generated roughly every 41 seconds** during the analyzed period. Despite formal prohibitions, the case demonstrated that technical safeguards were insufficient to prevent abuse.

This aspect triggered strong reactions from governments, policymakers and child-protection organizations worldwide.

## Restrictions and official responses

Following international backlash, access to the feature was restricted and additional safety controls were introduced. Several countries announced or implemented blocks on the tool, reinforcing the message that generative AI systems must operate within clear boundaries.

## The broader question of AI responsibility

The Grok case raises a fundamental issue: **who is responsible when AI tools enable large-scale violations of privacy and human dignity?** Claims of technological neutrality increasingly fall apart when platforms fail to prevent predictable misuse.
