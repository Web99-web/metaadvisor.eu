# The Collapse of the Truebit (TRU) Token After a Hacker Attack

The crypto market has once again faced one of its most severe scenarios: a hacker attack that wiped out nearly the entire value of a token within a very short time. This time, the target was **Truebit (TRU)** — a project that had been developed over several years as a solution for scalable and verifiable computation on blockchain networks.

After approximately **8,535 ETH** were drained from the protocol — valued at around **$26.6 million** at the time — the price of the TRU token collapsed by roughly **99.9%**, leaving investors and the community facing what was effectively a total loss.

## What Is Truebit and What Was TRU Used For?

Truebit is a project with roots dating back to the late 2010s, designed to enable complex computations to be performed off-chain while remaining cryptographically verifiable. The goal was to overcome one of the core limitations of smart contracts: restricted computational capacity.

The TRU token functioned as a **utility token** within the Truebit ecosystem, incentivizing participants who performed and verified computational tasks. While the project never reached mainstream adoption, it was widely regarded as technically ambitious and academically grounded.

## How the Attack Happened and Why the Impact Was So Severe

According to available information, the attacker exploited a vulnerability in the protocol’s smart contracts, allowing them to extract large amounts of ether in a short period of time. In such incidents, response time is critical, but once funds are moved across addresses, recovery becomes extremely difficult or impossible.

Because TRU already had limited market liquidity, the sudden loss of funds and confidence triggered panic selling, accelerating the token’s collapse.

## A Broader Lesson for the Crypto Market

The Truebit case serves as another reminder of the structural risks inherent in decentralized finance and blockchain projects. Regardless of technical sophistication or years of development, **a single security flaw** can erase trust, value, and momentum within minutes.

The incident underscores the importance of independent security audits, ongoing code reviews, bug bounty programs, and conservative treasury management — especially for projects handling significant capital.

## Our Take (MetaAdvisor)

- Hacker attacks remain one of the most significant systemic risks in crypto.
- Technical ambition alone does not guarantee security.
- Smaller tokens with limited liquidity are particularly vulnerable to shocks.
- Trust, once lost, is extremely difficult to rebuild.
- This case highlights the ongoing need for caution and diversification.
