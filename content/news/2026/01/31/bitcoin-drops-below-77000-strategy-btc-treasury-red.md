# Bitcoin Drops Below $77,000 as Strategy Turns Red

Bitcoin fell to around **$76,550** over the weekend, marking its lowest level since spring 2025. The break below the key **$80,000** threshold intensified selling pressure in a market already strained by heavy leverage and fragile liquidity.

The move also carried symbolic weight: **Strategy**, the world’s largest corporate holder of Bitcoin, slipped **below its average acquisition cost** for the first time.

## What triggered the sell-off

Several forces converged:

- **Thin weekend liquidity**
- **Excessive long leverage**
- **Cascading forced liquidations** (over $800 million)
- **Macro uncertainty** (U.S. partial shutdown, strong dollar, geopolitics)

Once $80,000 failed, stop-losses and risk controls accelerated the downside.

## Strategy and the “True Market Mean”

Strategy holds roughly **700,000 BTC** accumulated over multiple years. Falling below the **true market mean** — the aggregate cost basis of active BTC supply — signals that even long-term institutional positions are temporarily underwater.

This does not imply selling pressure from Strategy, but it highlights how sharp deleveraging events can overwhelm fundamentals in the short term.

## Why price slipped this far

- Major technical supports broke  
- Buyers stepped aside amid volatility  
- ETF inflows remained muted  
- Large players preferred stabilization over catching falling knives  

## What comes next

Such episodes are often viewed as **market resets** rather than trend reversals. Historically, heavy liquidation phases tend to precede accumulation — but rarely an immediate rebound.

Markets will now watch:
- institutional buying behavior  
- post-weekend liquidity conditions  
- macro signals and policy developments  

## Our view (Metaadvisor):

- This was a mechanical deleveraging event, not a fundamental breakdown  
- Strategy turning red is symbolic, not structural  
- Large capital buys stability, not panic  
- These phases often lay the groundwork for the next leg higher
