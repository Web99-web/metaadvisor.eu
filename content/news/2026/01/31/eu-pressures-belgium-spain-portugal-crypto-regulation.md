# EU Pressures Belgium, Spain, Portugal and Others Over Crypto Regulation

The European Commission is stepping up pressure on several member states that have **failed to fully implement EU crypto regulation and tax transparency rules**. As part of its January infringement package, the Commission announced formal notices to **12 EU countries**.

The goal is clear: crypto markets must be subject to the same **reporting and oversight standards** as traditional finance — not only legislated, but effectively enforced at the national level.

## Which countries received formal notices?

According to the Commission, the following member states were cited:

- Belgium  
- Bulgaria  
- Czech Republic  
- Estonia  
- Greece  
- Spain  
- Cyprus  
- Luxembourg  
- Malta  
- Netherlands  
- Poland  
- Portugal  

All were flagged for shortcomings in implementing the EU’s **crypto tax reporting and information-exchange rules**.

## What regulation is at stake?

Under the expanded EU framework, crypto asset service providers must:

- report user data  
- disclose transaction information  
- enable cross-border tax data exchange  

The measures aim to combat **tax evasion, avoidance and regulatory loopholes** in digital assets and are closely aligned with the **OECD’s global crypto framework**.

## Hungary singled out over MiCA

In the same package, **Hungary** received a separate formal notice for failing to comply with the **MiCA framework** (Markets in Crypto-Assets), the EU’s cornerstone regulation for crypto assets, stablecoins and service providers.

Hungary has two months to respond before further legal steps may follow.

## What happens if countries fail to comply?

If member states do not act, the Commission may:

- issue a reasoned opinion  
- escalate infringement proceedings  
- refer the case to the European Court of Justice  

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

- The EU is signaling that regulatory leniency is over.
- Enforcement, not legislation, is now the priority.
- Crypto platforms will face tighter oversight.
- Retail investors are not the primary target, but transparency will increase.
- MiCA and tax rules are becoming the backbone of Europe’s crypto market.
