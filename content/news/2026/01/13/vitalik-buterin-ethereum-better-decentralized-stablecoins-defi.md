# Ethereum and the Question of Real Stable Money

As decentralized finance (DeFi) enters a more mature phase, one fundamental question becomes increasingly important: **what kind of money are we actually using inside these systems?**  
According to Ethereum co-founder **Vitalik Buterin**, the current answer is deeply flawed.

In early 2026, Buterin publicly stated that Ethereum and the broader DeFi ecosystem urgently need **better decentralized stablecoins** if they are to provide genuine financial independence rather than a digital replica of the existing banking system.

## Problem 1: Overreliance on the US Dollar

Today, roughly **95% of all stablecoins are pegged to the US dollar**. While this simplifies pricing and adoption in the short term, it leaves most of DeFi structurally dependent on a single currency, a single state and a single monetary policy.

Buterin argues that this creates a long-term vulnerability. If a fiat currency experiences inflation, debt crises or political instability, any stablecoin tied to it inherits those risks — regardless of how robust the underlying blockchain may be.

A truly independent form of digital money, he says, should not rely on the long-term stability of one nation-state.

## Problem 2: Oracles as a Hidden Weak Point

Another major challenge lies in **oracles** — the systems that feed real-world data such as prices and collateral values into blockchains.

Buterin highlights a difficult balance:
- Weak oracles expose systems to manipulation.
- Overly complex or expensive oracles increase costs and limit accessibility.

For stablecoins to work at scale, oracle systems must be **secure, resilient and affordable**, without creating new barriers for everyday users.

## Problem 3: Yields That Encourage Speculation

Buterin is also critical of high DeFi yields, which often attract users but undermine stability over time. Excessive yields incentivize speculation rather than real-world usage and can destabilize collateral structures.

As an alternative, he suggests **very low but sustainable base yields**, potentially as low as 0.2% annually, combined with new mechanisms that prioritize long-term stability over short-term growth. While unpopular in a yield-driven market, this approach aligns with systemic resilience.

## The Bigger Question: DeFi or Just a New Banking Layer?

At its core, Buterin’s critique asks a deeper question:  
**Will DeFi become a true alternative to traditional finance — or merely a new digital layer of the same system?**

If stablecoins remain centralized, state-dependent and tightly controlled, DeFi risks losing its original purpose. Instead of financial freedom, users may simply inherit a more technical version of existing dependencies.

## Why This Matters for Ordinary People

Stablecoins are the backbone of DeFi. They are used for saving, lending, payments and inflation protection. If their design is flawed, **the risk ultimately falls on users**, not institutions.

Better decentralized stablecoins would mean:
- reduced dependence on banks and governments  
- stronger resilience during crises  
- fairer global access to financial services  

This makes the debate not just technical, but deeply social and economic.
