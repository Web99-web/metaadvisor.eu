# Coinbase’s Base Under Pressure: Zora, Creators and a Developer Backlash

Coinbase’s Base blockchain enters 2026 with rising activity — and rising tension. While the network continues to grow on the surface, a deeper conflict is unfolding inside its ecosystem. An increasing number of developers argue that Base is drifting away from its original mission and prioritizing visibility and hype over long-term building.

At the center of the controversy is Zora, a platform that allows content such as posts, images and videos to be tokenized and traded on-chain. For many creators, this represents an exciting way to monetize content and participate in blockchain without technical barriers. For developers who helped build Base in its early days, however, the shift feels like a clear change in direction — and not in their favor.

## How Base arrived at this moment

Base launched as an Ethereum layer-2 with a straightforward promise: faster, cheaper transactions and a welcoming environment for developers building real-world applications. Early on, the network attracted a range of serious projects focused on finance, infrastructure and tooling. Much of this work happened quietly, without heavy marketing, but it laid the groundwork for the ecosystem’s growth.

Over time, the narrative evolved. Coinbase began highlighting the cultural and social side of blockchain more aggressively, with Zora becoming the flagship of that vision. Creator coins and tokenized content increasingly dominated official messaging and social media promotion, reshaping how Base was perceived by the wider public.

## Why developers feel sidelined

The frustration expressed by developers is not primarily about Zora itself. Instead, it stems from a growing sense of imbalance. Many builders report struggling to gain even minimal recognition for their launches, while Zora-linked initiatives regularly receive visibility and support from official Base channels.

This dynamic has fueled concerns that Base is rewarding viral potential rather than sustained effort and technical depth. Developers warn that an ecosystem cannot rely on attention alone. Without strong infrastructure and long-term commitment from builders, they argue, short-term trends may come at the cost of durability and trust.

## A familiar tension across the tech industry

The situation unfolding at Base reflects a broader pattern seen throughout technology platforms. At some point, ecosystems are forced to choose between depth and reach, between slow, steady construction and rapid growth driven by hype. These decisions rarely satisfy everyone, and they often expose underlying cultural divides.

Base now finds itself at this crossroads. Lean too heavily into creator-driven narratives, and it risks alienating the developers who give the network substance. Ignore creators altogether, and it could lose momentum and public interest. The challenge lies in striking a balance that allows both to coexist.

## What this means for users and the market

To outsiders, the debate may appear like internal drama, but its implications are far-reaching. Platforms that lose developer confidence often lose innovation as well. Conversely, if Base manages to reconcile creators and builders, it could emerge as one of the few blockchains that successfully blend technology, culture and practical utility.

How Coinbase responds to this growing criticism will be closely watched. What is clear is that this is not a fleeting social media dispute, but a deeper conversation about the future direction of one of the most prominent blockchain projects backed by a major centralized exchange.

## Our Take (MetaAdvisor)

Base is at a delicate turning point. Zora has brought energy, attention and new users, but without satisfied developers, sustainable growth is difficult to achieve. If Coinbase can align creator enthusiasm with long-term building, Base could strengthen its position. If not, key contributors may choose to build elsewhere.
