# X offers $1,000,000 for the most-read article

The platform **X** has published an announcement that immediately caught the attention of writers, creators and media professionals: the author of the **most-read article** posted on X over the next **two weeks** could earn **up to $1,000,000**.

The message was shared by **Nikita Bier**, a key figure involved in X’s growth and creator strategy. In a short but direct statement, X sent a clear signal to authors worldwide: *it’s time to write*.

## What does “most-read article” actually mean?

X has not released formal rules or a detailed framework for this initiative. However, the wording strongly suggests that success will be measured by:
- **total number of reads**,  
- **audience engagement**,  
- and overall **virality during the two-week period**.

This means the winning content does not have to follow a traditional journalistic format. Instead, the focus is on articles that capture attention, spark discussion and spread widely across the platform.

## Why is X doing this now?

X has been steadily evolving from a social media network into a **platform for news, analysis and public debate**. With existing creator monetization tools and ad revenue sharing already in place, this announcement represents a much more aggressive step.

By publicly referencing a potential seven-figure payout, X is signaling that **long-form written content can compete economically with video**, provided it reaches enough readers.

## Two weeks as a live experiment

The two-week time frame appears intentional. Rather than launching a permanent program, X is running a **short, high-intensity experiment** designed to:
- stimulate rapid content creation,  
- test how users consume long-form articles,  
- identify creators capable of achieving massive reach.

For authors, the message is clear: background, credentials or affiliation matter less than the **impact and reach of the text itself**.

- Emphasizing readership and reach restores value to long-form writing.  
- This move could significantly reshape how independent writers compete with traditional media.
