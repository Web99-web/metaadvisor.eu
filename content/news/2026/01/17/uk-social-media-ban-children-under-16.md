# More than 100,000 people in the UK call for a social media ban for children

Public pressure is rapidly growing in the United Kingdom. **More than 100,000 people** have contacted their local Members of Parliament, calling for a **ban on social media platforms for children under the age of 16**. The campaign reflects increasing concern about the impact of digital platforms on children’s mental health and development.

The initiative was launched by **Smartphone Free Childhood**, a grassroots movement that quickly mobilized supporters by providing a template letter urging lawmakers to introduce age-appropriate boundaries for online platforms.

## What are campaigners asking for?

Rather than opposing technology altogether, campaigners are calling for **clear and enforceable age limits**. They argue that current safeguards are ineffective, while social media algorithms are designed to maximize engagement rather than protect young users.

Key concerns raised by parents and health experts include:
- rising anxiety and depression among teenagers  
- reduced attention span and sleep disruption  
- exposure to extreme or harmful content  
- addictive usage patterns driven by algorithmic design  

## Government response in the UK

Prime Minister **Keir Starmer** has acknowledged the growing public concern, stating that the government is actively exploring further protections for children online. He indicated that **a ban on social media for under-16s is among the options being considered**, noting that the UK is closely watching developments in **Australia**, where similar restrictions are already being discussed or implemented.

Starmer also highlighted concerns about **very young children**, pointing out that many are entering school at age four having spent excessive time on screens, potentially affecting their cognitive and social development.

## Health experts raise the alarm

Health Secretary **Wes Streeting** has warned that while social media can help people connect, it also poses serious risks to young minds. He compared unrestricted access to social media to leaving children unsupervised with dangerous tools — emphasizing that no responsible society would allow that offline.

According to Streeting, the issue is not about banning technology itself, but about recognizing that **children require stronger safeguards** in digital environments that were never designed with their well-being as the primary goal.

## A wider global debate

The UK debate is part of a broader international reassessment of social media’s role in society. Governments around the world are increasingly questioning:
- whether platforms can regulate themselves  
- how much responsibility should fall on tech companies  
- and when state intervention becomes necessary  

Although no law has yet been passed, the scale of public support suggests that **stricter regulation of social media in the UK is becoming increasingly likely**.

- Social media is increasingly framed as a public health concern, not just a tech issue.  
- Decisions made in the UK could influence future policy across Europe.
