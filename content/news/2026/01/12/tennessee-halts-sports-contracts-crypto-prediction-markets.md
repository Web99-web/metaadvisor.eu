# Tennessee moves against sports contracts on crypto prediction markets

The U.S. state of Tennessee has ordered **Kalshi, Polymarket, and Crypto.com** to **stop offering sports-related contracts**, opening a new regulatory battle over the boundary between prediction markets and traditional sports betting.

Importantly, the order **does not apply to all contracts** offered by these platforms. It specifically targets products tied to sporting events.

## Why regulators stepped in

According to Tennessee regulators, sports-related contracts on these platforms **function in practice much like traditional sports bets**, even if they are marketed under different terminology and built on modern technological infrastructure.

While the platforms argue that these products are **financial instruments driven by market-based price discovery**, regulators maintain that betting on sports outcomes falls under gambling laws and therefore requires specific licenses and oversight.

## Prediction markets vs. sports betting

Prediction markets allow users to buy and sell contracts whose value depends on the outcome of real-world events. In theory, such markets help **aggregate collective expectations and probabilities**, often producing accurate forecasts.

When applied to sports, however, the distinction becomes blurred. Outcomes are binary, time-limited, and emotionally charged — factors regulators cite when classifying these products as **sports betting rather than financial markets**.

## Why Kalshi, Polymarket, and Crypto.com matter

Each platform represents a different model:
- **Kalshi** operates within a U.S. regulatory framework for prediction markets.
- **Polymarket** relies on decentralized infrastructure and is popular within the Web3 ecosystem.
- **Crypto.com** is a major global crypto exchange expanding beyond traditional trading services.

What they share is an attempt to **frame sports outcomes as market-based products**, an approach now facing growing regulatory resistance.

## Broader implications for the crypto industry

Tennessee’s action could become a **precedent for other U.S. states**. If similar measures spread, prediction markets may face fragmented regulation, with certain contract types permitted in some jurisdictions and banned in others.

For the crypto industry, this adds another layer of uncertainty and highlights the need for clearer regulatory guidance — especially for products that sit at the intersection of finance, technology, and entertainment.

## The core question: innovation or legal loophole?

The Tennessee case raises a broader issue:  
**How should regulators treat blockchain-based financial products that closely resemble traditional gambling models?**

Regulation based purely on form risks stifling innovation, while ignoring substance risks undermining existing laws. Finding the right balance remains an unresolved challenge.
