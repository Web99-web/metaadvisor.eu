## Why is crypto content on X losing visibility?

In recent months, a growing number of crypto profiles have noticed the same pattern: posts receive significantly less reach, engagement drops sharply, and content often fails to appear even in the feeds of existing followers. Notably, this is not limited to large or aggressive accounts, but also affects smaller, newer, or carefully managed profiles that post infrequently and show no signs of spam behavior.

This trend has sparked debate within the crypto community over whether X is now effectively restricting the crypto topic itself rather than addressing problematic accounts individually.

## Bot spam as the trigger for algorithmic restrictions

Publicly available data points to a sharp increase in automated posts associated with the keyword “crypto.” On certain days, millions of such posts are generated, most of them offering little to no informational value and often serving spam, phishing, or manipulation purposes.

X’s algorithms respond to these patterns by limiting reach. The problem arises when the system fails to reliably distinguish bots from real users. Instead of removing spam accounts selectively, visibility is reduced across an entire topic — including legitimate crypto media outlets, analysts, and educational profiles.

## X’s position: too many posts, too little value?

From X’s perspective, part of the issue is attributed to user behavior. According to this view, many crypto accounts exhaust their daily reach by overposting or engaging in low-value interactions. Since users see only a limited number of posts per day, later content — even if meaningful — receives less exposure.

However, this explanation falls short. It does not account for profiles that post rarely, avoid mass replies, and display no spam patterns at all. This suggests that the core issue lies less in user behavior and more in how crypto-related content is categorized and treated by the algorithm.

## Why are “clean” profiles affected as well?

In practice, simply using certain keywords can place an account under heightened algorithmic scrutiny. Crypto topics are currently among the most affected by bot spam, prompting the algorithm to react in a broad and precautionary manner.

As a result, even infrequent, factual, and informative posts may end up with minimal reach, regardless of quality or intent. The consequences of this approach are visible across numerous legitimate crypto profiles that operate fully within the platform’s rules.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor)

- This is not an isolated issue but a structural problem in how X handles crypto-related content  
- The algorithm currently penalizes entire topics rather than specific spam behavior  
- Paid verification has failed to resolve the bot problem and has lost its role as a quality filter  
- Legitimate crypto content is increasingly treated the same way as automated spam
