# Google DeepMind unveils AlphaGenome: AI aims to decode the genetic drivers of disease

Google DeepMind has unveiled **AlphaGenome**, a new artificial intelligence model designed to help scientists better understand the genetic mechanisms behind complex diseases. The system can analyze **up to one million DNA base pairs in a single pass**, allowing it to predict how genetic variations affect gene regulation.

AlphaGenome targets one of the most challenging questions in modern medicine: **why genes switch on or off in specific cells — and how subtle genetic changes can trigger serious disease**.

## Why most diseases don’t start in protein-coding genes

Scientists have long known that most inherited diseases — from cardiovascular and autoimmune disorders to mental health conditions and many cancers — **cannot be explained solely by mutations in protein-coding genes**.

The human genome consists of roughly **3 billion base pairs** (A, T, C, and G). Only about **2% of DNA directly codes for proteins**. The remaining **98%** was long considered the *“dark matter of biology”*: it does not produce proteins, but it contains critical instructions that regulate **when, where, and how strongly genes are expressed**.

This regulatory layer is where AlphaGenome focuses its analysis.

## What makes AlphaGenome different

AlphaGenome was trained on large public datasets from human and mouse genetics. It is designed to predict how mutations:

- alter gene activity  
- affect specific cell types  
- disrupt regulatory elements  
- interfere with biological control mechanisms  

Unlike earlier models, AlphaGenome can process **very long stretches of DNA at once**, enabling it to capture interactions that were previously difficult or impossible to detect.

## Implications for medicine and research

According to DeepMind, AlphaGenome could:

- accelerate the identification of disease-causing mutations  
- help uncover new therapeutic targets  
- support the development of personalized medicine  
- significantly speed up basic genetic research  

This is particularly relevant for diseases where a genetic link is known, but **the precise regulatory cause remains unclear**.

## A step beyond AlphaFold

Following the success of **AlphaFold**, which revolutionized protein structure prediction, AlphaGenome represents a shift from *what proteins look like* to *how genes are controlled*.

DeepMind emphasizes that AlphaGenome is **not a clinical diagnostic tool**, but a research system intended to help scientists generate and test hypotheses more efficiently.
