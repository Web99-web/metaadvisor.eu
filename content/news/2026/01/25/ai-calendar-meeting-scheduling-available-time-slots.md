# AI Takes Over Meeting Scheduling: An AI Calendar Finds Available Time Slots for You

Scheduling meetings is one of the most common — and most frustrating — tasks in modern work life. Endless email threads, back-and-forth messages and calendar comparisons consume time that could be spent on actual work. A new startup believes artificial intelligence can take over this process entirely.

## Who is behind the idea?

The company behind the concept is **Blockit**, a startup founded by former Sequoia Capital partner **Kais Khimji**. Together with co-founder **John Hahn**, who previously worked on calendar products such as Google Calendar, Timeful and Clockwise, Khimji is building an AI-powered calendar assistant that negotiates meeting times on its own.

Blockit has also secured backing from Khimji’s former employer. **Sequoia Capital** led the company’s $5 million seed round, signaling strong confidence in the idea.

## How do AI calendars work?

Unlike traditional tools such as Calendly, which rely on users sharing availability links, Blockit’s AI agents handle the entire scheduling process autonomously. The system communicates with other calendars, analyzes available time slots, accounts for priorities and constraints, and proposes suitable meeting times — without any human involvement.

The result is effectively a **social network for time**, where calendars actively negotiate with one another instead of passively displaying availability.

## Why is this possible now?

Previous attempts to automate scheduling struggled with flexibility and context. According to Khimji, recent advances in large language models (LLMs) now allow AI systems to understand nuance, intent and timing well enough to manage complex scheduling conversations reliably.

## What does this mean for the future of work?

If this model proves successful, meeting scheduling could become an **invisible background process**, freeing people to focus on decisions and content rather than coordination. At the same time, it raises important questions about control, privacy and trust: how much authority are users willing to hand over to AI when it comes to managing their time?

Blockit is currently targeting business users and teams, but the concept of AI calendars that independently negotiate meeting times could soon extend to a much broader audience.
