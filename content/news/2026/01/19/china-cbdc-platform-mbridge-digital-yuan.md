# China’s mBridge CBDC Platform and the Next Phase of Global Payments

While public debate often focuses on cryptocurrencies, ETFs, and exchange regulation, a parallel financial infrastructure is quietly taking shape — institutional, coordinated, and largely outside the spotlight. At the center of this shift is **mBridge**, an international platform for cross-border payments based on central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), led by China.

According to available data, mBridge has already processed **more than $55 billion in cross-border transactions**, representing an almost **2,500-fold increase** compared with its pilot phase in 2022. Most notably, **approximately 95% of total transaction volume is denominated in China’s digital yuan (e-CNY)**.

That makes mBridge far more than an experiment — it is an operational system in an early deployment phase.

## What exactly is mBridge?

mBridge is a **multinational CBDC platform** designed to enable direct cross-border payments between central banks and commercial banks, without relying on traditional intermediaries such as SWIFT or correspondent banking networks.

In practical terms, mBridge enables:
- direct settlement between participating countries  
- near-instant international transfers  
- lower costs and reduced settlement delays  
- payment flows outside dollar-dominated infrastructures  

Current participants and test jurisdictions include:
- China (mainland and Hong Kong)  
- Thailand  
- the United Arab Emirates  
- Saudi Arabia  

At the core of the system is the **digital yuan**, which already dominates transaction activity on the platform.

## Why did the BIS withdraw from the project?

The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) — often described as the “central bank of central banks” — participated in the early stages of mBridge. However, **in 2024 the BIS stepped back from the project**.

Officially, the decision was framed around reputational and regulatory concerns. Unofficially, analysts increasingly point to:
- **sanctions-related risks**
- rising geopolitical tensions  
- concerns that mBridge could be perceived as an alternative payments rail outside Western financial oversight  

The BIS withdrawal did not halt mBridge’s development, but it did mark a turning point: the platform shifted from a neutral technical initiative to a **strategically sensitive monetary infrastructure**.

## The digital yuan and a new scale of adoption

Alongside mBridge’s international expansion, China continues to scale domestic use of the digital yuan. According to official figures, the e-CNY has already processed:
- more than **3.4 billion transactions**  
- with a cumulative value of approximately **16.7 trillion yuan** (around $2.4 trillion)  
- representing growth of over **800% year-on-year**  

China’s central bank is also introducing a framework that allows **interest payments on e-CNY wallet balances**, pushing the digital yuan beyond a cash-like instrument and deeper into core banking and monetary policy functions.

## A tool to bypass Western-dominated financial systems?

The key question increasingly raised by policymakers and analysts is straightforward:  
Is mBridge merely a technological upgrade — or the foundation of a **parallel global payments architecture**?

For participating countries, mBridge offers:
- reduced dependence on the US dollar  
- lower exposure to sanctions risk  
- greater monetary autonomy  

For Western institutions, it raises concerns about fragmentation of the global financial system and the long-term erosion of dollar dominance in international settlements.

## What does this mean for the rest of the world?

For now, mBridge remains limited to institutional participants. But financial history suggests that **infrastructure precedes mass adoption**.

If the model proves stable and scalable, mBridge could become the backbone of future regional payment networks — first across Asia and the Middle East, and potentially beyond.
