# The Best Bitcoin Explanation Ever

In the world of crypto, consensus is rare. Yet if there is one explanation of Bitcoin that has been quoted, cited, and referenced for years across regulatory hearings, academic circles, and serious journalism, it is this one — the moment Bitcoin was explained calmly, precisely, and in plain human language.

That explanation was given by **Peter Van Valkenburgh**, Director of Research at **Coin Center**, during an official hearing before the **United States Congress**, in response to a fundamental question lawmakers were struggling to answer:

What exactly is Bitcoin — and how can it be regulated?

Not because the answer was viral.  
Not because it promised profits.  
But because it clearly separated what Bitcoin **is** from what many people wrongly assume it to be.

## The sentence that changed the narrative

At the center of that hearing was a single sentence that dismantled years of confusion:

> **“Bitcoin is not a company. — Not a product. — Not a network with a CEO. — Bitcoin is a protocol, just like the internet.”**

In one line, it explained why Bitcoin does not behave like the systems regulators are used to dealing with. There is no board, no headquarters, and no individual who can change the rules or shut it down.

There are only open rules — and people who freely choose to follow them.

## Why the word “protocol” matters

The word *protocol* is not a technical footnote. It is the key to understanding Bitcoin.

A protocol is a shared set of rules that allows systems to communicate or exchange value without centralized control. The internet itself runs on protocols such as TCP/IP. No one owns the internet, yet everyone uses it.

Bitcoin applies the same logic — but to money.

This is why Bitcoin cannot be regulated like a company or banned like a product. Not because it is above the law, but because **it is not an organization**. Laws can apply to exchanges, custodians, platforms, and intermediaries — not to the protocol itself.

## Why Bitcoin is so often misunderstood

Much of the fear and resistance surrounding Bitcoin comes from trying to force a new technology into old mental models. When something has no CEO, no headquarters, and no central authority, traditional systems struggle to understand where control begins and ends.

This leads to the wrong questions:  
Who is behind Bitcoin?  
Who is responsible?  
Who controls it?

The correct question is far simpler: **How does the protocol work — and who chooses to use it?**

## Neutrality as Bitcoin’s core feature

Perhaps the most important part of this explanation is philosophical rather than technical.

Bitcoin was not designed to comply with the existing system. It was designed to be **neutral toward the system**.

Neutrality means the same rules apply to everyone. No privileged users. No hidden shortcuts. No special access for the powerful. This neutrality is precisely what makes Bitcoin resilient — and deeply challenging to existing financial and political structures.

## Why this explanation matters more than ever

As Bitcoin is increasingly discussed in the context of institutional adoption, sovereign reserves, and monetary policy, this explanation has become more relevant than ever. Not to promote Bitcoin — but to **understand it correctly**.

Without this framing, debates around regulation, control, or prohibition continue to miss the point.

## Conclusion

The best Bitcoin explanation is not the one that promises fast returns or technical fascination. It is the one that clearly states what Bitcoin is — and just as importantly, what it is not.

Bitcoin is not a company.  
Not a product.  
Not a network with a CEO.  

Bitcoin is a protocol.

Just like the internet.
