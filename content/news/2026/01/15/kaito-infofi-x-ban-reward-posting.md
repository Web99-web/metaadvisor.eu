# Kaito, InfoFi, and X’s Abrupt Cutoff

The **InfoFi (Information Finance)** sector has suffered a major setback after **X** decided to **ban applications that reward users for posting or engagement**. One of the most affected projects is **Kaito (KAITO)**, whose business model relied heavily on integration with X’s platform.

X justified the move by stating that reward-for-posting models incentivize spam, bots, and mass AI-generated content, ultimately degrading the quality of discourse on the platform.

## What Is InfoFi — and Why It Became a Problem

InfoFi aims to **tokenize attention and information**, rewarding users with tokens for:
- creating posts  
- engaging with content  
- amplifying narratives and trends  

In practice, this often led to **quantity over quality**: automated AI posts, farming rewards, and coordinated campaigns with little real informational value.

X ultimately decided that such behavior was incompatible with the platform’s long-term health.

## How the Ban Impacted Kaito

After X:
- revoked API access for InfoFi apps  
- prohibited rewards tied to posting and engagement  
- disabled tracking and scoring mechanisms  

the **KAITO token fell roughly 15–20% within hours**. For many investors, the episode exposed how deeply Kaito depended on a single platform.

Kaito’s team has since announced:
- the shutdown of certain reward-based features  
- the removal of public leaderboards and incentives  
- a strategic shift toward B2B products and analytics tools outside of X  

## A Warning for Crypto and AI Projects

X’s decision sends a clear signal to the broader market:
- social platforms do not want to become token farms  
- business models built on third-party APIs carry structural risk  
- InfoFi without its own infrastructure remains fragile  

For investors, this is a stark reminder that **a token alone does not equal a sustainable business model**.

## Our Take (MetaAdvisor)

- X has drawn a firm line between engagement and manipulation  
- The InfoFi concept is innovative but currently immature  
- Overreliance on a single platform proved costly for Kaito  
- The project must now demonstrate value beyond X  
- This move could accelerate the decline of classic “post-to-earn” models
