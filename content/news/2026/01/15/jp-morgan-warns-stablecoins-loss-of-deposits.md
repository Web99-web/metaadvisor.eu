# JP Morgan raises alarm over stablecoins

JP Morgan’s chief financial officer has warned that paying yields on stablecoins effectively creates a “parallel banking system” operating outside the safeguards that apply to traditional banks. According to the bank, such models replicate deposit-like products without being subject to requirements such as deposit insurance, capital buffers or strict liquidity rules.

The warning comes as U.S. lawmakers debate new market-structure rules for crypto assets, with stablecoins emerging as one of the most contentious issues.

## Stablecoins as a threat to bank deposits

From the perspective of large banks, yield-bearing stablecoins represent direct competition to traditional deposits. If users can hold digital dollars outside the banking system while earning returns, banks risk losing a core source of low-cost funding that underpins much of their business model.

As a result, calls for tighter regulation are often framed as concerns over financial stability and consumer protection. Critics argue, however, that these concerns also reflect fears of losing control over capital flows and customer balances.

## Regulation, choice and asymmetric risk

Crypto investors already face significantly higher risks than traditional bank customers. When crypto platforms fail, claims are typically settled in U.S. dollars rather than in the original crypto assets held. This effectively strips investors of any future upside, even when they knowingly accepted the risks associated with crypto markets.

In an open financial system, the choice should remain with individuals: whether to keep funds in the regulated banking sector or to take on the risks of a newer, more volatile crypto ecosystem.
