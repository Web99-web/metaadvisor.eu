# Michael O’Leary, Ryanair CEO, calls Musk an “idiot” as airline launches ticket promo

The public clash between Elon Musk and Ryanair CEO Michael O’Leary escalated into a scenario rarely seen in the business world. What began as a technical disagreement over in-flight satellite internet ended with a direct insult — and an **official airline ticket promotion** built around it.

## From Starlink debate to personal insult

The dispute started when O’Leary dismissed the idea of installing **Starlink** satellite internet across Ryanair’s fleet. He argued that additional antennas would increase aerodynamic drag, pushing fuel costs up by an estimated **$200–250 million per year**.

Musk, whose companies include SpaceX and Starlink, publicly pushed back, calling that assessment **misinformed**. Shortly after, O’Leary openly referred to Musk as an **“idiot”**, taking the exchange beyond a conventional business disagreement.

## Ryanair turns the feud into a ticket sale

Rather than backing away, Ryanair leaned into the controversy. On its official X account, the airline launched a campaign titled **“Ryanair’s Big ‘Idiot’ Seat Sale”**, accompanied by the message: *“Don’t thank us, thank that big ‘IDIOT’ @elonmusk.”*

Discounted fares were offered as part of the promotion, transforming a personal insult into a **commercial marketing strategy** and a highly shareable moment online.

## Musk’s one-word response

Musk responded to the campaign with a single word: **“Terrible.”**  
The brief reply only amplified attention around the promotion, driving further engagement and visibility for Ryanair’s campaign.

## When marketing overtakes the original issue

The technical debate over Starlink’s feasibility quickly faded into the background. Instead, the spotlight shifted to provocation, irony, and how quickly public disputes can be converted into measurable reach and ticket sales.

Ryanair has long been known for its confrontational and irreverent marketing style. This episode illustrates how deliberately the airline uses controversy as part of its brand communication strategy.
