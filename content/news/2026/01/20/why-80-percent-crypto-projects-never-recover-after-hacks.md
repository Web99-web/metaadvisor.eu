# Why around 80% of crypto projects never fully recover after a hack

In the crypto industry, hacks are often framed as technical incidents — problems that can be fixed with patches, audits and compensation funds. However, long-term data and expert assessments tell a very different story. According to **Mitchell Amador, CEO of Web3 security platform Immunefi**, roughly **80% of crypto projects that experience a major hack never fully recover**.

Recovery, in this context, does not mean that a project continues to exist on paper. It means regaining trust, liquidity, user activity and market relevance comparable to pre-incident levels. This is where most projects fail.

## The technical fix is only the beginning

Immediately after an attack, teams focus on stopping the exploit, freezing compromised smart contracts and identifying the attack vector. If handled quickly, further losses can sometimes be limited.

Yet even when the technical response is successful, the deeper crisis begins afterward. Projects often enter a prolonged period of damage control that can last months or even years.

## Trust loss is the real damage

The most severe and lasting impact of a hack is rarely financial alone — it is reputational. Users who lose funds or confidence rarely return, regardless of subsequent audits, security upgrades or marketing efforts.

Common consequences include:
- large-scale liquidity withdrawals  
- sharp declines in platform usage  
- loss of strategic partners and investors  
- heightened regulatory and media scrutiny  

Even projects that continue operating remain permanently marked by the incident.

## Operational and organizational breakdown

Major security breaches often expose structural weaknesses within teams. Projects without clear crisis protocols face chaotic communication, internal disputes and sustained community pressure.

Roadmaps are delayed, development slows and resources are redirected toward reputation management instead of innovation. At this stage, projects are no longer building value — they are merely trying to survive.

## Why most projects never regain their former strength

Even after stabilization, the long-term effects persist. Reduced liquidity, diminished trust and ongoing security doubts prevent projects from reclaiming their previous growth trajectory.

This is why hackers rarely destroy projects outright. **In most cases, the long-term consequences of an attack permanently weaken them**. Projects may continue to exist, but their real influence within the crypto ecosystem is irreversibly diminished.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor)

- The 80% non-recovery figure highlights that security is existential, not optional  
- Trust is the core currency of the crypto industry  
- Technical fixes without reputational recovery are insufficient  
- Many projects survive hacks only nominally, not economically
