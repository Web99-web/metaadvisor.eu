# US courts halt the halt on offshore wind

US federal courts delivered a significant legal setback to the Trump administration this week by allowing **construction on several major offshore wind farms** along the East Coast to resume. The projects had been paused in late 2025 after the Department of the Interior ordered a stop over alleged national security concerns.

Judges ruled that the government failed to provide sufficiently clear and compelling evidence that the wind farms posed an unavoidable threat to radar or defense systems.

## Which projects are moving forward

Court orders allow construction to continue on several large-scale projects:

- **Revolution Wind** off Rhode Island  
- **Empire Wind** near the coast of New York  
- **Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind** in Virginia  

Together, these projects form a key part of long-term plans to strengthen the US power grid and reduce reliance on fossil fuels, particularly in densely populated coastal regions.

## Security concerns versus technical reality

The Trump administration argued that rotating turbine blades could interfere with military and civilian radar systems. While such concerns exist in theory, courts noted that:

- similar offshore wind projects already operate alongside radar infrastructure  
- modern radar systems can be upgraded to filter turbine-related interference  
- the government did not demonstrate why technical mitigation would be insufficient  

Notably, even judges appointed during Trump’s presidency expressed **skepticism toward the administration’s arguments**, highlighting gaps in the legal reasoning.

## Why this matters for the energy transition

Offshore wind is widely viewed as a critical pillar of a **resilient and future-proof electricity system**, especially amid:

- rising electricity demand  
- accelerating electrification of transport and industry  
- increasing climate-related risks and extreme weather events  

Halting projects at advanced stages of development could have driven up energy costs and slowed the broader transition toward cleaner power sources.
