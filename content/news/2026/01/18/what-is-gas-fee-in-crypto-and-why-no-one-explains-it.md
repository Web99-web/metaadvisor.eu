# You’ve heard of gas fees — but no one really explains them

One of the biggest sources of confusion in crypto is the **gas fee**. The term is used constantly, yet rarely explained in plain language — clearly, upfront, and in one place. The result is that users hesitate to click “Confirm,” don’t understand why a transaction fails, or why they’re “missing something” even though they have funds.

This isn’t because users are uninformed.  
It’s because the system **fails to explain its own rules**.

## What is a gas fee (as simply as possible)

A **gas fee** is the **cost of executing a transaction on a blockchain**.

In simple terms:
- blockchains don’t run for free  
- transactions must be processed  
- someone does the work  
- gas is the price of that work  

👉 Gas is **not a tax**, **not an exchange fee**, and **not a penalty**.  
👉 It is the **fuel of the network**.

No fuel → no transaction.

## When gas fees are required

Gas fees apply **every time you do something directly on a blockchain (on-chain)**.

### Examples when gas **is required**:
- sending crypto from one wallet to another  
- swapping tokens on a DEX  
- bridging assets between blockchains  
- minting NFTs  
- staking or unstaking  
- approving a smart contract (“Approve”)  
- withdrawing funds from DeFi protocols  

### Examples when gas **is NOT required**:
- trading inside centralized exchanges  
- buying or selling without withdrawing to a wallet  
- internal transfers within an exchange  

📌 The moment you leave an exchange and go on-chain → **gas is mandatory**.

## What gas is paid in (the most common trap)

Gas is **always paid in the native coin of the blockchain**, not in the token you’re sending.

Examples:
- Ethereum → gas is paid in **ETH**  
- Solana → gas is paid in **SOL**  
- BNB Chain → gas is paid in **BNB**  
- Polygon → gas is paid in **MATIC**  
- Avalanche → gas is paid in **AVAX**  
- Arbitrum → gas is paid in **ETH**  
- Optimism → gas is paid in **ETH**  
- Base → gas is paid in **ETH**  

👉 If you have tokens but **no native coin**, the transaction will fail.

That’s why users often see:
> “Insufficient funds for gas”

## Where you must have gas — wallet by wallet

This is the part almost no one explains clearly.  
**Wallets don’t pay gas — blockchains do.** Wallets are just interfaces.

### 🦊 MetaMask — the most common pain point

MetaMask is used for:
- Ethereum  
- Arbitrum  
- Optimism  
- Base  
- BNB Chain  
- Polygon  
- Avalanche  

You must hold the **native coin of each chain**:
- **ETH** → Ethereum, Arbitrum, Optimism, Base  
- **BNB** → BNB Chain  
- **MATIC** → Polygon  
- **AVAX** → Avalanche  

📌 Having USDT or USDC is not enough.  
📌 MetaMask does **not lend gas** and **does not clearly warn you upfront**.

### 🟣 Phantom — simpler, same rules

Phantom is primarily used for **Solana**.

- gas is paid in **SOL**  
- fees are cheap, but **SOL is required**

Only holding SPL tokens without SOL → nothing works.

### 🔵 Trust Wallet — flexible but risky for beginners

Trust Wallet supports many chains:
- Ethereum  
- BNB Chain  
- Polygon  
- Avalanche  
- others  

You must:
- know exactly **which chain you’re on**
- hold the **correct native coin**

The app often doesn’t explain this clearly.

### 🔐 Ledger — a common misunderstanding

Ledger **does not pay gas for you**.  
It only **signs transactions**.

If Ledger is connected to MetaMask or Trust Wallet, **all the same gas rules apply**.

## The most common mistakes (and why they happen)

❌ USDT available, but no ETH for gas  
❌ ETH available, but on the wrong chain  
❌ Token on L2, but no ETH for L2 gas  
❌ Not knowing that “Approve” also costs gas  
❌ Not knowing gas prices change with network congestion  

These are not beginner mistakes.  
They are **design failures without explanation**.

## Why this is a massive UX problem

In traditional finance:
- you don’t need to understand infrastructure  
- you don’t care how banks process transactions  
- fees are shown clearly in advance  

In crypto:
- users are expected to understand infrastructure  
- distinguish blockchains  
- know which coin pays gas  

And this is treated as normal.

## Conclusion

Gas fees themselves are not the problem.  
The problem is expecting users to understand a system **that refuses to explain itself**.

If crypto wants mass adoption:
- gas must become invisible  
- or at least clearly explained upfront  

Until then, fear of MetaMask and the wrong click will remain the norm — not the exception.
