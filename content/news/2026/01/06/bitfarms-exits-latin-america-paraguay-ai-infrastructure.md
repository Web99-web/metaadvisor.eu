# Bitfarms closes its Latin American mining chapter

Bitfarms, a publicly listed Bitcoin mining company, has decided to fully withdraw from Latin America. The company sold its **70-megawatt mining facility in Paraguay** for **$30 million**, effectively ending its regional operations.

Following the transaction, Bitfarms’ energy and data center activities are now **entirely focused on North America**, reflecting a broader strategic repositioning.

*Note: The image is symbolic.*

## From Bitcoin mining to AI and HPC infrastructure

Proceeds from the sale will be reinvested into **artificial intelligence (AI)** and **high-performance computing (HPC)** infrastructure. Demand for these segments is accelerating, driven by large language models, data-intensive workloads, and enterprise compute needs.

Bitfarms is currently developing around **430 MW** of capacity in the United States, with multi-year plans that envision up to **2.1 gigawatts** of infrastructure across North America.

## Why Latin America is no longer a priority

For years, Latin America — including Paraguay — attracted miners with relatively low electricity costs. However, the rapid expansion of AI workloads and shifting market dynamics are redirecting capital toward data centers that offer higher and more stable returns.

Bitfarms had previously announced plans to gradually convert parts of its mining fleet toward AI-oriented uses; the Paraguay exit represents a decisive continuation of that strategy.

## Miners evolve into infrastructure providers

Bitfarms’ move reflects a broader industry trend in which crypto mining companies are transforming into **energy and compute infrastructure providers**. Rather than relying solely on volatile mining revenues, firms are seeking more predictable, long-term business models.

As a result, the boundaries between crypto, cloud computing, and AI continue to blur — with infrastructure emerging as the common denominator.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor)

- The Paraguay exit highlights a structural shift across the mining industry.  
- AI and HPC offer more stable revenue profiles than traditional mining.  
- Low-cost energy alone is no longer a sufficient competitive advantage.  
- North America is consolidating its role as a hub for digital infrastructure.  
- Crypto miners are repositioning as technology infrastructure operators.
