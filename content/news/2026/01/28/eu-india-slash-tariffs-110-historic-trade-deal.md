# EU and India Slash Tariffs of Up to 110%: Historic Trade Deal After 20 Years

At a time when tariffs are once again being used as political weapons, **the European Union and India have chosen a radically different path**. After nearly **20 years of stalled and difficult negotiations**, both sides reached an agreement to **eliminate or drastically reduce Indian import tariffs that had reached as high as 110%** in some sectors.

This is one of the most significant trade agreements of recent years, with the potential to reshape global trade flows between two major economic blocs.

## What actually changes

For decades, India was known for some of the world’s highest protective tariffs, particularly affecting European exporters. The most impacted sectors included:

- automobiles and auto components  
- industrial machinery and equipment  
- chemicals and pharmaceutical products  
- alcohol, luxury and consumer goods  
- selected food products  

Under the new agreement, these tariffs will be **phased out or sharply reduced**, opening access for European companies to a market of roughly **1.4 billion consumers**, while India gains improved access to EU markets.

## Why this matters for Germany and France

For **Germany**, especially industrial hubs such as **Stuttgart, Wolfsburg, Munich and Berlin**, the agreement is strategically important. German automakers and suppliers were among the most affected by India’s previous tariff regime.

**French companies** are also set to benefit, particularly in the automotive, chemicals, luxury goods and food sectors. In **Paris**, the deal is viewed as a key step toward strengthening Europe’s global competitiveness.

European Commission President **Ursula von der Leyen** has repeatedly emphasized the importance of deepening economic ties with India, making this agreement both an economic and political milestone for the EU.

## More than just cars

Despite the public focus on automobiles, the agreement **extends far beyond the auto sector**. It includes:

- regulatory simplification  
- improved investment conditions  
- greater legal certainty for exporters and investors  
- long-term cooperation mechanisms  

As such, the deal represents a **structural shift** in EU–India economic relations rather than a symbolic gesture.

## A counterweight to protectionism

While tariff threats dominate political rhetoric elsewhere, this agreement demonstrates that **trade liberalization remains possible**. The EU and India are positioning themselves as advocates of openness and cooperation in a period of increasing global fragmentation — a signal with clear geopolitical weight.
