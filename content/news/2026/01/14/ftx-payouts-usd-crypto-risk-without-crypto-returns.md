# FTX Payouts in USD: Why Crypto Risk Is Accepted, but Crypto Returns Are Erased

The bankruptcy proceedings of the former crypto exchange FTX have entered a new phase with the announcement of another creditor payout date. Although the collapse of the exchange happened more than three years ago, the legal and financial consequences continue to ripple through the crypto industry and raise questions that go far beyond FTX itself.

As part of a multi-stage bankruptcy process, the FTX estate is continuing to liquidate assets and distribute proceeds to customers and other creditors. The process is long and highly complex, involving multiple court disputes, clawback claims, and asset valuations that have shifted significantly alongside the broader crypto market.

## USD accounting as the rule, not the exception

The most painful reality for many creditors is that all claims are **calculated in U.S. dollars based on the value of assets on the bankruptcy filing date**. Whether users held Bitcoin, Ether, or other tokens makes no legal difference — at the moment of collapse, crypto is effectively frozen into a fixed USD amount.

As a result, any subsequent rally in the crypto market does not benefit creditors. The volatility risk was willingly accepted, but the potential upside was legally erased at the moment of bankruptcy.

## Legal battles add further complexity

Alongside the payout process, Genesis Digital Assets is attempting to dismiss a lawsuit worth roughly $1 billion related to so-called *clawback* claims — efforts to recover funds transferred shortly before FTX collapsed. While such claims are common in bankruptcy cases, they highlight just how deeply intertwined exchanges, mining firms, and institutional counterparties had become.

The outcome of these disputes could set important precedents for how future crypto bankruptcies are handled, particularly when it comes to accountability and the treatment of user funds.
