# Why AVAX One Stock Fell Over 30% — and Why This Has Nothing to Do with the AVAX Token

At first glance, the headline sounds dramatic: **“AVAX drops more than 30%.”**  
Many readers automatically assumed this referred to the AVAX crypto token.

But that is **not correct.**

What actually dropped was the **stock of the company AVAX One**, not the AVAX cryptocurrency. Even experienced market participants (including us) can initially mix up these two concepts — which is why it’s important to clarify the difference before drawing the wrong conclusions.

---

## AVAX One Stock ≠ AVAX Token

This distinction is critical:

### AVAX One (Stock)
- a publicly listed company  
- traded on traditional stock markets  
- the share price depends heavily on investor confidence and market sentiment  
- the company holds crypto assets on its balance sheet (including AVAX tokens)

### AVAX (Token)
- a cryptocurrency running on the Avalanche blockchain  
- traded on crypto exchanges  
- the price depends on supply, demand and network activity  

In other words:  
**A decline in AVAX One stock does not mean the AVAX token has fallen.**

It’s similar to an oil company’s stock falling while oil prices remain unchanged.

---

## What actually happened at AVAX One

AVAX One registered approximately **74 million insider shares** with the U.S. regulator **SEC (U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission – the American securities regulator)**. This registration legally allows those shares to be **eligible for potential resale on the public market**.

Important:
- Registration **is not a sale**  
- No timing for a sale has been announced  
- There is no confirmation that any shares will actually be sold  

Despite this, the market reacted sharply.

Why?  
Because the possibility that a large number of new shares could enter the market creates fear of **dilution** — more shares with the same underlying company value usually means a lower price per share.

---

## Why people often confuse “insider trading” with “insider shares registered”

This is where many misunderstandings arise.

**Insider trading** means:
- insiders actually buy or sell shares  
- they use non-public, privileged information  
- it is highly regulated and often illegal  

In this case, **none of that happened.**

What did happen is this:

> **Insider shares were registered for potential resale, but there was no insider trading and no actual selling. The price drop reflects market fear, not confirmed insider activity.**

The confusion comes from a mental shortcut many investors make:

**registered → can sell → will sell → selling now**

This chain of assumptions — rather than real transactions — often triggers panic selling.

---

## Why the market reaction was so strong

AVAX One belongs to a group of so-called **digital asset treasury companies** — firms whose balance sheets are heavily exposed to crypto assets. These stocks tend to be:

- more volatile than traditional companies  
- highly sensitive to investor sentiment  
- prone to exaggerated reactions to news  

Even though the company had previously announced a **share buyback program of up to $40 million**, the combination of mixed signals created uncertainty among investors.

In such environments, markets tend to react emotionally rather than analytically.

---

## The broader lesson for investors

This case clearly shows:

- why it’s essential to distinguish between **stocks and tokens**  
- how markets often react to possibilities rather than facts  
- how strongly psychology influences price movements  

Especially in crypto-adjacent markets, reading headlines carefully and understanding terminology can prevent costly misunderstandings.

---

## Our take (MetaAdvisor):

- The drop affected AVAX One stock, not the AVAX token  
- The market reacted to fear, not to actual selling  
- Share registration is not the same as insider trading  
- Situations like this can create opportunities — but with high risk  
- Investor education remains critical in hybrid crypto markets
