# US crypto bill fails — and DeFi says that’s good news

While parts of the crypto industry reacted with disappointment to the failure of a new US crypto regulation bill, the response from the DeFi community has been notably different. For many in decentralized finance, the collapse of this proposal is not a setback, but a relief — and a warning narrowly avoided.

The bill was intended to establish a new regulatory framework for the US crypto market and was widely described as a **crypto market structure bill**, often linked conceptually to the **FIT21 proposal (Financial Innovation and Technology for the 21st Century Act)**. However, the version that reached Congress in January 2026 went significantly further than earlier drafts, triggering strong opposition from DeFi advocates.

## How the bill failed and why it mattered

The proposal failed to secure sufficient support in the US Congress, reflecting a deep divide between lawmakers pushing for tighter controls on crypto and those concerned about the impact on innovation and technological competitiveness.

Although vote counts varied across amendments and procedural stages, the outcome was clear: the bill **did not achieve a stable majority** and was effectively shelved. For the DeFi sector, this moment marked a crucial turning point.

## What the bill aimed to regulate

The proposed legislation sought to:
- clarify regulatory authority between the **SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)** and the **CFTC (Commodity Futures Trading Commission)**,
- impose stricter rules on crypto platforms and service providers,
- define the legal status of different types of crypto assets.

The most controversial elements, however, focused on **decentralized protocols**.

## Why DeFi considered the bill dangerous

From the DeFi community’s perspective, the proposal suffered from several fundamental flaws:

### 1️⃣ Treating DeFi like banks  
The bill attempted to apply traditional financial regulations to systems that have **no central operator, no management team and no controlling legal entity**. DeFi protocols operate through smart contracts, making bank-style compliance structurally impossible.

### 2️⃣ KYC and AML requirements that cannot be enforced  
Provisions implying mandatory **Know Your Customer (KYC)** and **Anti-Money Laundering (AML)** controls would have required autonomous protocols to identify users — something technically infeasible without dismantling DeFi itself.

### 3️⃣ Shifting legal liability to developers and DAOs  
One of the most alarming aspects was the potential transfer of legal responsibility to **developers**, open-source contributors or **DAOs (Decentralized Autonomous Organizations)**. DeFi critics warned this would criminalize writing code and chill innovation across the ecosystem.

### 4️⃣ Risk of pushing innovation out of the US  
Rather than increasing safety, the bill would likely have driven DeFi projects offshore, weakening the US position in blockchain and financial innovation.

## Why DeFi sees this as a win

DeFi leaders are not arguing against regulation as such. Their message is more nuanced:  
**bad regulation is worse than no regulation.**

The bill’s failure provides:
- time for a **more informed and technical policy debate**,  
- an opportunity to clearly distinguish **centralized platforms from decentralized protocols**,  
- space to design rules that reflect how blockchain systems actually work.

## What comes next?

Following the collapse of the bill, observers expect:
- a **revised legislative proposal**,  
- greater involvement of technical experts and industry participants,  
- clearer separation between **TradFi** and **DeFi** regulatory approaches.

For DeFi, the outcome sends a clear signal: regulation is coming — but there is still a chance it can be **measured, workable and innovation-friendly**.
