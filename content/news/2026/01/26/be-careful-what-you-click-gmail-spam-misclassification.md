# Be careful what you click in Gmail

If your Gmail inbox has felt “off” in recent days, you’re not imagining it. Google has confirmed technical issues that caused emails to be misclassified, allowing suspicious messages into inboxes while legitimate emails were mistakenly flagged as spam.

For users who rely on Gmail’s automated filters every day, this serves as an uncomfortable but important reminder: **even advanced email security systems can fail**.

## What exactly happened with Gmail?

According to Google’s official Workspace status dashboard, the issues began early Saturday morning around 5 a.m. Pacific Time. Users started reporting unusual inbox behavior, including:

- spam emails appearing in the Primary inbox  
- warnings shown on emails from trusted senders  
- Promotions and Updates messages showing up as personal emails  

Social media quickly filled with complaints that Gmail’s filters were “suddenly broken.”

## Why this matters more than it seems

Gmail heavily depends on **automation and artificial intelligence** to protect users from phishing, scams, and malicious content. Over time, many people have stopped carefully checking senders, trusting that Gmail has already done the filtering for them.

When that trust breaks down, the risks rise sharply:
- users may click malicious links  
- phishing emails can appear legitimate  
- fake messages may impersonate real invoices, alerts, or security notices  

This is why even short disruptions in filtering accuracy can have serious consequences.

## Google’s response and warning to users

Google acknowledged the issue and said it is actively working to resolve it. In the meantime, users were advised to be extra cautious when interacting with emails from unknown or unexpected senders.

As is often the case with complex, AI-driven systems, Google has not shared detailed technical explanations for the incident.

## What should users do right now?

Until Gmail’s systems are fully stable, basic security habits matter more than ever:
- avoid clicking links in suspicious emails  
- double-check sender addresses  
- be cautious with unexpected attachments  
- never enter passwords or personal data via email links  

Automation is helpful — **but it does not replace human judgment**.

## The bigger question: are we relying too much on AI?

This incident raises a broader issue: how much trust should we place in fully automated security systems? As algorithms grow more complex, they also become less transparent, making it harder for users to understand when — and why — something goes wrong.

In a world where email remains a critical channel for personal, financial, and business communication, even temporary disruptions can expose millions of users to risk.
