# Wall Street moves onchain

For years, major Wall Street banks viewed cryptocurrencies with skepticism, often describing them as risky, unregulated or incompatible with traditional finance. As 2026 begins, that stance has clearly changed.

Today, the world’s largest financial institutions are no longer debating **whether** to engage with crypto, but **how** to integrate blockchain technology in a controlled and regulatory-compliant way.

## JPMorgan and the tokenized dollar

The most advanced move comes from **JPMorgan**, which is actively developing its **tokenized dollar — JPM Coin**. This digital form of bank deposits is used to settle institutional transactions and is connected to modern blockchain infrastructure such as the **Canton Network**.  
We covered this strategy in detail in our article  
<a href="https://metaadvisor.eu/hr/news/jp-morgan-jpm-coin-multichain-likvidnost/" style="color:#1e40af; text-decoration:none; font-weight:700;">
JP Morgan expands JPM Coin across multiple blockchain networks to increase institutional liquidity
</a>, which shows how the bank is using blockchain technology to enable faster settlement and greater flexibility within the institutional financial system.

Unlike public blockchains, these are closed, regulatory-compliant networks designed for professional market participants, with a strong focus on security, interoperability and risk control.

## Morgan Stanley opens the door to crypto ETFs

**Morgan Stanley** is positioning itself as a key gateway for wealthy private clients seeking regulated exposure to crypto assets. The bank plans to offer access to **Bitcoin (BTC)** and **Solana (SOL)** through **exchange-traded funds (ETFs)**.

This approach allows clients to gain crypto exposure without dealing with wallets, private keys or the technical risks associated with direct ownership.

## Barclays invests in stablecoin infrastructure

UK-based **Barclays** has taken its first concrete step into digital assets by backing **stablecoin infrastructure**. Rather than focusing on speculation, the bank is targeting settlement rails and payment systems designed for regulated financial institutions.

This move highlights a broader trend: crypto is increasingly viewed as financial infrastructure, not just an investment asset.

## Bank of America normalizes Bitcoin ETFs

**Bank of America** has made another symbolic shift by allowing its financial advisers to actively recommend **spot Bitcoin ETFs** to clients.

This decision further embeds Bitcoin into traditional investment portfolios and signals growing acceptance within the financial establishment.

## What this means for the crypto market

The entry of major banks brings additional legitimacy to crypto, but also introduces tighter regulation and institutional oversight. While some see this as a departure from crypto’s original decentralization ethos, others view it as a necessary step toward global adoption.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor)

- **JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Barclays and Bank of America** are already building, not experimenting.
- The focus is on regulated products and infrastructure, not open DeFi.
- ETFs, tokenized money and stablecoins act as bridges between TradFi and blockchain.
- Institutional involvement increases legitimacy but also control.
- The line between traditional finance and crypto continues to blur.
