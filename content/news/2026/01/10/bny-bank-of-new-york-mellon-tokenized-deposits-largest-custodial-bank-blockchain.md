# BNY Mellon Moves Toward Tokenized Bank Deposits

**Bank of New York Mellon (BNY Mellon)**, the world’s largest custodial bank, has announced plans to offer **tokenized bank deposits** to institutional clients. This move places one of the most influential pillars of global finance firmly on the path toward blockchain-based infrastructure.

BNY Mellon currently safeguards more than **$40 trillion in assets**, making it a critical backbone of global capital markets, settlement systems and institutional custody.

## What Are Tokenized Deposits?

Tokenized deposits are fundamentally different from stablecoins. They represent **traditional bank deposits**, issued in tokenized form on a blockchain.

These deposits:
- correspond to real money held on bank balance sheets,
- remain fully within the regulated banking system,
- comply with existing financial and legal frameworks.

The primary objective is to **speed up settlement**, improve liquidity management and increase transparency between institutional counterparties.

## Why This Matters

When a bank like BNY Mellon adopts blockchain technology, it is not an experiment — it is an infrastructure decision. Tokenized deposits allow near-instant settlement, reduced operational friction and greater efficiency without sacrificing regulatory oversight.

For institutional investors, this creates a bridge between established financial systems and emerging digital rails.

## The Mellon Legacy

The “Mellon” name traces back to the influential **Mellon family**, one of the most powerful financial dynasties in U.S. history. The modern BNY Mellon was formed in 2007 through the merger of the **Bank of New York**, America’s oldest bank, and **Mellon Financial Corporation**.

This heritage combines centuries of banking tradition with long-standing institutional power.

## Tokenization and the Future of Banking

BNY Mellon is part of a broader shift among global banks toward **tokenization of cash and real-world assets (RWA)**. Unlike public crypto markets, these initiatives typically rely on **permissioned, compliance-focused blockchain networks** designed specifically for institutional use.

The message is clear: blockchain is increasingly viewed as foundational financial infrastructure, not a parallel or oppositional system.
