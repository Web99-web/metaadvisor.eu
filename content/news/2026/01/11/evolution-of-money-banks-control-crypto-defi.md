# Evolution of Money or Defense of Interests?

At the beginning of 2026, the debate around cryptocurrencies and decentralized finance (DeFi) has entered a new phase. It is no longer just about technology, volatility, or consumer protection, but about a **fundamental question: who will control the future monetary system?**

As the U.S. Senate moves toward a vote on legislation designed to define the structure of the crypto market, pressure from traditional financial institutions is rising in parallel to limit, dilute, or exclude DeFi from that framework.

## Banks and the fear of losing control

For decades, traditional banks have been the central custodians of money, deposits, and credit. DeFi, by contrast, offers an alternative model in which users can:
- lend and borrow without intermediaries  
- earn yield without a bank account  
- access financial markets globally, without permission  

This is the core of the conflict. DeFi does not threaten banks because it is technically perfect, but because it **shifts power dynamics**. When financial services function without centralized institutions, banks lose their privileged role as gatekeepers.

## Regulation as a tool — not a neutral framework

In public discourse, stricter regulation is often presented as necessary to protect consumers and ensure stability. At the same time, a key question is increasingly being asked: **are these rules written for everyone, or primarily for incumbent players?**

Lobbying against DeFi, media campaigns, and political calls to exclude decentralized finance from regulatory frameworks suggest an attempt to **slow down or redirect the evolution of money** in a way that preserves banks’ central role.

## DeFi: a threat or a necessary step forward?

Estimates that widespread adoption of stablecoins and **DeFi solutions could pull trillions of dollars out of the traditional banking system** have further intensified resistance from the financial sector. This “capital outflow” is not an abstract concept, but the result of **concrete changes in how people save, borrow, and manage capital**.

In DeFi systems, users no longer need to keep funds in bank accounts to earn interest or access credit. Capital instead flows into **stablecoins, decentralized lending protocols, and on-chain financial applications**, where users participate directly in markets without intermediaries. While banks often offer only **0–2% annual interest on savings**, DeFi protocols can, under certain conditions, provide **significantly higher yields**, without long lockups or bank approval.

When someone lends crypto to other users via a smart contract, no bank is required — no deposit, no credit committee, no margin between savings and loan rates.

Rather than adapting to this new model, parts of the financial sector have chosen a strategy of control: if DeFi cannot be stopped, it must be **forced into an existing system that neutralizes its disruptive potential** — through regulation, licensing, and compliance requirements that decentralized protocols are structurally ill-equipped to meet.

<p style="font-size:0.85em; color:#666; margin-top:14px;">
<strong>Why does this matter to ordinary people?</strong><br>
Because DeFi changes everyday financial realities. A freelancer working with international clients can receive payments in stablecoins and earn yield without bank fees, delays, or costly currency conversions. A small business can use crypto assets as collateral for short-term liquidity without loan applications, paperwork, or bank rejection. Even an ordinary worker, earning minimal interest on a savings account, gains an alternative way to preserve value without a bank deciding on their behalf.
</p>

In the process, one of crypto’s core ideas is at risk: **financial accessibility and equality**, regardless of geography, status, or relationship with the banking system. This is why the conflict around DeFi is not only about safety, but about **control over money flows in the digital age**.

## The bigger picture: who writes the rules of the future?

What is unfolding today is not a technical debate about blockchains, but a political and economic power struggle. Will crypto become just another bank-dominated financial product under strict institutional control — or will it preserve the decentralized qualities that made it relevant in the first place?

The answer will shape not only the crypto market, but the **future global monetary system**.
