# Bitcoin hard fork eCash in August: who actually receives the new coins?

The announcement of a new Bitcoin hard fork called eCash has once again sparked debate across the crypto community. The project, led by Bitcoin developer Paul Sztorc, is scheduled for August and introduces a model that sounds simple at first glance — anyone holding Bitcoin receives a new coin.

However, while the idea appears attractive, the reality behind it is far more complex and raises both technical and ideological questions.

## How the model works

In a hard fork, a snapshot of the blockchain is taken. At that moment, all wallet balances are copied to a new network. This means that anyone holding BTC at the time of the snapshot will have the same balance in eCash.

It’s important to understand that this is not a “free reward” in the traditional sense. It is simply a duplicated state that begins operating independently on a separate blockchain.

## Satoshi wallets at the center of controversy

What makes this fork particularly sensitive is the focus on inactive wallets, especially those believed to belong to Satoshi Nakamoto. These wallets contain a significant amount of Bitcoin that has never been moved and represents a notable portion of the total supply.

Any idea of including such coins in a new distribution has triggered strong reactions. For many in the Bitcoin community, this crosses a fundamental line — ownership must remain untouched, regardless of whether a wallet is active or not.

Because of this, the project is not seen only as a technical experiment, but also as a potential ideological conflict within the crypto ecosystem.

## Where you store your BTC matters

One of the most important — yet often overlooked — factors is where Bitcoin is stored at the time of the snapshot.

If users hold BTC in a private wallet where they control the private keys — such as hardware wallets like Ledger or Trezor, or software solutions like Electrum — they will have access to the new eCash coins.

However, if Bitcoin is held on exchanges such as Binance or Coinbase, the situation changes. In that case, the exchange controls the assets and decides whether to support the fork. If it chooses not to, users will likely receive nothing.

A similar limitation may apply to solutions like Tangem wallet. While it is a self-custody model, its more closed ecosystem means access to forked coins depends on whether support is implemented.

## Receiving a coin doesn’t mean it has value

Even if users technically receive eCash, that does not guarantee any market value. For a coin to be worth something, it needs demand, liquidity, and exchange support.

Crypto history shows that most forks fail to gain long-term traction. Without broad adoption, new coins quickly lose relevance.

## August as a key moment

The planned launch in August provides a clear timeline, but also leaves room for uncertainty. In the crypto space, such plans are often delayed, changed, or never fully realized.

That is why most media coverage frames this development as an experiment rather than a guaranteed breakthrough.

## Conclusion

The eCash hard fork once again raises a fundamental question: how much can Bitcoin evolve without compromising its core principles? The idea of receiving new coins attracts attention, but the real outcome will depend on whether the project gains meaningful support.
