# What happens to your crypto if you don’t use it for 5 years?

For most crypto users, the idea that assets can simply “sit” for years is completely normal. Crypto held on an exchange is often treated like money in a bank account — you buy it, leave it there, and wait for a better moment or long-term growth. That’s exactly why many people never even consider that long periods of inactivity could have any consequences.

However, the financial system — including crypto platforms — operates under certain rules that don’t apply only to cryptocurrencies, but also to traditional accounts, savings, and stocks. One of those rules concerns so-called inactive or abandoned accounts — situations where there has been no activity for years and no contact with the owner.

## What does an “inactive account” actually mean?

In practice, inactivity does not mean something is wrong or lost. It simply means the account hasn’t been used for a long period of time. If there are no logins, transactions, or responses to platform communications over an extended period — often between three and five years — the account may be classified as abandoned. This is where a process begins that is often misunderstood.

It’s important to clarify that this is not a seizure of assets or a penalty. **“This is not a confiscation of assets, but a system of ‘unclaimed property,’ where the state temporarily holds funds until the owner comes forward.”** This type of system has existed for decades and applies to many forms of financial assets — crypto has only recently been added to this framework.

## The difference between exchanges and private wallets

Crypto adds another layer of complexity because not all storage methods are the same. These rules apply only to assets held on exchanges or platforms that have custody of the account. If a user holds crypto in a private wallet and controls their own private keys, those assets are not subject to these rules, simply because no third party has access to them.

## How states used to handle crypto

As regulation evolved, real-world issues began to appear. In some jurisdictions, crypto transferred to the state was sold almost immediately. This meant that if an owner later came forward, they would not receive their original cryptocurrency, but only the cash value at the time of the sale. In a volatile market, this could result in a significant loss of potential gains if prices had increased.

## A new approach: holding crypto instead of selling it

Because of this, the approach is beginning to change. New regulations in some US states introduce a different model for handling digital assets. For example, Virginia has passed a law requiring crypto to be held in its original form (“in-kind”) for a certain period before any potential liquidation. This gives users a better chance of reclaiming the actual cryptocurrency, rather than just its past cash value.

A similar direction can be seen in states like Arizona and California, which have also started incorporating digital assets into unclaimed property laws, though with different approaches to liquidation versus custody. This clearly shows that regulation is adapting to the specific nature of the crypto market, which differs significantly from traditional financial systems.

## Who determines the rules?

Another common source of confusion is jurisdiction. Many assume that the rules of their home country apply, but in reality, it works differently. **“For crypto accounts on international platforms, the rules are determined not by the user’s country, but by the country where the platform is registered.”** This means that a user in Europe using a US-based exchange is effectively subject to US regulatory frameworks.

## Can the crypto be recovered?

Despite all of this, one key fact remains — ownership does not disappear simply because an account has been inactive. In most cases, even after long periods of time, users can file a claim, prove ownership, and recover their assets. The process may take time and involve administrative steps, but the right to the assets remains intact.

## Conclusion

In the end, the situation is far less dramatic than it may initially sound. Keeping crypto on an exchange for years is not a problem in itself, but complete inactivity over long periods can trigger administrative processes that are part of the broader financial system. For most users, the solution is simple — occasional access to the account is enough to avoid these scenarios entirely.
