# What is Morpho and how DeFi lending works (explained for beginners)

Morpho is one of the largest and most widely used DeFi lending protocols (decentralized finance) on the blockchain. It is an open, permissionless infrastructure that allows users to lend and borrow cryptocurrencies, mainly on Ethereum and Base, as well as other EVM-compatible networks.

Unlike traditional financial systems, where banks manage funds and set interest rates, Morpho operates without a central intermediary. Users interact directly within the system — some provide liquidity, while others borrow and pay interest.

## What Morpho does differently from Aave and Compound

The key difference between Morpho and traditional DeFi protocols like Aave or Compound lies in how capital is allocated.

Morpho uses **peer-to-peer matching**, attempting to directly connect lenders and borrowers whenever possible. This can result in:

* higher yields for lenders
* lower borrowing costs

When direct matching is not possible, funds are automatically routed into traditional liquidity pools to maintain system stability.

## Two layers of the system: Markets and Vaults

Morpho operates through two main components.

The first is **Morpho Blue (Markets)** — a set of simple, immutable lending markets. Each market is isolated and has clearly defined parameters such as collateral types, loan-to-value ratios (LLTV), and price oracles.

The second, and more relevant for most users, is **Morpho Vaults (MetaMorpho)**. These are “smart” vaults where users deposit funds, while curators automatically allocate capital across different markets to optimize yield. This is also the easiest entry point for beginners, as it requires no active management.

## How big Morpho is today

As of 2026, Morpho manages over $10 billion in deposits, placing it among the top two or three DeFi lending protocols. It has become a key infrastructure layer increasingly used in institutional products and services.

## Institutions and DeFi: what’s happening behind the scenes

While DeFi is often seen as a space for individual users, a growing share of activity comes from institutions. Protocols like Morpho and Aave are now also used through infrastructure platforms such as Fireblocks, which allow banks, funds, and large companies to access DeFi markets without directly managing wallets.

In other words, while individual users must handle technical details and risks themselves, institutions often rely on intermediated infrastructure that simplifies access — but does not remove risk.

## How an individual user can use Morpho

For most users, the simplest way to use Morpho is through vaults, where the process is relatively straightforward.

Users need a crypto wallet (such as MetaMask or Rabby), transfer assets to Ethereum or Base (typically USDC, ETH, or similar tokens), and connect to the Morpho application. From there, they can choose a vault, review the expected yield (APY) and associated risks, and deposit funds.

In return, users receive a vault token representing their share and accumulated yield, and funds can be withdrawn at any time.

For borrowing, users must provide collateral that exceeds the borrowed amount, following the standard DeFi model of overcollateralization.

## Risks you need to understand

Even though Morpho simplifies the process and optimizes yields, it is not a risk-free system.

Key risks include:

* smart contract risk (potential code vulnerabilities)
* collateral price drops → liquidation
* risks related to vault curators
* high gas fees on Ethereum

There is also a very practical risk that many beginners underestimate — technical mistakes when sending funds.

If the wrong network is used (for example, sending tokens to an incompatible blockchain or address), funds can in some cases be permanently lost. Unlike bank transfers, these transactions are usually irreversible.

## Conclusion

Morpho represents a new generation of DeFi lending protocols that aim to improve existing systems by combining peer-to-peer matching with liquidity pools. While it offers potentially higher yields and easier access through vaults, it remains part of the broader DeFi ecosystem and is therefore exposed to the same systemic risks.

That is why Morpho is increasingly used not only by individuals but also by institutions. However, regardless of who is using it, the fundamental rule remains the same — understanding the system and managing risk is essential.
