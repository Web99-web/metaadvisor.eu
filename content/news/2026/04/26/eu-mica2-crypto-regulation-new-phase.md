# EU is already talking about ‘MiCA 2’: crypto regulation enters a new phase

The European Union is already preparing the ground for the next phase of cryptocurrency regulation, even though the current MiCA framework has not yet been fully implemented.

At Paris Blockchain Week 2026, European Commission adviser Peter Kerstens made it clear that “MiCA 2” is a highly likely scenario as the market continues to mature.

In other words — regulation is just getting started.

## MiCA is not the end — but the beginning

MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets) was introduced as the first major regulatory framework for crypto in the EU, but it is already becoming clear that it was never intended to be a final solution.

Kerstens emphasized that EU financial legislation almost always evolves in stages, making it “rather unusual” for MiCA to remain unchanged over time.

A key detail: the regulation already includes a review clause, requiring an official assessment by **June 30, 2027**.

{{< support1 >}}

## The market evolved faster than regulation

When MiCA was originally designed, the crypto market was far simpler:

* a few large projects
* many smaller tokens
* relatively limited use cases

Today, the landscape looks very different:

* DeFi (decentralized finance) has expanded rapidly
* tokenization of real-world assets is growing
* AI and crypto are beginning to intersect
* complex financial products are becoming standard

This means regulation is no longer aligned with the current structure of the market.

## What “MiCA 2” could bring

While there are no official details yet, the direction is already becoming visible:

* regulation of the DeFi sector
* additional rules for staking and yield products
* stricter KYC (Know Your Customer) and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) requirements
* potential oversight of wallets and infrastructure

In other words — an expansion of regulation into areas that were previously outside its reach.

{{< support2 >}}

## It’s not a question of if — but when

Kerstens stressed that the review is not a sign that MiCA is “broken,” but rather part of a normal process of adapting to a rapidly evolving market.

The EU also plans to launch public consultations with industry participants to gather feedback and adjust rules to real-world conditions.

This means that:

* exchanges
* fintech companies
* crypto projects

will actively participate in shaping the next phase of regulation.

## The bigger picture: control vs decentralization

At the core of this development lies a much larger question.

Crypto was created as a decentralized system without central control.

Regulation moves in the opposite direction:

* more oversight
* more rules
* less anonymity

“MiCA 2” could mark the moment when the EU begins regulating precisely what crypto was meant to disrupt.

## Conclusion

MiCA is not the end of regulation — it is just the beginning.

As the market matures, the EU is clearly signaling that rules will become more complex and far-reaching.

The question is no longer whether “MiCA 2” will happen, but how deeply it will reshape the crypto market in Europe.
