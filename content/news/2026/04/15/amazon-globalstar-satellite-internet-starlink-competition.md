# Amazon to buy Globalstar for $11.57B: the satellite internet war with Starlink begins

Amazon is making a serious move into the race for control of internet infrastructure from space. In a $11.57 billion deal, Jeff Bezos’ company plans to acquire Globalstar — a clear signal that it no longer intends to stay in the shadow of Elon Musk’s Starlink.

This acquisition comes at a time when satellite internet is becoming one of the most critical global infrastructures. This is no longer just about technology — it is about controlling access to information in the future. As Metaadvisor continues to track these shifts, it is becoming clear that the space economy is entering a new phase of competition.

## What is Amazon actually acquiring?

Globalstar operates a network of satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO – Low Earth Orbit, meaning satellites positioned relatively close to Earth), enabling communication even in areas without traditional mobile coverage.

The company is widely known for its partnership with Apple, powering features such as Emergency SOS — a system that allows users to send messages via satellite when there is no signal. This means Amazon is not just buying technology, but a proven infrastructure already in real-world use.

In addition, Globalstar is developing an expanded satellite network with Apple’s backing, aiming to significantly increase capacity and coverage.

## Starlink remains the dominant player — for now

Despite this major move, Amazon still has a significant gap to close. Starlink, part of Elon Musk’s SpaceX, already has around 10,000 satellites in orbit and serves more than 9 million users globally.

Amazon currently operates just over 200 satellites, but plans to deploy as many as 3,200 by 2029. A key milestone is mid-2026, when a substantial portion of the network must be operational to meet regulatory requirements.

This highlights how fast this race is accelerating — and how high the stakes are. Metaadvisor has repeatedly pointed out that infrastructure wars often define the next phase of global power.

## Why this race matters

Satellite internet is becoming critical across multiple sectors:

* remote and underserved areas without infrastructure
* emergency and disaster situations
* military and secure communications
* global IoT (Internet of Things) systems

Whoever controls this infrastructure controls access to information. That makes this far more than a simple tech rivalry.

## Apple as a silent partner in the background

Interestingly, Apple already plays a major role in Globalstar, with an investment of about $1.5 billion. Amazon and Apple now share an interest in expanding satellite-based services.

This raises an important question:
could we see strategic alliances between major tech companies forming against Starlink’s dominance?

## A new global conflict — from space

Satellite internet is no longer a future concept — it is already here. The only question is who will dominate the market.

Starlink leads for now, but Amazon’s move shows serious intent. With its financial power and global logistics, Amazon could close the gap faster than expected.

The key question remains:
will the market support multiple major players — or evolve into a single dominant system?

## Conclusion

Amazon has just made one of the most significant moves in the space and tech industry.
