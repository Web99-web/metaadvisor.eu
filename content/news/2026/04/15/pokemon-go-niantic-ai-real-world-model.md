# Do you remember Pokémon Go? Niantic is now building a real-world model for AI

Do you remember the summer when people flooded the streets catching Pokémon? Parks, squares, and entire cities were filled with users staring at their phones, searching for virtual creatures in the real world.

At the time, it looked like a global gaming phenomenon. Entertainment, trend, and a short-lived digital craze.

Today, nearly a decade later, that same story is taking on a completely new meaning — and a much deeper context. As Metaadvisor observes, what once looked like a game now increasingly resembles something far more significant.

## A game that quietly mapped the real world

Pokémon Go, one of the most successful mobile games of all time, was also one of the first mass-adopted augmented reality systems (AR – Augmented Reality, meaning digital elements layered onto the real world).

But behind the game, another process was taking place.

Millions of users — some estimates suggest up to 140 million — were walking through cities, stopping at specific locations, taking photos, scanning objects, and interacting with digital points anchored in physical space. Each of these actions generated data — not just about location, but about the structure of the world itself. It is estimated that up to 30 billion images have been collected.

This process, although visible through app features, is only now being fully understood — especially the scale of AI data that was created in the process.

## From game to artificial intelligence

Niantic is no longer operating purely as a gaming company. Its focus is gradually shifting toward building artificial intelligence that can understand the physical world.

At the center of this shift is the so-called Large Geospatial Model (LGM) — a system designed to allow AI to “see” and interpret space, in a way that resembles human perception.

Unlike traditional AI models that process text or images, this type of model understands where objects are located, how they relate to each other, and how to navigate through them.

## 30 billion images of the real world

According to available information, Niantic is working with a dataset of more than 30 billion real-world images. A portion of this data comes from applications like Pokémon Go, where users actively participated in mapping their surroundings through AR interactions.

It is important to note that this data was not collected secretly, but through features users chose to engage with. However, the scale of what was created — and how it may be used — is only now becoming fully visible.

## AI that understands space

These systems already have real-world applications:

- navigation in environments where GPS is unreliable  
- autonomous robots and delivery systems  
- development of AR applications and smart glasses  
- creation of digital replicas of the physical world  

This represents a new layer of technology connecting the physical and digital worlds in ways not previously possible. As Metaadvisor highlights, this is not just innovation — it is infrastructure.

## Games as a tool, not the end product

While Pokémon Go still exists and maintains an active user base, its role is changing. Games are no longer just the final product — they are becoming tools.

Tools for collecting data, training models, and testing technology in real-world environments.

In this context, users are no longer just players. They are active participants in building complex technological systems.

## A new technological reality

This story raises a broader question: how much do everyday digital activities contribute to the development of artificial intelligence?

The line between entertainment, technology, and infrastructure is becoming increasingly blurred. What appears today as a simple app or game may tomorrow become the foundation of an entire industry.

## Conclusion

Pokémon Go may have started as a game.

But today, it increasingly looks like the beginning of one of the largest real-world mapping projects for artificial intelligence. Metaadvisor will continue to follow how this transformation shapes the future of AI.
