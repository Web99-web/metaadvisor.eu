# Bitcoin fell to $66,000

## War, dollar and market fear bring pressure back

Bitcoin is once again under strong pressure and at one point fell below the **$66,000 level**, with a daily drop of around 2%, but with significantly higher intraday pressure during trading. This decline is not an isolated event — stocks, bonds, and parts of the commodities market also fell simultaneously, clearly pointing to broader macroeconomic stress.

Following the latest address by the US president, which failed to deliver the expected de-escalation and instead left room for further escalation of the conflict with Iran, investors reacted by pulling capital out of risk assets. Every recovery attempt in recent weeks has quickly been sold off as soon as new uncertainty appears, creating a highly unstable market environment.

## The real cause: dollar and energy, not headlines

While it may seem that the market is reacting to political statements, more and more analysts warn that the key drivers lie much deeper. The US dollar is strengthening again and showing signs of a potential breakout to higher levels, which historically represents a negative signal for Bitcoin and other risk assets.

If the US dollar confirms further strength, as analysts are already suggesting, it could put additional pressure on the crypto market. Bitcoin and the dollar traditionally have an inverse correlation — a stronger dollar almost always means a weaker Bitcoin.

The situation is further worsened by developments in the energy market. Oil prices are already trading above $100 per barrel, increasing inflationary pressures and strengthening the dollar — a combination that historically does not favor the crypto market.

## Market trapped between panic and hope

Over the past few weeks, Bitcoin has been moving within an unstable range between approximately $65,000 and $73,000, with every new escalation quickly erasing previous gains and pushing the price back toward the lower end of the range. While this may appear as stabilization at first glance, it actually reflects a highly sensitive market reacting to every external shock.

The Fear and Greed Index has remained in extreme fear territory for an extended period, indicating that investors are making decisions more emotionally than rationally. Such phases often precede larger market moves.

## $600 billion in losses: pressure from within

It is estimated that around 44% of the total Bitcoin supply is currently in loss, representing approximately $600 billion in unrealized losses, according to data from analytics firm Glassnode.

This means millions of investors are holding Bitcoin below their purchase price. Some long-term holders are beginning to realize losses, which is a classic signal of capitulation and often appears in later stages of market downturns.

Historically, a similar situation was seen in 2022, when significant market “cleansing” was required before a new growth cycle could begin.

## Weak demand limits recovery

Data suggests weakening spot demand, particularly from the US, indicating that institutional and retail capital is not currently entering the market strongly enough to support growth.

Without strong capital inflows, recovery attempts remain fragile and short-lived. This is why Bitcoin struggles to hold higher levels even during periods of positive news.

## What’s next for Bitcoin?

The market is currently not driven by a single event, but by a chain reaction: geopolitical uncertainty pushes energy prices higher, which increases inflation, strengthens the dollar, and ultimately puts pressure on risk assets like Bitcoin.

Three key forces are currently shaping the market — geopolitics, macroeconomics, and internal market conditions. All of them are currently moving in the same direction, increasing the likelihood of continued downside pressure.

If these trends persist, Bitcoin could test lower levels in the coming weeks. In other words, the market is no longer reacting only to news — but to structural pressures that cannot be resolved with a single positive headline.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

This situation clearly shows how important it is to distinguish short-term news from long-term trends. Headlines drive reactions, but the real direction of the market is determined by liquidity, the dollar, and energy.

Metaadvisor.eu sees the market as being in an adjustment phase — not necessarily the end of the cycle, but certainly a period of increased risk and uncertainty. It is precisely in such phases that the most important decisions are made — and the most expensive mistakes.
