# AI mania: Allbirds, a sneaker maker, becomes tech company NewBird AI and the stock jumps 300% overnight

In one of the most unusual business transformations in recent years, US-based **Allbirds** — known for its minimalist and eco-friendly sneakers such as the **Wool Runners** made from merino wool, popular among tech workers in Silicon Valley — has decided to abandon its core business and pivot to artificial intelligence.

The result? Its stock surged by around 300% in a very short period of time.

Behind that surge lies a much deeper story about market hype, capital flows, and the global race for AI infrastructure.

## From sneakers to GPU servers

Allbirds sold its footwear brand and operations for around $39 million and announced a rebrand into a new company called **NewBird AI**.

The new strategy has almost nothing to do with its original products.

Instead of sneakers, the company now plans to:

* build AI infrastructure
* offer GPU-as-a-Service
* develop cloud solutions for artificial intelligence

In other words, it is moving from fashion into one of the most technical and competitive industries in the world.

{{< support1 >}}

## AI infrastructure: the new gold

The reason behind this radical shift comes down to one thing — the massive demand for computing power required to support AI.

GPUs (graphics processing units) have become a critical resource in the AI economy, while supply remains limited.

As a result:

* prices are rising
* demand is exploding
* companies are looking for any way to enter the space

Even Bitcoin miners have already started pivoting toward AI compute — and now completely unexpected players are joining the trend.

## $50 million to start over

To fund this transformation, Allbirds secured around $50 million through a convertible financing structure.

This means:

* investors provide capital as debt
* it can later be converted into equity

This model allows fast access to funding but carries the risk of dilution for existing shareholders.

Notably, the amount is roughly double the company’s market value before the announcement.

## Market reaction: +300%

The most visible impact of the announcement was seen in the stock market.

Shares jumped by approximately 300%, highlighting how strongly markets are currently driven by AI narratives.

Right now:

* AI = capital
* AI = investor attention
* AI = rapid price movement

Regardless of how realistic the underlying business model may be.

## History repeating itself?

This move recalls a well-known case from 2017, when Long Island Iced Tea rebranded itself as “Long Blockchain” and saw its stock surge.

That experiment did not end well — the company was eventually delisted.

Which raises the question:

Is this the beginning of a successful AI company, or just another example of market hype?

## Reality: between opportunity and risk

On one hand:

* AI infrastructure is a real need
* the market is massive
* capital is flowing into the sector

On the other:

* Allbirds has no experience in AI
* competition includes the largest tech companies in the world
* the pivot is extremely risky

## Conclusion

The Allbirds story shows how strongly markets are currently driven by artificial intelligence.

Industry boundaries are blurring, and companies are willing to completely reinvent themselves to capture a share of the AI wave.

The real question is not whether this trend will continue — but how many of these transformations will actually succeed.
