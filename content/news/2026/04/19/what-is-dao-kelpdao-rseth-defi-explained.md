# What are DAO, KelpDAO and rsETH: a simple explanation of the DeFi system and its risks

In the crypto world, terms like DAO, restaking and tokens such as rsETH are frequently used — but for many users they remain unclear.

As DeFi becomes increasingly complex, understanding these concepts is essential for evaluating risk and making informed decisions.

## What is a DAO

A DAO (Decentralized Autonomous Organization) is an organization that operates without a traditional hierarchy such as a CEO or management team.

Instead:

* members hold tokens that represent voting power
* decisions are made through voting
* rules are written in smart contracts on the blockchain

This means the organization operates through code rather than centralized control.

## How a DAO works in practice

A typical DAO functions as follows:

* a member proposes a change or decision
* the community votes
* if approved, the smart contract automatically executes the decision

Everything is transparent and public — without intermediaries.

## The reality of the DAO model

Although DAO sounds fully decentralized, the reality is often different.

In many projects:

* a small number of holders control most of the tokens
* they have greater influence over decisions

Because of this, a DAO often represents a mix of decentralization and centralized influence.

## What is KelpDAO

KelpDAO is a DeFi protocol that uses a DAO governance model and focuses on so-called **liquid restaking** — one of the fastest-growing segments in crypto.

Its core idea is to increase capital efficiency by using the same ETH multiple times.

In practice, this looks like:

* users deposit ETH or staking tokens (e.g. stETH, rETH)
* KelpDAO routes that capital into additional systems like EigenLayer
* users receive a token in return — **rsETH**

This allows the same capital to work across multiple layers simultaneously.

{{< support1 >}}

## What is liquid restaking and why it exists

Traditional staking has one limitation:

👉 ETH is locked and cannot be used elsewhere

Restaking introduces an additional layer:

👉 the same ETH is used to secure additional networks and services

KelpDAO goes one step further:

👉 it allows that ETH to remain liquid through the rsETH token

This means:

* you earn base staking yield
* you receive additional restaking rewards
* you can still use your capital in DeFi

This model is often described as:

👉 “maximizing capital efficiency”

## What rsETH actually represents

rsETH is not “new ETH”, but a derivative.

It represents:

* ETH that is already staked
* additional participation in restaking systems
* exposure to multiple infrastructure layers

It is important to understand:

👉 the value of rsETH depends on the entire underlying system

If everything works:

* rsETH tracks ETH
* it may slightly increase due to rewards

If something goes wrong:

* it can lose reliability as collateral
* liquidity may drop
* its price can deviate from ETH

## How KelpDAO functions as a DAO

Although KelpDAO uses a DAO model, it is not fully decentralized from the beginning.

Like most DeFi projects:

* it is initially launched by a team
* governance is gradually transferred to the community
* decisions are made through a mix of DAO voting and expert groups (e.g. risk committee)

This means:

👉 a DAO exists, but development and security still depend on the team

## Where the biggest risk comes from

The KelpDAO system includes multiple layers:

* staking
* restaking (EigenLayer)
* bridge infrastructure
* DeFi protocols that accept rsETH

This creates a so-called **stacked risk model**.

If one part of the system fails:

👉 the problem can spread across the entire ecosystem

This is exactly what recent events demonstrate, where issues with rsETH impact broader DeFi systems.

## Why this matters for users

For users, KelpDAO offers:

✔ greater flexibility
✔ additional rewards
✔ liquidity

But at the same time:

⚠️ increased complexity
⚠️ more potential failure points
⚠️ higher systemic risk

Which leads to one key rule:

👉 higher returns almost always come with higher risk

## Where risk comes from

The main issue lies in system complexity.

It includes multiple layers:

* staking
* restaking (EigenLayer)
* bridge infrastructure
* DeFi protocols

If one component fails:

👉 the consequences can spread across the system

## Can you exit the system

Users typically have two options:

* sell rsETH immediately on the market (fast, but with price risk)
* withdraw through the protocol (slower, with waiting time)

Under normal conditions:

* there is no fixed lock-up period
* but there is a delay due to system mechanics

In crisis situations:

* withdrawals can be paused
* liquidity may become limited

## Conclusion

DAO, KelpDAO and rsETH represent advanced concepts within the DeFi ecosystem that enable greater flexibility and potential returns.

However:

👉 they increase system complexity
👉 they introduce additional layers of risk

Understanding these mechanisms is essential for anyone participating in DeFi.
