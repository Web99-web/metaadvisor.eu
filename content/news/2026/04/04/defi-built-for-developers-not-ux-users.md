# DeFi is built for developers — not for people

Decentralized finance has been promising a revolution in money for years. No banks, no intermediaries, no restrictions — just open protocols accessible to everyone. But when an average user tries DeFi for the first time, they quickly realize something else: the system wasn’t built for them.

Instead of a simple and intuitive experience, users face technical terminology, complex workflows, and a series of decisions they must make without clear guidance. What should be a financial experience turns into a technical challenge that requires developer-level understanding rather than user-level clarity.

## A system without instructions

One of the biggest problems in DeFi is obvious, yet rarely stated clearly:
👉 there is no user manual.

Users enter:

* liquidity pools
* lending protocols
* leveraged positions

…without understanding:

* how the system works
* what the actual risks are
* what liquidation really means

Unlike traditional finance, where there are clear rules, support, and protections, DeFi assumes the user already understands everything. If they don’t — the system won’t warn them.

{{< support1 >}}

## Technology dictates the rules

This design is not accidental. DeFi was built within the technical constraints of blockchain systems, where every operation has a cost. To make systems affordable to use, developers had to simplify logic and reduce computational complexity.

But this optimization comes at a cost:
👉 financial logic is simplified
👉 systems become rigid
👉 responsibility is shifted to the user

In other words, DeFi is not optimized for markets — it is optimized for gas.

## When markets move — DeFi doesn’t adapt

Under stable conditions, such systems can function relatively well. But when volatility increases, when markets move fast, or when unexpected events occur, weaknesses become visible.

Risk models are often static, liquidation engines rely on fixed formulas, and adjustments happen slowly through governance processes. The system does not react — it simply executes.

## Users without protection

For users, this leads to a harsh reality: there is no safety net. If something goes wrong, the system will not stop, will not warn, and will not adapt.

This results in situations where:

* users do not understand what is happening
* positions are closed automatically
* losses happen quickly and without control

All within a system that presents itself as the future of finance.

{{< support2 >}}

## The gap between technology and reality

Today, there is a clear gap between what DeFi technology can do and what users actually need. On one side, there are sophisticated protocols and innovations. On the other, users who cannot understand even the basic functions.

This is not just a UX problem — it is an accessibility problem. If a system is not understandable, it is not usable.

## Conclusion: a future not yet ready for people

DeFi has enormous potential, but without a shift in focus, that potential remains limited. Systems that cannot explain themselves will struggle to build trust with mass users.

If DeFi wants to become mainstream, it will need to do what it has not done yet: adapt to people.

## MetaAdvisor take:

* DeFi is currently built more for developers than for average users
* Lack of clear instructions and UX is the biggest barrier to adoption
* Focus on gas optimization limits the development of more advanced systems
* Users often enter without understanding the risks
* Without a user-first approach, DeFi will struggle to go mainstream
