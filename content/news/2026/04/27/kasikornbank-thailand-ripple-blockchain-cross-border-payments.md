# Kasikornbank (Thailand) and Ripple: banks test blockchain for cross-border payments

While much of the crypto market remains focused on price movements and short-term trends, more structural developments are unfolding in the background. One of them comes from the banking sector, where blockchain technology is increasingly being tested for cross-border payments.

According to available reports, Kasikornbank in Thailand is working with Ripple to develop and test solutions for international transfers. The project is currently in a pilot phase, aimed at evaluating how blockchain can improve the speed and efficiency of cross-border transactions.

**Multiple crypto media outlets confirm that KBank and Ripple are running a multi-phase pilot project to test blockchain-based international transfers, including simulated on-chain transactions between different countries.**

## Why banks are testing blockchain

Traditional cross-border payments still rely on complex systems involving multiple intermediaries, longer settlement times, and higher costs. While reliable, these systems were not designed for the speed required by today’s digital economy.

Blockchain technology offers an alternative approach — faster execution, greater transparency, and potentially lower costs. This is why banks are increasingly experimenting with such solutions through pilot programs before considering wider adoption.

## Ripple’s role in the banking system

Unlike many other crypto projects, Ripple has focused from the start on building technology for financial institutions. Its goal is not to replace banks, but to modernize existing infrastructure for cross-border payments.

In this model, blockchain operates as a backend layer, while banks remain central to the financial system.

## What a “pilot project” means

It is important to note that this is not a live, large-scale implementation. Pilot projects are used to test key aspects of the technology, including:

* transaction speed and reliability
* system stability
* regulatory conditions
* security mechanisms

Only after such testing phases can banks decide whether to deploy the technology in real-world operations.
