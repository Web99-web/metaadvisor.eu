# Elon Musk vs Sam Altman: OpenAI trial enters a decisive phase

The conflict between Elon Musk and Sam Altman over OpenAI is entering its most critical stage. The trial begins on April 27, 2026, in a federal court in Oakland, California, starting with jury selection, followed by opening statements.

The case is expected to last several weeks and could bring some of the most influential figures in the tech industry to the witness stand. But this is more than a personal dispute — at its core lies a fundamental question: who controls the future of artificial intelligence.

## The beginning: a nonprofit vision

When OpenAI was founded in 2015, its mission was clear — to develop artificial intelligence for the benefit of humanity, openly and without being driven purely by commercial interests.

The organization started as a nonprofit initiative built around shared principles of safety, transparency, and long-term societal value. Elon Musk was one of the key early supporters and financial backers.

## The turning point: structural shift

After Musk left OpenAI in 2018, the organization began to evolve. It introduced a capped-profit model and formed a strategic partnership with Microsoft.

This shift enabled rapid growth and the development of commercial products such as ChatGPT, but it also raised questions about whether the original mission had been compromised.

## Musk’s claims

In his 2024 lawsuit, Musk argues that OpenAI fundamentally deviated from its founding principles.

The key claims include:

* breach of charitable trust
* unjust enrichment

According to Musk, OpenAI transformed into a highly profitable entity worth hundreds of billions of dollars, contradicting its original commitments.

He is seeking:

* the removal of Sam Altman and Greg Brockman
* a restructuring of the organization
* damages estimated between $134 billion and $187 billion

Musk has stated that he does not intend to personally receive the money, but that it should be redirected to OpenAI’s nonprofit arm.

## OpenAI’s response

OpenAI strongly disputes Musk’s claims, presenting a different version of events.

According to the company:

* Musk was aware of and supported the transition to a for-profit structure as early as 2017
* he sought greater control over the organization
* after failing to achieve that, he left and later became a competitor through xAI

OpenAI has also published internal emails and communications to support its position.

Additionally, the company argues that Musk’s financial contributions were donations, not investments, and therefore do not grant ownership rights.

## The trial and key witnesses

The trial begins with jury selection and is expected to last around four weeks.

Potential witnesses include:

* Elon Musk
* Sam Altman
* Greg Brockman
* Satya Nadella

Some claims have already been dismissed by the judge, but the core issues will now be decided by a jury, making the outcome particularly significant.

## What’s at stake: AI and trillion-dollar ambitions

The case comes at a time when OpenAI is valued in the hundreds of billions, with potential plans for an IPO that could push its valuation toward $1 trillion.

This means the dispute is not just legal — it involves:

* control over a transformative technology
* access to a potentially trillion-dollar market
* the balance between public interest and private capital

## Conclusion

The Musk vs Altman case goes far beyond a standard business dispute. It exposes deep tensions within the AI industry — between idealism and commercialization, openness and control, safety and speed.

As the trial begins, one thing is clear: its outcome could shape not only OpenAI, but the future direction of artificial intelligence itself.
