# Bitcoin whale shorts $38 million: signal for a drop or just another position

## A big number that immediately attracts attention

In the crypto market, a single large position is often enough to trigger a wave of speculation. That’s exactly what happened after one of the more well-known whale accounts on the Hyperliquid platform opened a short position on Bitcoin worth around $38 million.

At first glance, the message seems clear: a large player expects prices to fall.

But reality is more complex.

## What the data actually shows

According to available data, the same trader is holding multiple positions at the same time:

* short on Bitcoin (~$38M)
* long on Ethereum (~$21M)
* total capital around $19M

In addition, the trading history shows:

* around **63% winning trades**
* more than **$159 million in profits over the past months**
* but also **recent losses over the last 30 days**

In other words, this is an active trader — not a single directional bet on the market.

## Hyperliquid and the new transparency of the market

This situation became visible directly on the Hyperliquid platform, where large positions are publicly accessible and can be tracked almost in real time.

Unlike traditional financial markets, where such information is often hidden or delayed, the crypto market allows near-instant insight into the behavior of large players.

That, however, comes with a problem.

## Seeing does not mean understanding

The fact that we can see a large short position does not mean we understand its context.

Large players often:

* hedge other positions
* use leverage in complex strategies
* hold both long and short positions simultaneously

That’s why a single position rarely tells the full story.

## Is this really a signal for a Bitcoin drop

This is the key question that comes up every time a situation like this appears. But the answer is not simple.

Large traders don’t trade based on “opinions” — they trade strategies. Their positions are often part of a broader structure involving multiple markets, instruments, and timeframes.

In other words, what looks like a clear bearish signal may simply be one piece of a more complex strategy.

## The bigger picture: a market everyone can watch

The crypto market today is in a unique position. On one hand, there is near-complete transparency — anyone can see what large players are doing.

On the other hand, interpreting that data is becoming increasingly difficult. Without understanding the strategy behind a position, the numbers themselves can easily be misinterpreted.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* a large short position does not automatically mean a price drop
* whales use complex strategies, not simple bets
* transparency increases information, but not clarity
* tracking positions can be useful, but without context it can mislead
* the key is not what we see — but how we interpret it
