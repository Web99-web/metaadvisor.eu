# PUSD: Shariah-compliant stablecoin enters ADI blockchain targeting a $3 trillion market

## A new step in connecting crypto with Islamic finance

The crypto market is increasingly seeing projects designed for specific segments of the financial system, and one of the most notable examples is PUSD — a shariah-compliant stablecoin now integrating with the ADI blockchain, an infrastructure focused on institutional settlement in the Middle East.

Unlike traditional stablecoins such as USDT or USDC, PUSD is not designed as a universal trading tool. Instead, it is built as a financial instrument aligned with Islamic finance principles and tailored for banks, corporations, and institutional users.

## What does shariah-compliant mean?

Shariah financial principles impose strict rules. Interest is prohibited, excessive speculation is discouraged, and financial products must be backed by real assets.

PUSD is structured accordingly. Its value is tied to Gulf region currencies such as the Saudi riyal and the UAE dirham, both of which are pegged to the US dollar. This ensures stability while maintaining compliance with Islamic financial standards.

## ADI blockchain and institutional infrastructure

The integration with the ADI blockchain is significant because this network is not built for retail users, but for institutional transactions. It enables banks and financial institutions to settle transactions efficiently and at scale.

By integrating PUSD, the system gains access to a stablecoin aligned with regulatory and financial requirements. This marks a shift where blockchain technology moves beyond trading and into real-world financial infrastructure.

## A $3 trillion market

One of the main drivers behind such projects is the size of the Islamic finance market, estimated at over $3 trillion globally. It is a highly structured sector with strict rules, but also significant potential for digital transformation.

PUSD aims to bridge these two worlds — traditional finance and blockchain — without violating the principles that define Islamic financial systems.

## Bigger picture: regional crypto ecosystems

This development fits into a broader trend where regions such as the UAE and Saudi Arabia are actively building their own blockchain ecosystems. Instead of relying solely on global platforms, they are developing local solutions aligned with regulatory and cultural frameworks.

This suggests that the crypto market is becoming increasingly fragmented, with regional systems operating under their own rules and standards.
