# US charges 10 individuals over crypto market manipulation

The US Department of Justice (DOJ – *Department of Justice*, US federal law enforcement authority) has filed charges against 10 foreign nationals linked to crypto firms Gotbit, Vortex, Antier, and Contrarian, in a case that could have broader implications for the entire digital asset market.

Three of the accused, including executives and employees of these firms, were arrested in Singapore and extradited to the United States, where they appeared before a federal court in Oakland. This move is part of a wider investigation that has been ongoing since 2024 and involves coordination between multiple regulatory bodies, including the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC – *Securities and Exchange Commission*).

## How was the market allegedly manipulated?

According to the US Department of Justice, the defendants used linked accounts and coordinated transactions to artificially increase the trading volume of certain tokens. This practice, known as wash trading, creates the impression that there is more demand for an asset than actually exists.

In other words, a market can appear active and liquid even when there are no real buyers and sellers behind that volume. This kind of “artificial activity” is at the core of the current case.

## A global investigation spanning years

This case is not an isolated event. The investigation began back in 2024, when US authorities launched an undercover operation targeting crypto manipulation and investment fraud. In earlier phases, a total of 18 individuals and entities were charged, indicating that this is a structural issue rather than a one-off incident.

The case spans multiple jurisdictions and highlights international cooperation, underlining how global the crypto market has become — and how increasingly willing regulators are to act across borders when they believe their domestic markets are affected.

## What does this mean for the crypto market?

This indictment does not mean that the entire crypto market is manipulated. However, it clearly shows that volume manipulation is not just a theory — it is a practice that occurs in certain cases.

For investors, this has direct implications. Trading volume is often used as a key indicator of interest and liquidity. If part of that volume is artificially generated, then conclusions based on those metrics may be misleading.

Smaller projects and altcoins are particularly vulnerable, as it is easier to create the illusion of activity in markets with lower liquidity. In such cases, a market may appear dynamic and promising, while actual demand remains limited.

## A market between reality and perception

The crypto market today operates on the boundary between real innovation and speculative dynamics. On one side are projects with real users and technology, while on the other there is space where perception can be shaped through volume and activity.

Cases like this highlight the need for greater caution and deeper understanding. Numbers alone are no longer enough — it is essential to understand how they are created.

## Conclusion: a signal of change

This case shows that regulators are taking the crypto market increasingly seriously. As the industry evolves, so does the pressure for transparency and accountability.

While this is not proof for the entire market, it makes clear that certain practices will no longer be tolerated. For investors, this means one thing: more analysis, and less reliance on surface-level indicators like volume.
