# Wall Street enters crypto — and it’s serious

Cryptocurrencies have long existed outside the traditional financial system, often seen as an experiment, an alternative, or even a threat to the existing model of banking and investing. But now something is happening that is changing the rules of the game: major Wall Street players are increasingly integrating crypto into their standard products and services.

One of the latest and most significant examples comes from the American brokerage giant Charles Schwab, which plans to launch spot trading for Bitcoin (BTC) and Ethereum (ETH) in the first half of 2026. This move brings crypto directly into platforms already used by millions of investors worldwide — without the need to switch to specialized crypto exchanges.

## Crypto is no longer an “alternative” — it is becoming the standard

Schwab is not a small player testing the market. It is one of the largest investment firms in the world, with nearly $12 trillion in client assets. When an institution of that scale introduces spot crypto trading, it is no longer an experiment — it is a signal of a structural shift in the market.

It is important to understand the difference between spot trading and derivatives. Spot trading means that users buy actual Bitcoin or Ethereum, rather than contracts that track their price. This brings crypto closer to traditional investments and increases its legitimacy among more conservative investors.

## One account for everything — stocks, bonds and crypto

One of the key goals of this move is to unify different asset classes within a single platform. Instead of having separate accounts for stocks, ETFs and crypto, Schwab aims to allow users to view and manage all their investments in one place.

This represents a major shift in user experience (UX – *user experience*). Crypto is no longer something “separate”, but becomes an integrated part of a portfolio, just as accessible as traditional financial instruments.

## Trust as the main barrier — and opportunity

One of the biggest challenges for crypto so far has been a lack of trust among the broader public. Complex wallets, security risks, hacks and unclear regulation have kept many people away from the market. Institutions like Schwab act as a bridge between the old and the new financial world.

For many users, trading crypto through a familiar platform feels safer than using standalone exchanges. This could open the door to millions of new investors who have been hesitant or simply did not understand how to enter the crypto space.

## Institutional capital is already here

This move does not come as a surprise. We have already seen strong institutional interest through Bitcoin ETFs, which have attracted billions of dollars in capital. Schwab already allows clients to invest in such ETFs and trade Bitcoin futures, making the move into spot trading a logical next step.

As more capital flows into crypto, the market becomes more stable — but also increasingly similar to traditional finance. Volatility (sudden price changes) may decrease, but the nature of the market is also evolving.

## Is crypto losing its “wild” side?

The entry of large financial institutions also has a downside. Crypto was originally designed as a decentralized system without intermediaries, where users have full control over their assets. With integration into banking and brokerage systems, some of that control shifts back to institutions.

This means many users will hold their crypto within platforms rather than in their own wallets. While this reduces technical complexity, it also moves away from the original idea of decentralization. The question is: is crypto becoming just another financial product?

## What this means for the future of the market

If this trend continues, it is very likely that in the coming years most new investors will enter crypto through banks and brokers, rather than traditional crypto platforms. The line between traditional finance (TradFi) and decentralized finance (DeFi) is increasingly blurring.

For the market, this means more liquidity, more capital and potentially greater stability. But for the original idea of crypto — it may represent a compromise between freedom and convenience.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

* Entry of Schwab confirms that crypto is becoming part of the global financial system
* Easier access could bring millions of new users into the market
* There is a risk that crypto may lose its decentralized nature
* Institutional capital stabilizes the market long-term, but also “tames” it
* The key question: will users understand the difference between true ownership and a custodial model?
