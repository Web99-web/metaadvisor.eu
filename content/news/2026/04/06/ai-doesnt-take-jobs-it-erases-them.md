# AI is already impacting jobs — but not in the way people think. It doesn’t just take them — it erases them.

AI is already transforming the labor market through automation, optimization, and layoffs, but at the same time something else is happening — something far less visible. While public discussion focuses on replacing workers with algorithms, a parallel process is unfolding with even more direct consequences — the erasure of existing jobs through AI moderation systems.

On platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and Messenger, more and more long-time users are simply disappearing. No warning, no clear explanation, and no real way to appeal. To others, it looks like they just became inactive. In reality, many have been permanently removed.

These are often not just private profiles. They include small business owners, freelancers, photographers, media accounts — but also administrators of groups and communities they have built over years. When an AI system removes an account, it doesn’t just delete a profile — it removes access to entire communities, contacts, and the digital infrastructure built around them.

In many cases, users also lose control over groups with hundreds or thousands of members. This doesn’t just erase one account — it disrupts entire networks of people connected to it, often without any warning or transition period.

## More cases are coming to light

Since we started tracking this issue, we are discovering a growing number of real cases. Users from the United States have reached out to us after being removed from Facebook and Instagram in 2025 — and they have never managed to recover their accounts.

The pattern is almost identical: accounts disappear overnight, without explanation and without a real appeal process. Users lose access to everything — photos, contacts, business communication, and years of accumulated work.

The problem is made worse by the fact that many affected users don’t even know that thousands of others are experiencing the same thing. Their voice disappears along with their account. As a result, some are trying to rebuild their presence on platforms like Mastodon or LinkedIn, but most still want their original accounts back — along with everything that was lost.

Users also pointed us to an online petition calling for account restoration, which shows how widespread the issue is. In just four days of tracking, around 400 new signatures were added, indicating a steady increase in affected users. Yet many people still haven’t even heard about the petition.

## The EU is trying — but reality looks different

Although the European Union is attempting to address these issues through regulations like the DSA and the AI Act, the reality on the ground looks very different. Users who attempt to use official channels, including appeals centres, often report receiving no response for weeks.

In theory, there is a right to appeal and a promise of transparency. In practice, many users report that there is no real human support and no way for their case to be reviewed by an actual person. The system feels like a final automated decision with no possibility of correction.

## AI and jobs — the other side of the story

In the broader discussion about AI and employment, this reveals another dimension. AI is not only replacing jobs through automation — it can also end existing jobs instantly, without transition, without alternatives, and without accountability.

For small businesses that depend on digital platforms, this is no longer hypothetical. Losing access to an account means losing income, clients, and visibility. In a digital environment where platforms have become infrastructure, such decisions have consequences similar to shutting down a physical business location or communication channel.

## Our take

AI moderation currently operates in a regulatory grey zone. While the EU AI Act defines many high-risk areas, decisions that directly impact people’s livelihoods through social platforms are still not clearly classified as such. In practice, losing access to these platforms often means losing a job — without a clear legal protection framework.

The key question is no longer whether AI will impact jobs — but how many are already disappearing, quietly and without visibility.
