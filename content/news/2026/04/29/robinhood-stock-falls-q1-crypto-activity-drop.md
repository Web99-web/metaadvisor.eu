# Robinhood stock falls nearly 10% in Q1 as crypto activity drops almost 50%

The crypto sector is showing signs of weakness that go beyond price action, extending into the financial results of companies heavily dependent on trading activity. Robinhood’s latest quarterly report confirmed exactly that — and markets reacted immediately.

## Weak earnings and declining crypto activity

Robinhood stock dropped nearly 10% after the company reported first-quarter results that missed analyst expectations, particularly in the crypto segment where activity declined sharply.

The company posted $1.07 billion in revenue and earnings per share of $0.38, both below forecasts. While Robinhood remains profitable, with net income rising 3% year-over-year to $346 million, investors reacted negatively to weaker performance in its core crypto business.

The biggest hit came from crypto operations. Crypto transaction revenue fell 47% — from $252 million to $134 million — while trading volume dropped 48% to $24 billion. This marks the third consecutive quarter of decline, highlighting reduced retail investor engagement in current market conditions.

## CEO Tenev: focus on infrastructure and tokenization

CEO Vladimir Tenev stated that the decline in revenue and trading volume is largely driven by market volatility, but emphasized that the company remains committed to building long-term crypto infrastructure. According to him, the focus is shifting toward assets and systems with real-world utility rather than purely speculative demand.

During the investor call, Tenev also noted that the market may be at the beginning of a “tokenization supercycle,” where blockchain technology could play a major role in digitizing assets and financial systems.

Meanwhile, Robinhood is actively diversifying its revenue streams. One of its newer products, a prediction markets platform built in partnership with Kalshi, recorded 8.8 billion contracts traded in Q1 — a significant growth signal pointing to new directions beyond traditional crypto trading.

## Sector-wide impact

Robinhood’s decline did not remain isolated. The earnings miss sent a negative signal across the sector, with other crypto-related companies also experiencing losses. Coinbase shares fell around 8%, while mining companies such as Riot Platforms and MARA also declined.

Notably, the drop in crypto-related stocks was more pronounced than the movement in crypto prices themselves. Bitcoin remained relatively stable, slipping slightly below the $76,000 level. This suggests that investors are currently more concerned about business models than about the underlying asset.

## Geopolitics, Hormuz Strait and FED in focus

This downturn is not happening in isolation. Behind the scenes, geopolitical tensions are escalating. The situation around the Hormuz Strait — one of the world’s most critical oil shipping routes — intensified after the U.S. administration rejected an Iranian proposal aimed at easing tensions.

This decision pushed oil prices higher by around 6%, with WTI surpassing $106 per barrel and Brent reaching $112. Such moves increase pressure on global markets and contribute to rising inflation risks.

Investors are also closely watching the Federal Reserve (FED – the United States central bank), whose monetary policy decisions could significantly impact market direction in the coming period.

Overall, the situation highlights a shift in the crypto market: macroeconomic forces and corporate performance are becoming increasingly important, while Bitcoin’s price alone is no longer the sole indicator of the industry’s health.
