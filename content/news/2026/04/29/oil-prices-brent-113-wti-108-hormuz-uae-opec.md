# Oil surges: Brent above $113, WTI at $108 as Hormuz tensions rise and UAE exits OPEC

Oil prices have surged again, with Brent trading above $113 and WTI above $108 per barrel, marking the highest levels in the past 7 to 12 days, following an early April peak when WTI reached $116 and Brent $114.

## Hormuz as the epicenter of global risk

The primary driver behind this surge is the escalation of tensions around the Strait of Hormuz, one of the most critical global routes for oil transportation. Any threat to this corridor has a direct impact on global energy supply and pricing.

Increasing reports suggest the possibility of a prolonged disruption, further fueling market anxiety and pushing prices higher.

{{< support1 >}}

## UAE exits OPEC: an additional shock

Adding another layer of uncertainty, the United Arab Emirates has decided to leave OPEC and OPEC+. This is one of the key members of the cartel and a major oil producer.

The decision comes at a time when global markets are already under pressure from geopolitical tensions and supply disruptions.

By exiting OPEC, the UAE gains the ability to increase production beyond agreed quotas, which could increase supply in the long term but also destabilize market expectations.

At the same time, this move weakens OPEC’s influence and highlights growing internal tensions within the organization.

## Market reaction: oil, inflation and uncertainty

The rise in oil prices is already having broader consequences. Brent above $113 and WTI above $108 are increasing inflationary pressure and impacting the global economy.

Oil has risen more than 6% in a short period, and analysts increasingly warn that the market is no longer viewing this as a short-term shock, but as a potential long-term supply disruption.

Further escalation could reduce global supply even more and push prices higher.

Such energy shocks directly impact transportation, production, and consumer costs, adding further strain on markets.

{{< support2 >}}

## FED under pressure

Investors are now closely watching the Federal Reserve (FED – the United States central bank), which must balance inflation control with economic stability.

Rising oil prices make this task more difficult and could influence future interest rate decisions.

Overall, the situation shows that energy is once again becoming one of the key drivers of global markets, while geopolitics and monetary policy together define the direction of the global economy.
