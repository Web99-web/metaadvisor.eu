# Bitcoin holds between $75K and $77K as oil rises and institutional capital moves slower than expected

Bitcoin is currently trading in a tight range between $75,000 and $77,000, with occasional brief dips below the lower level but without strong selling pressure. On the surface, the market appears calm, yet significant structural changes are taking place behind the scenes.

## Institutional buying as the main driver

According to a Bitwise analysis, Bitcoin’s rally since February has not been driven by retail investors, but by consistent buying from Strategy. Over the past eight weeks, the company has acquired approximately $7.2 billion worth of Bitcoin, making it the dominant force behind recent price action.

Bitcoin has gained roughly 20% since February, rising from around $62,800 to current levels. Strategy now holds more than 818,000 BTC, making it the largest publicly listed corporate holder of Bitcoin. Its model relies on issuing perpetual preferred stock (STRC) and converting raised capital directly into Bitcoin.

## Institutional capital is coming — but slowly

The entry of major financial institutions into spot Bitcoin ETFs has raised expectations across the market. However, according to Adam Back, CEO of Blockstream, the pace of institutional adoption is much slower than many anticipate.

While allocations of 2% to 4% in Bitcoin are being recommended, many fund managers have yet to implement these strategies. Back estimates that full adoption could take anywhere from one year to 18 months.

In other words, institutional capital is coming — but gradually.

## Market under pressure from macro factors

While Bitcoin shows resilience, the broader crypto market is weaker. Ether, XRP, and Solana are declining, while Bitcoin dominance is increasing — a typical pattern during periods of macro uncertainty.

However, the most important factors are coming from outside the crypto space.

Geopolitical tensions around the Hormuz Strait have intensified following reports of a potential extended U.S. naval blockade of this critical oil route. As one of the world’s most important energy corridors, any disruption has direct global economic consequences.

## Oil rising, FED in focus

Markets reacted quickly. Brent crude moved above $111 per barrel, while WTI surpassed $106, increasing inflation pressure and uncertainty across global markets.

Investors are now closely watching the Federal Reserve (FED – the United States central bank), whose decisions on interest rates and monetary policy will directly influence liquidity conditions.

Overall, the situation highlights a shift in the crypto market. Bitcoin is no longer moving purely on hype or retail sentiment, but on structured institutional demand and broader macroeconomic forces.
