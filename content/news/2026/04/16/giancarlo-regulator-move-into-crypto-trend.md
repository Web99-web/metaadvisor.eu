# Christopher Giancarlo, former CFTC chair known as “Crypto Dad”, moves into the crypto industry he once regulated — a trend gaining momentum

Christopher Giancarlo, former chairman of the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), has announced that he is stepping away from the legal profession to fully focus on advising crypto and fintech companies.

He is widely regarded as one of the most prominent regulators who supported the early development of the crypto market, and his move clearly reflects how much the industry has evolved in recent years.

## From regulator to advisor

Giancarlo served as a member of the CFTC from 2014 and as its chairman between 2017 and 2018 during the administration of Donald Trump.

During that time, he oversaw the approval of the first Bitcoin futures markets in the United States, marking a key milestone in the institutionalization of the crypto sector.

{{< support1 >}}

## “Crypto Dad” and early market support

Throughout his tenure, Giancarlo was one of the few regulators who openly advocated for a better understanding and development of cryptocurrencies.

This approach earned him the nickname “Crypto Dad”, as he sought to balance investor protection with innovation.

## The broader trend: regulators moving into industry

Such transitions are not an exception, but rather a pattern — former regulators frequently move into industries they once oversaw.

The crypto sector is becoming increasingly attractive because it requires exactly what these individuals bring: knowledge of regulation, global markets, and institutional processes.

{{< support2 >}}

## Experience as a new currency

For crypto companies, these moves carry clear value. A former regulator brings insight into laws, networks of contacts, and an understanding of how the system operates from the inside.

In an industry still searching for stable regulatory frameworks, this can become a decisive advantage for growth and expansion.

## Conclusion

Giancarlo’s move is not an isolated case, but part of a broader shift in which the lines between regulation and industry are becoming increasingly blurred.

Crypto is no longer an experiment — it is evolving into a system that now includes those who once regulated it.
