# WhatsApp under pressure: Musk and Pavel comment on claims of possible data leaks

The debate around privacy on digital platforms has gained new momentum in recent days following claims of potential access to WhatsApp messages outside of the encrypted system. The topic has been further amplified by public comments from Elon Musk and Pavel Durov, who each in their own way questioned the actual level of user data protection.

## Musk: short message, strong impact

<p style="text-align:center;">
  <img src="/images/news/Whatsapp-Musk.png" alt="Elon Musk WhatsApp privacy comment" style="max-width:100%; border-radius:10px;" />
</p>

Elon Musk stated in a brief post that WhatsApp cannot be trusted, without providing further explanation. Despite its simplicity, the message triggered strong reactions and quickly spread across the platform.

Statements like these often have a significant impact due to the influence and reach of individuals involved in the tech space.

Pavel Durov, founder of Telegram, has for some time been pointing out potential weaknesses in how WhatsApp handles data. In his statements, he emphasizes that the issue may not lie in encryption itself, but in the ways data can leave that system.

In the context of current claims, attention once again turns to topics such as:
- data access through specific processes  
- potential involvement of external partners  
- the broader ecosystem in which the application operates  

## Privacy beyond encryption

The discussion is increasingly shifting from the question “Is encryption in place?” to “Where can data ultimately end up?”

Although WhatsApp uses end-to-end encryption, there are scenarios in which communication may leave that protected environment, such as through:
- cloud backup systems  
- content reporting features  
- additional functionalities that involve data processing

## Legal dimension: the case in court

Additional weight is given to the topic by the filing of a so-called *class action lawsuit* in the United States, in which claims are made that under certain circumstances access to communications may be possible beyond the expected privacy framework.

This is a collective lawsuit filed on behalf of a broader group of users, with the exact number of participants not publicly defined at this stage. Such cases often begin with a smaller number of plaintiffs but can expand significantly if the claims are substantiated during legal proceedings.

It is important to emphasize that these are allegations that have not yet been proven in court. However, the mere existence of the case has already intensified public discussion around digital privacy.

In this context, the issue is no longer reduced to the simplified claim that someone is “reading messages,” but rather to understanding a broader system in which multiple points exist where communication can leave the encrypted environment.

This topic is not new and has been developing for months, and we have already covered it in more detail in a previous article.

## Conclusion

The comments from Musk and Durov are not part of a direct conflict, but rather reflect a broader issue that is increasingly coming into focus — trust in how platforms manage private data.

As technology becomes more complex, the boundaries of privacy become less clear, and users are increasingly demanding transparency.
