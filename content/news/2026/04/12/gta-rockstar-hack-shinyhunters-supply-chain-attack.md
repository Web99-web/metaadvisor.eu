# GTA (Grand Theft Auto) under hacker attack: Rockstar Games given until April 14 to pay

Will we get to see **GTA VI**, or will hackers change the plans? This question no longer sounds like a theory, but a realistic scenario following the latest security incident that hit **Rockstar Games**.

At first glance, it looks like just another hack of a major company. But this case reveals a much deeper issue — how modern companies operate and how dependent they are on external partners. **Rockstar Games** was not hacked directly. The attack happened through a supplier, which was enough to compromise the entire system.

## An attack that changes the rules of the game

Behind the attack is the hacker group **ShinyHunters**, active since **2019**, known for data theft and the “pay or leak” extortion model. Over the years, they have been linked to attacks on **Microsoft**, **Cisco**, **Ticketmaster**, and even institutions within the **European Union**.

It is especially important to note that **ShinyHunters** were linked just two weeks ago to a cyberattack on the EU, which we analyzed in the article <a href="https://metaadvisor.eu/news/eu-hack-trufflehog-supply-chain-attack/" style="color:inherit; text-decoration:none;">EU hacked through a security tool: how a single download opened the door to an attack</a>. This shows that this is not an isolated incident, but part of a repeating pattern.

## Supply chain attacks as the new reality

This was not a traditional attack. Instead of directly breaching defenses, access was gained through an external partner. These types of attacks, known as “supply chain attacks,” are becoming increasingly common because they target the weakest link in the system.

In practice, this means a company can have strong internal security but still be vulnerable if one of its partners is not equally protected. That is exactly what happened in this case, where hackers gained access using legitimate credentials.

## Deadline set for **April 14, 2026**

The situation escalates further due to the ultimatum. **ShinyHunters** have set a deadline of **April 14, 2026**. If the ransom is not paid, they threaten to release the data.

Such deadlines create pressure not only on the company but on the entire market. Each passing day increases uncertainty, especially for projects like GTA VI, which is one of the most anticipated products in the world of entertainment.

## A problem bigger than Rockstar

This incident highlights a broader issue that goes beyond a single company. Modern digital systems are built on interconnected dependencies. AI relies on APIs, crypto on infrastructure, and corporations on SaaS tools and external partners.

This means security is no longer a matter of a single company, but of an entire ecosystem. One weak link can be enough to compromise the whole system.

## Conclusion

The attack on **Rockstar Games** shows that the rules of security are changing. It is no longer about how strong a single company is, but how secure its entire chain of partners is.

GTA VI may not be directly at risk, but this incident clearly shows how even the largest projects can be vulnerable in a connected digital world.
