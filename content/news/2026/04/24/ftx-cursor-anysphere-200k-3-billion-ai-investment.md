# FTX sold its stake in Cursor for $200,000 — today it would be worth $3 billion

## One of the most expensive “neutral” decisions in crypto

In the world of investing, scenarios like this are not uncommon: you buy something, sell it for the same price — and only later realize you missed out on billions.

That is exactly what happened to FTX and its stake in an AI startup that is now worth tens of billions.

<img src="/images/companies/Cursor-AI-Anysphere.png" alt="Cursor AI Anysphere former FTX stake" />
<p><em>Cursor AI (Anysphere) — one of the fastest-growing AI coding tools</em></p>

The company in question is Anysphere, which develops Cursor — an AI-powered coding tool.

## How FTX entered — and exited

Back in 2022, Alameda Research, a firm affiliated with FTX, invested around **$200,000** in Anysphere.

For that amount, they received approximately **a 5% stake**, at a valuation of about $4 million.

Then came the familiar scenario.

The collapse of FTX in 2022 triggered bankruptcy proceedings, and assets began to be sold to repay creditors.

In 2023, that same stake was sold for:

👉 **$200,000**

No profit. No loss. Just an exit.

## The AI boom changed everything

In the meantime, the artificial intelligence market exploded.

Cursor became one of the fastest-growing tools for developers, and interest from major players pushed its valuation even higher.

According to available information, SpaceX has the option to acquire the company at a valuation of:

👉 **$60 billion**

This means that FTX’s stake would today be worth approximately:

👉 **$3 billion**

The gap between the sale price and the current valuation is estimated at:

👉 **15,000x**

## Not a bad trade — but bad timing

This is not a typical story of a bad investment.

FTX did not lose money because of a poor decision.

👉 it lost time

At the moment the stake was sold, the crypto market was in collapse, and liquidation was necessary.

The AI boom came later — and completely changed the outcome.

## Does this raise questions about the bankruptcy process?

This case raises an uncomfortable question:

👉 Did the FTX bankruptcy estate sell assets too early?

The goal of such processes is to maximize returns for creditors. In this case, however, potential billions in value ended up in the hands of a buyer from the bankruptcy sale.

It is also notable that Sam Bankman-Fried has been arguing for some time that FTX had more value than was presented — and that part of that value was lost through rapid asset sales.

## AI vs crypto: a clash of cycles

This story is not just about FTX.

It reflects a broader pattern:

* 2022 → crypto market collapse
* 2023–2026 → AI industry explosion

The same asset can have completely different values depending on the cycle.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

* FTX missed a potential 15,000x return
* Cursor (Anysphere) is emerging as a key player in AI coding tools
* Bankruptcy sales may have destroyed billions in value
* The AI boom completely reshaped the perception of the investment
* This case shows how critical timing is in technology and finance
