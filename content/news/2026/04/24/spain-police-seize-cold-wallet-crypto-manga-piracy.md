# Spanish police seize crypto cold wallets in manga piracy crackdown

## Operation targets a network that generated millions over the years

Spanish authorities have dismantled a large network responsible for the illegal distribution of manga content, which according to available information had been operating since 2014. During the operation, crypto cold wallets were also seized, preventing further access to significant amounts of digital funds.

This is one of the larger actions of its kind and highlights how digital piracy and cryptocurrencies are increasingly interconnected. Although the content was distributed globally, the operation was carried out in Spain, allowing authorities to identify those responsible and seize both physical devices and digital assets.

## What is manga — and why piracy is so widespread

Manga is a Japanese form of comics and graphic novels with a massive global audience. Many series are released in chapters and attract millions of readers worldwide.

Because of this popularity, manga has become one of the most common targets of piracy. Illegal platforms publish scanned and translated versions without authorization from authors or publishers, offering free access to content that would normally be paid.

These platforms often generate revenue through advertising, donations, or subscriptions, and part of that income is converted into cryptocurrency to make tracking financial flows more difficult.

## A cold wallet is not “invisible”

A key element of the operation was the seizure of so-called cold wallets — devices used for offline storage of cryptocurrencies, often considered more secure because they are not connected to the internet.

However, this case demonstrates that even such protection is not sufficient if authorities gain physical access to the devices. Once seized, the assets are no longer under the control of their owners, regardless of their presence on the blockchain.

This sends a clear message to the broader crypto community: anonymity and decentralization do not guarantee complete immunity.

## Piracy as a long-term business model

According to available data, this network operated for more than a decade, generating millions in revenue through illegal content distribution. The combination of global reach and digital distribution enabled a sustainable and profitable model.

Cryptocurrencies were used as a tool for managing revenue — not as the root cause, but as an enabler of such operations.

## Bigger picture: crypto is not beyond reach

This operation sends a clear signal: cryptocurrencies are not beyond the reach of law enforcement. While they offer a certain level of privacy, the combination of blockchain forensics and traditional police work is increasingly effective.

At the same time, the case highlights growing cooperation between local and international authorities in tackling digital crime.

The line between technology, freedom, and control is becoming increasingly thin — and the crypto industry is right at that intersection.
