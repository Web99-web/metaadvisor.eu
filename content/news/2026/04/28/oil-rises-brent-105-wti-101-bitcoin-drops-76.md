# Oil rises again: Brent above 105, WTI above 101, while Bitcoin drops to 76

After several days of relative stagnation, the energy market has moved again. Oil prices have surged, while Bitcoin has weakened at the same time — clearly showing how closely crypto is tied to global macro conditions.

Brent climbed above $105 per barrel during the day, while WTI briefly moved above $101 before stabilizing around the $100 level. This shift marks the end of a short calm period and a return of tension in the energy market.

At the same time, Bitcoin dropped from around $76,900 to approximately $76,160, reacting to a shift in investor sentiment as market participants once again reduced exposure to risk assets.

## Return of energy pressure

The rise in oil prices comes at a time when markets were already sensitive to geopolitical risks around the Strait of Hormuz and potential supply disruptions. Any sharp move in energy quickly feeds into the broader economy, especially through inflation expectations and operational costs.

The $100 per barrel level has a strong psychological impact. Once it is crossed, markets often shift into a more cautious mode, and investors begin reassessing positions in more volatile sectors.

## Bitcoin as a risk asset

Although often seen as an alternative to traditional finance, Bitcoin behaves like a typical risk asset in situations like this.

When uncertainty rises:
- capital flows out of volatile investments  
- demand for more stable or strategic sectors increases  
- the crypto market comes under pressure  

The move toward $76,000 is not driven by internal crypto weakness, but by broader macro conditions.

## A market without panic

Despite the decline, it is important to note that Bitcoin is not experiencing a sharp crash, but rather a controlled pullback. This suggests that the market is not in panic mode, but in a phase of adjustment.

Investors are not reacting impulsively, but are watching how the situation develops, especially in the energy sector.

{{< support2 >}}

## Conclusion

Today’s market action makes one thing clear: crypto no longer operates in isolation. Oil, geopolitics, and macroeconomic factors have a direct impact on digital assets.

The rise in oil and the drop in Bitcoin are not a coincidence, but part of the same pattern. As global uncertainty increases, capital moves away from risk assets and into safer positions.

For now, the market remains balanced between caution and expectation. The next move will depend less on crypto itself — and more on what happens in the world around it.
