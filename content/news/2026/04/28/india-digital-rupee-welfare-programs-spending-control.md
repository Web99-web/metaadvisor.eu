# India pushes digital rupee through welfare programs: the state tests control over spending

India is accelerating the rollout of its central bank digital currency, known as the digital rupee, by using welfare programs as a primary distribution channel. The goal is not only to increase adoption, but also to test how such a system functions in real-world conditions.

Unlike traditional financial innovation, this is not just about technology — it represents a shift in how money itself can operate, including the possibility of controlling where and how it is spent.

## How the pilot programs work

The country’s central bank, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), is currently running multiple pilot programs where portions of welfare payments are distributed using the digital rupee. The system covers approximately $80 billion, providing a large-scale environment for testing.

In practice, this means users receive funds that are not fully flexible. For example, agricultural subsidies can only be spent at approved vendors and for specific purposes.

This effectively turns money from a neutral medium of exchange into a programmable instrument.

## Programmable money in action

This model demonstrates how central bank digital currencies can introduce features that traditional money does not have — namely conditional usage.

In other words, the state can define:
- where money can be spent  
- what it can be spent on  
- and within what timeframe  

While this is currently framed as a way to reduce corruption and misuse of subsidies, it also raises broader questions about how far such control could extend in the future.

## Slow adoption and real challenges

Despite ambitious plans, the digital rupee has not yet achieved widespread adoption. User numbers are growing, but actual usage remains limited.

Total transaction volumes are still small compared to India’s existing payment systems, which process hundreds of billions of dollars each month. This highlights a key issue: users are not switching to the new system organically.

As a result, the government is leveraging existing mechanisms such as welfare programs to drive adoption.

{{< support2 >}}

## BRICS and the broader strategy

India is not viewing the digital rupee as a purely domestic project. Policymakers are also exploring its role in a wider geopolitical context, particularly within the BRICS group (Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa).

The long-term goal is to link digital currencies across these economies, potentially reducing reliance on the US dollar in international trade.

## Conclusion

The digital rupee is more than just an experiment with a new form of money — it is a test of an entirely new financial model. By using welfare systems to accelerate adoption, India is also demonstrating how money can become a tool with predefined rules of use.

This raises important questions about financial freedom and the role of governments in the future of money.
