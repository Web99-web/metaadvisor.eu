# DoorDash shuts down Zesty after just a few months: the problem wasn’t AI — it was where it was used

DoorDash quietly shut down Zesty, an experimental AI app for restaurant discovery, less than half a year after launch. At first glance, it looks like another short-lived tech experiment that didn’t work. But a closer look reveals something far more important: how AI should actually be implemented — and why many attempts fail.

Zesty launched in late 2025 as a standalone app designed to change how people choose food. Instead of filters and lists, users could simply describe what they wanted — for example, “homemade pasta, quiet place, no crowds” — and the AI would suggest matching restaurants.

The app combined data from multiple sources — social media, reviews, and order history — aiming to create a personalized discovery experience that felt closer to TikTok than traditional food delivery apps.

{{< support1 >}}

## The idea was strong — the execution wasn’t

On paper, everything made sense. This is exactly how users make decisions today: they search, watch videos, read experiences, and combine multiple sources before choosing.

AI was supposed to be the layer that connects all of that.

Yet Zesty didn’t survive.

The issue wasn’t the technology or the concept. The problem was that the app was separated from the core product.

Users had to take an extra step — open a new app, explore restaurants, then go back to DoorDash to place an order.

In digital products, every extra step reduces conversion.

## The real problem: just another app

Zesty asked users for something they no longer want.

People don’t want dozens of new apps for every small task. They don’t want to learn new interfaces, build new habits, or jump between platforms just to complete a single action.

They want everything in one place.

That’s why platforms that combine discovery and transaction within a single app are winning.

Zesty was just another app.

And users don’t need another app.

{{< support2 >}}

## Why DoorDash actually made the right move

Instead of abandoning the idea, DoorDash decided to integrate Zesty’s features into its main app.

Where over 37 million monthly active users already are — and where real transactions happen.

This move isn’t a failure. It’s confirmation of one of the biggest trends in AI.

More and more companies are realizing the same thing:

👉 AI as a standalone product rarely works
👉 AI inside an existing product almost always does

Google integrates AI into search. Amazon uses it inside shopping. Meta builds it into Instagram and WhatsApp.

No one wants users to open another app.

## The bigger trend: AI as an invisible layer

Conversational AI and personalized recommendations are most powerful when directly tied to an action — a booking, a purchase, or an order.

When isolated, they lose much of their value.

In that context, Zesty wasn’t a failure.

It was a test.

And like many AI projects today, its real value isn’t in how long it lasted — but in what it revealed: what works, and what doesn’t.
