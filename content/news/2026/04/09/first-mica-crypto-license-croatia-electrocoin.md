# First MiCA crypto license in Croatia — the start of a new phase

Croatia has issued its first officially licensed crypto service provider under the European MiCA regulation. The company Electrocoin has received approval from the Croatian Financial Services Supervisory Agency (HANFA) to operate as a CASP — Crypto Asset Service Provider.

At first glance, this may seem like a formal step, but in practice it marks a significant shift. Crypto is no longer operating only as a parallel system — it is entering a framework defined by rules, supervision, and accountability.

## What actually changes with MiCA

MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets) introduces, for the first time, a unified regulatory framework for crypto across the European Union. This means that companies providing crypto services can no longer operate without a clear regulatory structure.

In practical terms, service providers must now obtain a license, demonstrate the security of their systems, implement risk management procedures, and operate with full transparency toward users.

## User protection and new rules of the game

One of the key changes relates to user protection. Companies are required to segregate client assets from their own funds, reducing risks in the event of operational issues or insolvency.

In addition, users must be clearly informed about risks, while stricter requirements are imposed on system security and access management. In practice, this leads to greater transparency — but less room for the “grey zone” that previously existed.

## Crypto moves out of the grey zone

Until now, a large part of the crypto market operated outside traditional financial rules. With MiCA, that space is closing. Crypto becomes part of the official financial system, under supervision and clearly defined requirements.

This does not mean the end of innovation — but it does mark the end of a fully unregulated environment.

{{< support2 >}}

## Electrocoin as the first licensed player

It is important to highlight that Electrocoin is not a new company. Founded in 2014, it has been providing crypto buying and selling services for years, as well as enabling real-world use through systems such as PayCek and partnerships with entities like the Croatian Post.

In other words, what is now being licensed already existed in the market. The difference is that it is now operating within an official regulatory framework.

## What this means for the market

This step marks a transition from an experimental phase into institutionalization. Crypto is no longer just technology or an alternative system — it is becoming part of financial infrastructure.

For users, this brings more clarity and security, but also a shift in how the market functions. A system that was once open and largely unregulated is now being shaped by formal rules that will define its future.
