# Signal: Who is behind the app more and more people are choosing instead of WhatsApp?

In recent months, alongside growing discussions about the privacy of communication platforms, one name has been appearing more and more often — Signal. An app that spent years in the shadow of major players is now increasingly emerging as an alternative to WhatsApp.

The reason is not only technology, but trust. As questions about data processing, moderation, and the role of third parties continue to grow, some users are looking for a simpler model — one where communication remains as closed and controlled as possible.

## Who is actually behind Signal

Unlike most popular apps, Signal is not run by a traditional technology company. It is backed by the **Signal Foundation**, a nonprofit organization created with the goal of developing private and secure communication without commercial pressure.

This means Signal has no advertising model, does not sell data, and does not attempt to monetize users in the same way large platforms do. The focus is simple — communication that stays between users.

## The people behind the project

One of the key figures behind Signal is Moxie Marlinspike, a cryptographer and security expert who developed the Signal protocol — a technology that now forms the foundation of encryption in many applications.

It is also notable that Signal received a major financial boost from Brian Acton, co-founder of WhatsApp, who invested around $50 million into the project after leaving Meta. This move attracted additional attention because it came directly from within the Big Tech ecosystem.

## Why Signal is gaining attention now

Signal is not a new app, but the context has changed. While users previously chose apps based on functionality and popularity, they are now increasingly looking at how the system works behind the scenes.

This is exactly why Signal is gaining relevance. Its model is simpler — fewer integrations, fewer additional layers, and fewer actors involved in data processing. That does not mean the system is perfect, but it does make it easier to understand where data goes and who can access it.

At the same time, discussions about privacy in technology communities are becoming more intense, and some users are already actively looking for alternatives to existing platforms.

## WhatsApp vs Signal — differences in the model

The key difference between WhatsApp and Signal is not only encryption, but the way the entire system operates.

WhatsApp is part of a large platform within Meta, with numerous integrations, business tools, and an increasing focus on AI features. Signal, on the other hand, remains focused on one thing — communication.

This difference may not be immediately visible in everyday use, but it becomes significant when questions about privacy and data control arise.

## What this shift tells us

Growing interest in Signal does not necessarily mean users will leave WhatsApp en masse. However, it does show that perception is changing.

Users today are not only looking at the app they use, but also at the system behind it. And it is precisely in that space, between technology and trust, that Signal is finding its role.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

Signal’s rise is not accidental. Its model fits a moment when users are beginning to question privacy, transparency, and the role of large platforms. Whether this leads to a real shift or remains at the level of discussion will depend on how willing users are to change their habits.
