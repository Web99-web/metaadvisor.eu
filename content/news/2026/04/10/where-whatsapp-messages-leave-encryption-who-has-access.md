# Where WhatsApp Messages Can Leave Encryption — and Who Sees Them

WhatsApp has for years emphasized that it uses end-to-end encryption, meaning that messages can only be read by the sender and the recipient. This model has become the standard and one of the key reasons users trust the platform, while **Meta**, as the owner, claims it has no access to message content and cannot decrypt it.

This is technically correct, but the discussion in 2025 and 2026 is increasingly shifting from whether encryption exists to when and where messages can leave that protected environment.

## Where communication leaves encryption

One of the key points is the reporting function. When a user reports a message or a conversation, part of that communication is sent to the system along with technical data, meaning the content leaves the strictly private context.

According to publicly available information and how the feature is described, when clicking “Report,” the user gives consent to send part of the conversation for review, usually through a brief in-app notice. While this is not a hidden process, in practice most users are not fully aware of how much specific data they are actually sharing. It is important to note that this description is based on available information and may vary depending on the app version and user settings.

Such content can be processed by automated systems, but also by human moderators, and in certain cases by external partners involved in operational processes. It is possible that many users were not aware that, in certain situations, their communication could be reviewed outside the platform itself. This is where the company Accenture enters the picture — a global operational system used by some Big Tech companies.

## Who Accenture really is

In this context, the company **Accenture** appears, often perceived by the public as unknown or relatively small. In reality, it is a global outsourcing system headquartered in Ireland, but operating worldwide.

**Accenture** employs between 780,000 and 800,000 people across more than 120 countries, including large operational centers in India, the Philippines, Europe, and the United States. By comparison, **Google** has around 182,000 employees, while **Meta** employs between 67,000 and 70,000 people. This means that **Accenture** has significantly more employees than those two companies combined. The reason lies in its business model — while tech companies build products, Accenture provides operational services and works for a large number of clients, including major technology companies.

## Perception of privacy and system reality

It is important to emphasize that not all **Accenture** employees work on projects related to **Meta** or WhatsApp, nor do they have access to user data. However, the fact that certain processes involve large, globally distributed teams raises questions about the perception of privacy. In any system where a large number of people are involved, the need for clear rules, controls, and transparency increases.

The larger the number of individuals involved in any data processing system, the greater the need for transparency, clear guidelines, and oversight, especially when sensitive information is involved.

## AI, cloud and new access points

Beyond reporting, additional points include AI features and cloud backups. As artificial intelligence tools are introduced, questions arise regarding data access and how information is processed, while backups through services such as Google Drive or iCloud further expand the ecosystem.

In technology and security communities, including discussions on Discord, Reddit, X, and Mastodon, there has been a growing intensity of debate in recent months about the real level of privacy on communication platforms. Some no longer seem to view WhatsApp as a fully trustworthy platform.

## Legal dimension: class action lawsuit

The topic has gained additional attention following the filing of a class action lawsuit in the United States, in which claims are made that under certain circumstances access to communications may be possible. This is a so-called **class action lawsuit**, filed on behalf of a broader group of users, with the exact number of participants not publicly defined at this stage.

Such lawsuits often begin with a smaller number of plaintiffs but can potentially expand to include a large number of users if the claims are proven. It is important to emphasize that these are allegations that have not yet been proven in court, but the very existence of the case has already triggered broader public discussion.

Ultimately, the issue is not reduced to a simple claim that someone is reading all messages. It is more complex and relates to understanding the system in which there are multiple points where communication can leave the encrypted environment.

This topic is not new and has been developing for months, and we have already covered it, with additional details available in this story.
