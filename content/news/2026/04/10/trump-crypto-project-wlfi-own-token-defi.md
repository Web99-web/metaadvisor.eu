# Trump’s crypto project borrows against its own token: a closed loop inside DeFi

The crypto project World Liberty Financial (WLFI), linked to the family of Donald Trump, has come under increased scrutiny following a series of transactions that raise fundamental questions about how decentralized finance actually operates when large capital flows and connected actors are involved. According to on-chain data, the project used its own token as collateral to borrow tens of millions of dollars in stablecoins within a system where it simultaneously controls a significant portion of total liquidity.

At first glance, this model does not differ dramatically from standard DeFi practices, where users deposit collateral and borrow against it. 

However, based on on-chain data reported by CoinDesk, the key difference here is that the collateral is the project’s own token, while borrowing takes place within a protocol with direct connections to the same ecosystem. This raises questions about system structure and the possibility of a closed-loop model.

{{< support1 >}}

## How the borrowing model works

According to transaction analysis, WLFI deposited around 14 million USD1, its own stablecoin, as collateral into the DeFi protocol Dolomite in early February and borrowed approximately 11.4 million USDC against it. Shortly after, those funds were transferred to Coinbase Prime, a platform typically used for institutional trading and converting crypto assets into fiat.

A few days later, an additional 12.5 million USD1 was sent directly from the treasury wallet to the same destination without passing through the protocol. This suggests that the project used its own liquidity to create a direct exit into fiat markets. The combination of internal DeFi activity and external off-ramp transactions adds another layer of complexity to the overall structure.

## Expansion of collateral and rising exposure

By mid-February and late March, activity accelerated significantly. WLFI deposited nearly 2 billion of its own tokens as collateral and borrowed additional stablecoins, bringing the total borrowed amount into the tens of millions of dollars. Estimates from various sources, including analyses shared on the platform X, suggest that total borrowing may have reached as much as $75 million.

At the same time, the total amount of WLFI tokens locked as collateral continued to grow, positioning the project as the dominant liquidity provider within the protocol itself. This increasingly blurs the line between a system participant and a system driver.

{{< support2 >}}

## Structural risk and concentration of power

The core issue with this model lies in concentration. WLFI accounts for roughly 55% of the total liquidity within the Dolomite protocol, giving it substantial influence over how the system functions. At the same time, the protocol is linked to individuals who have advisory roles within the project, further reinforcing the perception of a closed loop.

In such a structure, the same entity can provide liquidity, use its own token as collateral, and borrow funds from a system it largely influences. While this may be technically possible, it raises important questions about long-term sustainability and transparency.

## Liquidity under pressure

Additional concern comes from liquidity utilization. Some unverified estimates shared on X suggest that the main stablecoin pool could be utilized at levels approaching 90%, meaning most of the available liquidity may already be borrowed. Under such conditions, users attempting to withdraw funds could face delays or limitations.

This does not necessarily indicate that the system is broken, but it does highlight increased risk in scenarios where multiple participants attempt to exit simultaneously. In such moments, liquidity becomes the defining factor of system stability.

## Timing and additional questions

Further attention is drawn to the fact that tens of millions of dollars were transferred to Coinbase Prime shortly before major political and geopolitical announcements related to Trump-linked developments. While the project denies any wrongdoing, this timing adds to speculation and calls for greater transparency.

These situations do not automatically imply misconduct, but they clearly demonstrate how sensitive DeFi systems become when large capital flows, connected actors, and limited liquidity intersect.

## Conclusion

The World Liberty Financial case raises important questions about the boundaries of decentralized finance. When a single actor simultaneously provides liquidity, uses its own token as collateral, and borrows from the same system, the distinction between decentralization and a controlled financial loop becomes increasingly blurred.

For users and investors, the key takeaway remains unchanged: understanding system structure is just as important as following price movements. Because when liquidity tightens, the rules of the game can shift faster than expected.
