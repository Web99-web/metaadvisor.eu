# Pakistan ends seven-year crypto restriction: banks can now serve crypto firms under strict control

Pakistan has made a significant shift in its crypto policy, removing restrictions that had been in place since 2018. The new rules allow banks and financial institutions to provide services to crypto companies — but under clearly defined conditions.

This move does not represent full market liberalization, but rather a transition toward a controlled regulatory model.

## What has changed

The State Bank of Pakistan has informed financial institutions that the restriction on providing services to the crypto sector is no longer in force. Banks can now open accounts and work with companies operating in digital assets.

However, key limitations remain:

* banks are not allowed to invest their own funds in crypto
* they cannot trade cryptocurrencies
* they cannot hold crypto assets for themselves or their clients

This effectively positions banks as service providers, but not as active participants in the market.

## New regulation: PVARA takes oversight

A central element of the new framework is the 2026 Virtual Assets Act, which establishes the Pakistan Virtual Asset Regulatory Authority (PVARA).

This authority is responsible for:

* licensing crypto companies
* supervising their operations
* ensuring compliance with regulatory standards

Without a PVARA license, no company can legally operate in the market.

## Strict rules and control

Although the market has formally opened, the regulatory framework remains highly strict. Banks must carry out detailed checks before engaging with crypto companies.

This includes:

* license verification
* enhanced due diligence
* continuous monitoring of transactions

Particular emphasis is placed on:

* AML (Anti-Money Laundering – prevention of illicit funds)
* KYC (Know Your Customer – customer identification)
* counter-terrorism financing measures

## Signal to the global market

This decision comes at a time when many countries are trying to balance between outright bans and full deregulation of crypto.

Pakistan is sending a clear signal:

* it does not want to remain outside global crypto development
* but it also seeks to maintain strong control over the sector

This model — allowing innovation within defined boundaries — is increasingly becoming the global standard.

## Links to global players

Pakistan is already exploring cooperation with major crypto companies. A memorandum of understanding with Binance includes the potential tokenization of up to $2 billion in bonds, treasury bills and commodity reserves.

This indicates that the move is not isolated, but part of a broader strategy to develop the digital economy.

## Conclusion

After years of restrictive policy, Pakistan is shifting toward a controlled opening of the crypto market.

Banks gain a new role, while the state retains key control through regulatory mechanisms.

This approach could serve as a model for other countries seeking a balance between innovation and security.
