# Students are losing skills: AI is changing how we think

Artificial intelligence is rapidly entering classrooms, but instead of only supporting learning, it is beginning to reshape how students think. According to a survey of teachers in England, an increasing number of students are losing core abilities such as writing, problem-solving, and critical thinking.

Around two-thirds of teachers reported a decline in these essential skills among students who regularly use AI tools. The shift is not just about knowledge — it affects the entire thinking process. Students are less likely to go through analytical steps because answers are instantly available.

## “Why think if AI can do it for me?”

In practice, this means students are developing fewer original ideas. Writing is being replaced by generated text, thinking by searching, and communication by short digital responses.

One teacher described the situation very directly: students are losing the ability to think, be creative, and even hold basic conversations. Others warned that the very definition of learning is changing — from an active process to passive tool usage.

## A digital revolution in schools — but at what cost?

The UK government is actively pushing the integration of AI into education. Plans include developing AI tutors that would provide personalized support to hundreds of thousands of students, especially those from disadvantaged backgrounds.

The goal is clear: make high-quality education accessible to everyone. However, many teachers are not convinced this is the right direction. Nearly half of those surveyed oppose the idea, while only a small portion support it.

## AI as a gateway to the digital world — and its consequences

AI education does not stop at learning. It naturally introduces students to constant online presence. The use of AI tools leads to more time spent online, and eventually to social media platforms, where young people spend increasing amounts of time.

The problem arises when this digital presence turns into dependency. Students become accustomed to constant connectivity, communication, and a digital identity that becomes part of their everyday life.

But that same system can suddenly turn against them. Platforms that attracted them can remove them just as quickly — often without explanation, through automated AI moderation. Such sudden removals can have real consequences, including stress, isolation, and even impacts on mental and physical health. This is already happening to adult users of platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and Messenger, where since 2025 AI moderation has been removing accounts built over 15–20 years. People are often completely devastated afterward.

## Where is the line between support and dependency?

The key question is no longer whether AI should be used, but how. When technology becomes a substitute for thinking rather than a tool for learning, the consequences begin to accumulate.

Students who rely on AI may get faster answers, but they lose the ability to solve problems independently. In the long term, this could create a generation that knows how to find information — but struggles to understand and apply it.

## Conclusion: knowledge without thinking?

AI undoubtedly brings a revolution to education. At the same time, it raises a fundamental question: are we teaching children more — or teaching them to think less?

If current trends continue, the education system may reach a point where technology overtakes the very skills it is meant to support.
