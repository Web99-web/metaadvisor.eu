# Salaries on blockchain no longer need to be public with Aptos “Confidential APT”

One of the biggest challenges of blockchain technology may never have been technical — but practical. Transparency, often seen as blockchain’s greatest advantage, has in reality become a barrier for many businesses and users.

On a blockchain, everything is public. Every transaction, every amount, and every wallet can be tracked. This means it is relatively easy to see how much someone owns, how they spend funds, and who they interact with. While this offers strong security and verifiability, it also creates significant risks for companies and individuals.

And this is where the real problem begins. If a company pays salaries on-chain, those amounts become publicly visible — to colleagues, competitors, recruiters, and anyone else. The same applies to treasury movements, settlement flows, and trading strategies.

For many businesses and individuals, this level of transparency is simply unacceptable and creates serious exposure to hackers, attackers, and scams.

## A long-ignored problem

Although blockchain offers a level of transparency and security that traditional systems cannot match, that same transparency has slowed adoption in real-world business environments.

Companies do not want to reveal:

* how much they pay employees
* how they manage funds
* what strategies they execute in the market

Such openness can result in competitive disadvantages and increased security risks.

This is why many organizations still avoid using blockchain for core operations.

## Aptos offers a solution: “Confidential APT”

The Aptos blockchain introduces “Confidential APT”, a token designed to enable privacy without sacrificing blockchain functionality.

It is pegged 1:1 to APT but adds the ability to hide sensitive data. Using zero-knowledge proofs, transactions can be verified without revealing amounts or balances.

In simple terms, a transaction can be confirmed — without showing how much was sent or how much someone holds.

## Privacy with regulatory compliance

Unlike fully anonymous cryptocurrencies, this solution still supports regulatory requirements.

In cases such as investigations or legal requests, data can be disclosed using special “auditor” keys. This enables compliance with KYC (Know Your Customer) and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) standards.

This approach aims to combine two seemingly opposing needs:

* user privacy
* regulatory transparency

## What this means for the future

If solutions like this prove effective, they could significantly accelerate blockchain adoption in the business world.

Companies would be able to use blockchain without exposing sensitive data, making it far more practical for everyday operations.

In that scenario, blockchain would move beyond being just a technological concept and become a real operational tool.
