# Did Baba Vanga Really Predict Bitcoin?

Articles about what Baba Vanga or Nostradamus allegedly predicted for the coming year regularly appear on media sites around the world. The headlines change depending on current events, but the pattern remains almost identical: wars, natural disasters, economic crises, artificial intelligence, extraterrestrials and major political upheavals.

Similar articles are published frequently or occasionally by tabloids, but also by established media outlets such as The Economic Times, AS and HISTORY, as well as numerous other international publications.

Cryptocurrencies are almost an ideal new topic for this type of content. Bitcoin is associated with money, technology, financial crises and major change, meaning that almost any vague statement about “new money” or the “collapse of the old system” can later be presented as a prophecy about crypto.

The problem is that there is no credible evidence that Baba Vanga ever spoke about Bitcoin, blockchain or cryptocurrencies.

## Why Do Media Outlets Keep Publishing Prophecies?

Articles about prophecies have an almost perfect structure for attracting clicks. The headline includes a famous name, a major threat or promise, a specific year and a question that nobody can answer with complete certainty.

Readers do not need to believe in the prophecy to open the article. It is enough that they are curious about what was allegedly said and whether it can be connected to events they already follow.

Such stories are especially common at the end of one year and the beginning of the next, when media outlets look for content about what may happen during the coming 12 months. The Economic Times published several articles about Baba Vanga’s alleged predictions for 2026, including wars, earthquakes, artificial intelligence and contact with extraterrestrials, while AS regularly covers similar themes involving disasters and the “end of the world.”

HISTORY has published content about Nostradamus and other famous prophets for years, often examining how their old writings were later connected with political leaders, wars and catastrophes.

## Are There Original Records of Baba Vanga’s Prophecies?

Baba Vanga, born Vangeliya Pandeva Gushterova, died in 1996, more than a decade before the Bitcoin white paper was published.

The central problem is that she did not leave behind a complete, verifiable written collection of prophecies with dates and clear wording. Most of the statements attributed to her come from later accounts by visitors, relatives, book authors, newspaper articles and social media posts.

As a result, it is often impossible to establish whether a particular statement was actually made before an event or whether it first appeared only after something had already happened.

The Guardian reported that many well-known claims about her predictions were never documented and that some stories were later developed and adapted for political, propaganda or commercial purposes.

## Did Baba Vanga Mention Bitcoin?

There is no credible source showing that Baba Vanga used the words Bitcoin, cryptocurrency, blockchain or digital token.

Despite that, online posts can be found attributing predictions to her about Bitcoin’s rise, a million-dollar price, a new form of digital money or the disappearance of traditional currencies. Such claims usually have no original source, date, recording or verifiable transcript.

The fact that a statement appears on a crypto platform, social network or news aggregator does not mean it came from Baba Vanga. In many cases, it is a modern interpretation in which an author takes a broad claim about economics or technology and connects it to today’s crypto market.

The most honest answer is therefore simple: there is no evidence that Baba Vanga predicted Bitcoin.

## Where Did the Story About “New Money” Come From?

Baba Vanga is often credited with broad statements about economic crises, changes in the global order, the decline of existing institutions or the arrival of new technologies.

Such wording can easily be connected to cryptocurrencies because Bitcoin supporters have spent years discussing an alternative financial system, digital ownership and money not controlled by a central bank.

However, the connection is created by modern authors. Saying that someone spoke vaguely about economic change is not the same as claiming that the person predicted Bitcoin.

The same statement could later be linked to stablecoins, the digital euro, artificial intelligence, a banking crisis or almost any other financial or technological development.

## What About Nostradamus?

Nostradamus died in 1566 and wrote his prophecies as short, symbolic and often highly ambiguous quatrains.

His texts do not mention Bitcoin, cryptocurrencies or digital networks. Claims that he predicted modern events usually arise when old verses are retroactively adapted to a current topic.

HISTORY explains that Nostradamus’ verses have been connected over the centuries with Napoleon, Hitler and other historical figures. Researchers have nevertheless pointed out that many interpretations appeared only after the events they were supposedly predicting.

His verses are vague enough to be associated with a wide range of unrelated situations. That ambiguity makes it possible for almost every new political, military or technological development to receive its own “Nostradamus prophecy.”

## How Does an Old Prophecy Become Crypto News?

The process is usually simple. A short and vague statement such as “the old system will fall,” “gold will lose its power” or “a new form of wealth will emerge” is selected. It is then connected to a current topic such as a Bitcoin rally, a banking crisis or the introduction of central bank digital currencies.

A headline is then written that creates the impression of a direct prediction: “Baba Vanga Predicted the Rise of Digital Money” or “Nostradamus Warned of a Financial Revolution.”

The article itself often contains no original quotation, document or information about when the alleged statement was first recorded. One publication cites another, that publication cites a third, and after enough repetition the claim begins to look like a confirmed fact.

Readers are therefore left with the impression that a historical figure spoke directly about cryptocurrencies, even though the actual connection was made by a modern author.

## Why Do People Want to Believe Such Predictions?

The crypto market is highly unpredictable, and investors constantly search for a signal telling them whether prices will rise or fall.

When technical analysis, macroeconomic data and expert forecasts produce conflicting conclusions, a mystical prophecy can appear attractive because it offers a simple and dramatic story.

Selective memory creates another problem. If one very broad statement can be connected to a later event, it is highlighted as proof. Dozens of other alleged prophecies that did not come true are quickly forgotten.

The same pattern exists with financial forecasts. Media outlets often revisit an analyst who correctly predicted one major price move, while numerous incorrect calls disappear from public discussion.

## Are Professional Crypto Forecasts More Reliable?

Professional forecasts can at least have a visible methodology. Analysts may use data on market liquidity, monetary policy, institutional investment, ETFs, regulatory changes and blockchain activity.

Even so, those forecasts are not guarantees. Bitcoin is influenced by a large number of changing factors, and models often cannot predict political decisions, security incidents, panic or sudden shifts in investor sentiment.

The difference is that a serious forecast can be examined. The data, assumptions and possible scenarios can be reviewed. With an alleged prophecy that has no original record, it may be impossible to verify either what was actually said or when the claim first appeared.

An analyst’s forecast can be completely wrong, but at least it has a clearly identified author and publication date. Claims attributed to Baba Vanga often have neither.

## How Can an Alleged Prophecy Be Verified?

Before believing that someone predicted Bitcoin, a war, a pandemic or a technological change, readers should check whether an original record existed before the event.

It is important to determine whether there is a direct quotation, who first published it, when it was documented and whether independent sources can confirm it. If all articles point to one another and none leads to an original document, the claim has no reliable foundation.

It is especially suspicious when an alleged prophecy first appears decades after the death of the person to whom it is attributed. In that case, it is more likely to be a modern interpretation, mistranslation or invention than an authentic statement.

A literal prediction must also be distinguished from a retrospective interpretation. A statement about an economic crisis is not the same as a prediction of Bitcoin, just as a broad claim about new technology does not prove that someone foresaw artificial intelligence.

## Baba Vanga Was Not a Crypto Analyst

Baba Vanga could not have commented on Bitcoin in its current form because she died before it was created. Nostradamus lived centuries before electricity, the internet and digital money.

That does not stop people from connecting their vague statements to modern technologies. On the contrary, the lack of precision allows almost unlimited interpretation.

The claim that someone “predicted Bitcoin” must therefore be separated from the fact that a modern author later gave an old text a new meaning.

The topic may be entertaining and attract an audience, but alleged prophecies are not a reliable basis for deciding whether to buy, sell or hold crypto assets. In a detailed analysis, The Guardian described how Baba Vanga’s name has been used for clickbait, propaganda and the retroactive attribution of prophecies that often lack verifiable sources.
