# Bitcoin breaks $72,000 as shorts get liquidated

Bitcoin accelerated sharply over the past two days and broke above **$72,000**, while the rally spread to Ethereum, Solana, Aave, HYPE and much of the broader crypto market.

At the time we were tracking the market, Ethereum briefly reached around **$2,300** before pulling back toward $2,286. Solana traded around **$87**, AAVE around **$97**, and HYPE around **$74**.

Risk-on sentiment was also visible outside crypto. Gold moved above $4,500, silver traded around $67, platinum around $1,803, while Strategy, formerly MicroStrategy, was trading near $115.

But the most important number in this rally may not be Bitcoin's price at all.

## More than $3 billion in shorts wiped out in two days

According to CoinGlass data cited by Cointelegraph, crypto short liquidations on August 19 and 20 reached around **$3.1 billion**.

Bitcoin alone accounted for approximately **$1.65 billion** of those short liquidations.

Cointelegraph reported that Thursday marked the largest single-day wipeout of crypto short positions on record.

Bitcoin reached a local high of $71,992 on Bitstamp before moving through the $72,000 area.

That shows how strongly the speed of this rally was amplified by a short squeeze.

As prices rise, traders using leverage to bet on a decline can be forcibly liquidated. Closing those short positions creates additional buying, which can push prices even higher and trigger another wave of liquidations.

## Bitcoin breaks out of a six-week range

CoinDesk reported that Bitcoin's move broke the cryptocurrency out of a trading range that had lasted for roughly six weeks.

The first major jump came on August 19 after markets reacted to US Treasury actions connected to liquidity and the government bond market. BTC initially pushed strongly through $68,000, before the rally continued toward $72,000 the following day.

At the same time, stronger institutional demand returned.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs attracted around **$517 million in net inflows**, while Ethereum ETFs drew approximately **$189 million**, according to data reported by CoinDesk.

That is important because it suggests the rally is not being driven only by forced short covering. New capital is also entering the spot market.

## Ethereum at $2,300, SOL 87, AAVE 97

Ethereum was one of the biggest winners of the rally.

ETH gained roughly 18% during the move, first breaking above $2,000, then $2,250, and briefly reaching around **$2,300** before pulling back toward approximately $2,286.

Its percentage gain was stronger than Bitcoin's, showing that capital is no longer flowing only into BTC.

Solana reached around **$87**, AAVE again approached the $100 level and traded near **$97**, while HYPE moved to around **$74**.

When a Bitcoin rally spreads into Ethereum, Solana, DeFi tokens and other major altcoins, it suggests that risk appetite is broadening across the crypto market.

## Trader loses $24 million in 12 seconds

An extreme case reported by CoinDesk shows how brutal the sudden market reversal was for short sellers.

A trader who had previously made around **$49 million shorting crypto** reportedly lost approximately **$24 million on an Ether position in just 12 seconds**.

The example highlights how dangerous leverage can become when a market moves almost vertically in the opposite direction.

With very high leverage, it is not enough to be right about the market's longer-term direction. A few seconds of extreme movement can be enough to liquidate a position before the trader has time to react.

## But major profit-taking has already started

After such a strong move, the other side of the market is also beginning to appear.

Cointelegraph reported that short-term Bitcoin holders — wallets holding BTC for less than 155 days — sent a record **43,300 BTC in profit to exchanges**.

That was their largest profit-taking move of 2026.

In other words, while short sellers are being forced to buy Bitcoin to close their positions, some holders who had been waiting months to return to profit are now using higher prices to sell.

That can create heavy trading volume and significant volatility around the $70,000–$72,000 area.

## Is this the start of a new bull run?

It is still too early to make that conclusion.

Several signals are clearly positive: Bitcoin has broken out of its six-week range, ETF inflows are strong again, Ethereum and major altcoins are participating in the rally, and more than $3 billion in short positions have been flushed from the market.

But the size of the liquidations is also a reason for caution.

Short sellers were not buying Bitcoin because they suddenly became bullish. Their positions were forcibly closed.

Once that mechanical buying pressure fades, the market will have to prove that enough genuine new demand exists to keep Bitcoin above the $70,000–$72,000 area.

## The rally is not limited to crypto

It is also notable that strong moves are appearing in other markets at the same time.

Gold moved above **$4,500**, silver traded around **$67**, and platinum around **$1,803**. Strategy, one of the stocks most directly linked to Bitcoin, traded near **$115**.

That suggests the move is not simply an isolated crypto pump, but part of a broader shift in capital across alternative and risk-on assets.

For Bitcoin, the key question now is whether fresh capital continues to arrive after the largest wave of short liquidations has passed.

## Our view

* Bitcoin's move above $72,000 is now more than a small daily rally. More than $3.1 billion in short liquidations over two days shows how heavily the market had been positioned on the wrong side of the move.
* Importantly, the rally did not remain limited to Bitcoin. Ethereum reached around $2,300, Solana $87, AAVE $97 and HYPE approximately $74.
* ETF inflows of around $517 million into Bitcoin and $189 million into Ethereum indicate that there is also genuine spot demand alongside the short squeeze.
* At the same time, short-term holders are already taking significant profits, which could increase volatility around current levels.
* The next real test will be whether Bitcoin can hold the $70,000–$72,000 zone once forced short liquidations are no longer the main engine of the rally.
