# GTA 6 leaker has his own meme token on Solana

The new GTA 6 story is no longer just a gaming leak. A person or group calling itself **CyberLeek** has been publishing alleged GTA VI gameplay footage, screenshots and other material over the past few days, while simultaneously circulating a manifesto against certain practices in the gaming industry and promoting its own meme token on Solana. Who CyberLeek actually is remains unknown. It has not been confirmed whether this is one person or a group, nor is it publicly known how they obtained the material.

New alleged GTA VI gameplay clips and other content began spreading rapidly across the internet and social media. Some original posts then disappear or are removed, while the same footage reappears on other accounts and platforms. Take-Two has issued copyright takedown requests, and at least some of the videos have been removed following those requests. At the same time, users continue reposting the material, making it difficult to determine where a particular clip was first published and where it was simply reposted.

That gives the story considerably more weight than an ordinary internet rumor, but it still does not confirm how CyberLeek obtained the material. For that reason, it would not currently be accurate to claim that Rockstar has been hacked again.

## CyberLeek publishes gameplay and an alleged Leonida map

CyberLeek has been linked to the publication of new GTA VI gameplay footage and screenshots, including scenes involving protagonist Jason and material that allegedly shows a broader map of Leonida. One clip reportedly shows Jason playing basketball, while other material includes driving, confrontations and additional gameplay elements.

Some of the published maps and individual details have been disputed by fan communities, so not every piece of material appearing under the GTA VI label should automatically be treated as confirmed. However, the fact that Take-Two is responding with copyright takedown requests gives at least some of the published content additional credibility.

## CyberLeek has its own manifesto

A website associated with CyberLeek has published what it calls the **“CYBERLEEK Edict.”** In it, the author or authors claim they are fighting anti-consumer practices in the gaming industry and set out three main demands for major publishers.

The first is an end to digital pre-orders. CyberLeek argues that digital games do not face physical scarcity, so there is no reason for consumers to hand over money months before release and before independent reviews are available.

The second demand concerns additional single-player content that is already present in the game files but is later unlocked through extra payment. CyberLeek calls this practice **“fake single-player DLC”** and argues that users should not have to pay again for access to content that already exists in the product they purchased.

The third demand concerns game preservation. CyberLeek says every game with single-player content should have an offline mode or a final patch that allows the game to continue functioning if the publisher eventually shuts down its servers. It cites Ubisoft’s **The Crew** as an example of a game that became inaccessible even to users who had previously purchased it after the servers were shut down.

## CyberLeek threatens more “leeks”

The tone of the manifesto is much more aggressive than a conventional petition for gamers’ rights. CyberLeek says publishers that violate its three rules will become targets and that the actions will continue until companies publicly acknowledge the issue, apologize and commit to concrete changes.

At the end of the manifesto, CyberLeek also warns other major companies: if CyberLeek can reach Rockstar, it claims, no one is safe. The person or group says it will continue disrupting companies that, in its view, break these rules and that the “leeks” will not stop until there is a public response and a change in policy.

Then the story moves from cyber activism into crypto.

## There is also a Solana meme token

On the same website, CyberLeek promotes **$CYBERLEEK**, a meme token issued on the Solana blockchain. According to information published by CyberLeek itself, the token has a total supply of **one billion tokens**. CyberLeek also says the liquidity pool is burned, mint and freeze authorities have been revoked, and buy and sell taxes are 0%.

More interesting than the tokenomics, however, is the explanation of why the token exists at all. CyberLeek says it is raising money for a **“secret project.”** It says it does not want to publicly explain exactly what the project is because doing so would give large corporations time to prepare. According to the group, the funds will be used for infrastructure needed for future activities as well as security and protection against possible corporate countermeasures.

In the same text, CyberLeek explicitly insists that the token is **“not a cash grab.”**

## The meme token is already worth millions

$CYBERLEEK very quickly turned from a side detail into a separate crypto story. According to data tracking the token on August 19 and 20, its market capitalization was moving at roughly **$1.7 million to just above $2 million**, while at an earlier point some trackers showed it approaching **$4 million**.

Le Monde reported that during the first 24 hours after the initial videos appeared, $CYBERLEEK was among the most actively traded tokens on Solana according to DEX Screener data. Nearly 10,000 people bought or sold the token, with theoretical trading volume of around **$15 million**.

That is an extraordinary amount of attention for a meme token that began spreading almost in parallel with the GTA VI leak story. The numbers, however, should be treated with considerable caution. A meme token’s market capitalization can move by tens of percent in a very short period, and high trading volume does not necessarily mean an equivalent amount of capital can easily be taken out of the market. Actual liquidity can be far lower than the nominal market capitalization.

## Is the motive really protecting gamers?

This is where the story becomes difficult to read. On one hand, the demands in the CyberLeek manifesto are not entirely unreasonable. Digital pre-orders, locking already existing content behind additional payments, and server shutdowns that can make purchased games unusable are real issues gaming communities have debated for years.

On the other hand, an anonymous person or group is publishing material from one of the most anticipated games in the world while simultaneously asking people to buy its own meme token to finance a secret project. That combination is a major reason for the skepticism surrounding the campaign’s real motives.

## Is this a real leak or a marketing campaign?

The story has become so viral that people in gaming and cyber communities have already begun asking whether this is a genuine unauthorized leak or perhaps some extremely unusual viral marketing campaign.

The timing certainly attracts attention. CyberLeek began publishing GTA VI material only a few days before the official presentation of new GTA VI gameplay, scheduled for **August 27**. Some people are therefore asking whether the entire spectacle could be deliberately building momentum just before the major official reveal.

There is currently no evidence supporting that theory. A real Solana meme token in which people are investing real money would make an official marketing campaign of this kind extremely unusual and potentially legally sensitive.

Rockstar and Take-Two have so far announced no official connection to the $CYBERLEEK token or to the person or group behind CyberLeek. However, because their identity remains unknown, it is also not currently possible to independently confirm who actually launched the token or whether there is some other background to the story that has not yet become public.

## For now, it is an extremely unusual combination

CyberLeek claims to be fighting for gamers’ rights, publishes alleged GTA VI material, demands changes to the business models of major gaming companies, threatens further “leeks” and at the same time promotes a meme token on Solana to finance a secret project.

This has already become much more than an ordinary GTA VI leak. The story has turned into a mix of gaming culture, cybersecurity, digital rights and one of the most speculative corners of the crypto market.

## Our view
