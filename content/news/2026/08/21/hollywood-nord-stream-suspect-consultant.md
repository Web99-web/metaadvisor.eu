# Nord Stream suspect arrested in Pula while working on the film Snake Island about Nord Stream with Sean Penn?

Almost four years after the explosions that severely damaged the Nord Stream 1 and Nord Stream 2 pipelines on the bottom of the Baltic Sea, one of the main suspects in the German investigation has been arrested in Croatia. Ukrainian national Vladimir Z., identified in other reports as Volodymyr Zhuravlov, was arrested in Pula on August 19 under a European Arrest Warrant issued by Germany.

German federal prosecutors suspect that, as a trained diver, he was part of a group that allegedly placed explosives on Nord Stream 1 and Nord Stream 2 near the Danish island of Bornholm in September 2022. The explosions on September 26 severely damaged three of the four pipeline strings. Vladimir Z. remains a suspect, and his guilt has not been established by a final court ruling.

But the story has taken an almost cinematic turn. According to reports published after his arrest, Zhuravlov was working in Croatia as a consultant on **Snake Island**, a film directed by Doug Liman and starring Sean Penn and Adrien Brody. The film deals precisely with the events surrounding the Nord Stream sabotage, and parts of the production were filmed in Zagreb and Pula.

In other words, a man whom German investigators suspect of personally taking part in the underwater operation was reportedly helping a film production reconstruct that same operation years later.

## What exactly were Nord Stream 1 and 2?

Nord Stream was far more than just another gas pipeline. It was one of the most important energy links between Russia and Germany and a major part of the model through which Europe’s largest economy received huge volumes of natural gas for decades.

Nord Stream 1 consists of two parallel subsea pipelines about 1,220 kilometres long, running from Russia across the Baltic Sea directly to Germany. The first line began operating in November 2011, and the second in 2012. Their combined capacity was around **55 billion cubic metres of gas per year**.

Gas travelled directly from Russia to Germany, from where it could be distributed onward to other European countries.

The key geopolitical fact was the route: **Nord Stream bypassed Ukraine and Poland.**

Russia was therefore able to deliver a large share of its gas to its biggest European customer without using the Ukrainian transit network. For years, this triggered opposition from Ukraine, Poland, the Baltic states and parts of the European and US political establishment, which warned that the project reduced Ukraine’s strategic importance as a transit country.

## Nord Stream 2 was meant to almost double capacity

Nord Stream 2 consisted of another two parallel pipelines, also with a combined annual capacity of around 55 billion cubic metres. The project was launched years after the first Nord Stream and became especially controversial politically after Russia’s annexation of Crimea in 2014.

The pipeline was completed in 2021 but never entered commercial operation. In February 2022, shortly before Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, Germany suspended the certification process.

Had both systems operated at full capacity, Nord Stream 1 and 2 together could have allowed Russia to transport up to **110 billion cubic metres of gas per year directly to Germany**, without relying on the Ukrainian transit route.

That does not prove the pipelines were built in preparation for war. It does, however, explain why their route had been a source of geopolitical tension for years: it gave Russia a direct energy relationship with Germany while significantly reducing dependence on Ukraine as a transit country.

## What happened before the explosions?

According to publicly available data from Germany’s energy regulator Bundesnetzagentur, flows through Nord Stream 1 were gradually reduced during the summer of 2022. From around 60% of maximum capacity in mid-June, flows fell to 40%, resumed at around 40% after summer maintenance, and then dropped to roughly 20% by the end of July.

From September 2, 2022, the reported commercial flow through Nord Stream 1 fell to zero. Gazprom cited technical reasons for the shutdown.

That happened more than three weeks before the explosions on September 26.

It is therefore important to distinguish two things: **according to the available operational data, Nord Stream 1 was not delivering gas at the time of the explosions, but the infrastructure itself still existed.**

A closed pipeline is not the same thing as a destroyed pipeline. Before the explosions, there was at least a theoretical possibility that flows could resume if there were a political agreement, a technical solution or a change in circumstances. After the explosions, three of the four pipeline strings were physically severely damaged.

## Why is Germany investigating the case so seriously?

For Germany, Nord Stream was not merely private infrastructure lying on the seabed. The pipelines terminated in Germany and were directly connected to the country’s energy security and industrial sector.

German federal prosecutors have therefore spent years trying to reconstruct who organised the operation, who placed the explosives and whether a broader structure stood behind the attack.

According to the German investigation, the group used a sailing yacht that departed from Rostock and was rented from a German company by intermediaries using false identification documents. Vladimir Z. allegedly took part in the dives required to place the explosives.

Another suspect, Serhii K., was previously arrested in Italy and extradited to Germany. German prosecutors charged him in June 2026.

Ukraine has rejected claims that the state organised the sabotage, while German legal proceedings are still ongoing.

## Why did the loss of Russian gas hit Germany so hard?

Germany’s industrial model had relied heavily on readily available energy for decades. Cheaper Russian gas played a particularly important role in chemicals, metals, glass and other energy-intensive industries.

After most Russian deliveries disappeared, Germany did not run out of gas, but it had to find alternative sources very quickly. Imports from Norway, Belgium and the Netherlands increased, while Germany rapidly built terminals for receiving liquefied natural gas — LNG.

That changed the economics of supply. Pipeline gas arriving from Russia and LNG arriving by tanker from the United States or Qatar are not logistically the same thing. LNG must first be cooled to around -162 °C and liquefied, transported by tanker, converted back into gas at a European terminal and then injected into the continental network.

Europe therefore became much more exposed to the global LNG market and to competition from other major buyers, especially in Asia.

## Why can US gas appear so much cheaper?

Comparing US and European gas prices can also be misleading because the prices are often quoted in different units. US Henry Hub, or NATGAS, is generally quoted in dollars per MMBtu, while European TTF is most often quoted in euros per MWh.

That is why a number such as 3.3 for US gas and 42 for European gas does not automatically mean European gas is almost 13 times more expensive. The prices first need to be converted into the same energy unit, after which the costs of liquefaction, transport, regasification and European infrastructure have to be taken into account.

## From underwater sabotage to a film set in Pula, Croatia

Germany issued a European Arrest Warrant for Vladimir Z. back in 2024. He was arrested in Pula on August 19, 2026, and a Croatian court ordered his detention while proceedings related to Germany’s extradition request continue.

The most unusual part of the story, however, remains the way the suspect was found.

According to The Guardian and other reports, Zhuravlov was working in Croatia as a consultant on **Snake Island**, directed by Doug Liman and starring Sean Penn and Adrien Brody. The film dramatises the Nord Stream sabotage itself.

If it is confirmed that he was hired specifically because of his diving expertise and his knowledge of the events being reconstructed, Hollywood would have ended up with a consultant whom German prosecutors simultaneously suspect of having been one of the actual participants in those events.

But the key word remains **suspect**. His responsibility still has to be proven in court.

## Our view
- Nord Stream 1 began operating in 2011/2012 and could transport around 55 billion cubic metres of Russian gas directly to Germany each year.
- Nord Stream 2 was meant to add another roughly 55 billion cubic metres of capacity, but never entered commercial operation.
- Both systems bypassed Ukraine, which had already caused serious geopolitical disputes years before 2022.
- According to publicly available operational data, commercial flows through Nord Stream 1 were stopped in early September 2022, before the explosions on September 26.
- The explosions were nevertheless far from insignificant: they physically damaged infrastructure through which flows might have been restored under different political circumstances.
- After losing most Russian gas supplies, Germany had to rapidly switch to other supply routes and LNG, increasing its exposure to the global market and higher energy costs.
- Germany therefore treats the case as a matter of strategic infrastructure and national energy security, not merely as an ordinary case of property destruction.
- The arrest of one of the suspects in Pula, reportedly while he was working on a Hollywood film with Sean Penn about the Nord Stream sabotage itself, gives the entire story an almost unbelievable final twist.
