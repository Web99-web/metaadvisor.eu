# Zcash ZEC toward $900: ETF euphoria after a crisis in which it could not be determined whether counterfeit ZEC had been created

Zcash is once again one of the biggest stories in the crypto market. ZEC approached **$900** on Sunday after gaining nearly **74% in just seven days**. Less than three months ago, the picture looked completely different: ZEC collapsed from around $630 toward $250 after the discovery of a serious security vulnerability, and one of the central questions was whether anyone could say with certainty that no additional, illegitimate ZEC had been created during the previous four years.

Today, that crisis has almost completely disappeared from the market’s focus. Instead of questions about supply, the market is talking about Grayscale, a possible ETF, institutional buyers and new multi-year price highs. But there is another important detail: the current rally is not being driven by spot trading alone. The ZEC derivatives market has grown to a scale suggesting that futures, leverage, short covering and momentum trading are very likely amplifying the entire move.

## Just 77 days ago, the question was whether Zcash could be trusted

Metaadvisor published the article <a href="/news/zcash-zec-recovery-420-trust-question/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">“Zcash after the panic: ZEC recovered toward 420 USD, but the trust question remains”</a> on June 7. At the time, ZEC was trying to recover from a dramatic drop that followed the disclosure of a critical vulnerability in the Orchard shielded pool. Today, just **77 days later**, the price is more than twice the roughly $420 level seen then and is once again approaching $900.

It is therefore worth refreshing the market’s collective memory. The vulnerability discovered by security researcher Taylor Hornby on May 29 was not merely a theoretical coding error. According to Shielded Labs, a working exploit was developed that made it possible in a test environment to create an **unlimited amount of counterfeit ZEC** that could not be detected inside Orchard. The problem had existed from Orchard’s activation in May 2022 until the emergency intervention in early June 2026.

The key question was uncomfortably simple: **had someone discovered the vulnerability earlier and exploited it on the live network?** The answer was unknown then. Even today, it is impossible to prove retroactively that the bug was never exploited during those years. Shielded Labs considers earlier exploitation unlikely, but because of the privacy protections inside Orchard, there was no way to cryptographically prove that it had never happened. In other words, **it was not established that “counterfeit ZEC” existed, but it was also impossible to prove that additional ZEC had never been created.**

## From a supply crisis to ETF euphoria

Today, a completely different story dominates the market. Grayscale announced on August 21 that it expects shares of the Grayscale Zcash Trust to begin trading on **NYSE Arca around August 25** under the ticker **ZCSH**. At the same time, the company intends to rename the product **The Zcash ETF**. An important caveat is that the start of trading remains subject to the necessary regulatory approvals, and there is no guarantee that the listing will actually begin according to the announced schedule.

Additional attention came from disclosures indicating that DCG International Investments, an affiliate of Grayscale parent Digital Currency Group, is discussing a possible contribution of approximately **200,000 ZEC** to the fund. This is not yet a binding transaction. The final amount could be larger or smaller, and the transaction may not take place at all. Even so, the possibility of a contribution of that size has become an important part of the narrative surrounding ZEC in recent days.

Institutional concentration of ZEC is also increasing outside Grayscale. Cypherpunk Technologies announced on August 18 the launch of what it describes as the largest active Zcash mining fleet through a **$33.33 million** transaction with Winklevoss Capital. The fleet currently represents approximately **18% of the total Zcash network hashrate**, while Cypherpunk already holds **323,394 ZEC**, equivalent to around 1.92% of the circulating supply. The company openly states that it aims to eventually hold 5% of the total ZEC supply. All of this gives ZEC a strong fundamental narrative. But the numbers from the derivatives market show that this is not the whole story.

## Nearly $10 billion in futures — spot around $1 billion

During ZEC’s latest major surge, futures volume exceeded **$9.5 billion in 24 hours**. Over the same period, volume on spot exchanges was around **$1.06 billion**, while open interest in ZEC derivatives reached approximately **$1.8 billion**. In other words, futures volume was about **nine times larger than spot volume**.

That does not mean there is no real buying of ZEC. More than a billion dollars in spot volume is itself very significant activity and shows that demand in the spot market has also increased sharply. But $9.5 billion in futures volume does not mean that $9.5 billion in new capital “entered Zcash”. Futures volume adds up trading in contracts that may change hands many times during the day, while positions can also be opened with leverage. That figure therefore should not be equated with the actual purchase of $9.5 billion worth of ZEC.

It does, however, say something very important: **the largest share of overall trading activity around ZEC is currently concentrated in the derivatives market.** That means the price rise can be further accelerated by leveraged long positions, short covering, forced liquidations and momentum traders entering after the price has already started rising quickly. Once such a mechanism begins, a relatively smaller shift in supply and demand on the spot market can produce a much larger price move. The same mechanism also works in reverse: if momentum fades, leveraged positions and forced liquidations can accelerate a decline just as strongly as they previously amplified the rally.

## Ironwood solved the biggest problem — but not the past

The Zcash community did not leave the Orchard problem unresolved. After the emergency patch, Ironwood was developed, and **NU6.3 was activated on July 28** on mainnet at block height 3,428,143. The old Orchard pool was closed to normal new activity, while funds leaving it must pass through a publicly measurable **turnstile** before moving into the new Ironwood pool.

This mechanism is especially important because of the possibility of counterfeit ZEC. The blockchain can track how much legitimate ZEC entered Orchard. As a result, no more ZEC can leave through the turnstile than was previously proven to have entered legitimately. If additional tokens had in fact been created inside Orchard, they could not simply move into Ironwood and become part of the new verified circulating supply above the permitted amount.

By August 22, approximately **3.04 million ZEC** had migrated out of the closed Orchard pool, or around **84.4% of the balance** that was there when it was closed. About 562,000 ZEC remained in Orchard. The migration so far has not produced any publicly reported problem with the turnstile balance. That is a very positive signal, but it does not answer every historical question. Ironwood is not a time machine. It cannot prove that, for example, nobody exploited the Orchard bug in 2023 or 2024 and created counterfeit ZEC that later remained inside the old pool. What it can do is prevent a hypothetical illegitimate excess from leaving the closed Orchard pool and becoming part of the verified new circulating supply.

That means we can now say that Zcash has a mechanism for restoring verifiable integrity to the circulating supply. **What we cannot say is that it has been retroactively proven that not a single counterfeit ZEC was ever created during the four years in which the vulnerability existed.**

## From fear to euphoria in less than three months

That is exactly what makes the current ZEC rally so interesting. In early June, the price collapsed while the market asked whether there might be ZEC in existence that should never have existed. Metaadvisor wrote on June 7, when the price recovered toward $420, that the trust question remained.

By late August, ZEC is once again approaching $900. Grayscale is announcing a possible start of trading on NYSE Arca. An affiliated company is considering a contribution of around 200,000 ZEC. Cypherpunk is accumulating ZEC and expanding its mining operations. Daily futures volume has approached $10 billion.

The fundamental situation has genuinely changed in the meantime. The Orchard vulnerability has been patched, Ironwood has been activated and the majority of ZEC from the old pool has already passed through the migration. It would therefore be inaccurate to describe today’s network as though nothing had changed since June. But it would be equally wrong to conclude that the historical question was automatically answered simply because ZEC has once again become one of the fastest-rising major crypto assets. It was not.

## What should be watched next for ZEC?

The first test comes as early as **August 25**. The key question is whether Grayscale’s ZCSH actually begins trading on NYSE Arca according to the announced schedule and, even more importantly, what real demand looks like after trading starts. Announcing an ETF and the capital that actually enters the product afterward are two different things.

Second, the relationship between spot volume and derivatives should be watched closely. If ZEC continues rising alongside increasing spot buying, that creates a different market structure from a scenario in which price is being driven mainly by exploding futures volume, rising open interest and leverage.

Third, the continued Orchard-Ironwood migration remains important. The current 84.4% already represents major progress. The larger the share of legitimate funds that successfully migrates through the turnstile, the narrower the area becomes in which a possible historical problem could have practical consequences for today’s Zcash.

And finally, after a gain of nearly 74% in just one week, it is worth remembering that the market is no longer trading only on Zcash technology. It is also trading the expectation of an ETF, the institutional narrative, momentum and a large amount of leverage.

## Our take

* **Just 77 days ago, the main question was whether the ZEC supply could be trusted.** Today, the question is almost the opposite: how high can ZEC still go?
* **Zcash has taken an important technical step in the meantime.** Ironwood and the turnstile allow the network to control how much legitimate ZEC can leave the old Orchard pool, while most of its balance has already migrated without any publicly reported problem.
* **The historical question has not disappeared.** The fact that ZEC is once again near $900 does not change the fact that, because of Orchard’s privacy design, it is still impossible to prove retroactively that the vulnerability was never exploited.
* **The current rally is not just empty speculation.** There is real spot demand, Grayscale’s NYSE Arca plan, institutional accumulation and renewed interest in Zcash as a privacy coin.
* **The biggest risk right now is the structure of the rally itself.** Futures volume of nearly $10 billion is several times larger than spot turnover of around $1 billion. That means a large part of market activity is coming from derivatives, leverage and short-term positioning rather than only from investors buying ZEC to hold it.
* **That structure can work in both directions.** Leverage and short covering can accelerate the rally while momentum continues, but a shift in positioning and liquidations can amplify a decline just as quickly.
* **That is why two questions should be kept in mind at the same time:** how much Zcash has fundamentally changed since June — and how much of today’s rally is being driven by real spot buying versus the derivatives market.
