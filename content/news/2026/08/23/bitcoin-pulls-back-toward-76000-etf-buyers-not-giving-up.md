# BTC falls from the $80,000 high while billions keep flowing in through ETFs

Bitcoin has pulled back toward **$76,000** after last week’s strong rally, but one of the more important signals that accompanied the move has not yet reversed: U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs ended the latest trading day with another **$307.5 million in net inflows**.

BTC is currently trading around **$76,040**, roughly 1.7% lower over the past 24 hours and about 4% below the recent high near $79,500. After such a rapid rise, the key question is whether this marks the beginning of a larger reversal or, for now, simply a correction within a stronger upward move.

## Bitcoin pulls back after attempting to break $80,000

Bitcoin came close to the psychologically important **$80,000** level on Friday before pulling back. Over the weekend, the price fell toward $76,000, giving back part of its latest sharp advance.

A decline of several percent after such a strong week is not yet enough to confirm a trend reversal. What matters more is whether BTC can hold lower support zones and make another attempt at the area between **$79,000 and $80,000**.

## ETFs added another $307.5 million on Friday

The latest complete data covers **Friday, August 21**, as U.S. ETFs do not trade over the weekend.

Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded a combined **+$307.5 million in net inflows** that day. The largest contribution once again came from BlackRock’s IBIT, which posted **+$239.3 million**.

Fidelity’s FBTC added **+$30.2 million**, the Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust roughly **+$13.6 million**, Bitwise BITB **+$9.2 million**, and Morgan Stanley’s MSBT **+$7.7 million**.

## Nearly $1.92 billion in just five days

The continuity of the inflows is even more notable. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs finished five consecutive trading days in positive territory:

**Aug. 17 +$297.5M → Aug. 18 +$189.3M → Aug. 19 +$517.2M → Aug. 20 +$606.3M → Aug. 21 +$307.5M.**

Together, those five days produced approximately **$1.918 billion in net inflows**.

BlackRock’s IBIT stands out in particular. IBIT alone attracted around **$1.33 billion** over those five trading days.

That matters because Bitcoin’s rally was not accompanied only by speculative trading on crypto exchanges. At the same time, a very strong stream of capital was entering the market through regulated U.S. spot ETF products.

There is, however, an important distinction in wording. When we say “BlackRock was buying Bitcoin,” it is more precise to say that investors were putting capital into IBIT. Those net inflows then create corresponding demand for Bitcoin through the ETF share creation mechanism.

## Monday could provide a more important signal

There will be no new U.S. ETF flow data over the weekend, which makes **Monday, August 24**, particularly interesting.

If BTC falls further while ETFs once again record strong inflows, it would suggest that part of institutional demand is using lower prices to accumulate more Bitcoin.

The opposite combination would be a more significant warning sign: **a further decline in BTC together with a shift in ETFs from strong inflows to substantial net outflows**. In that case, both price momentum and capital flows would begin deteriorating at the same time.

## Our view

For now, the data is more consistent with a **correction after an exceptionally strong rally** than with a confirmed bearish reversal. Bitcoin has pulled back several percent from its local high, but the latest available ETF data still shows strong net buying.

It is particularly difficult to ignore almost **$1.92 billion in net inflows over five consecutive trading days**. One positive ETF day can be the result of a few large orders or simply an isolated event. Five consecutive positive sessions provide a much stronger signal of changing demand.

That still does not mean Bitcoin has to continue higher immediately. After such a rapid rise, a substantially deeper correction is possible without automatically invalidating the broader bullish picture.

That is why, at the start of the new week, it will be more useful to watch **price and ETF flows together**, rather than looking at either factor in isolation. If Bitcoin falls while ETF capital continues to flow in, the picture is very different from a simultaneous decline in both price and ETF flows.
