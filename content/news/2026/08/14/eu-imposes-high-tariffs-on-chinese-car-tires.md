# EU imposes high tariffs on Chinese car tires

The European Union has not banned Chinese car tires, but it has imposed new anti-dumping tariffs that could make some of them significantly more expensive in the European market.

In early July 2026, the European Commission adopted definitive anti-dumping measures on new pneumatic tires from China intended for passenger cars and light commercial vehicles.

## Tariffs range from a few percent to more than 45 percent

The tariff rate depends on the Chinese manufacturer and is not the same for everyone. For certain Chinese plants linked to Hankook, the rate is **4.3%**, while some other manufacturers that cooperated with the investigation are subject to a rate of **24.4%**.

For certain manufacturers and other Chinese exporters that were not granted an individual rate, the tariff can reach as much as **45.3%**. This means the impact on prices will not be the same for all Chinese tires.

## Why is the EU imposing these tariffs?

The European Commission launched an anti-dumping investigation in May 2025 following a complaint from the European tire industry. The investigation concluded that certain Chinese tires were being sold on the European market at dumping prices, meaning prices that the EU considers not to reflect normal market conditions and that cause harm to European manufacturers.

The market for passenger car and light commercial vehicle tires in the European Union is very large, with an estimated annual value of more than **€18 billion**.

## Will Chinese tires now become more expensive?

Very likely, but consumers should not expect retail prices to automatically rise by exactly the same percentage as the tariff. If, for example, a particular manufacturer is subject to a 45% tariff, that does not necessarily mean a tire that previously cost €60 will now cost €87.

The tariff is calculated on the import value of the goods, while the final retail price also includes transport, distribution, retailer margins and VAT. Chinese manufacturers or European importers may absorb part of the increase by reducing their margins, but manufacturers facing the highest tariff rates will have much less room to maintain current prices.

## The cheapest Chinese tires could be hit the hardest

Consumers may see the biggest changes among cheaper Chinese tires, which in recent years have competed strongly with European, Korean and Japanese manufacturers. The difference between a tariff of 4.3% and 45.3% is large enough to completely change the price relationship between individual brands.

That is why it is not accurate to say that “all Chinese tires” will become equally more expensive. Some could remain highly competitive, while others could lose a large part of their previous price advantage.

## The story is not over yet

Anti-dumping tariffs are not the only proceedings the EU is pursuing against the Chinese tire industry. The European Commission has also launched a separate anti-subsidy investigation to determine whether Chinese manufacturers receive state support that allows them to sell tires in Europe at artificially low prices.

If that investigation also leads to additional measures, pressure on the prices of certain Chinese tires could increase further.

## Our take

* **This is not a ban on Chinese tires.** They can still be sold in the European Union, but some manufacturers now face significantly higher import costs.
* **The huge difference between individual tariff rates is the key point.** A manufacturer facing a 4.3% tariff is in a completely different position from one subject to 45.3%.
* **Consumers may not immediately see the full tariff reflected in retail prices.** Manufacturers, distributors or retailers may absorb part of the increase, but that will become difficult to sustain over time at the highest rates.
* **The segment of very cheap Chinese tires could be hit the hardest**, because price has been their main competitive advantage in the European market.
* If additional anti-subsidy measures are introduced after the anti-dumping investigation, the price gap between Chinese and European manufacturers could shift even further.

For European manufacturers, the new tariffs provide protection from competition they consider unfair. For European drivers, however, the picture is somewhat different — less competition in the cheapest segment could ultimately mean **higher prices the next time they buy a set of tires**.
