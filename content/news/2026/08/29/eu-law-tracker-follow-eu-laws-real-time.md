# EU Law Tracker brings the EU law-making process together in one place

The European Union is now promoting more widely a tool that could be highly useful for anyone who wants to know what Brussels is preparing before a new regulation becomes headline news. The **EU Law Tracker** brings together legislative procedures from the European Commission, European Parliament and Council of the EU, allowing users to follow proposals from the first document through to final adoption.

Instead of searching for one document on the Commission website, another in the European Parliament’s systems, then the Council’s position and additional material across other EU portals, users get an overview of the entire procedure together with direct links to available official documents.

**The tool is available in all 24 official languages of the European Union, and the information is updated as the institutions publish new documents and events.**

## From a legislative proposal to final adoption

The EU Law Tracker is designed as a kind of roadmap through European law-making. When the European Commission presents a new legislative proposal, users can follow what happens next in Parliament and the Council, which stages have been completed, what comes next and which documents are connected to each step.

This is particularly useful because the European law-making process is rarely simple to follow across the different official websites. A single piece of legislation can generate a large number of documents, amendments, opinions, negotiating positions and decisions from different institutions.

The Tracker attempts to connect them chronologically so users do not need to know in advance where every individual document is located.

The homepage also allows users to follow new legislative proposals and the latest developments in ongoing procedures, making the tool useful not only for lawyers but also for journalists, businesses, analysts and citizens who want to identify regulatory changes earlier.

## Search by topic, stage and institution

The EU Law Tracker is more than a list of laws. Its search function allows procedures to be found using different criteria, while advanced search makes it possible to filter the results more precisely.

Users can search by the title of a proposal, procedure reference number, current stage, institution involved, legislative event, legal basis and related documents.

This means that someone interested in artificial intelligence, digital markets, data protection, finance, labour law, energy or another area does not necessarily need to know the official title of a specific legislative proposal in advance.

## Trilogue, COREPER and negotiations become easier to follow

One of the more interesting aspects of the Tracker is the ability to follow more easily what happens between the presentation of a proposal and its final adoption.

The legislative story does not consist only of votes in the European Parliament. Proposals pass through Council working parties, meetings of COREPER — the Committee of Permanent Representatives of the Member States — and often trilogues between Parliament, the Council and the Commission, where compromises on the final text are negotiated.

It is precisely during these stages that the original proposal can change significantly.

The EU Law Tracker connects events and available documents to a timeline of the procedure, making it easier to understand how a particular version of a proposal ultimately became law.

## Documents are added as institutions publish them

The European institutions present the EU Law Tracker as a tool that is updated in real time. In practice, information is added as the institutions publish new documents and data connected with a particular legislative procedure.

Registered users can save procedures they want to follow and use available features to monitor further developments.

For anyone who regularly follows European regulation, this can significantly reduce the need to check several different institutional websites every day.

## The EU Law Tracker does not cover all EU law

The name “EU Law Tracker” can give the impression that every European legal act can be followed through the platform, but its scope is currently more clearly defined.

The focus is primarily on proposals that go through the **ordinary legislative procedure**, the main process in which the European Parliament and the Council of the EU jointly adopt legislation on a proposal from the European Commission.

This means that not every type of EU legal procedure is presented in the same way.

There is another important limitation: the EU Law Tracker does not change existing rules on public access to official documents. If a particular document is not publicly available, the fact that the procedure appears in the Tracker does not automatically make its contents public.

## The tool has existed since 2024, but is now entering wider use

Although the European Parliament is now promoting the EU Law Tracker more strongly, the tool was not created only in the summer of 2026.

A pilot version was launched in 2024. The system was then gradually developed and expanded through cooperation between the European Parliament, the Council of the European Union, the European Commission and the Publications Office of the European Union.

During 2026, the project also received additional attention from EU institutions as an example of efforts to make the legislative process more accessible and understandable to the wider public.

## Why should people who are not lawyers care?

Many citizens first hear about a European law when it is almost adopted, when it begins to apply or when the media report that it will introduce a new obligation, restriction or cost.

For businesses, that can sometimes be very late.

Changes involving artificial intelligence, data protection, cybersecurity, platform work, taxation, crypto-assets, energy or business rules can be in preparation for months or even years before they begin to have concrete consequences.

The EU Law Tracker can therefore serve as a kind of regulatory early-warning system. It does not tell users whether a proposal is good or bad, but it can show that the proposal exists, how far it has progressed and how it is changing.

For journalists and analysts, this also creates an opportunity to identify and follow a story while the law is still being shaped, rather than only after a final press release is published.

## From artificial intelligence to new business rules

For the technology and business sectors, proposals concerning artificial intelligence, digital platforms, data, cybersecurity, financial services, crypto-assets and new rules for the single market are particularly interesting.

But the EU Law Tracker is not limited to technology.

Procedures can be followed across a wide range of areas — from environment, energy and transport to consumer rights, agriculture, labour and business.

This also makes it possible to do something that previously required much more time: not just follow an individual law, but observe the broader direction of European policy and see which legislative themes are progressing at the same time.

## Our view

* **The greatest value of the EU Law Tracker is early detection of change.** There is no need to wait until a new regulation becomes major news.
* **For businesses, it can serve as a regulatory radar.** Earlier information about new rules means more time to prepare.
* **Journalists and analysts can follow stories while laws are still changing**, including negotiations between EU institutions.
* **The tool does not cover every EU legal procedure** and does not provide access to documents that are not public under existing rules.
