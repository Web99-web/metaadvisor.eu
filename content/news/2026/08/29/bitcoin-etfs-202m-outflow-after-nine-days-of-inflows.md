# Bitcoin falls toward $77,000 as ETFs record $202M in outflows

Bitcoin slipped back toward the important $77,000 support zone overnight, while another negative signal emerged for the market: US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded their first daily net outflow after nine consecutive trading sessions of inflows.

According to data for Friday, August 28, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately **$201.8 million in net outflows**. This ended a run in which the funds attracted more than **$3 billion in fresh capital** over just nine trading sessions.

The shift comes just as Bitcoin failed to hold its previous move toward approximately **$81,300–$81,500** and returned toward an area the market is now closely watching as a potential support zone.

## Bitcoin returns to the $77,000 zone

Bitcoin began losing value quickly after attempting to break above $81,000 and fell below $78,000 overnight. At one point, the price also dropped below $77,000, before recovering toward approximately **$77,500–$77,700** at the time this article was prepared.

That means Bitcoin lost almost $4,000 in a relatively short period compared with its local high above $81,000.

What makes the move particularly important is the unusual combination seen over the previous few days: Bitcoin had already started weakening while ETFs were still recording strong inflows. Institutional demand through ETFs therefore remained positive even as the spot market began showing signs of weakness.

Friday's data changes that picture. The price is falling, and for the first time in nine trading sessions the net ETF flow has also turned negative.

## ARKB led Bitcoin ETF outflows

The largest individual outflow on Friday came from the **ARK 21Shares Bitcoin ETF (ARKB)**, which recorded approximately **$114.9 million** in net withdrawals.

Bitwise's **BITB** posted around **$49.7 million** in net outflows, while BlackRock's **IBIT**, the largest US spot Bitcoin ETF by assets, ended the session with approximately **$33.4 million in outflows**.

Morgan Stanley's **MSBT** was one of the few positive exceptions, recording approximately **$9.3 million in inflows**.

Total assets held by US spot Bitcoin ETFs also fell back below the $100 billion threshold, to approximately **$97.6 billion**.

## More than $3 billion had previously flowed into Bitcoin ETFs

A single day of outflows is not yet evidence that institutional demand for Bitcoin has ended.

During the previous nine-day streak, US spot Bitcoin ETFs attracted more than **$3 billion in net capital**, while total flows for August remain strongly positive.

Friday's roughly $202 million outflow therefore needs to be viewed in a broader context. It represents only a relatively small portion of the capital that entered the ETFs during the previous nine trading sessions.

What matters for the market, however, is the change in direction.

A few days ago Bitcoin was falling despite strong ETF inflows. Now, for the first time after an extended period, **a falling Bitcoin price and net ETF outflows are appearing at the same time**.

If that combination continues across several US trading sessions, the signal would become significantly more negative than a single isolated day of withdrawals.

## More than $480 million in crypto positions liquidated

Bitcoin's decline was accompanied by another wave of liquidations in the derivatives market.

Over roughly 24 hours, approximately **$488 million in crypto positions** were liquidated, with long positions accounting for the largest share. More than **$360 million** involved traders positioned for further price increases.

This is an important part of the current decline.

After Bitcoin's strong recovery toward $81,000, part of the market increased leverage again and opened long positions expecting the breakout to continue toward higher prices.

When Bitcoin moved in the opposite direction, the decline began triggering forced closures of leveraged positions. Those liquidations create additional selling and can accelerate the move lower.

The market can therefore enter a short-term negative cycle: falling prices trigger liquidations, liquidations create additional selling pressure, and that pressure pushes prices lower again, placing the next group of leveraged positions at risk.

## The $75,000–$77,000 zone now becomes critical

Bitcoin is now trading directly above an area that could determine the market's short-term direction.

The zone between approximately **$75,000 and $77,000** represents the first significant support area after the failed attempt to extend the rally above $81,000.

If Bitcoin manages to hold this zone and recover toward $79,000–$80,000, the current decline could still remain a correction following the rapid move higher.

However, a sustained move below approximately $77,000, and especially a break below $75,000 without a quick recovery, would represent a much more serious deterioration in the short-term technical picture.

In that case, the market could again begin looking for lower areas where new demand might emerge.

For positive momentum to return more convincingly, Bitcoin would first need to reclaim the **$79,000–$80,000** area and then show that it can challenge the local high above $81,000 again.

## Bitcoin ETFs are becoming a key market signal again

Spot Bitcoin ETFs have been one of the most important sources of new Bitcoin demand in recent weeks.

That is why one day of outflows is not enough to conclude that institutional investors are leaving the market. Friday's data becomes more meaningful, however, because it appeared alongside falling prices, heavy long liquidations and Bitcoin's failed attempt to hold above $81,000.

The next several US trading sessions will therefore be particularly important.

If ETF inflows return quickly, Friday's outflow could remain a one-day correction or profit-taking event following a very strong nine-day streak.

If, however, several consecutive sessions of net outflows appear while Bitcoin remains below $77,000–$78,000, the market would receive much stronger confirmation that short-term institutional sentiment has changed.

That is why watching Bitcoin's price alone is no longer enough. The combination of **BTC price action, daily ETF inflows and outflows, liquidations and overall leverage in the derivatives market** can provide a much clearer picture of whether this is simply a short-term correction or the beginning of a broader risk-off move.

## Our view

* **The most important change is not simply Bitcoin's fall toward $77,000.** For the first time in nine trading sessions, we are seeing a declining price and net outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs at the same time.
* **Around $202 million in outflows is not dramatic on its own** when compared with more than $3 billion that entered the ETFs during the previous nine-day streak. What matters now is whether the outflows continue.
* **More than $360 million in long liquidations shows how heavily the market had once again leaned on leverage** after Bitcoin's move toward $81,000. Leveraged positions can accelerate declines when important price levels break.
* **The $75,000–$77,000 zone is now more important than daily fluctuations of a few hundred dollars.** Losing that area without a quick recovery would represent a significantly more negative signal.
* **A return above approximately $79,000–$80,000 would materially improve Bitcoin's short-term picture.** Until that happens, the market remains under increased pressure.
* **For now, the short-term Bitcoin signal has clearly deteriorated, but there is still no evidence that the multibillion-dollar institutional inflow wave has ended.** The next ETF flow data will therefore be especially important in determining whether Friday's outflow was an isolated event or the beginning of a new trend.
