# Trump posts map: Hormuz as "new U.S. territory"

U.S. President Donald Trump posted a map on his **Truth Social** network on Tuesday, August 18, showing the Strait of Hormuz labeled **"NEW U.S. Territory"**.

The post comes only days after Trump publicly raised the possibility of the United States declaring the Strait of Hormuz U.S. territory.

It is important to stress that a map posted on a social network **does not in itself represent a formal change to the territorial or legal status of the Strait of Hormuz**.

## Trump posts the map on Truth Social

The map published on Trump's Truth Social account highlights the Strait of Hormuz between Iran and Oman, accompanied by the large words **"NEW U.S. Territory"**.

Trump did not provide a detailed legal explanation alongside the map setting out the basis on which such a territorial claim could be implemented.

For now, it is therefore more accurate to describe this as **a Trump post and a political message**, rather than an actual change in sovereignty over the strait.

## Similar remarks came just days earlier

Trump had previously said that the Strait of Hormuz could soon be declared territory of the United States.

On August 12, he also claimed on Truth Social that the United States had **"complete control"** over the Strait of Hormuz.

Following his later comments about possible U.S. territory, White House officials reportedly characterized the remarks as a joke.

The latest map posted on Truth Social therefore once again raises questions about what Trump intends to signal regarding the future status and control of this strategically important waterway.

## Why is the Strait of Hormuz so important?

The Strait of Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and is one of the world's most important maritime energy routes.

Under normal conditions, a significant share of global oil and liquefied natural gas trade passes through the strait, meaning that any disruption to shipping can affect energy prices and global markets.

The situation is currently particularly sensitive because of tensions between the United States and Iran and conflicting claims from the two sides over the status of the passage.

Trump says the Strait of Hormuz is open and operational and that mines have been removed or detonated.

Iran, meanwhile, says the strait remains closed because the conditions of an earlier temporary agreement have not been fulfilled.

Shipping data also indicate that traffic through the area remains significantly below levels seen before the conflict.

## A social-media post does not change borders

Although Trump's map is unusual and politically significant, it cannot by itself be taken as evidence that the Strait of Hormuz has formally become U.S. territory.

The Strait of Hormuz lies between Iran and Oman and is an internationally important maritime passage.

At this stage, there is no confirmation of any formal international legal process that would change its territorial status as a result of the Truth Social post.

The key question now is whether Trump's post will be followed by any concrete action from the U.S. administration, or whether it is primarily a political message amid extremely tense relations with Iran.

## Our view

At this point, the most important distinction is between **what was actually posted and what might merely be inferred from it**.

The confirmed fact is that Donald Trump posted a map on his Truth Social network labeling the Strait of Hormuz **"New U.S. Territory"**.

The map itself, however, is not evidence that the legal status of the strait has changed.

With Hormuz currently at the center of military, diplomatic and energy tensions, the next actions taken by Washington and Tehran will matter more than the wording placed on the map.
