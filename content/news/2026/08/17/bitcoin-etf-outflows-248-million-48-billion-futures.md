U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded nearly **$248 million in net outflows over the last three completed trading days**, while Bitcoin, despite a weaker dollar and more favorable expectations for U.S. interest rates, has still failed to make a convincing break above $64,000.

Bitcoin briefly climbed above $64,000 during Asian trading on Monday before falling back toward the $63,500 area. The daily move remains small, while BTC has lost around 3% over the past seven days.

## Three days of Bitcoin ETF outflows

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows for three consecutive trading days: around **$61.1 million on August 12, $131.1 million on August 13 and another $56.2 million on August 14**.

In total, that amounts to approximately **$248.4 million in net outflows**. This does not yet mean that institutional investors have completely abandoned Bitcoin — the picture looked considerably more positive at the beginning of August, with a strong run of inflows into U.S. spot ETFs.

## Bitcoin gets good macro news — but does not rise

The macroeconomic environment has become more favorable for risk assets in recent days. The U.S. dollar has weakened, government bond yields have fallen, and following weaker U.S. retail sales data, the market has significantly reduced expectations that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again in September.

Around a week ago, markets were pricing in roughly a 50% probability of another rate increase. That estimate has now fallen to approximately one-quarter to one-third.

Under normal circumstances, this should provide a more favorable environment for Bitcoin and technology stocks. Bitcoin, however, remains stuck around $63,000 to $64,000.

This weak reaction to improving macro conditions is one reason traders are now paying closer attention to actual demand in the spot market.

## Trading volume falls while open positions remain high

According to data cited by CoinDesk, Bitcoin spot trading volume has fallen to its lowest level in approximately two and a half years, while perpetual futures volumes are at their lowest levels in around three years.

Volatility is also near multi-year lows. At first glance, the market appears almost asleep.

At the same time, total Bitcoin futures open interest stands at around **$48 billion**, while daily futures trading volume is approximately **$25 billion**.

That does not mean all $48 billion in open positions must be closed through a market capable of handling only $25 billion per day. Daily volume is not the market's maximum capacity, and futures positions can remain open for days or weeks.

## Why $48 billion still matters

High open interest shows how much leverage remains in the system while actual trading activity is relatively subdued.

As long as Bitcoin stays within a narrow range, a large number of traders are simply waiting for the next major price move.

If BTC suddenly moves sharply lower, leveraged long positions may begin reaching their liquidation levels. Exchanges then automatically close those positions, creating additional selling pressure that can trigger further liquidations.

The same applies in the opposite direction. A sharp rise can force short traders to close their positions by buying Bitcoin, potentially creating a short squeeze.

That is why **$48 billion in futures open interest is not a signal that Bitcoin must fall**. It is a signal that leverage could significantly accelerate the next major move once the market finally chooses a direction.

## The $62,000 to $64,000 range becomes crucial

Bitcoin is currently trading within a very tight range. On one side, it has repeatedly failed to make a convincing break above $64,000, while on the other, the area around $62,000 to $63,000 has so far acted as an important support zone.

If BTC loses the lower boundary, attention could shift back toward $60,000 and potentially lower levels.

If it manages to break above $64,000 and continue toward $66,000 to $69,000 alongside rising volume and renewed ETF inflows, the bullish scenario would become considerably more convincing.

## FOMC minutes could provide the next catalyst

Traders are also waiting this week for the minutes from the Federal Reserve meeting held on July 28 and 29.

Markets will be looking for clues about how concerned Fed officials remain about inflation, how much room they see for further interest-rate increases and whether weaker U.S. economic data are changing their view of monetary policy.

Bitcoin's reaction could be particularly interesting because the market has already significantly reduced expectations for a September rate increase.

If the minutes confirm a less restrictive Fed stance and Bitcoin once again fails to rise, the question of weak spot demand will become even more important.
