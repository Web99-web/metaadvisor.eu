# Harmony confirms attack: Nearly 4 billion ONE allegedly created

Harmony is facing a serious security incident following claims that an attacker managed to create nearly **four billion new ONE tokens without authorization** – an amount equivalent to approximately **26 percent of the token’s previous supply**.

Harmony has confirmed that it is investigating the incident and taking emergency measures, but so far it has not confirmed the exact cause of the attack, the number of newly created tokens or the amount that reached exchanges.

According to an on-chain analysis published by an account called Juiceberg, the unauthorized tokens were allegedly created through so-called **empty blocks**, while approximately **2.8 billion ONE** were quickly transferred to centralized crypto exchanges.

## Attacker allegedly created 26 percent of new supply

Before the incident, approximately 15 billion ONE tokens were in circulation. If it is confirmed that the attacker really created almost another four billion, this would represent a sudden increase of roughly one quarter of the existing supply.

That makes this incident significantly different from a traditional theft of cryptocurrencies from a compromised wallet or smart contract. In this case, the attacker allegedly did not simply take existing tokens but found a way to **create ONE tokens that did not previously exist**.

Juiceberg claims that around 2.8 billion unauthorized ONE tokens were quickly transferred to exchanges. According to the same analysis, approximately 115 million ONE remained on-chain, representing only around 2.9 percent of the allegedly created amount. The rest may have been sold or could still be held in exchange deposit wallets.

Cointelegraph said it could not independently verify those claims.

## Harmony asks exchanges to freeze funds

Harmony published four wallet addresses it linked to the incident and called on crypto exchanges to block and freeze funds that can be traced back to those addresses.

The project has also **paused its token bridge**, while operators of the Harmony network were instructed to install an emergency software update designed to prevent any potential further unauthorized token minting.

Harmony also said it was preparing an additional patch and evaluating different options for dealing with the tokens that had already been created.

Among those options is one of the most controversial interventions a blockchain can make – a **network rollback**.

## ONE falls more than 30 percent

The market reacted almost immediately to the news.

Cointelegraph recorded a **33.9 percent decline in ONE over 24 hours** at the time of publication, while CoinDesk reported a drop of approximately **40 percent** during Asian trading.

Because of the sharp price decline, the financial value of four billion tokens may look considerably smaller than the enormous token count initially suggests.

At a ONE price of approximately **$0.0008 following the decline**, four billion tokens would be worth around **$3.2 million**, while the 2.8 billion tokens allegedly transferred to exchanges would be worth approximately **$2.2 million**.

Before the sharp price decline, the market value of the same amount would have been significantly higher.

It is therefore important to distinguish between two things: the claim that four billion ONE were created does not mean that four billion dollars were stolen. The seriousness of the incident lies primarily in the possibility that, if the claims are confirmed, someone was able to arbitrarily increase the token supply.

## What would a Harmony blockchain rollback mean?

A rollback would mean returning the Harmony blockchain to a state before the exploit occurred and continuing the network from that earlier point.

This could potentially remove unauthorized tokens that are still within the system. However, such a move also raises a much bigger question: **what happens to all legitimate transactions that were completed after the point to which the network is rolled back?**

Blockchains are often described as immutable records of transactions. A rollback shows that, in extreme situations, that immutability can still become subject to decisions by developers, validators and other participants in the network.

The situation is further complicated by the fact that a rollback would not automatically recover assets that have already left the Harmony network or reached a centralized exchange and been exchanged for another cryptocurrency.

That is why Harmony is now simultaneously trying to stop the problem technically within the blockchain and cooperate with exchanges that may be able to freeze funds outside the project’s direct control.

## Harmony has already survived a $100 million hack

This is not Harmony’s first major security problem. In June 2022, Harmony’s **Horizon Bridge** was attacked, resulting in the theft of approximately **$100 million in cryptocurrency**.

The US Federal Bureau of Investigation later attributed the attack to North Korea’s Lazarus Group.

That incident was one of several major cross-chain bridge attacks during a period when bridge protocols became particularly attractive targets for hackers because of the large amounts of cryptocurrency held in their smart contracts and related infrastructure.

The new incident appears to be different. If the on-chain claims are confirmed, the issue this time is not only access to existing assets but the ability to **create an unauthorized additional supply of ONE tokens**.

## We still do not know how the attack was carried out

The most important technical detail is still missing.

Harmony has not disclosed what exactly enabled the token creation, how many tokens were actually created or how many the attacker managed to sell.

The claims involving nearly four billion new ONE, 2.8 billion tokens transferred to exchanges and the use of empty blocks currently come from Juiceberg’s on-chain analysis.

Harmony’s response nevertheless shows how seriously the project is treating the situation: the bridge has been paused, network operators received an emergency update, exchanges were asked to freeze funds and the project is openly evaluating a blockchain rollback.

## Our take

* **The most important number in this story may not be four billion ONE or the few million dollars they are currently worth.** The much bigger question is how someone could create tokens that were never supposed to exist.
* **In a conventional theft, an attacker takes assets that already exist.** If an attacker can arbitrarily increase a token’s supply, the problem reaches into the fundamental trust in the rules under which a blockchain operates.
* **Harmony is now turning to centralized exchanges for help** and asking them to freeze funds while at the same time considering whether to return the blockchain to an earlier state.
* **That is almost the opposite of the simple idea that blockchain transactions are final, immutable and independent of centralized intermediaries.** In a real security incident, a decentralized network can end up relying on centralized exchanges to help stop an attacker.
* **A rollback raises another question:** should developers and validators be able to effectively erase part of a blockchain’s history in order to correct the consequences of an attack?
* **For now, four key questions remain:** how many ONE tokens were actually created, how many the attacker managed to sell, what vulnerability made the incident possible and whether Harmony will really attempt to roll back the network.
* Until Harmony publishes a detailed technical analysis, the figures of nearly four billion newly created ONE tokens and 2.8 billion transferred to exchanges should be treated as **on-chain estimates rather than finally confirmed figures**.
