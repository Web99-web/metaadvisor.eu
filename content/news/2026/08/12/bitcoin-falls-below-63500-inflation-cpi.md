# Bitcoin falls below $63,500 despite softer inflation data

US inflation in July came in exactly in line with expectations, doing little to increase pressure on the Federal Reserve for another interest-rate hike. Such an environment would normally be considered supportive for Bitcoin and other risk assets. Bitcoin, however, moved in the opposite direction and fell below **$63,500**, erasing its earlier daily gains.

The US Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased **0.1 percent month-on-month and 3.4 percent year-on-year** in July, exactly as markets expected. Core CPI, which excludes the more volatile food and energy components, rose 0.2 percent on the month and 2.5 percent from a year earlier. The data did not deliver another major downside surprise in inflation, but neither did it give markets a reason to revive fears of a more aggressive Fed.

## Why didn’t Bitcoin rise?

Just days earlier, weaker US labor-market data had increased expectations that the Fed would avoid raising rates in September. Following the CPI release, market expectations for rates to remain unchanged stayed around 60 percent.

In theory, that should be supportive for Bitcoin. Lower interest rates, or at least the absence of further hikes, generally mean more favorable liquidity conditions and greater willingness among investors to take exposure to riskier assets. US stocks reacted relatively calmly to the inflation data, while Bitcoin weakened again.

One explanation may be very simple: markets had already expected exactly this result. CPI was not low enough to trigger a fresh wave of optimism, but it was not high enough to cause panic either. Bitcoin was therefore left more exposed to its own market dynamics, which currently appear weaker.

## $63,000 back under pressure

Short-term attention is now returning to the **$63,000** area. If Bitcoin fails to hold that level, market focus could quickly shift toward the psychologically and technically more important zone around **$60,000**.

That does not mean every move below $63,000 will automatically send Bitcoin to $60,000. Crypto markets frequently break short-term levels only to move back above them a few hours later. Still, the fact that Bitcoin failed to react more strongly to a relatively favorable macro environment may concern short-term buyers.

## The Fed is not finished with inflation

Although inflation is gradually cooling, the annual rate of **3.4 percent** remains well above the Federal Reserve’s long-term target of around 2 percent. The Fed therefore has little reason to declare victory or move rapidly toward easier monetary policy.

At the same time, another problem is emerging: the US labor market is showing signs of weakness, while energy prices remain sensitive to geopolitical developments. The Fed is therefore balancing two risks — keeping rates high for too long could further slow the economy, while easing policy too early could allow inflation to accelerate again.

For Bitcoin, this means that a single CPI report that is merely “in line with expectations” is no longer enough to automatically trigger a strong rally.

## What comes after CPI?

The next important US data release is the Producer Price Index (PPI), which could again influence expectations around the Federal Reserve’s next move. If producer prices rise more strongly than expected, markets could increase the probability of a more restrictive Fed stance. A weaker reading could have the opposite effect.

For Bitcoin, two things will therefore matter in the short term: whether the $63,000 area can hold and whether the next round of macro data finally gives buyers a strong enough reason to return.

## Our take

* **The most interesting part of today’s reaction is not the Bitcoin decline itself, but the fact that it happened after data that was not theoretically negative for Bitcoin.**
* **CPI came in exactly as expected.** It created no new reason to sell, but it was clearly not enough to bring in a strong new wave of buyers either.
* **If Bitcoin fails to hold the $63,000 area, attention could quickly shift toward $60,000**, which is a much more important medium-term zone.
* **When favorable macro news cannot push the price higher, that can sometimes say more about the current strength of the market than the news itself.**
* Bitcoin now needs to show that the weakness around $63,000 is only a short-term reaction rather than the beginning of another move toward the lower end of its recent trading range.
