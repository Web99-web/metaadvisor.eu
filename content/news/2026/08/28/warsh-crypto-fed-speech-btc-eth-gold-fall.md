# Fed Chair Kevin Warsh shakes markets: Bitcoin, Ethereum and gold fall

The US Federal Reserve once again showed how quickly a shift in interest-rate expectations can spread across almost every major financial market. After Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh spoke in Jackson Hole, the market-implied probability that the Fed could raise rates again as early as September jumped from around **35% to roughly 60%**. The reaction was nearly immediate: Bitcoin fell from a daily high above $81,000 toward $77,000, Ethereum dropped toward $2,400, altcoins declined even more sharply, while gold, silver and US equities also came under pressure.

This did not look like an isolated crypto sell-off or a problem specific to Bitcoin or Ethereum. Within a matter of hours, markets began repricing the cost of money, while rising US Treasury yields and a stronger dollar reinforced the view that the trigger was primarily macroeconomic.

## Bitcoin loses nearly 5%, Ethereum falls toward $2,400

Bitcoin reached roughly **$81,300** earlier in the day before falling toward the **$77,400** area, a decline of about **4.8% from the daily high**. Ethereum dropped from around **$2,534** to approximately **$2,408**, or about 5%, while MEXC briefly showed a price near **$2,404**, putting ETH slightly more than **5% below its daily high**.

Altcoins reacted even more strongly. Solana fell from close to $110 toward roughly $103.70, a decline of around **5.7% from the daily high**, while XRP dropped from approximately $1.472 to around $1.379, an intraday decline of more than **6%**. On a 24-hour basis, Cardano was down around 6%, SUI roughly 5.8%, Dogecoin around 5%, Bittensor's TAO more than 7%, and Hyperliquid's HYPE more than 8%. The total crypto market value was at one point more than 5% lower on a 24-hour basis during the reaction.

## Gold and silver also came under pressure

The selling pressure did not stop with crypto. Gold reached around **$4,631 per ounce** during the day before falling to approximately **$4,465**, an intraday decline of about **3.6% from peak to trough**. The eventual daily loss was smaller as the price partially recovered, but the sharp drop following the change in Fed expectations showed that investors were not selling only high-risk digital assets.

Silver made a very similar short-term move. From approximately **$70.95 per ounce**, it fell to around **$68.45**, putting it roughly **3.5% below its intraday high**, before recovering a significant part of the loss. The 3.5% decline therefore does not represent its final daily result, but rather the size of the immediate market reaction following Warsh's speech.

## What did Kevin Warsh actually say?

Warsh did not say that the Fed would raise interest rates in September, nor did he announce a predetermined decision. However, his assessment of the US economy gave markets enough reason to seriously reconsider the possibility of another rate increase. The Fed's preferred inflation measure, the PCE index, is running at around **3.7% year over year**, while the six-month pace is approximately **4.1%**, well above the Federal Reserve's official 2% target.

At the same time, Warsh described the US labor market as stable and close to full employment. That means the Fed is not currently facing a sudden economic slowdown that would force it to rapidly ease monetary policy. Inflation remains too high, while the economy is strong enough for the central bank to maintain restrictive policy or, if necessary, raise the cost of money again.

Warsh said the Fed must be confident that underlying inflation is moving **clearly and fast enough** toward the 2% target. If that is not happening, he said the Fed still **"has work to do."** That wording alone was enough to significantly shift market expectations.

## September rate-hike odds jump from 35% to around 60%

Before Warsh's speech, markets were pricing in roughly a **35% probability** that the Fed could raise interest rates again at its September meeting. Immediately after his comments, that estimate jumped toward **60%**, with some market indicators briefly moving slightly above 61%.

That does not mean the Fed will definitely raise rates. It means that within only a few hours, markets nearly doubled the estimated probability of such an outcome, which was enough to trigger significant repositioning across multiple asset classes.

The yield on the two-year US Treasury, which is particularly sensitive to expectations about future Fed policy, moved sharply higher while the US dollar strengthened at the same time. For crypto, technology stocks and gold, that is an uncomfortable combination: investors can suddenly earn higher returns on significantly safer US government debt.

## Why do higher rates hurt Bitcoin, gold and tech stocks?

When US government bonds offer higher yields, some capital has less incentive to seek extra returns in more volatile assets. Investors can earn relatively attractive returns without taking on the risks associated with cryptocurrencies, technology stocks or other more speculative investments, while a higher cost of capital also increases financing expenses for companies.

Gold faces an additional problem because it does not pay interest. When yields on safe US government bonds rise, the opportunity cost of holding gold increases, while a stronger dollar puts further pressure on its price. Bitcoin and Ethereum do not have the same economic profile as gold, but during sharp shifts in monetary-policy expectations, markets still often treat them as part of a broader risk-asset complex.

That is why Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP, gold and technology stocks were able to fall almost simultaneously, even though there was no single fundamental problem directly affecting all of those markets.

## The Fed is now watching the AI boom too

Warsh also specifically highlighted artificial intelligence in his speech, estimating that more than half of this year's growth in US business capital spending can probably be linked to the AI buildout. That means artificial intelligence is no longer just a technology or stock-market story, but is becoming a macroeconomic factor that the Fed must consider when assessing growth, productivity, demand and inflationary pressures.

Massive investment in data centers, AI chips, energy infrastructure, networks and other equipment can support US economic growth, but at the same time sustain very strong demand for capital, energy and infrastructure. The Fed therefore has to assess not only whether AI will raise productivity over the long term, but also how much the current investment boom could influence inflation and economic activity in the short term.

## The Fed wants to tell markets less in advance

Another important part of Warsh's appearance concerned so-called **forward guidance**, the practice through which the Fed gives markets relatively clear advance signals about the likely direction of future interest-rate policy. Warsh believes that excessive reliance on such communication can constrain the central bank itself if economic data suddenly changes.

His closing message was that he is committed to monetary discipline rather than to a predetermined decision. For markets, that could mean more uncertainty and greater volatility, because each new inflation, labor-market or growth report may have an even stronger effect on expectations surrounding the Fed's next move.

## Our take

* **The most important signal of the day is not simply Ethereum falling toward $2,400, but the fact that Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP and gold all dropped almost at the same time.** That reaction shows that markets were not responding to a crypto-specific problem, but to a shift in expectations surrounding the cost of US-dollar capital.
* **The move in the market-implied probability of a September rate hike from around 35% toward 60% was large enough to trigger repositioning across multiple asset classes.** Bitcoin lost almost 5% from its daily high, Ethereum around 5%, Solana nearly 6%, XRP more than 6%, and gold about 3.6%.
* **What makes the move especially interesting is that it comes while institutional capital continues to flow into crypto.** ETF inflows can create strong demand for Bitcoin and Ethereum, but the Fed simultaneously sets the price of capital underpinning a large part of the global financial system.
* **If US Treasury yields continue rising, investors gain a more attractive alternative to riskier assets.** That means watching ETF inflows, liquidations and crypto sentiment alone may no longer be enough to understand the next move in Bitcoin and Ethereum. Two-year Treasury yields, the US dollar and market expectations for upcoming Fed meetings matter just as much.
* **Warsh did not announce a rate hike, but he made it clear that the Fed does not consider the fight against inflation finished.** That gap between what the Fed actually said and what markets concluded from it explains how such a strong reaction can occur even without a formal change in interest rates.
