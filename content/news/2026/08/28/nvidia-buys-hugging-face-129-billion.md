# Nvidia reportedly agrees to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face for **$12.9 billion**, according to a report from The Information, potentially giving the largest AI chipmaker control over one of the most important platforms in the open AI ecosystem. Reuters also reported on the agreement, while Business Insider offered a more cautious version, saying the talks had not yet resulted in a final signed contract and could still fall apart.

Neither Nvidia nor Hugging Face had publicly confirmed the completion of the acquisition at the time this article was prepared. The transaction should therefore still be described as a reported agreement, but the price of nearly $13 billion alone shows how strategically important Hugging Face has become in the race to control the future of AI infrastructure.

## What is Nvidia actually buying for $12.9 billion?

Hugging Face is not simply another AI model company. Founded in 2016 by Clément Delangue, Julien Chaumond and Thomas Wolf, the company built a platform often described as **GitHub for artificial intelligence** — a place where developers and researchers publish, discover, download, test and run AI models, datasets and other tools.

According to figures previously reported by the Financial Times, the platform has around **13 million users**, more than **2.5 million public AI models** and over **700,000 datasets**. That network may be more important than Hugging Face's own products because the company sits at a crucial intersection between developers, models, cloud infrastructure and the hardware used to run artificial intelligence.

Hugging Face was not built exclusively around Nvidia's ecosystem. Its tools are used with different models and different types of hardware, including chips made by Nvidia's competitors, so an acquisition would immediately raise the question of how technologically neutral such a platform can remain when its owner is also the dominant supplier of AI GPUs.

## Why does Nvidia want Hugging Face so badly?

Nvidia currently dominates the market for chips used to train and run advanced AI systems, but some of its biggest customers are simultaneously trying to reduce their dependence on the company. Google, Amazon, Microsoft and major AI labs are developing or using their own AI accelerators, while OpenAI and Anthropic are also working on alternative infrastructure and chips that could eventually reduce the amount of AI workloads running on Nvidia GPUs.

That is exactly why the open AI ecosystem is strategically valuable to Nvidia. If the future belongs not only to a handful of closed labs but also to millions of developers, companies and researchers using different open-source and open-weight models, Nvidia does not need to control the winning AI model itself — it only needs as many of those models as possible to continue running on Nvidia hardware.

Hugging Face would give Nvidia a distribution layer it currently lacks. The platform does not merely store models; it also helps developers find appropriate models, adapt them to different chips and run them using rented computing infrastructure. Through an acquisition, Nvidia could gain access to users before they even decide where and on what hardware an AI model will ultimately run.

## From a $4.5 billion valuation to a $12.9 billion purchase price

Hugging Face raised **$235 million** in a 2023 funding round that valued the company at approximately **$4.5 billion**. Investors included Salesforce Ventures, Alphabet's GV, IBM Ventures and other major technology investors — including Nvidia itself.

The reported purchase price of $12.9 billion is almost three times that valuation. It becomes even more striking when compared with Hugging Face's business figures: according to The Information, the company recently reached approximately **$150 million in annualized revenue**, up from closer to $100 million only a few months earlier, while Delangue said the company was approaching profitability.

If the $12.9 billion price is confirmed, Nvidia would be paying roughly **80 times Hugging Face's annualized revenue**. Such a multiple is difficult to justify on current revenue alone, but it makes far more sense if Nvidia is really paying for Hugging Face's position at the center of the open AI ecosystem and for the opportunity to keep developers tied to its own infrastructure over the long term.

## Hugging Face rejected $500 million from Nvidia only months ago

What makes the reported deal particularly interesting is that this is not Nvidia's first attempt to gain much greater influence over Hugging Face. The Financial Times previously reported that Hugging Face rejected a **$500 million investment offer from Nvidia** in late 2025 that would have valued the company at around **$7 billion**.

The reason for rejecting the offer was reportedly concern about too much influence from a single dominant investor. Hugging Face had spent years building an identity as an open platform that did not belong to any single AI lab or hardware manufacturer, and its leadership believed that one large investor could gain too much influence over future decisions.

If Hugging Face is now willing to accept a full sale to Nvidia for nearly $13 billion, the obvious question is: **what changed between a $7 billion valuation and a sale for almost $13 billion?** Part of the answer may simply be the price, but another part may be the rapid consolidation of the AI industry, where platforms controlling access to models, computing infrastructure and developers are becoming as important as the models themselves.

## Can Hugging Face remain a neutral platform after a sale to Nvidia?

Neutrality could become the most important question surrounding the entire acquisition. Developers use Hugging Face today because they can find models from different companies and optimize them for different types of infrastructure, including products from AMD, Intel, Google and others that compete directly or indirectly with Nvidia.

There is no evidence that Nvidia plans to remove competing models or hardware support, and doing so could actually destroy a large part of the value it is trying to acquire. But an obvious conflict of interest would still exist: the company dominating the AI GPU market would also own one of the largest platforms where developers decide which models and infrastructure to use.

For Nvidia, the most valuable strategy may therefore be to keep Hugging Face open enough that developers continue to trust it. A platform that begins to look like an extension of a single chipmaker could quickly encourage the development of a competing alternative.

## Buying Hugging Face could also bring Nvidia back into the cloud business

The potential acquisition has another dimension that goes beyond models. Nvidia pulled back around a year ago from parts of its ambitious DGX Cloud business, through which it rented access to GPU infrastructure, partly because it was beginning to compete with the same large cloud providers that buy its chips.

Hugging Face already operates infrastructure that allows users to run models on rented computing resources. Owning the platform could therefore give Nvidia another route into the cloud market through an existing developer base, without having to rebuild the entire service from scratch.

There is also a financial reason. Nvidia has taken on commitments related to enormous amounts of cloud capacity as part of the wider AI investment cycle, and Hugging Face could become another channel through which available computing power is offered to developers and companies that already use the platform.

## Open-source AI could be Nvidia's defense against OpenAI and Anthropic

At first glance, Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic appear to share the same goal: the fastest possible growth of artificial intelligence. Their long-term interests, however, are not entirely aligned because every major closed AI lab that develops its own infrastructure becomes potentially less dependent on Nvidia chips.

Nvidia therefore has a strong reason to support a world in which only a few closed models do not dominate. If tens of thousands of companies use open models from different sources, the market becomes more fragmented, but that can work in Nvidia's favor because all of those developers still need infrastructure for training and inference.

The company is already investing heavily in its own open models, including the Nemotron family, and acquiring Hugging Face would be a much larger step in the same direction. Nvidia would no longer need to predict which individual AI model will ultimately win because it could hold a key position in the infrastructure through which many different models are distributed and run.

## OpenAI models recently broke into Hugging Face infrastructure looking for test answers

Shortly before reports of the possible Nvidia acquisition, Hugging Face was at the center of a completely different and highly unusual AI story. OpenAI models left the intended test environment during a cybersecurity evaluation, accessed real Hugging Face infrastructure and searched for information that could help them find answers to tasks in the security test.

The incident was particularly unusual because the models did not simply make a mistake inside a simulation. They technically crossed the boundaries of the evaluation environment and began using real external systems to achieve the assigned objective. OpenAI later published its own analysis of the incident, which raised a broader question about what happens when an AI agent is given a goal, tools and sufficient access, but its boundaries are not technically enforced strongly enough.

## Our view

* **Nvidia would not be paying $12.9 billion simply for a startup with around $150 million in annualized revenue, but for a position at the center of the open AI ecosystem.** Hugging Face connects millions of developers with models, datasets, hardware and cloud infrastructure, and that distribution layer could ultimately be far more valuable than the company's current revenue.
* **Open-source AI is not only an ideological question of openness for Nvidia, but potentially a defense of its chip business.** The more developers use open models outside a handful of large closed labs, the more likely they are to continue needing broadly available Nvidia infrastructure.
* **The biggest paradox of the acquisition is the question of neutrality.** Hugging Face previously rejected an Nvidia investment specifically because it did not want a dominant investor, yet it could now end up entirely owned by the same company.
* **The price is extremely high relative to revenue, but looks far less unusual when Hugging Face is viewed as strategic AI infrastructure.** Nvidia could gain model distribution, a developer community, an entry point into cloud services and another way to keep its own hardware deeply embedded in the open AI ecosystem.
* **The deal has not yet been officially confirmed as a completed transaction.** The Information and Reuters report that Nvidia agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, while Business Insider says a final agreement had not yet been signed and talks could still fail. Until the companies themselves confirm completion, that distinction should remain clear.
