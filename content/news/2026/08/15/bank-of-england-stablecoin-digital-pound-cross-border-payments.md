# Bank of England tests stablecoin and digital pound in the same international payment

The Bank of England is testing how a stablecoin and a potential future digital pound could work together within the same cross-border payment flow. The experiment is being conducted through the Digital Pound Lab, a simulated environment where the central bank and private companies explore possible uses of digital money without involving real customers or real funds.

The project involves NOBO Finance, Dun & Bradstreet and Polygon Labs. The idea is to simulate a cross-border trade transaction in which one part of the payment is made through a stablecoin rail, while the other is settled using simulated digital British pounds.

## Stablecoin on one side, digital pound on the other

The experiment is trying to answer a very practical question: can different forms of digital money participate in the same transaction without one side having to wait days for the other?

In the simulated international trade scenario, an exporter receives an advance through stablecoin infrastructure, while a UK importer completes settlement using simulated digital pounds. Polygon infrastructure is used for the stablecoin side of the transaction and for smart contracts, while the digital pound is settled within the Bank of England's test environment.

The goal is not simply to show that the two technologies can technically work together. The project is also exploring whether international settlement can become faster and simpler, especially for small and medium-sized businesses that often wait days to receive payment after goods have been shipped.

## For small businesses, waiting several days can be a major problem

In cross-border trade, goods may already have been shipped while payment does not arrive until several days later. For a large company that may not be critical, but for a smaller business it can mean that a significant part of its working capital is tied up just when it needs to finance new production, wages or the next shipment.

NOBO was already working in the first phase of the Digital Pound Lab on models exploring how conditional business payments could improve access to trade finance for small and medium-sized enterprises. In the second phase, that concept is being expanded by bringing stablecoins, a simulated digital pound and blockchain infrastructure into the same payment flow.

## The project is also testing a kind of digital credit profile for businesses

Another part of the experiment is not directly about the payment itself. NOBO Finance is developing what it calls an SME Bankable Profile — a portable credit profile for a small business that could follow the company across different financial transactions.

The model combines transaction data, open-finance information and commercial and credit-risk data from Dun & Bradstreet. Polygon provides the blockchain infrastructure that allows such a profile to be used alongside the payment process.

The idea is that a company would not need to prove almost everything about its business again every time it applies for financing. A reliable and reusable digital profile could provide financial institutions with the information needed to assess credit risk more quickly.

## The digital pound does not exist yet

Although the name Digital Pound Lab may sound as if Britain's digital currency is close to launch, that is not the case. The Bank of England explicitly describes it as an experimental environment.

The tests involve no real users and no real monetary transactions, and the Digital Pound Lab is not a regulatory sandbox. The Bank of England and the UK Treasury have not yet decided whether a digital pound will be issued at all.

The current project is designed to explore technology, business models and possible ways such money could function in the future. The central bank also stresses that projects designed by private companies inside the Lab should not be interpreted as indications of future Bank of England policy or as official endorsements of those companies or their products.

## Why is a stablecoin being tested alongside a CBDC?

This may be the most interesting part of the story. The Bank of England is not only exploring a future in which there is a single form of state-issued digital money, but also a system in which bank money, stablecoins and a potential central bank digital currency could exist at the same time.

Bank of England Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden has previously spoken about the importance of allowing users of different banks or different stablecoin issuers to transfer money easily between one another, rather than allowing each system to become a closed ecosystem.

In other words, the question is no longer only whether the UK will one day have a digital pound. It is increasingly about how that digital pound could connect with privately issued digital money that already exists.

## Our take

* **The most interesting point is that the Bank of England is not testing the digital pound in isolation from stablecoins.** The experiment specifically examines whether a privately issued stablecoin and central bank digital money can operate within the same transaction.
* **That points toward a possible future financial system in which there is not necessarily only one form of digital money.** Bank deposits, stablecoins and CBDCs could exist in parallel, with interoperability between them becoming the key issue.
* **For cross-border trade, the biggest value is not simply that blockchain is being used, but whether settlement can actually become faster.** If a small exporter can receive money in seconds or minutes instead of waiting several days, the impact on cash flow can be very real.
* **The digital credit profile for small businesses could be just as interesting as the payment itself.** If reliable business and credit data can be reused securely, financing for smaller companies could become faster and easier.
* **This is still not an announcement that the digital pound is being launched.** The Bank of England has not decided whether to issue it, and the current tests use simulated money with no real users.
* **For the crypto market, the signal itself matters: one of the world's major central banks is not treating stablecoins only as competition to state-issued money.** In this experiment, the stablecoin is one component of a potentially interoperable financial system.

If a model like this eventually moves from the laboratory into the real financial system, the biggest change may not be the digital pound itself. A much bigger shift could be the ability for state-issued and privately issued digital money to move through the same payment system almost invisibly — without users having to think about which technology is operating behind the transaction.
