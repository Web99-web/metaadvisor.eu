# LinkedIn UX: Why commenting as a LinkedIn Page is so difficult

LinkedIn wants companies to be active, follow relevant topics and participate in conversations. Commenting on other posts is one of the simplest ways for a business page to reach beyond its own followers and become visible to people already following a particular industry, company or topic.

That is exactly why it is surprising how awkwardly such a basic task for a LinkedIn Page administrator is still handled.

The problem is not that LinkedIn does not allow commenting as a Company Page. It does, and LinkedIn even says that this kind of participation can increase Page visibility and help companies build their presence on the platform. The problem begins when you try to do it continuously.

## You start Search as a Page, but comment as a private person

The example is simple. You are in the admin view of your own LinkedIn Page and want to see what is being posted about a particular company or topic. You open Search, enter a term, select Posts and start browsing the results.

Even though the entire search started while you were working from the Company Page, LinkedIn puts your personal profile back in front when it comes to reactions and comments.

If you want to comment as the company, you have to open the identity selector, find your Page, select it and save the choice. LinkedIn describes essentially this same process in its official instructions for reacting and commenting on behalf of an organization.

That would not be particularly problematic if the platform then remembered that you currently want to work as the Page. But on the next relevant post, the interaction returns to your personal identity and the Page has to be selected again.

## And then the screen literally starts jumping

We encountered an even more frustrating problem. At first, we wondered whether perhaps only our LinkedIn Page had some kind of glitch.

The issue becomes especially obvious after you have already scrolled a long way through Search. You may have passed ten, twenty or more posts before finally finding one you want to respond to as the company. You click the selector, switch from your personal profile to the Page — and suddenly the screen jumps.

But it does not make just one jump. For the next second or two, LinkedIn literally starts **jumping from one post to another in front of your eyes**. An earlier post appears, then another, then a third. The content jumps up and down through part of the feed you have already reviewed, almost as if the interface is trying to find your previous position again. Only after that short trip through old results does the screen finally return to the post you wanted to comment on in the first place. The first time it almost looked funny. Then we realized it happened every time.

The further down you are in the results, the more noticeable the effect becomes.

In practice, the workflow looks roughly like this:

**Search → scroll → find a post → Personal → select Page → screen jumps → posts start jumping one after another → wait → LinkedIn finally returns to the post → comment.**

This is no longer just about one or two extra clicks. The interface visibly interrupts the workflow and forces the user to wait while the content rearranges itself.

## Is the problem only happening to us?

That is why we initially wondered whether the problem was specific to our Page, browser or LinkedIn account. But when we started looking for experiences from other administrators, it became clear that we are not the only ones frustrated by the way LinkedIn handles Company Page identity.

As far back as 2023, a Reddit user wrote that they use LinkedIn almost entirely through their organization’s Page, yet every time they want to comment, like or share something, they have to manually select the Page again. The question was simple: is there a way to make the Page the default identity?

A similar discussion appeared again in 2025. A user managing a company page described how the selected Company Page applies only to a single post and has to be selected again on the next one. In the same discussion, a social media manager wrote in May 2026 that account switching had become a real disruption to everyday work.

We did not find a public post describing exactly the same screen-jumping behavior that we see in our Search experience, so we cannot claim that every administrator has the identical visual problem. But complaints about the broader workflow — repeatedly switching between a personal profile and Company Page — have existed for years.

## The problem is older than LinkedIn’s current interface

Interestingly, commenting as a company on LinkedIn was not always even this straightforward.

In older discussions, users described methods that required finding a company ID and manually adding it to a post URL just to be able to react or comment as the company. Today’s identity selector is therefore clearly an improvement.

But the fundamental problem remains: LinkedIn still does not provide a simple way for an administrator to say **“I am working as this Page now”** and remain in that identity until they decide otherwise.

That is an important distinction between having an individual feature and having a good workflow. LinkedIn has the feature that allows users to comment as a Page. What it lacks is continuity when working as a Page.

## A LinkedIn Page still feels like a secondary identity

This is where the broader UX problem becomes visible. A LinkedIn Company Page does not behave like a separate workspace where an administrator can spend the next 30 minutes or several hours working on behalf of the company.

The personal profile remains the primary identity, while the Company Page feels like something temporarily selected for a particular action.

For someone who wants to write one comment per week, this may not matter much. For a social media manager, business owner, editor or anyone systematically following industry topics and participating in relevant conversations, the difference is significant.

Every time, you have to check the same thing: **am I commenting as myself or as the company?**

These small interruptions take only a few seconds individually. When they happen dozens of times, they are no longer small.

## For a platform of LinkedIn’s scale, this is surprisingly poorly handled

For a platform of this size and importance, it is surprising how awkwardly LinkedIn handles something as basic as continuously commenting on behalf of a Company Page.

This is not a hidden feature intended for a tiny group of users. LinkedIn is a professional social network whose value is built largely around networking, publishing, reactions and conversations between people and companies.

LinkedIn already has almost everything it needs. It knows which Pages a user manages, knows their administrator permissions, allows commenting as an organization and provides identity switching.

Only one seemingly small thing is missing: remembering which identity the user currently wants to work as.

## Facebook solved this particular workflow years ago

For all the criticism Facebook receives, it solved this particular workflow years ago. Page administrators can work continuously as a Page without having to reselect their identity for every individual interaction.

If an administrator manages several Pages, they can switch from one Page to another and then publish, browse content, react, comment and reply from that Page. Facebook keeps the last selected identity active until the user changes it themselves — either by returning to their personal profile or switching to another Page.

In other words, Facebook remembers who you are currently working as. The user does not have to explain to the platform on every new post whether they represent themselves or a company.

That does not mean Facebook is the better platform overall or that it has no serious problems elsewhere. In this case, the comparison simply shows that a persistent business identity is not an unusual UX requirement and that the problem is not technically unavoidable.

LinkedIn still treats Company Page management more like a secondary mode attached to a personal profile than a genuine, continuous professional identity.

## The solution does not require a major redesign

The simplest solution could be an option such as **“Continue as Page.”**

An administrator would select a Company Page once, and LinkedIn would keep that identity active across Search, feed, reactions and comments until the user chooses to return to their personal profile or select another Page.

Equally important, switching identity on an individual post should not move the user away from their current position. The scroll position should remain unchanged, without rebuilding the feed and without forcing the user to watch ten already-reviewed posts travel across the screen again.

This does not require a major new feature. The functionality already exists. What is missing is better UX around it.

LinkedIn is one of the world’s most important professional communities, and that is precisely why small workflow frictions matter. Most professionals would probably agree with a simple principle: time is money. For people working in marketing, communications or social media management, who may need to review and respond to dozens of posts in a session, waiting for the interface to jump through ten already-viewed posts is not just visually annoying - it is lost time repeated throughout the working day.

This is also the kind of UX issue that can be easy to overlook in a product as large as LinkedIn, but immediately obvious to people who use a particular workflow intensively. Sometimes the most valuable improvements do not come from adding another major feature, but from watching how experienced users actually work and removing the small interruptions that slow them down.

## Our view

* **LinkedIn has the functionality to comment as a Company Page, but the way it is integrated into Search and the feed unnecessarily interrupts the workflow.**
* **The biggest problem is not simply one extra click.** Page identity is not persistent, so administrators repeatedly have to check whether they are commenting as a private person or as the company.
* **The feed jumping after an identity switch is a particularly frustrating UX issue we observed during our own use.** At first, we thought it might be a glitch specific to our Page.
* **Other users’ experiences show that the broader problem of repeatedly selecting Company Page identity is not new.** Similar complaints have appeared in public discussions for years.
* **Facebook shows that persistent Page identity can work more simply.** An administrator should not have to repeatedly tell the platform who they are currently working as.
* **LinkedIn does not need an entirely new system.** A persistent Page mode and preservation of scroll position would solve much of the problem without a major redesign.
* **For a platform built around professional engagement, commenting on behalf of a company should be an almost invisible part of the workflow — not a moment when you have to wait for the screen to stop jumping.**
* LinkedIn already has the community, the functionality and the professional use case. **Our proposed UX improvement is relatively small - persistent Page identity and preserved scroll position - but it could make the experience better match the quality and importance of the platform itself.**
