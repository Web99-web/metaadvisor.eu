# New US strike on Iran puts Hormuz, oil and market risk back in focus

Financial markets are entering the new week with another major source of uncertainty. **US forces struck targets inside Iran again on Sunday**, reopening a military phase of the conflict after several weeks and doing so near the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most important energy routes.

News of the strike began spreading at around **3:40 p.m. ET on Sunday**, or around 21:40 Central European Summer Time. Axios, citing a US official, reported that American forces struck and destroyed two Iranian launchers on Larak Island. According to the US account, forces linked to Iran's Revolutionary Guard were preparing missiles associated with the deployment of sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

Reuters soon reported an explosion near Larak and later said Iran's Revolutionary Guard had confirmed deaths and injuries among fighters and civilians while promising a response. That adds another geopolitical risk to a market already shaken on Friday by renewed expectations of higher US interest rates.

For investors, the location matters as much as the strike itself. This was not an attack deep inside Iran with little immediate connection to global trade. It took place near the **Strait of Hormuz**, where security conditions can directly affect oil prices, inflation, rate expectations and virtually every major asset class.

## Markets were already under pressure after Warsh

The renewed Iran risk comes only two days after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh changed market expectations again at Jackson Hole. Warsh said the Federal Reserve still had work to do if it was not sufficiently confident that underlying inflation was returning toward the 2% target, which investors interpreted as a more open door to another rate increase.

According to CME FedWatch, the estimated probability of a rate hike at the September meeting jumped from **35.4% to 55.7%**. The two-year US Treasury yield rose by nearly 13 basis points toward 4.36%, while the dollar posted its strongest daily gain in roughly two and a half months.

The reaction spread across most major markets. The S&P 500 lost around 0.25% on Friday, the Nasdaq 0.52%, and the Russell 2000 about 1.4%. Bitcoin fell more than 3%, while gold and silver were among the hardest-hit markets: gold dropped roughly 3.2%, and silver around 4.3%.

A new geopolitical factor is now being added to that environment. If escalation with Iran pushes oil higher, markets could again increase inflation expectations and price in higher-for-longer interest rates. That means even traditional safe-haven assets such as gold do not necessarily have a straightforward path higher.

## Bitcoin is not showing a panic reaction to Iran so far

Crypto is effectively the only major global market that trades continuously over the weekend, so Bitcoin provides the first indication of how investors are assessing the new US strike. So far, the reaction is not panicked.

At the time this article was prepared, Bitcoin was trading at around **$78,700**, remaining above the area below $77,000 tested during the previous decline. No major new wave of selling followed the strike headlines, and BTC spent much of the evening between approximately $78,700 and $79,200.

That does not mean the crypto market has fully returned to risk-on mode. Total crypto market capitalization remains roughly **2.6% lower over 24 hours**, while the largest coins are holding up relatively better. Bitcoin dominance has moved toward 59.5%, suggesting that capital inside crypto is currently concentrating more heavily in the largest assets rather than across the broader altcoin market.

For Bitcoin, the area around $77,000 is now particularly important. As long as price remains above it, the new Iran strike has not produced a confirmed fresh breakdown. Losing that area, and especially a move below roughly $75,000, would significantly worsen the short-term market picture again.

## Bitcoin ETFs ended their nine-day inflow streak

At the moment of the renewed geopolitical escalation, Bitcoin also has another signal to watch. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded around **$201.8 million in net outflows** on Friday, ending nine consecutive trading sessions of inflows.

More than **$3 billion** had entered the funds during those previous nine sessions, so one negative day on its own does not represent an institutional exit from Bitcoin. The shift did, however, occur just as Bitcoin failed to hold its move toward $81,000–$81,500 and the derivatives market experienced another wave of heavy long liquidations.

Monday will therefore provide a much more important signal than Friday. If ETF inflows return quickly, Friday's $202 million outflow may remain an isolated episode after a very strong buying streak. If a second significant outflow session appears while BTC weakens again, that would provide the first stronger confirmation that institutional flows are beginning to change.

Leverage also deserves attention. During the latest larger decline, around **$488 million in crypto positions** were liquidated, including more than $360 million in longs. That shows how strongly the market had once again positioned for a continuation of the rally after Bitcoin moved toward $81,000.

## Oil now becomes one of the most important market signals

The new US strike happened while traditional markets were closed, so the real reaction in oil, gold and silver has not yet been seen. That is why the opening of the new futures session will provide the first major test of how seriously markets are pricing the risk.

Oil will be the primary focus. US forces had spent recent weeks working to fully reopen the main shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, and only days before the new strike Trump said mines had been removed from the international shipping lane. According to the US version of events, the latest strike is directly connected to an attempt to deploy mines again.

If oil opens sharply higher and holds those gains, it would signal that markets do not see the new attack as merely a limited military incident. Higher oil prices would again increase inflation risk and could support the dollar and US yields, which in turn could renew pressure on Tech, Small Caps, crypto and other rate-sensitive assets.

If oil reacts only briefly or remains relatively calm, markets will appear to be assuming for now that there is no immediate new disruption to shipping through Hormuz. That is why the first reaction in oil may be even more important than the initial Bitcoin move.

## Gold and silver now face two opposing market signals

The situation for gold and silver is especially interesting. Geopolitical escalation traditionally increases demand for safer assets, so the new US strike could trigger an initial rise in precious metals. At the same time, it was precisely the stronger dollar, higher rate expectations and rising yields that pushed both metals sharply lower on Friday.

That creates two opposing forces. On one side are Iran, Hormuz and the possibility of a new regional escalation. On the other are Warsh, higher expectations for further Fed rate hikes and a strengthening dollar.

Markets have already shown this year that the simple formula **war = gold rises** does not always work. If geopolitical escalation sends oil higher, more expensive energy can increase inflation expectations, which may then reinforce expectations for higher interest rates. In that scenario, a stronger dollar and higher yields can pressure gold again even while geopolitical risk is rising.

That is why it will not be enough to look only at the first move after futures open. What matters more is whether gold can hold any safe-haven rally through the first 15 to 30 minutes and whether silver confirms that move or starts underperforming again.

## Capital rotation currently looks more like de-risking

The broader market picture does not currently show a simple rotation in which capital is leaving crypto and moving directly into AI, metals or another clearly defined sector. For now, it looks more like **selective de-risking**.

US equity funds recorded approximately **$22.3 billion in net outflows** in the week through August 26, the largest weekly outflow since March. Bond funds continued to attract capital, while the picture inside equities remained highly uneven.

AI and Big Tech still retain investor interest, as shown by the strong reaction following Nvidia's results, but rate-sensitive and high-beta areas become more vulnerable as Treasury yields rise. Small Caps are already showing weakness, while within crypto, capital is concentrating more heavily in Bitcoin and the largest coins.

Metals do not yet have a clear rotation-in signal because geopolitics supports them while higher rates simultaneously create pressure. Energy is once again one of the most important sectors to watch after the new strike on Iran, but confirmation will only come with the reaction in oil prices.

In other words, there is no obvious new destination for capital yet. The clearest message from markets over the past several days is **less high-beta risk and more caution**, rather than a massive shift from one industry into another.

## What the market needs to show now

The first important signal will arrive with the opening of commodity futures. If oil rises sharply, gold and silver hold safe-haven gains and Bitcoin starts losing the $77,000–$78,000 area again, we will have a much clearer geopolitical risk-off signal.

If oil remains relatively calm, precious metals fail to hold any initial rise and BTC stays near $79,000, markets will likely treat the new US strike as a limited escalation for now.

The next test will be the Asian session, followed by Europe and Wall Street. For Bitcoin, the new ETF flow data will be particularly important, while for the broader market the key combination remains **oil + dollar + Treasury yields**.

In only a few days, sentiment has shifted from strong Bitcoin ETF inflows and renewed AI optimism after Nvidia to a hawkish Fed, major crypto liquidations, the first BTC ETF outflow after nine days and a new US military operation in Iran.

The question for the start of the new week is therefore no longer only whether Bitcoin can reclaim $80,000. Markets must now assess whether renewed escalation around Hormuz can push energy prices and inflation risk higher again at exactly the moment when the Fed is once more openly discussing the possibility of higher rates.

## Our view

* **The new US strike on Iran comes at an especially sensitive moment for markets.** Warsh had sharply increased expectations for a September rate hike only two days earlier, while Bitcoin, gold, silver and equities were already under pressure.
* **Bitcoin is not showing a panic reaction to the new escalation so far.** Holding above roughly $77,000 prevents a fresh confirmed bearish signal for now, but losing that zone would increase the risk of a deeper decline again.
* **Friday's BTC ETF outflow of around $202 million is not enough to conclude that institutional capital is leaving Bitcoin.** After more than $3 billion in inflows over the previous nine sessions, the first new ETF data on Monday will be far more important.
* **Oil may now be the single most important market signal.** A strong and sustained move higher would suggest markets are seriously repricing Hormuz risk again, with possible consequences for inflation, interest rates, Tech and crypto.
* **Gold and silver face conflicting signals.** Geopolitics can push both higher as safe havens, while a stronger dollar and higher yields can quickly erase those gains.
* **Capital rotation currently looks more like de-risking than a clear move into a new sector.** Energy is moving back into focus, while AI, Tech, Metals, Small Caps and crypto still need clearer confirmation of the next direction.
* **The start of the new week will therefore be shaped by three factors: Iran and Hormuz, the Fed and interest-rate expectations, and new institutional capital flows into Bitcoin.**
