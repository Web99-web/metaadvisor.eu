# Robots took to the streets of Warsaw to warn people about AI

**At first glance, the scene looks like a sketch from the future: humanoid robots and robot dogs protesting outside a ministry, demanding protection for jobs and stricter rules for artificial intelligence. But that was precisely the point. Humans sent robots into the street to warn other humans about what could happen if AI and robotisation begin changing the labour market faster than society can adapt.**

On Monday, 7 September 2026, around 30 humanoid and quadruped robots appeared outside Poland’s Ministry of Digital Affairs in Warsaw. They waved Polish flags, moved in front of the building and, with messages such as **“Defend workplaces”**, **“Time for rules”** and **“Don't wait, regulate”**, called for stronger regulation of artificial intelligence and protection of human jobs.

Of course, the robots did not decide by themselves to go out and protest. They did not form a union, organise a strike or conclude that they disliked the direction in which artificial intelligence was developing. The entire action was conceived by humans.

And that is exactly what makes the story much more interesting.

## Around 30 robots arrived outside the Ministry of Digital Affairs

The protest was organised by the **Democratism** initiative, which wants to open a discussion about how artificial intelligence and robotisation are changing the labour market. The machines included two-legged humanoid robots as well as four-legged robotic models resembling dogs.

The slogans they appeared to be “shouting” did not originate from some rebellious robotic intelligence. The messages were played through loudspeakers while the robots demonstrated their capabilities.

Still, one detail made the action particularly striking. The humanoid **Agibot A3** also answered journalists’ questions and said the presence of the robots was intended to show that this technology no longer belongs to some distant future.

Robots already exist. They move. They communicate. They perform increasingly complex tasks. And their capabilities continue to advance.

That made the entire protest both a demonstration of the technology and a warning about the consequences of that same technology.

## Robots were warning humans about robots

The organiser of the action, **Grzegorz Kuliś**, explained that they wanted to raise the question of people being replaced not only by humanoid robots, but also by artificial intelligence in jobs that are not physical at all.

That is an important distinction.

For decades, automation was mainly associated with factories, production lines and machines replacing physical labour. Today’s AI is breaking through that boundary. Artificial intelligence is entering administration, customer service, programming, marketing, finance, analytics, design, media, legal work and numerous other fields that until recently were considered relatively safe from traditional robotisation.

The organisers’ message is not that technological development should be stopped. The question is what happens to people while that development accelerates.

Who retrains a worker whose job disappears? Who pays for the transition period? How quickly can the economy create new jobs? Will AI simply increase employee productivity, or will it allow companies to perform the same work with significantly fewer people?

Those are the questions hidden behind the unusual image of robots carrying protest signs.

## The man behind the protest comes from robotics and recruitment

The story gains another layer of irony when we look at who stands behind the entire action. Grzegorz Kuliś is not an opponent of robotics observing the technology from the outside. He is connected precisely to the industries at the centre of the debate.

Kuliś runs the recruitment company Weegree and is also connected to the robotics industry. The robots used in the demonstration came from Delta Robots, a Polish company working with humanoid and quadruped robots.

So people involved in robotics and recruitment sent robots into the streets to warn that robots and artificial intelligence could significantly change employment.

At first glance, it is almost perfect irony.

But that is exactly why their message carries additional weight. The warning does not come only from people who are afraid of new technology, but also from an industry that can see very clearly how quickly that technology is advancing.

The organisers say their goal is not to fight robotics, artificial intelligence or technological progress, but to open a discussion about their consequences and about how society can reduce the negative effects.

## Only 61 signatures, but the images travelled around the world

Perhaps the best example of how effective the action was as communication can be found in an almost comical figure.

According to data reported by Notes from Poland after the protest, the Democratism initiative had collected only **61 signatures of support** on its website at the time. At the same time, there were around **30 robots** in the street. Almost one robot for every two people who had supported the campaign online.

But those thirty or so robots were enough for photographs and videos from Warsaw to begin circulating through international media. Reuters, Euronews and other major outlets carried images of humanoid robots and robot dogs standing outside a government ministry demanding protection for human jobs.

That may say just as much about today’s information environment as it does about artificial intelligence itself. You can publish hundreds of analyses about how automation will change the labour market and most people will never open them.

But put 30 robots outside a ministry, give them flags and a sign saying **“Protect jobs”**, and almost everyone understands the message within seconds.

## The minister came outside to speak with the organisers

The protest did not end with photographs of robots outside the building. Poland’s Minister of Digital Affairs, **Krzysztof Gawkowski**, came out of the ministry and spoke with the organisers of the action.

Gawkowski warned about the risks of uncontrolled AI models that could be implemented in humanoid robots whose capabilities will continue to develop along with advances in robotics.

That allowed the staged robot protest to achieve something that many real campaigns struggle to accomplish: the responsible minister personally came out to meet the demonstrators. – Except that this time the demonstrators were machines.

And the image of a minister speaking to people while surrounded by humanoid robots may be just as powerful as the protest signs themselves. The boundary between a debate about the “future of AI” and technology that is already standing in the street suddenly becomes very thin.

## Poland already has an artificial intelligence law

There is another important detail that can easily be lost in viral posts about the robot protest. The robots were not protesting in front of a state that had no rules at all for artificial intelligence.

During 2026, the Polish parliament adopted an **Act on Artificial Intelligence Systems**, which President Karol Nawrocki signed on 24 July. The law was published on 27 July and its main part entered into force on 11 August 2026, while some provisions have later implementation deadlines.

Poland is therefore establishing a national regulatory framework connected with the implementation of the European **AI Act**. That means the message **“Time for rules”** should not be understood literally as a claim that no rules exist at all.

The real question raised by the protest is much broader: are rules governing the safety, transparency and use of AI systems enough to address the economic and social consequences of automation?

You can regulate how a company uses a particular AI system. It is much harder to answer through legislation what happens if the same technology allows a company to perform work that once required one hundred people with just twenty.

## This is not only a story about physical robots

A humanoid robot attracts attention most easily because it physically looks like a potential replacement for a human being. We see it walking, lifting objects or talking and can immediately imagine the kind of job it might one day perform.

But a much larger transformation may already be happening without any humanoid machines at all. – An **AI agent** does not need arms or legs to take over part of a person’s work.

Software-based AI can analyse documents, answer customers, prepare reports, translate, programme, plan campaigns, process data, handle parts of administration or coordinate other systems.

That is why it would be a mistake to reduce the Warsaw action only to the question of whether a humanoid robot will one day stock shelves or work in a warehouse. The real question is how many jobs can be partially or fully automated when AI software, autonomous agents and increasingly capable robotics are combined.

## Funny at first glance, much more serious after a few minutes

The photographs are almost perfect for the internet. Robot dogs, humanoids, flags, a ministry and machines supposedly demanding protection for human jobs. It is difficult not to laugh at the whole situation.

Of course, the robots in Warsaw did not suddenly become politically conscious. They did not wake up one morning, look at the state of the labour market and decide to organise a demonstration.

Humans brought them there. – But the people who came up with this action deserve recognition for something else: they created a highly effective and deeply symbolic demonstration. Instead of holding yet another conference where experts spend an hour talking about the “transformation of the labour market”, they physically brought the problem to the ministry’s doorstep.

If we say that artificial intelligence could change millions of jobs, it sounds like just another prediction about the future. But when a humanoid robot stands outside a ministry holding a sign saying **“Defend workplaces”**, the same message is understood without further explanation.

And that may be the most powerful part of the entire performance. Humans are building machines that are becoming increasingly capable of replacing parts of human work. Then they send those same machines to stand outside the government to warn people that they should think about what will happen when machines actually begin doing that on a large scale.

We can laugh at robots that are “protesting”.

But the message was never really directed at the robots.

It was a message from humans – to other humans.

* **The action showed how a powerful visual message can be more effective than hundreds of pages of discussion about the future of work.**

* **The most important question is not whether every human will be replaced by a robot. The question is how many people will still be needed to perform the same work when AI, autonomous agents and robotics become even more capable.**
* Perhaps that is exactly why this seemingly funny demonstration is one of the most striking AI messages of the year: robots were used to warn humans about a future being created by humans themselves.
