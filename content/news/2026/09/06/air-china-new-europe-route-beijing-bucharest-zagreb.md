# Air China launches new Europe route: Beijing–Bucharest–Zagreb

**Air China** has expanded its European network with the new **Beijing–Bucharest–Zagreb** service, which operated its inaugural flight on September 4, 2026. The route connects the Chinese capital with Romania and Croatia and is particularly significant for Zagreb, as it creates the first scheduled passenger air connection between China and Croatia, although the service includes a stop in Bucharest.

The route operates **three times a week, on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays**, with Air China initially using an **Airbus A330-200**. Zagreb is the final European point on the route. The aircraft first flies from Beijing to Bucharest, where it has a scheduled stop of several hours before continuing to Croatia.

## A new European route connecting three capitals

The new service is important not only for Zagreb. Air China is simultaneously restoring scheduled passenger flights between **China and Romania** after more than two decades while opening its first regular connection to Croatia. The route therefore links three capitals – **Beijing, Bucharest and Zagreb** – and strengthens the Chinese flag carrier’s presence in Central and Southeastern Europe.

Romania lost its last scheduled direct air connection with China in 2003. Air China had previously served Bucharest until 1994, after which the route was operated for a period by Romanian carrier TAROM. For Bucharest, the new service therefore restores a link that had been absent for more than twenty years, while Zagreb enters Air China’s scheduled network for the first time.

For Air China, the launch is another step in the expansion of its European operations. The airline now serves **27 European destinations** and operates nearly **260 flights per week between China and Europe**, making Bucharest and Zagreb part of a much broader strategy to increase capacity across the European market.

## Schedule: three times a week and around 15 hours to Zagreb

According to Air China’s official schedule, flight **CA913** departs Beijing Capital International Airport at **00:30**, arrives in Bucharest at **06:30**, and after the stop continues at **09:20** toward Zagreb, where it lands at **09:55**. All times are local.

Taking the time zones and the stop in Bucharest into account, the total journey from Beijing to Zagreb takes approximately **15 hours and 25 minutes**. The Bucharest–Zagreb sector itself takes around one hour and 35 minutes of actual flight time, although the difference between Romanian and Croatian local time makes the scheduled departure and arrival appear much closer together.

The return flight **CA914** departs Zagreb at **11:55**, reaches Bucharest at **14:25**, departs again at **17:25**, and arrives in Beijing the following morning at **08:05**. The overall journey from Zagreb to Beijing therefore takes approximately **14 hours and 10 minutes**.

It is important to distinguish between a direct service and a nonstop flight. Passengers travelling between Beijing and Zagreb use the same Air China route, but the aircraft makes a scheduled stop in Bucharest. It is therefore a regular China–Croatia connection via Romania rather than a nonstop Beijing–Zagreb flight.

## Croatia gets its first scheduled passenger connection with China

The arrival of the first Air China flight in Zagreb was marked by the traditional water salute at Zagreb Airport. Representatives of Croatian state institutions, the tourism sector, the Chinese Embassy, the business community and the aviation industry attended the official welcome.

For Croatia, the significance of the new route goes beyond adding another destination to the timetable. China is a major tourism market, and Zagreb now gains more direct access for travellers from Beijing who no longer need to rely on a connection through one of the major Western European or Middle Eastern aviation hubs to reach Croatia.

The route is also useful in the opposite direction. Croatian travellers gain another option for reaching China through Air China’s own network, while Beijing serves as a major international hub with onward connections to numerous destinations across China and the wider Asian region.

## Air China continues expanding across Europe

The wider emphasis of the new service is therefore not only Croatia but **Europe**. Air China has been rebuilding and expanding the number of European routes and the capacity it offers between China and European cities. The new Bucharest and Zagreb service follows other European additions, including Beijing–Venice during the summer of 2026.

The strategy is particularly interesting because the Chinese carrier is not limiting its expansion to Europe’s largest aviation hubs such as London, Paris or Frankfurt. Adding cities such as Zagreb and Bucharest points to growing interest in **Central, Eastern and Southeastern Europe**, where there is still room for new tourism and business traffic.

Zagreb occupies an interesting position within that network. As Croatia’s capital and largest aviation hub, it can serve as a destination in its own right, but also as a gateway to the Croatian coast, Slovenia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and other parts of the region.

## What the new route could mean for Croatian tourism

The Chinese market was already an important and fast-growing source of visitors to Croatia before the pandemic. More direct air connectivity can help attract more Chinese tourists again because, on long-haul journeys, the number of transfers, total travel time and availability of regular flights often play an important role when travellers choose a destination.

The new route also arrives as outbound international tourism from China continues to recover. For Croatia, this creates an opportunity to attract visitors outside the main summer season as well. Zagreb, Plitvice Lakes, Dubrovnik, Split, Istria and other destinations can be promoted as part of a broader Central European or Balkan itinerary.

Air China’s route through Bucharest may provide additional value because the same service connects two European countries and can make multi-destination travel easier within a single trip.

## The route is not only about tourism

Croatia’s Ministry of the Sea, Transport and Infrastructure highlighted that the new scheduled service is expected to support not only tourism but also **business travel, trade links, investment and broader economic cooperation** between Croatia and China.

Regular air connectivity has practical value for companies. Business travel becomes simpler and more predictable, while improved access can matter for corporate delegations, trade fairs, investment activity, logistics and direct contacts between Croatian and Chinese companies.

The same applies to Romania. Restoring an air connection with China after more than twenty years gives Bucharest more direct access to one of the world’s largest economies, while Air China gains access to two markets in this part of Europe through a single route.

## Zagreb joins a broader Europe–Asia network

For Zagreb, the arrival of Air China is also symbolically important. A major Asian carrier is adding the Croatian capital to its regular international network for the first time, expanding the geographic reach of Zagreb’s Franjo Tuđman Airport.

Zagreb already has strong connections to major European hubs, the Middle East and a range of leisure destinations, but a regular service from Beijing represents a different type of international connectivity. It brings Croatia closer to one of Asia’s largest markets without requiring the entire journey to depend on a transfer to another airline.

Whether the route proves successful in the long term will depend primarily on passenger loads, tourism demand, business travel and Air China’s ability to bring travellers into Zagreb through its wider network. Starting with three weekly flights gives the route a frequency that is practical for both leisure and business passengers.
