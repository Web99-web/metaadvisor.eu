# ChatGPT, Claude and Grok Hit Almost Simultaneously, With Problems Also Reported for Gemini

Several of the world’s largest AI services experienced **availability problems at almost the same time on September 3**, making it difficult or temporarily impossible for users to access some of the most popular generative AI tools. OpenAI officially confirmed elevated errors affecting **ChatGPT and Codex**, Anthropic reported problems across multiple Claude models, while xAI confirmed an outage affecting Grok across several platforms.

At the same time, numerous users also reported problems with **Google Gemini**, although the situation there was somewhat different. Google did not publish a similarly broad official notice confirming a general Gemini outage. It is therefore not possible to say that all four systems experienced the same type or scale of incident, but the timing attracted attention because it is unusual to see so many major AI platforms experiencing problems during the same period.

## OpenAI Confirmed Problems With ChatGPT and Codex

OpenAI reported **elevated errors across ChatGPT and Codex** on its official status page. The incident affected several ChatGPT components as well as parts of Codex. During the disruption, the company said it was investigating the issue, applying mitigation measures and later monitoring the recovery of its systems.

That did not mean ChatGPT stopped working for every user. OpenAI notes that availability can differ depending on the subscription plan, model and feature being used. As a result, some users encountered errors when opening conversations, signing in or using specific functions, while others noticed little or no disruption throughout the incident.

According to services tracking user outage reports, ChatGPT recorded **the highest number of reported problems among the major AI platforms**. That also reflects the enormous size of its active user base, where even a relatively short service degradation can generate tens of thousands of reports.

## Claude Experienced Problems Across Multiple Models

Anthropic officially reported an **elevated error rate affecting several Claude models** on the same day. The problems were not limited to a single model but affected different versions across Anthropic’s offering, including models from the Mythos, Fable and Opus families. Earlier in the day, separate problems had also been reported with Claude Sonnet 5.

Anthropic published more detailed incident updates than many other providers. The company said it had identified the cause, was working on a fix and was gradually restoring models to normal operation. Some models recovered earlier than others, again explaining why the experience differed between users.

What made the incident particularly notable was that Claude’s problems occurred during a period that significantly overlapped with disruptions at OpenAI and xAI. **That timing is not evidence that the incidents were connected**, but it is precisely what made the September 3 events unusual.

## Grok Affected Across Web, Apps and API

xAI also officially confirmed **problems affecting Grok models**, and the incident was not limited to a single version of the service. xAI’s status information showed issues affecting Grok on the web, the Android app, Grok inside the X platform and its API infrastructure.

Parts of the xAI infrastructure were affected for several hours. The company said it was investigating the outage and working to restore service, while users reported temporary model unavailability and messages associated with overload or service disruption.

This strengthened the impression of a broader AI incident because **Grok, Claude and ChatGPT did not simply appear on Downdetector at the same time — their respective companies independently acknowledged problems with their own infrastructure.**

## What Happened to Gemini?

Google Gemini requires an important distinction. During the same period there was a **rise in user reports of problems**, including slower responses, errors and temporary unavailability. Some Gemini API users also reported longer waiting times, internal errors and temporary service disruptions on September 3.

According to figures cited by US media during the incident, the number of Gemini reports was significantly lower than for ChatGPT, but high enough for Google’s AI service to appear among the platforms experiencing problems that day.

However, **Google did not confirm a broad global Gemini outage comparable to the official incidents reported by OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI**. It is therefore more accurate to say that Gemini experienced reported problems during the same period rather than to claim that all four services were confirmed to have failed because of the same event.

## Were All the Incidents Connected?

That is currently the most interesting question, and there is still no confirmed answer. The fact that three major AI providers officially reported problems at almost the same time naturally raised questions about whether there could have been a **shared infrastructure-related cause**, such as a major cloud provider, network infrastructure or another service used by several AI companies.

For now, however, there is not enough evidence to support that conclusion. OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI operate separate AI infrastructures, and none of the companies has said that its incident was connected to problems experienced by competitors.

It is therefore possible that this was simply an unusual overlap of several independent technical problems. Individual outages are not uncommon in the technology industry. What made September 3 unusual was **how many major AI services experienced problems during the same few hours**.

## AI Is No Longer Just a Chatbot in a Browser

A few years ago, the outage of an AI chatbot would mostly have been an inconvenience for users trying to ask a question. Today, the situation is very different. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok are increasingly integrated into **software development, business processes, research, analytics, customer support, automation and programming**.

OpenAI’s incident, for example, did not affect only ChatGPT but also **Codex**, an AI system used for software development and coding. Claude is connected with Claude Code and Anthropic’s API, Gemini has an extensive developer ecosystem, while Grok also offers API access and business integrations.

This means an AI outage is no longer simply about whether someone can open a chatbot. For companies that have already integrated AI into their workflows, **model unavailability can temporarily interrupt real business operations**.

## Using Multiple AI Providers May Not Be Enough

One strategy increasingly adopted by companies is to connect to several different AI providers so that they are not dependent on a single model. If ChatGPT or the OpenAI API becomes unavailable, an application may be able to redirect some tasks to Claude, Gemini or another model.

The events of September 3 show the limits of that strategy. If several of the largest providers experience problems at the same time, **a multi-model strategy alone does not guarantee full resilience**.

For critical business systems, the surrounding infrastructure will therefore become increasingly important alongside model choice: backup providers, locally hosted models, fallback systems and procedures that allow operations to continue when an external AI service becomes temporarily unavailable.

## Chinese AI Services Did Not Show the Same Pattern

An interesting contrast came from China. While **ChatGPT, Claude and Grok had officially confirmed problems**, and issues were also reported for Gemini, leading Chinese AI services did not show the same pattern. **DeepSeek reported no incident on September 3**, and its status page showed both chat and API services as operational. **Kimi, developed by Chinese company Moonshot AI**, also had no reported major outage that day, while **Qwen, Alibaba’s major AI model**, likewise had no confirmed platform-wide disruption comparable to those affecting Western AI services.

That does not mean Chinese models are necessarily more resilient, nor does it prove that the problem was limited to Western infrastructure. Still, the timing is notable: **several of the largest US-based AI providers experienced problems during the same period, while leading Chinese services did not record a comparable widespread disruption**. That leaves open the question of whether the incidents were simply a coincidence involving several separate failures, or whether some shared infrastructure factor affected Western providers without reaching the Chinese AI ecosystem.

## Our Take

* **OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI officially confirmed problems on September 3**, and the incidents significantly overlapped in time.
* **ChatGPT and Codex experienced elevated error rates**, although not all users, models and features were affected equally.
* **Claude experienced problems across several different models**, with Anthropic gradually restoring normal operation.
* **Grok’s incident affected multiple platforms**, including the web, apps and API infrastructure.
* **Numerous problems were reported for Gemini**, but Google did not confirm a broad outage comparable to the official incidents reported by OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI.
* **There is no confirmation that one common failure brought down all major AI services.** The timing may be connected, but it could also reflect several separate infrastructure problems occurring at the same time.
* **The key question is no longer simply whether a chatbot works.** As AI becomes embedded in software development and business processes, model availability is becoming an infrastructure issue similar to the availability of cloud services, networks and other critical digital systems.
* **An interesting contrast came from China:** DeepSeek and Kimi reported no major outage on September 3, while there was also no confirmation of a comparable platform-wide failure affecting Qwen.
* **That does not prove Chinese models are more resilient**, but it shows that the disruption pattern did not affect the entire global AI ecosystem in the same way.
* **That contrast raises further questions about shared infrastructure:** if part of the problem involved services, networks or cloud infrastructure used by Western AI companies, Chinese providers may simply have remained outside that dependency chain.
