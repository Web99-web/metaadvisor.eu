# Netherlands Cuts Gold Held in New York by Nearly 80 Tonnes as It Prepares for Serious Crises

The Dutch central bank has made a major change to the geographical distribution of its gold reserves. Between March and August 2026, **De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB)** shifted around **86 tonnes of gold from the United States and Canada toward London**, with the largest reduction coming from reserves previously held in New York.

The central bank’s explanation is particularly notable. DNB explicitly points to **growing geopolitical uncertainty**, stronger crisis preparedness and the need to hold a larger share of its gold where it can be sold or otherwise mobilized quickly if necessary.

## The Netherlands Holds More Than 600 Tonnes of Gold

DNB holds around **612.4 tonnes of gold**, worth approximately **€72.2 billion at the end of 2025**. The reserves have long been spread across several locations rather than stored entirely in the Netherlands, including DNB’s own vault in Zeist, New York, Ottawa and London.

That distribution combines physical security, geographical diversification and access to major international gold markets. What has now changed significantly is the balance between those locations.

Before the latest operation, **31.3% of Dutch gold was held in New York**, 19.7% in Ottawa, 18.1% in London and 30.8% in the Netherlands. After the change, New York and Ottawa each hold **18.5%**, London’s share has risen to **32.1%**, while the amount stored in the Netherlands remains essentially unchanged.

## New York Loses Nearly 80 Tonnes of Dutch Gold

The biggest shift is in New York. DNB previously stored around **192 tonnes of gold** there, while approximately **113 tonnes** remain after the reallocation. That means the amount of Dutch gold in New York has been reduced by nearly **79 tonnes**, or more than 40% compared with the previous level.

London has simultaneously become the largest single foreign location for Dutch gold reserves, now holding roughly one-third of the total.

This does not mean the Netherlands is abandoning the US financial system. More than **110 tonnes of Dutch gold remain in New York**, but the central bank no longer wants almost one-third of its entire gold reserve concentrated at a single US location.

## Not All 86 Tonnes Were Physically Shipped Across the Atlantic

The move did not involve loading all 86 tonnes of gold onto aircraft and transporting it from North America to Europe. A large part of the operation was carried out through a combination of market transactions and physical movements of gold bars.

Around **59 tonnes of gold were sold in New York**, while a corresponding amount of gold was purchased in London. A further **27 tonnes were physically moved from the US and Canada to DNB’s vault in Zeist**, while a similar amount of suitable gold bars was transferred from Zeist to London.

The Netherlands did not reduce its total gold holdings. DNB instead **changed the geographical distribution of the reserves and increased the amount of gold that meets London market standards**.

## Why London?

The main reason is **liquidity**. London is one of the world’s most important centers for physical gold trading, and bars held at the Bank of England meet standards that make them relatively easy to trade on international markets.

For a central bank, that can matter greatly in an extreme situation. Gold is not useful as a reserve asset simply because it exists physically; what also matters is how quickly it can be turned into liquidity or used in a financial operation.

DNB therefore considers gold held in London to be **faster and easier to mobilize in a serious crisis** than part of the reserves stored in New York or Ottawa.

## Why Do Countries Store Gold Abroad at All?

At first glance, it may seem more logical for a central bank to keep all of its gold at home. Large central banks, however, have long distributed reserves across several international vaults in order to combine security, market access and geographical diversification.

Gold held in London provides immediate access to one of the world’s largest physical gold markets. Reserves in New York are closely connected to the US financial system, while gold stored domestically remains under direct physical control of the central bank.

The Netherlands has therefore not decided to bring all of its gold home. **The share held inside the Netherlands remains at 30.8%.** What has mainly changed is the balance between New York and London.

## DNB Explicitly Talks About Serious Crises

Perhaps the most interesting part of the decision is not the number of tonnes but the language DNB uses to explain it. The central bank explicitly refers to **growing geopolitical uncertainty, stronger resilience and improved crisis preparedness**.

That does not mean the Netherlands has declared that it no longer trusts the United States. New York remains one of the key storage locations for Dutch reserves, while Canada also continues to hold a significant share.

The shift instead suggests that DNB wants to reduce concentration risk and keep a larger share of its reserve assets in a location where they could be deployed very quickly during a serious disruption of the financial system.

## Why Is Gold Becoming So Important Again?

Physical gold has one characteristic that most other reserve assets do not: **it is nobody else’s liability**. A government bond depends on the issuer, a bank deposit depends on the financial institution and a currency depends on confidence in the monetary system.

A gold bar sitting in a vault does not require another government, bank or company to fulfill a financial promise. That is precisely why central banks still hold huge quantities of physical gold decades after the end of the gold standard.

Under normal conditions, that characteristic may seem less important. In a severe financial, geopolitical or monetary disruption, however, it becomes the reason gold is treated as an **ultimate reserve asset**.

The Dutch move is therefore more than a logistical transfer of gold bars between vaults. **DNB is adjusting the geography of its most important crisis reserves to a world it considers increasingly geopolitically unstable.**

## Our Take

* **The Netherlands has not reduced its total gold reserves.** DNB still holds around 612.4 tonnes; only the geographical distribution has changed.
* **The largest shift took place in New York**, where Dutch gold holdings were reduced by nearly 79 tonnes.
* **London is now the largest foreign location for Dutch reserves**, holding around 32.1% of the total.
* **Liquidity is the main reason.** DNB wants a larger share of its gold to be quickly sold, exchanged or otherwise used in a serious crisis.
* **The central bank explicitly cites growing geopolitical uncertainty and preparation for serious crises.**
* **This is not a complete withdrawal from the United States.** More than 110 tonnes of Dutch gold remain in New York.
* **The broader message goes beyond the Netherlands:** for central banks, physical gold is not a historical relic sitting in a vault. It is a reserve asset whose location is actively planned in advance for the possibility of a serious financial or geopolitical disruption.
