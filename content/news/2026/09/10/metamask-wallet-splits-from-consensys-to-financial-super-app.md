# MetaMask splits from Consensys and moves toward savings, payments, stocks and a broader financial platform

**Consensys Software Inc., the Ethereum company behind MetaMask, plans to split into two independent companies by the end of 2026, turning one of the world’s best-known crypto wallets into a standalone business for the first time.** The existing Consensys Software Inc. will be renamed MetaMask and focus on consumer products, while a new company retaining the Consensys name will take over Ethereum protocols and institutional blockchain infrastructure.

The split is far more interesting than a routine corporate restructuring because it comes at a time when MetaMask is rapidly becoming more than a wallet for holding ETH and accessing decentralized applications. Payments, savings, a stablecoin, a payment card, trading, prediction markets and tokenized traditional assets all point in the same direction: **from a crypto wallet toward a broader self-custodial financial platform**.

## MetaMask becomes a standalone company

Under the announced plan, Joe Lubin will serve as **chairman and CEO of the standalone MetaMask**, while also remaining executive chairman of the new Consensys. The institutional company will be led by CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham, with the separation expected to be completed by the end of 2026.

MetaMask will focus on consumer finance and self-custody, while the new Consensys will house products including **Linea, Besu and Teku** and work with banks, asset managers and other financial institutions on tokenization, stablecoins, settlement and other blockchain-based financial services. In practice, two very different companies are emerging: one wants to become a product used directly by individuals, while the other will provide infrastructure for financial institutions.

## MetaMask’s consumer business is growing differently from institutional Consensys

Joe Lubin told Fortune that the decision to split the company developed after Consensys recognized that **MetaMask’s consumer business was creating value faster than the company’s other business units**. That is an important point because it suggests the separation is not simply about making different teams easier to manage, but also about acknowledging that MetaMask has grown into a distinct and potentially very valuable business of its own.

Consensys says MetaMask has recorded **more than 100 million downloads across roughly 190 countries** and facilitated trillions of dollars in transaction volume. It is important to distinguish more than 100 million downloads from 100 million active users, however, because the company has not published an equivalent figure for active accounts.

## MetaMask is no longer just an Ethereum wallet

MetaMask launched in 2016 as a browser extension for managing Ethereum assets and accessing decentralized applications. Over the years it added swaps, bridges, staking and other crypto functions, but products introduced during 2026 point to a much broader strategic shift toward everyday financial needs.

The company now openly talks about **payments, savings, investing and traditional financial products**. In other words, MetaMask wants to remain a self-custodial product in which users control their own assets, while also offering functions for which people currently use bank accounts, brokerage apps, payment cards and investment platforms.

## Money Account brings MetaMask closer to a savings account

One of the clearest examples is the new **MetaMask Money Account**, a self-custodial account built around MetaMask’s mUSD stablecoin. Users can hold mUSD, automatically earn a variable yield and keep the same balance available for trading or spending, without a traditional lock-up period or withdrawal penalty.

MetaMask currently advertises a standard yield of up to around **4% APY**, alongside a limited promotional offer of roughly 6% through the end of September. It is not, however, a traditional savings account or an insured bank deposit. It is a self-custodial crypto product in which yield is generated through blockchain and DeFi infrastructure, meaning it carries different risks from conventional bank savings.

## MetaMask Card turns the wallet into a payment tool

Another major step toward everyday finance is the **MetaMask Card**, which allows users to spend assets held in their MetaMask wallet wherever Mastercard is accepted. According to MetaMask, the card can be used at more than **150 million merchant locations worldwide**, while users retain self-custody of their funds until the moment a payment is made.

For users in Croatia, one particularly relevant detail is that **Croatia is among the countries where MetaMask Card is available**. That means MetaMask is no longer only a place where crypto is stored or exchanged for another token, but increasingly a system from which digital assets can be used for everyday spending.

## Tokenized stocks bring Wall Street into a crypto wallet

MetaMask took another major step in February by integrating with Ondo Global Markets and adding tokenized U.S. stocks, ETFs and commodities. The initial announcement referred to **more than 200 tokenized U.S. securities**, including economic exposure to companies such as Apple, Tesla, Nvidia, Microsoft and Amazon, as well as instruments linked to gold, silver and the Nasdaq.

MetaMask’s broader current RWA offering now lists **more than 430 tokenized instruments**, although availability depends on jurisdiction. These tokens do not mean that a user directly owns an Apple or Tesla share, and they do not provide traditional shareholder rights such as voting. Instead, the token provides economic exposure to the underlying security held through relevant custodial arrangements.

## Tokenized stocks are not available everywhere

There is an important regulatory difference between MetaMask’s various products. While MetaMask Card is available in Croatia, **Ondo tokenized stocks and ETFs are currently unavailable to users in the European Economic Area**, as well as in the United States, the United Kingdom and several other jurisdictions.

The technical vision of a global wallet providing access to both crypto and traditional assets therefore already exists, but regulation still determines which functions an individual user can actually access. This will likely remain one of the main challenges for an independent MetaMask if it wants to develop into a truly global financial platform.

## Robinhood and MetaMask are coming from opposite directions

MetaMask’s transformation is especially interesting when compared with Robinhood’s development. Robinhood started in traditional retail trading and is moving increasingly deeper into **crypto, blockchain, tokenization and DeFi**, while MetaMask is moving from the opposite direction – from Ethereum and self-custody toward **stocks, payments, savings and traditional finance**.

The two platforms are therefore approaching the same space from completely different starting points. Robinhood is trying to bring blockchain into a conventional financial app, while MetaMask is trying to bring traditional financial products into a self-custodial blockchain wallet.

## The new Consensys is moving toward banks and institutions

While MetaMask is targeting individuals, the new Consensys intends to specialize even more clearly in the institutional side of blockchain. It will retain **Linea**, an Ethereum Layer 2 network, as well as **Besu and Teku**, Ethereum software used for infrastructure and network operations.

The strategy is based on the expectation that banks, asset managers and other financial institutions are moving beyond experimental blockchain projects toward real-world deployment of tokenization, stablecoins and onchain settlement. Consensys wants to provide the infrastructure for that transition, while MetaMask aims to become the place where end users access the emerging digital financial economy.

## Is the split preparing MetaMask for an IPO?

The timing of the restructuring has revived questions about a possible **IPO**. Consensys had previously been preparing for a potential U.S. stock-market listing, and earlier reports said JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs had been involved in the process, but those plans were delayed after market conditions deteriorated.

The new structure could make it easier to value the two very different businesses separately, particularly if MetaMask’s consumer business is indeed growing faster. However, **a MetaMask IPO has not been confirmed**. Fortune has suggested that the separation could open the door to a future standalone MetaMask listing, but Joe Lubin has not announced a new official timeline, and the company has not said whether MetaMask, the new Consensys, both companies or neither will ultimately go public.

## A MetaMask token is still not confirmed

The situation is similar with the long-discussed possibility of a MetaMask token. Lubin has previously spoken publicly about the possibility of launching a token, but the restructuring announcement did not include any confirmation that one will actually be issued.

That distinction matters when separating what the company has officially announced from market expectations. **An independent MetaMask is confirmed and its expansion into consumer finance is confirmed, but an IPO and a native MetaMask token remain open questions.**

## MetaMask wants to become a financial app without a bank in the middle

The most important part of the story may therefore not be the Consensys split itself. MetaMask is trying to combine the principle of self-custody – allowing users to control their own digital assets – with functions for which people currently rely on banks, payment cards, brokerage apps and investment platforms.

If MetaMask can combine a wallet, stablecoin, savings, card payments, trading, DeFi and tokenized traditional assets into a sufficiently simple product, it could become something very different from the browser extension most crypto users still associate with the brand. The Consensys split therefore looks like the point at which the company is formally recognizing what has already been happening at the product level: **MetaMask has grown into a standalone consumer-finance business.**
