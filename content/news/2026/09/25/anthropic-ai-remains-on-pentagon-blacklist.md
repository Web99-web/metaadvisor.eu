# Anthropic Remains on Pentagon Blacklist After Appeals Court Ruling

**A U.S. federal appeals court on September 25 left in place the Pentagon decision designating Anthropic as a national security supply-chain risk and excluding the company from certain military contracts. The ruling marks another major development in the months-long dispute between the U.S. Department of Defense and one of the world's leading AI companies over the conditions under which the U.S. military may use Claude models.**

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit rejected Anthropic's legal challenge to the Pentagon designation. The company says the decision has already cost it billions of dollars in lost business opportunities and caused reputational damage as Anthropic prepares for an expected initial public offering. Following the ruling, Anthropic said it disagreed with the decision and was considering further judicial review.

## Court Does Not Remove Pentagon Restrictions

The ruling means that the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a national security supply-chain risk remains in place in this proceeding. The appeals court therefore upheld the measure restricting the company's access to certain military contracts.

That distinction is important because the legal situation has become increasingly complicated in recent weeks. A federal court in California ruled in August, in a separate case, that another Pentagon action against Anthropic was unlawful. That decision created the impression that Anthropic had been removed entirely from the blacklist.

## California Court Previously Sided With Anthropic

Judge Rita F. Lin concluded in August that the challenged measures amounted to unlawful retaliation against Anthropic and that the company had not been given adequate procedural protections before the decision was made. She also found that the supply-chain risk designation at issue in that case violated the relevant legal framework.

However, that was a parallel proceeding brought under a different legal basis. The September 25 decision by the D.C. Circuit concerns a separate Pentagon action, which is why Anthropic still faces restrictions despite its earlier victory in California.

## The Dispute Began Over Restrictions on Claude

The conflict between Anthropic and the Pentagon began after the company refused to remove certain safety restrictions on the military use of its AI models. Anthropic objected in particular to the use of Claude for mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons systems.

The Pentagon argued that a private company should not be able to preemptively restrict lawful ways in which the U.S. military can use purchased technology. The Department of Defense also said it did not plan to use AI for mass surveillance of Americans or weapons operating without human control, but wanted to preserve access to all lawful uses.

What began as negotiations over a single contract therefore evolved into a much broader dispute over who controls the use of the most advanced AI models.

## Pentagon Considers Anthropic a Security Risk

The Pentagon earlier this year designated Anthropic as a national security supply-chain risk. The designation excluded the company from certain military contracts while the Department of Defense continued expanding its partnerships with other major AI and technology companies.

The Pentagon has since reached agreements with companies including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, Nvidia and SpaceX to integrate their AI systems into classified military networks. Anthropic has remained outside that group.

## Consequences Could Extend Beyond Military Contracts

Anthropic argues that the effects of the blacklist are not limited to direct contracts with the Pentagon. Many large U.S. companies simultaneously do business with the federal government and use commercial AI services, meaning that a national-security risk designation could also influence the decisions of other corporate customers.

The company says the dispute has already cost it billions of dollars in potential business and damaged its reputation shortly before a planned IPO. Reuters has reported that Anthropic and the White House had gradually attempted to improve relations in recent months, while the Pentagon security designation remained unresolved.

The September 25 ruling therefore increases legal and commercial uncertainty for the company once again.

## The Dispute Raises a Much Bigger Question About AI Control

The Anthropic case raises a question that will become increasingly important as AI systems grow more capable: can the company that developed a model determine how a government may use it after the technology has been incorporated into government systems?

Anthropic argues that certain limits must remain in place while models are not sufficiently reliable to make fully autonomous decisions with potentially severe consequences. The Pentagon's position is that private vendors should not restrict lawful military operations after entering into a contract.

This is therefore no longer simply a commercial dispute between an AI laboratory and a government customer. Advanced models are increasingly being used in cybersecurity, defense systems, data analysis and other areas of national security, making control over their use a strategic question for both governments and technology companies.

## The Legal Battle Is Not Over

Anthropic said after the decision that it disagreed with the ruling and was considering further legal options, including additional judicial review. That means the September 25 decision is unlikely to be the final stage of the dispute.

At the same time, Anthropic has already successfully challenged a separate Pentagon measure in the California case. The two proceedings must therefore be considered separately: the company won an important legal victory in one case while suffering a significant setback in the other.
