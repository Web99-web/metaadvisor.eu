# Bitget hacked: attackers stole $351.6 million, with XRP making up the largest share

**Crypto exchange Bitget has suffered one of the largest security incidents of the year. Attackers drained crypto assets worth around $351.6 million from part of its hot and warm wallet infrastructure, with more than 100 million stolen XRP standing out in particular. Bitget temporarily suspended withdrawals after the incident, while the company says its cold wallets were not affected and users will not bear the losses.**

Bitget detected the unauthorized transfers on September 24 at 18:31 UTC. After identifying the incident, the exchange activated security protocols, flagged addresses linked to the attack and brought in blockchain security specialists and relevant authorities to investigate.

Based on information published so far, this does not appear to have been a simple case of a stolen private key. Preliminary analysis points to a compromise of part of Bitget's backend system responsible for wallet infrastructure and transaction authorization.

## Around $351.6 million stolen

The estimated value of the stolen assets is approximately $351.6 million. The attackers moved funds across several blockchain networks, including Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, BNB Smart Chain and Base.

According to the data available so far, XRP represents the largest single part of the stolen assets. Around 102.9 million XRP were drained from Bitget wallets, worth approximately $157.5 million at the time of the incident.

In addition to XRP, attackers stole around 31,890 ETH worth approximately $85.8 million, about $75 million in USDT and USDC stablecoins, and roughly 3,000 XAUt tokens, representing tokenized gold worth around $13 million. BNB, AVAX and other tokens were also among the affected assets.

## XRP made up the largest share of the stolen assets

The fact that XRP represented the largest single portion of the stolen assets stands out in the structure of the attack. More than 100 million XRP were available in the compromised Bitget wallets, showing how significant the token balance was within the exchange's operational wallet infrastructure.

There is currently no evidence that XRP was deliberately targeted as a specific objective of the attack. A simpler explanation may be that the attackers drained assets that were available in the affected hot and warm wallets at the time.

Still, the size of the XRP position raises additional questions about the structure of Bitget's reserves and the amount of XRP the exchange kept in operational wallets. This may become clearer once more data about wallet balances before the attack becomes available.

## Private keys may not have been stolen

According to preliminary information from Bitget and security researchers, the private keys may not have been directly compromised at all. Instead, the attacker reportedly managed to compromise a critical backend system within the wallet infrastructure.

Such access may have allowed the attacker to generate spoofed transfer data and trigger Bitget's own transaction authorization and signing process.

That would mean the system itself may have signed transactions that appeared legitimate even though they had not been requested by actual users or authorized operators.

If this is confirmed by the final technical report, the incident would be significantly more serious than the compromise of a single hot wallet because it would point to a weakness in the system responsible for deciding which transactions are allowed to be signed.

## Cold wallets were not affected, Bitget says

Bitget says its cold wallet infrastructure was not affected and that most assets on the platform remain outside the compromised systems. The exchange also says user account balances remain accurate.

The company temporarily suspended withdrawals after the incident while the infrastructure undergoes security checks. Trading on the centralized exchange continued, while some other functions were temporarily restricted or delayed.

Bitget says users will not bear the losses and points to its User Protection Fund as the main financial safeguard.

## Protection Fund is worth more than $460 million

According to Bitget, its User Protection Fund is worth more than $460 million and is designed to provide financial protection for users in the event of major security incidents.

If Bitget were required to cover the full amount of approximately $351.6 million from the fund, that would represent a very large portion of its total value.

The company currently says it has enough resources to cover the losses and that user balances will not be affected.

A key question will be how much of the stolen crypto can be recovered or frozen before the protection fund needs to be used on a larger scale.

## Circle and Tether have already frozen part of the funds

Circle and Tether have already responded by freezing approximately $318,000 in USDT and USDC held at an address linked to the attacker.

Compared with the total $351.6 million stolen, this represents only a very small share, but the case highlights an important distinction between centralized stablecoins and cryptocurrencies such as XRP or ETH.

Stablecoin issuers can, under certain circumstances, block specific addresses and prevent further use of certain tokens. More decentralized cryptocurrencies do not have the same type of central control mechanism.

A significant part of the investigation will therefore focus on tracing blockchain transactions and identifying points where the attackers attempt to move, swap or cash out the stolen assets.

## Bitget suspects North Korean hackers

Bitget has said patterns in IP activity and the movement of funds on-chain strongly resemble previous operations linked to North Korean hacker groups.

The company has not, however, published a final attribution and has not definitively identified any specific hacking group.

For that reason, it would currently be premature to state that Lazarus Group or another specific organization was definitely responsible for the attack.

North Korean hacking groups have previously been linked to several major crypto thefts, including the 2025 attack on Bybit in which approximately $1.5 billion in ether was stolen.

## SlowMist and Mandiant are involved in the investigation

Bitget has brought security companies and blockchain analysts into the investigation, including SlowMist and Mandiant.

SlowMist currently classifies the incident as an internal system compromise and says preliminary findings point to a problem in the wallet backend infrastructure.

Bitget has not yet published a full technical report explaining the attacker's initial entry point, the exact nature of the system compromise or the sequence of events that enabled unauthorized transactions to be signed.

Such a report will be important for determining whether the vulnerability was specific to Bitget or whether similar security architectures are used by other major centralized exchanges.

## Why did the attack not immediately become a major headline?

Given the scale of the theft, worth more than $350 million, the incident initially attracted relatively little attention outside the crypto community.

One reason was timing. The unauthorized transfers were detected on the evening of September 24, while many of the details only began to emerge overnight and during September 25.

The first information was circulated by blockchain analysts and specialized crypto media, while larger international news organizations began picking up the story during the following day.

Reuters, CoinDesk, Forbes, BleepingComputer and The Hacker News have since reported on the incident, meaning the story is now spreading beyond specialist crypto media.

## The key question is how the attacker gained access

The value of the stolen assets naturally attracts the most attention, but the more important long-term question is how the attacker gained access to a system capable of authorizing transfers.

If the private keys were indeed not stolen, then a compromise of the backend infrastructure could represent an even more significant security problem.

Exchanges can store private keys in highly protected environments, but if a compromised internal system can generate a request that is then automatically approved by a legitimate signing system, an attacker may not need to steal the private key itself.

That is why Bitget's final incident report will matter not only to users of the exchange, but also to the wider centralized crypto exchange industry.
