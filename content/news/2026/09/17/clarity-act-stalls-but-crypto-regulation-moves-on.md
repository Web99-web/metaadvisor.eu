# CLARITY Act stalls in the Senate, but U.S. crypto regulation keeps moving

**The U.S. effort to create a comprehensive legal framework for crypto market structure suffered a major setback, but the regulatory process is far from over. The CLARITY Act failed to clear a key procedural hurdle in the Senate on September 15, with the vote ending 49–50 while 60 votes were needed to move forward. Just one day later, another major crypto bill received broad support in the House, while lawmakers are also discussing a possible new attempt to advance CLARITY after the elections.**

At the same time, the SEC and CFTC continue working on their own rules for digital assets. U.S. crypto regulation therefore no longer depends on a single sweeping bill but is developing through several parallel processes in Congress and at the regulatory agencies.

## CLARITY failed, but it is not procedurally dead

The Senate voted on September 15 on the procedural step needed to continue consideration of the CLARITY Act. Cloture required 60 votes, but the result was **49–50**, preventing the bill from advancing.

One important detail is that Senator Thom Tillis filed a motion to reconsider the vote, leaving open the procedural possibility of another attempt. That does not mean enough votes already exist or that another vote will definitely take place, but the defeat does not necessarily mark the final end of the bill in the current Congress.

## CLARITY could return after the elections

Adrian Wall, managing director of the Digital Sovereignty Alliance, said he had spoken with senators from both parties who are considering another attempt during the **lame-duck session**, the period after the elections when the current Congress continues to sit before the newly elected Congress takes office.

Wall himself stressed that such an effort would be difficult and that the outcome remains uncertain. CLARITY would still need to gain enough additional support to reach the 60-vote threshold in the Senate.

## While CLARITY stalls, the crypto tax bill moves forward

Just one day after the setback for CLARITY, the House Ways and Means Committee approved the **Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act, H.R. 10357**, and sent it toward consideration by the full House. The bill passed by 38–5, but its substance is more important than the vote count: it aims to create a broader tax framework for digital assets.

**The Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act addresses the tax treatment of mining and staking, crypto trading and lending, reporting requirements and mark-to-market rules for certain traders and dealers, while extending existing wash-sale and other anti-abuse rules to digital assets.** It also proposes relief for small everyday crypto transactions and certain blockchain fees so that minor amounts do not create unnecessary tax calculations and reporting obligations.

## CLARITY and the tax bill address different problems

The CLARITY Act is designed to define the broader market structure of the U.S. crypto sector, including regulatory jurisdiction and the rules applied to different types of digital assets and market activities.

The Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act has a narrower goal: establishing how digital assets are treated under the tax system. The fact that one bill stalled therefore does not mean the entire U.S. legislative process for crypto has stopped.

The **38–5** committee vote also shows that individual crypto issues can attract broad bipartisan support even when a larger and more politically complex market-structure bill remains blocked.

## SEC and CFTC gain a larger role

The CLARITY setback increases the importance of the SEC and CFTC. Both agencies can continue developing rules under their existing statutory authority even if Congress does not pass a comprehensive market-structure law.

SEC Chair Paul Atkins said in August that the agency is working on rules better suited to crypto markets, while also arguing that legislation from Congress would be important to create a more durable framework that is less vulnerable to changes in regulatory leadership.

Bernstein analysts expect faster activity from the SEC and CFTC after the CLARITY setback in areas such as crypto asset classification, DeFi, self-custody, tokenized securities and derivatives. That is an analyst expectation, not an announced regulatory policy.

## Three parallel paths for U.S. crypto regulation

After this week's developments, it is no longer enough to look only at whether the CLARITY Act passes or fails. The U.S. regulatory framework is now developing through at least three parallel paths.

CLARITY could receive another attempt during the lame-duck session. The Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act has already cleared an important House committee and is moving to the next stage. At the same time, the SEC and CFTC can continue building rules under their existing authority.

If CLARITY fails again, the broader market-structure debate could return in the next Congress, while tax and regulatory rules continue moving forward through other channels in the meantime.

## The crypto industry is not left without regulatory direction

The failure of CLARITY's procedural vote does not mean Washington has returned to doing nothing on crypto. Quite the opposite: only 24 hours later, another significant crypto bill passed a House committee by a wide margin.

The difference is that CLARITY is intended to create a broad market-structure framework, while the tax bill addresses a narrower and more specific set of issues. At the same time, regulators continue using their existing powers.

The U.S. crypto framework may therefore emerge gradually through a combination of several laws and regulatory rules rather than through one sweeping act of Congress.
