# John Ternus is Apple's new CEO from September 1, 2026: a new era begins after 15 years of Tim Cook

Apple has had a new chief executive since September 1, 2026. After 15 years at the helm of the company, Tim Cook handed the CEO role to John Ternus, Apple's longtime head of hardware engineering and an executive who has spent much of his career behind the products that shaped the Apple we know today.

Cook is not leaving the company. He has taken on the role of Executive Chairman of Apple's board and will continue to be involved in selected areas of the business, including relationships with political leaders and regulators around the world. Apple announced the transition in April following a long-term succession process, and the board unanimously supported Ternus' appointment.

## Tim Cook leaves behind an Apple worth around $4.5 trillion

It is difficult to understand the significance of Ternus taking over without first looking at the company Cook is handing to him. Tim Cook became CEO in 2011, succeeding Steve Jobs, and over the following 15 years transformed Apple into one of the largest and most profitable companies in the world.

Under Cook, Apple's market value grew from around **$350 billion to approximately $4.5 trillion**. Annual revenue increased from roughly $108 billion to around $416 billion over the same period, while annual profit exceeded $112 billion.

Cook's greatest strength was never presenting himself as a product visionary in the style of Steve Jobs. His expertise was operations, global supply chains, manufacturing, cost control and the creation of a business model that allowed Apple to generate exceptionally high margins. During his tenure, Apple's services business also expanded dramatically, from the App Store and iCloud to Apple Music, Apple TV and a growing range of subscription products.

The result is a company whose revenue, profit and market value are now many times greater than when Cook took over. Ternus is therefore not inheriting Apple during a financial crisis. Quite the opposite: he is taking control of an extraordinarily wealthy company whose biggest challenge is no longer how to make money, but how to find its next major technological step.

## John Ternus is not a finance executive, but a product engineer

Ternus represents an almost completely different type of CEO. He is a mechanical engineer who studied Mechanical Engineering at the University of Pennsylvania and worked as an engineer at Virtual Research Systems before joining Apple.

He arrived at Apple in 2001, while Steve Jobs was still running the company. He became Vice President of Hardware Engineering in 2013 and joined Apple's senior executive team as Senior Vice President of Hardware Engineering in 2021. By the time he took over as CEO, he had spent approximately a quarter of a century inside the same company.

His professional background matters because it is not built primarily around finance, sales or administrative management. Ternus built his career around physical product development — materials, construction, manufacturing, reliability and the process of turning multiple hardware components into a device that customers eventually hold in their hands.

## His influence is already visible across iPhone, Mac, iPad and Apple Watch

Over the years, Ternus has either participated in the development of or led hardware engineering for a large part of Apple's product portfolio. His influence can be found across the iPhone, Mac, iPad, Apple Watch and AirPods, while one of the most important technological transitions associated with his leadership was the move of Mac computers away from Intel processors and onto Apple's own chips.

Apple Silicon has proved to be one of the company's most important strategic decisions in recent years. It gave Apple much greater control over the relationship between hardware and software, performance, power efficiency and the development of future generations of Mac computers.

Ternus has also worked on issues that are less visible to customers but critical to Apple's products, including durability, new materials, reducing carbon emissions and improving repairability. Apple has highlighted developments such as recycled aluminium alloys, the use of 3D-printed titanium in Apple Watch Ultra models and design changes that make certain devices easier to repair and potentially usable for longer.

A former colleague from his pre-Apple career described Ternus as an engineer who could discuss an idea, turn it into a technical design and then effectively build a working prototype later the same day. That detail helps explain why his move into the CEO role is so interesting: after the strongly operations-focused Cook era, Apple is once again placing someone at the very top of the company whose career grew directly out of product engineering.

## Why John Ternus is being compared with Steve Jobs

John Ternus is not the new Steve Jobs, and a direct comparison would not be entirely realistic. Jobs was Apple's co-founder, a powerful visionary, an exceptional communicator and a leader whose personal ideas about how technology should look and work shaped the company's identity for decades.

Ternus does, however, bring qualities that make his thinking closer to Jobs' product-first approach than to Cook's. Reuters has described him as a product-focused perfectionist with deep engineering roots, and his approach to new technologies appears to be based on the idea that Apple should not put them into products simply because they are currently fashionable.

That is particularly relevant to artificial intelligence. While Microsoft, Google, OpenAI, Meta and other companies have rapidly developed generative AI products and models over recent years, Apple has often appeared slower and more cautious. Ternus' approach does not seem to be about putting an AI label on everything, but about using the technology where it has a clear purpose and genuinely improves the product.

That philosophy could become an advantage if it produces better devices, but it also creates risk at a time when the entire technology industry is changing rapidly. Apple can no longer afford to treat artificial intelligence merely as an additional feature if AI becomes a fundamental way in which people use computers, smartphones and future personal devices.

## Ternus takes over Apple as it searches for its next major product cycle

Apple does not currently have a problem selling iPhones or generating enormous amounts of cash. The challenge is that the iPhone is no longer a new product capable of fundamentally reshaping the technology market every year. It is a mature platform that continues to improve, but it is difficult to expect every new generation to create a revolution comparable to the early years of the iPhone.

Vision Pro has not become Apple's next major mass-market product, while the company's long-running car project ended without a commercial vehicle. At the same time, the rise of generative artificial intelligence has created a new market in which Apple no longer holds an obvious position as the technological leader.

That may be one of the most important reasons why the choice of Ternus is so interesting. Cook's major challenge was to take over Apple after Steve Jobs and prove that the company could continue to function, grow and become even larger without its founder. Ternus faces a different problem: Apple already knows how to manage an enormous corporation, but it now has to prove once again that it can create products that determine the direction of an entire industry.

This also raises the question of future device categories. Apple already controls its own chips, operating systems, much of its software ecosystem and one of the most valuable user bases in the world. If it can combine those advantages with a new generation of AI features and new device formats, Ternus could lead an entirely new phase of the company.

## The first major test comes only days after he takes over

Ternus will not have a long adjustment period. Apple is holding a major product event on September 9, the first such event since he officially became CEO.

A new generation of iPhone is expected, while one of the products attracting the most speculation is Apple's possible first foldable iPhone. Entering the foldable category would matter not only because of a new design, but because Apple has not radically changed the fundamental form of its most important product for years.

Ternus is around 50 years old, roughly the same age Tim Cook was when he took over Apple in 2011. That suggests Apple has not chosen a transitional CEO who will simply maintain the existing system for a few years, but someone who could potentially lead the company throughout the next decade.

Cook will remain close to the top of the company as Executive Chairman and can provide continuity, particularly in relationships with regulators, governments and major business partners. Since September 1, however, ultimate responsibility for Apple's strategic direction belongs to its new CEO.

## Our take

* **The change at the top of Apple is more significant than a routine executive replacement.** After 15 years of strongly operations-driven leadership, the company is now being led by someone who has spent almost his entire career in product development and hardware engineering.
* **Tim Cook leaves Ternus an exceptionally strong company.** During Cook's tenure, Apple's market value grew from around $350 billion to approximately $4.5 trillion, while revenue and profit reached levels that would have been difficult to imagine in 2011.
* **Ternus does not need to save Apple — he needs to find its next major direction.** In some ways, that may be the harder challenge because the market expects a company of Apple's size to produce a product or technology capable of opening another major phase of growth.
* **His product-first approach may be exactly what Apple needs right now**, particularly after the cancellation of the car project, slower progress in AI products and the limited market reach of Vision Pro.
* **AI is the biggest risk.** A cautious strategy makes sense if Apple ultimately delivers a better integrated experience, but the technology industry is moving so quickly that waiting too long could itself become a strategic disadvantage.
* **The first major test of the Ternus era arrives almost immediately.** Apple's September 9 event will be the first major product event under the new CEO and the first indication of how Apple intends to present its new era.
* The most interesting question is no longer whether Apple can continue generating hundreds of billions of dollars. The question is whether it can once again create a product that forces the rest of the technology industry to change direction.
