# Avalanche Expands Beyond Crypto and Moves Deeper Into Traditional Finance

Avalanche increasingly looks like a blockchain whose story extends far beyond the AVAX token and the traditional crypto market. While the AVAX price remains under pressure alongside much of the broader market, the network itself is attracting projects linked to **banks, major brokers, tokenized securities, stablecoin payments and institutional financial infrastructure**.

In just the past few weeks, **Charles Schwab** has announced plans to add AVAX to its crypto platform, Japan’s **Progmat** is using Avalanche infrastructure for more than ¥452 billion in tokenized securities, South Korea’s **KB Kookmin Card** is developing a stablecoin payment system, and **Ethena Pay** has been built on Avalanche. At the same time, Helicon, a major technical upgrade, is being prepared with several changes designed to make the network more practical for professional capital.

## Charles Schwab Is Adding AVAX

One of the most recent developments came at the end of August. **Charles Schwab, one of the largest brokerage and investment firms in the United States**, announced plans to add Solana, Avalanche and Chainlink to its Schwab Crypto platform in the coming months. Schwab launched its crypto offering with direct trading in Bitcoin and Ether, placing AVAX in the next group of supported digital assets.

The significance becomes clearer when looking at Schwab’s scale. The company has around **39 million active brokerage accounts and more than $11 trillion in client assets**. This does not mean Schwab is adopting the Avalanche blockchain for its own infrastructure, but it does bring AVAX closer to a large group of investors who operate through a traditional financial institution and may never have used a conventional crypto exchange.

## Japan Is Managing Billions in Tokenized Assets on Avalanche

An even more direct connection to traditional finance comes from Japan. **Progmat is a Japanese infrastructure platform for issuing and managing tokenized financial assets**, developed with the participation of major financial institutions and companies.

Progmat has completed the migration of digital securities products onto its own Avalanche L1 infrastructure. The value of the tokenized assets involved exceeds **¥452 billion, or roughly $3 billion**.

This is no longer about launching another crypto token. These are traditional securities using blockchain as part of the infrastructure for issuance, record-keeping and transfer. Projects like this help explain why Avalanche is targeting financial institutions that want to use blockchain without moving their entire business onto a fully open public chain.

## Stablecoins and Payments Become Another Major Focus

A similar shift is taking place in South Korea. **KB Kookmin Card is one of the country’s largest card companies and part of the wider KB Financial Group ecosystem.** The company is working with fintech partners on stablecoin payment infrastructure connected to its own Avalanche L1.

The goal is to connect stablecoin payments with existing card infrastructure, making it possible to use digital assets through payment systems consumers already understand.

Another example arrived on September 1. **Ethena, the project behind USDe and one of the largest crypto-dollar ecosystems, launched Ethena Pay on Avalanche.** The application combines digital dollars, transfers, savings functionality and card payments, while users keep their assets in their own wallets.

Avalanche C-Chain activity also points to growing usage. During the second quarter, the network processed around **235.6 million transactions**, while stablecoin transfer volume reached approximately **$84.4 billion**.

## Why Do Banks Need Their Own Avalanche L1s?

A central part of Avalanche’s strategy goes beyond the public C-Chain. Financial institutions can build **their own Avalanche L1 networks** tailored to specific rules, users and regulatory requirements.

A bank or securities issuer may not want to operate entirely on an open blockchain where it cannot control access or validator participation. A dedicated L1 can be permissioned, have clearly defined participants and still use Avalanche technology.

That changes how institutional blockchain adoption should be understood. A bank does not have to give customers an AVAX wallet or even tell them that blockchain is being used underneath. Avalanche can operate as **backend financial infrastructure**, while the end user still sees a card, an investment product or a conventional banking app.

This area already includes companies such as **Securitize, a regulated US platform for tokenizing traditional financial assets**, which has worked with major names including BlackRock, KKR and Hamilton Lane.

There is also **Dinari**, a US fintech and blockchain company that tokenizes American stocks and ETFs and is developing its own infrastructure for tokenized securities on Avalanche. Dinari is interesting enough for a separate story, particularly because its name has a completely different and very familiar meaning across the former Yugoslavia.

## Helicon Is Preparing Avalanche for Institutional Capital

The next major technical change is the **Helicon upgrade**. Importantly, Helicon is not yet active on the Avalanche mainnet. It has been running on the Fuji testnet since late July, while the mainnet activation date has not yet been announced.

Helicon introduces several technical changes, including Continuous Execution on the C-Chain, adjustments to gas pricing, automatic staking renewal and changes to validator economics.

One change is particularly relevant for professional investors: the minimum validator staking period is expected to fall **from two weeks to just 48 hours**. Avalanche’s technical documentation directly links the shorter period to the needs of institutional allocators that often cannot lock capital for multiple weeks because of redemption windows and liquidity-management requirements.

That suggests Helicon is not only about improving technical performance. Some of the changes are clearly designed to make Avalanche fit more naturally into the way professional capital actually operates.

## Avalanche Treasury Connects AVAX With Nasdaq

Another bridge to traditional capital markets comes through **Avalanche Treasury Company, a publicly traded company listed on Nasdaq under the ticker AVAT**. Following a business combination, the company became publicly listed and built a strategy heavily focused on holding and staking AVAX.

At the end of the second quarter, the company held approximately **15.3 million AVAX**, worth around **$100 million** at the time. Staking generated roughly **$1.5 million in net revenue** during the quarter.

The results also show the risk of this model. AVAT reported a net loss of **$44.7 million**, driven in large part by the decline in the value of its AVAX position, realized losses and other accounting effects.

It is an important reminder that expanding Avalanche infrastructure and greater institutional use do not automatically translate into a rising AVAX price.

## Infrastructure Is Growing Faster Than the Token Price

Viewed separately, these developments can look like unrelated announcements: Schwab adds AVAX, a Japanese platform tokenizes securities, a Korean card company develops stablecoin payments, Ethena launches a new app, a treasury company trades on Nasdaq and developers prepare a network upgrade.

Taken together, however, the direction becomes much clearer. **Avalanche is trying to occupy the space between crypto and traditional finance.**

On one side, it remains a public blockchain with the AVAX token, DeFi applications and traditional crypto activity. On the other, it is building infrastructure on which banks, payment companies, brokers and securities issuers can create their own financial systems.

The most interesting part may be the gap between the network and the token. Institutional projects and infrastructure usage are growing, while the AVAX price has not yet reflected the same level of market enthusiasm. Whether that gap eventually closes is a separate question.

## Our Take

* **Avalanche is increasingly positioning itself as blockchain infrastructure for traditional finance, not only as a network for crypto applications.**
* **Charles Schwab plans to add AVAX to its crypto platform**, giving the token exposure to clients of one of the largest US brokerage systems.
* **Japan’s Progmat is using Avalanche infrastructure for more than ¥452 billion, or roughly $3 billion, in tokenized securities.**
* **KB Kookmin Card and Ethena highlight another major direction: stablecoin and payment infrastructure.**
* **The C-Chain processed around 235.6 million transactions in Q2 and approximately $84.4 billion in stablecoin transfer volume.**
* **Helicon is not yet on mainnet, but part of the upgrade is directly aimed at institutional capital**, including reducing minimum validator staking to 48 hours.
* **Avalanche Treasury Company also shows the other side of an institutional AVAX strategy:** staking can generate income, but a falling token price can hit the balance sheet hard.
* **The key question is no longer only whether AVAX will rise.** A more important long-term question is whether Avalanche can become one of the blockchain layers on which traditional financial institutions actually run part of their future business.
