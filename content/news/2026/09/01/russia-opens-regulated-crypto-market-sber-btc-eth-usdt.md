# Russia Opens Its Regulated Crypto Market Today: Sber Plans BTC, ETH and USDT-Backed Loans

Russia enters a new phase of crypto regulation on September 1, and the country’s largest bank is already preparing to operate within the new framework. Sber plans to expand products linked to digital assets and enable loans in which Bitcoin, Ether and Tether’s USDT could serve as collateral.

The move comes as key provisions of Russia’s new law on regulated cryptocurrency trading take effect. Crypto is therefore gaining a more formal legal framework in Russia, while the Bank of Russia takes on a much larger role in determining which digital assets can be offered on regulated platforms.

## Sber Wants BTC, ETH and USDT as Collateral

Sber does not want to limit its crypto involvement to treating digital assets simply as investments. The bank plans to adapt existing products and gradually expand its offering as the new regulatory framework is implemented.

One of the most important changes concerns loans secured by digital assets. Alongside Bitcoin, Sber plans to accept Ether and USDT as collateral once the regulator formally allows the relevant assets for public trading.

This is an important step because cryptocurrencies would no longer be used only as assets to buy, sell or hold. They would also become collateral that a major traditional bank can accept as security for a loan.

## Bank of Russia Sets the Market Framework

The new rules do not mean that every cryptocurrency will automatically gain access to the regulated market. The Bank of Russia will have the authority to decide which assets meet the necessary criteria and can be traded on regulated platforms.

Key criteria include market size, liquidity and a sufficiently long pricing history on international markets. Bitcoin, Ether and USDT are already among the most frequently discussed assets in this context, which also helps explain why Sber wants to include them in future products.

For retail investors, this also means that access to the market will not be completely open or unrestricted. Russia’s regulated crypto market will be shaped by clear limits and by a selection of assets that the regulator considers sufficiently large and stable for public trading.

## Regulated Trading Does Not Mean Free Use of Crypto

Opening a regulated crypto market does not mean that Russia is turning Bitcoin or other cryptocurrencies into everyday payment methods. The new framework primarily concerns trading, investing and the use of digital assets within the financial system.

In other words, the state is not giving crypto the role of a replacement for the ruble. Instead, it is creating space for digital assets inside a more tightly controlled investment and trading environment. This is an important distinction because legalizing or regulating crypto does not automatically mean treating it in the same way as traditional money.

This approach also allows banks and exchanges to develop new products without fully abandoning the existing monetary framework.

## Sber Sees a Large New Market

Sber clearly does not view the new regulation only as a legal obligation. The bank also sees a serious business opportunity. Its expectations suggest that it is preparing for significant growth in trading volumes and interest in regulated crypto products.

If those growth expectations are realized, Russia could develop a digital asset market that, within a relatively short period, moves beyond being a marginal or experimental part of the financial system and becomes one of its more visible new segments.

That is precisely why loans backed by BTC, ETH and USDT are so interesting. They show that banks are no longer asking only whether crypto should be tolerated, but how digital assets can be integrated into existing financial products.

## What Is Happening With the Digital Ruble?

Perhaps the most interesting part of the story is not only the opening of the market, but the contrast between interest in decentralized and privately issued digital assets on one side and the digital ruble on the other.

While Sber is developing products linked to Bitcoin, Ether and USDT, the bank has already indicated that demand for the digital ruble does not currently appear particularly strong. This creates an interesting paradox: the state is developing its own central bank digital currency, while the country’s largest commercial bank sees a more immediate business opportunity in Bitcoin, Ether and stablecoins.

That does not mean the digital ruble is unimportant. But for banks operating on global markets, BTC, ETH and USDT currently have one major advantage: international liquidity, developed market infrastructure and real demand already exist. That can make their practical use clearer than that of a CBDC that is primarily tied to a domestic monetary system.

* **The Bank of Russia will retain key control over which assets can be offered on the regulated market**, so this is not a completely unrestricted market opening.
