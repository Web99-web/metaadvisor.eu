# Ireland Gives Tax Perks to Stocks and ETFs – but Not Crypto

Ireland is preparing a new investment model for retail investors that aims to simplify investing and provide more favorable tax treatment for certain financial products. Cryptocurrencies and derivatives, however, will not be included in the new system because the Irish government considers them more complex and risky products.

The Department of Finance has published a roadmap for reforming the taxation of retail investment. One of the central elements of the plan is a new investment account through which residents would be able to invest in stocks, bonds, ETFs and other investment funds.

## New Investment Accounts for Retail Investors

The reform is intended to encourage more people to direct part of their savings toward investments while also simplifying tax rules that currently differ depending on the type of financial product.

The new investment accounts are expected to become available to Irish residents during 2027. The final tax rate and any potential tax-free threshold have not yet been determined, with further details expected to be announced as part of Ireland’s Budget 2027.

The system is intended to give retail investors a simpler way to invest and clearer rules on which products can be held within a single account.

## Crypto and Derivatives Remain Excluded

While stocks, bonds, ETFs and investment funds will be eligible for the new account, crypto assets and derivatives will be excluded.

The Irish government classifies them as highly complex and risky investment products. This means investors in Bitcoin, Ether and other cryptocurrencies will not have access to the tax advantages being prepared for more traditional forms of investment.

The decision does not mean that Ireland is banning crypto investment. Residents will still be able to buy and sell digital assets under the existing rules, but crypto will not be part of the new tax-advantaged investment system.

## Ireland Wants More People to Become Investors

The reform is part of a broader effort to increase retail participation in capital markets. A large share of private savings across Europe still remains in bank accounts, while stocks and investment funds are less widely held among retail investors than in some other major markets.

Ireland therefore wants to create a simpler framework in which people can more easily understand how their investments are taxed and which products they can hold within one investment account.

The final details of the system have not yet been published, meaning some of the rules are likely to become clear only during preparations for Budget 2027.

## Tax Advantage Does Not Mean Tax-Free

The term tax-advantaged investment account does not necessarily mean that every investment held inside it will be completely exempt from tax. The government has not yet announced the final tax rate or whether there will be a tax-free threshold.

The key distinction is that certain traditional investment products will gain access to a special and simplified tax framework, while crypto will remain outside that system.

Ireland is therefore drawing a clear line between products it wants to encourage as longer-term investments for households and assets that it continues to regard as significantly riskier.

## Crypto Again Remains a Separate Category

The decision is also notable in the wider context of Europe’s approach to digital assets. Through the MiCA framework, the European Union is gradually creating a more unified regulatory environment for crypto markets. That does not mean, however, that member states automatically treat crypto in the same way as stocks, bonds or traditional investment funds.

Ireland is making exactly that distinction. Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies can be legal and regulated investments, while still being excluded from a system designed to encourage retail investing through tax incentives.

The move shows that crypto regulation and tax equality with traditional financial assets remain two separate issues.
