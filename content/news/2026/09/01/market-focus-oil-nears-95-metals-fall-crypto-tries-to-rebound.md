# Market Focus: Oil nears $95, metals fall, crypto tries to rebound

Global financial markets entered September under renewed pressure. **Rising oil prices, sharply higher government bond yields and ongoing geopolitical tensions** increased investor caution again, while capital continued rotating visibly between sectors during the day.

This is not a classic risk-off setup in which gold, the US dollar and government bonds all rise at the same time. Instead, **oil and energy stocks are climbing, bond prices are falling as yields rise, while gold and silver are also dropping sharply**. Technology stocks are under pressure as well, while crypto is trying to find short-term stability after another deeper selloff.

## Brent moves toward $95

One of the strongest moves of the day came from the energy market. **Brent crude moved close to $95 per barrel**, while US WTI climbed back above $90. The rise is linked to another escalation in tensions between the **United States and Iran** and renewed concern about possible supply disruptions from the Middle East.

For markets, the oil price itself is only part of the story. More expensive energy adds fresh inflation pressure at a time when investors are already trying to determine how long interest rates could remain elevated.

That is why the **energy sector** was one of the few parts of the market receiving clear support during the session. In Europe, energy stocks were also among the few gainers while many other sectors came under pressure.

## Global bond selloff becomes the second major problem

Another important signal is coming from government bond markets. The yield on the **10-year US Treasury** reached around **4.80%** during the day, its highest level since January 2025, before easing slightly.

An even more symbolic move happened in **Japan**, where the 10-year government bond yield reached **3% for the first time since 1996**.

The increase in yields is not limited to the US and Japan. Long-term yields also rose sharply in **Germany, France and the United Kingdom**. Germany's 30-year yield reached its highest level in 15 years, while the French equivalent climbed to its highest level since 2008.

Higher yields increase financing costs for governments, companies and consumers. At the same time, they place particular pressure on sectors whose valuations rely heavily on future growth expectations, including technology and other growth stocks.

## Wall Street starts September under pressure

The main US equity indexes traded lower during the session. **The S&P 500, Dow Jones and Nasdaq** all moved into negative territory as investors monitored higher yields, rising oil prices and new signals about the future direction of US interest rates.

The **Nasdaq** was again among the weaker major indexes, showing how strongly higher yields are affecting the technology sector. Large technology companies including **Nvidia, Amazon and AMD** were also under pressure, while energy stocks benefited from the sharp rise in oil.

Additional pressure is coming from expectations around the **Federal Reserve**. After fresh hawkish comments from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, markets increased expectations that the US central bank could raise rates again if inflation does not continue to cool.

That leaves the US market facing two developments that traditionally weigh on growth stocks at the same time: **more expensive energy and higher yields**.

## Europe falls, Japan proves more resilient, Hong Kong weakens again

The weakness was not limited to the United States. The pan-European **STOXX 600 fell around 0.6%**, dropping to its lowest level in more than a month. Germany's **DAX lost about 1.1%**, France's **CAC 40 around 0.4%**, while the UK's **FTSE 100** declined roughly 0.3%.

Additional pressure in Europe came from inflation data. Eurozone inflation moved back above **3%**, increasing expectations that the **European Central Bank** could raise interest rates again.

Asian markets were not moving completely in sync. **Japan** showed more resilience during the session, while **Hong Kong** weakened again.

Particular attention went to **Shein's Hong Kong market debut**. Shares in the global fast-fashion retailer fell about 4% on their first trading day after being down as much as roughly 10% during the session. Investors focused on slower company growth, regulatory risks and the impact of changing tariff rules in the United States and Europe.

## Gold and silver are not acting like traditional safe havens

One of the more unusual market stories on September 1 came from precious metals. Despite ongoing geopolitical tensions and weaker equity markets, **gold and silver fell sharply**.

Spot gold lost about **2.4% during the US session and moved toward $4,342 per ounce**, while silver fell around **2.9% toward $64.57**. Platinum and palladium were also under pressure.

The main reason is the strong rise in yields and the US dollar. Gold does not generate interest income, so higher government bond yields increase the opportunity cost of holding the metal.

That creates an unusual split in the geopolitical trade. **Oil is surging because of supply risks, while gold and silver are falling because markets are reacting even more strongly to inflation concerns and higher interest rates.**

## Crypto tries to stabilize after another selloff

The crypto market was also under pressure during the day, but began showing early signs of stabilization in the evening. **Bitcoin** fell toward the key area around **$77,000** before attempting to bounce from that level.

**Ethereum** is trading around the important **$2,400 to $2,420** zone, while **Solana** moved back toward the psychologically important **$100** level after its decline.

**HYPE** showed somewhat stronger relative performance than parts of the broader crypto market and moved back above $82. That is notable because BTC, ETH and SOL remain under pressure on a daily basis.

The large-cap altcoin overview should also include **XRP**, which is followed especially closely by US crypto investors. XRP is trading around **$1.35**, down roughly 3% on the day, and remains among the five largest crypto assets by market capitalization.

For now, the move looks more like an attempt to stabilize after the selloff than a confirmed return of a broader uptrend. For Bitcoin, the **$77,000** area remains especially important, while Ethereum is watching **$2,400** and Solana the **$100** level.

If those levels hold, the market has room for a continuation of the short-term recovery. Losing them would increase pressure on the broader crypto segment again.

## ETF flows remain an important contrast to price action

Another important part of the crypto picture comes from US spot ETFs. The latest available data continued to show institutional interest in **Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs**, while BTC and ETH prices themselves remained under pressure.

That creates a divergence between institutional capital flows and short-term market price action.

The next key data point will be the actual ETF inflows and outflows for **September 1**. If another round of strong inflows appears while BTC and ETH manage to hold current support zones, it would show that institutional demand remained present during the latest decline.

If ETF flows weaken or turn into meaningful net outflows at the same time as key support levels fail, the market picture would shift again.
