# Raiffeisen and Bitpanda Expand Crypto Access Across a Network of 18 Million Bank Customers

**Raiffeisen Bank International and Austrian fintech Bitpanda are expanding their partnership to the level of the entire banking group, potentially making digital asset infrastructure available to banks that together serve around 18 million customers. The partnership is no longer based only on an individual local project, but on a common framework that banks across the RBI network can use to gradually introduce crypto services depending on local demand and regulatory conditions.**

Bitpanda Enterprise will provide the technology infrastructure needed to introduce digital asset services, while individual banks will decide which services to offer, when to launch them and how to adapt the offering to each local market.

The figure of 18 million customers therefore does not mean that 18 million people will automatically receive crypto services. It represents the potential reach of the banking network across which Bitpanda's infrastructure can gradually be rolled out.

## From a local project to a group-wide model

The cooperation between Raiffeisen and Bitpanda did not begin today. The model was already tested in Austria, where Raiffeisenlandesbank Niederösterreich-Wien integrated access to digital assets using Bitpanda's technology in 2024.

The concept is now being expanded to the level of Raiffeisen Bank International. Instead of separate local pilot projects, RBI gains a common technology framework that its banks can use to develop their own crypto offerings.

RBI CEO Michael Höllerer said the group is seeing growing demand for digital assets across its markets. For the bank, working with an established crypto infrastructure provider means it does not have to build an entire digital asset system from scratch.

## RBI operates banks across 11 Central and Eastern European markets

Raiffeisen Bank International operates through subsidiary banks in 11 Central and Eastern European markets: **Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, Czechia, Hungary, Kosovo, Romania, Russia, Serbia, Slovakia and Ukraine.**

The group serves around 18.8 million customers and employs roughly 42,000 people, giving the partnership a potential reach far beyond a typical local crypto pilot.

Austria is RBI's home market and the group's headquarters are in Vienna. The 11 markets listed above refer to its network of subsidiary banks across Central and Eastern Europe.

## The first new rollout is expected in 2027

The expansion is still at an early stage and will not happen simultaneously across all markets. According to information RBI provided to Cointelegraph, after the existing Austrian model, the broader rollout is expected to begin first in **Albania, Czechia and Slovakia during the first half of 2027**.

Other markets are expected to follow gradually, depending on regulatory requirements, local demand and the decisions of individual banks.

The announcement therefore should not be read as meaning that all 18 million customers will soon automatically be able to buy Bitcoin. The more important shift is in the model itself: digital asset infrastructure is becoming available to the entire banking group rather than to just one bank.

## Bitpanda is becoming much more than a crypto broker

Bitpanda is an Austrian fintech company founded in Vienna in 2014. It started as a platform for buying and selling digital assets, but in recent years it has increasingly positioned itself as an infrastructure partner for traditional financial institutions.

Bitpanda Enterprise allows banks, brokers, fintech companies and other institutions to integrate access to digital assets into their own apps and products without having to build the entire crypto infrastructure themselves.

That is one of the most important parts of the new partnership. End users may not need to use a separate Bitpanda app at all. Crypto services can appear inside their own bank's app while Bitpanda's technology operates in the background.

## Banks no longer want to leave crypto only to exchanges

For traditional banks, the logic is becoming increasingly clear. If some of their customers are already buying Bitcoin, Ethereum and other digital assets through separate platforms, banks have a growing incentive to offer similar services inside their own ecosystems.

For customers, that model can reduce the need to open additional accounts or use separate crypto exchanges. The bank, meanwhile, can keep more of the customer's financial activity inside its own app and ecosystem.

At the same time, banks do not necessarily have to build the entire infrastructure for order execution, liquidity, digital asset custody and other technical components themselves. Part of that work can be handled by specialised providers such as Bitpanda.

## MiCA is changing how European banks look at crypto

A clearer regulatory framework in Europe is also helping institutional crypto services expand. Bitpanda operates within the European regulatory framework for digital assets, including the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, better known as MiCA.

For traditional financial institutions, regulatory clarity is especially important because it allows them to view digital assets less as a completely separate market and more as an additional category of financial products.

That does not remove crypto market risk, nor does it mean banks will suddenly offer every token and product available on specialised crypto exchanges. But it does reduce one of the biggest barriers to institutional adoption: regulatory uncertainty.

## 18 million customers does not mean 18 million crypto users

The figure of around 18 million customers is eye-catching, but it needs to be interpreted correctly. It roughly represents the existing customer base of the RBI network. The actual number of crypto users will depend on how many banks introduce the service, when they launch it and how much customer demand there is.

The offering also does not have to be identical in every market. Individual banks may offer different products, different digital assets and different features.

Still, the fact that a major European banking network can use shared crypto infrastructure shows how much the relationship between traditional banking and digital assets has changed.

## Traditional banking and crypto are increasingly converging

A few years ago, the main competition for crypto platforms came from other crypto platforms. That is changing. Banks, brokers, fintech companies and investment platforms increasingly want to integrate digital assets into existing financial products.

In that model, companies such as Bitpanda can play a very different role than before. Their growth no longer has to depend only on how many new retail users open accounts directly on their own platform.

If the same infrastructure is used by major banks with millions of existing customers, Bitpanda can also grow as a technology layer behind the traditional financial system.

For RBI, the logic runs in the opposite direction. Instead of leaving demand for digital assets entirely to specialised crypto exchanges, the bank can try to retain part of that business within its own banking network.

## Our take
* **Raiffeisen and Bitpanda are not simply launching another crypto app.** The partnership is designed to integrate digital assets into existing banking infrastructure.
* **The potential reach of around 18 million customers matters because of the scale of the existing banking network**, but that figure does not represent the number of future crypto users.
* **Bitpanda is increasingly becoming a B2B infrastructure company.** Instead of relying only on its own retail platform, it can provide technology to banks that already serve millions of customers.
* **RBI gains the ability to introduce crypto services without building the entire system from scratch.** That can significantly reduce the technical complexity of bringing traditional banking into digital assets.
* **The rollout will be gradual.** After the existing Austrian model, Albania, Czechia and Slovakia are expected to be among the first new markets during the first half of 2027.
* **MiCA and a clearer European regulatory framework are making it easier for traditional banks to move closer to digital assets.**
* **The most important signal is not only the number 18 million.** What matters more is that the boundary between traditional banking and crypto infrastructure continues to blur.
