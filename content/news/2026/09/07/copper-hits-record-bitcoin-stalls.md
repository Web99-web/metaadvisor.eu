# Copper hits a record while Bitcoin stalls: the AI boom is moving from chips into metals

While **Bitcoin is once again trading around the $79,000–$80,000 area**, another part of the global market is sending a very different signal. **Copper reached a new all-time high on the London Metal Exchange on September 7**, and the move increasingly looks like more than a short-lived price spike. It is being supported by a combination of strong infrastructure demand, production problems and growing investor interest in industrial metals.

What makes today’s market especially interesting is not only that copper is rising while Bitcoin lacks the same momentum. At the same time, **gold is weakening**, showing that capital is not simply moving into all metals. Markets are increasingly distinguishing between **industrial metals**, supported by physical demand and infrastructure investment, and **precious metals**, whose prices are currently reacting more strongly to interest rates and expectations around the Federal Reserve.

## Copper reaches a new record above $14,500

The benchmark three-month copper contract on the London Metal Exchange rose to around **$14,510 per metric tonne**, after reaching an intraday record near **$14,533**. That exceeded the previous high set in January and brought copper’s gain since the beginning of 2026 to approximately **16%**.

This move looks different from a typical short-term commodity spike because several fundamental factors are working at the same time. Copper demand is rising because of investment in power grids, renewable energy, electric vehicles, data centers and artificial-intelligence infrastructure, while problems are also emerging on the mining side of the supply chain.

That does not mean the world is currently running out of refined copper. In fact, refined copper production is still increasing and the market recorded a surplus during the first half of the year. However, mine disruptions, the movement of inventories between markets and expectations of significantly stronger future demand create a very different picture when viewed over several years.

## The AI boom is increasingly becoming an infrastructure story

Much of the investment story around artificial intelligence over the past few years has focused on **processors, chipmakers, cloud companies and data centers**. But as more AI infrastructure is physically built, it is becoming increasingly clear that new data centers need much more than GPUs.

They require enormous power connections, transformers, cooling systems, new substations, cables and expanded transmission grids. That is precisely why copper is one of the most important materials in the entire process. An AI data center can contain the most advanced processors in the world, but without sufficient electricity and grid infrastructure it cannot operate.

The AI investment story is therefore gradually expanding beyond the traditional technology sector. After chipmakers and server manufacturers, more attention is shifting toward **power grids, energy infrastructure and industrial metals** required to build the physical backbone of the new AI economy.

## Production problems are adding support to copper

Global copper mine production fell by around **1.1% in the first half of 2026**, while copper concentrate output declined by approximately **2.6%**. Countries reporting lower production included **Chile, Indonesia and the Democratic Republic of Congo**, three extremely important regions in the global copper supply chain.

Part of the decline was offset by higher production in Peru and Mongolia, but the market is increasingly reacting to the fact that developing major new mines can take many years. Demand from electrification and data centers can increase relatively quickly, while new mining capacity cannot be brought online at the same speed.

An additional complication comes from expectations of possible new U.S. tariffs on refined copper. Large quantities of metal have already been pulled toward the U.S. market in anticipation of such measures, which could reduce available inventories elsewhere even if global refined-copper production remains sufficient on paper.

## Crypto lacks the same momentum, but ETF money has not disappeared

The picture in crypto is much less convincing. **Bitcoin is trading around $79,000 on September 7**, without a clear new upward breakout, while the total cryptocurrency market capitalization has weakened over the past 24 hours.

Still, it would be wrong to conclude that institutional capital is simply leaving Bitcoin and moving into copper. The latest completed U.S. trading days for spot Bitcoin ETFs show the opposite.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs ended **September 4 with approximately $174.6 million in net inflows**, after an exceptionally strong **$730.8 million inflow on September 3**. A day earlier, on September 2, net inflows were around $101.1 million, while September 1 recorded a net outflow of $236.5 million.

Together, the first four trading days of September produced approximately **$770 million in net inflows into U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs**. Institutional demand has therefore not disappeared, but Bitcoin’s price has not yet produced a breakout comparable with the one currently visible in copper.

## Gold weakens while an industrial metal breaks records

An even more important signal is coming from within the metals sector itself. If investors were simply fleeing toward metals or traditional safe-haven assets, it would be reasonable to expect both copper and gold to rise strongly at the same time. Instead, the opposite is happening.

The spot price of **gold fell toward approximately $4,413 per ounce on September 7**, after a stronger U.S. employment report increased market expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates again at its September 16 meeting.

Higher interest rates are generally a problem for gold because the metal does not generate interest or income. If investors can earn higher returns from U.S. government bonds or other yield-bearing instruments, the opportunity cost of holding gold rises.

Copper is currently responding to a completely different set of forces. Its price is being supported by **industrial demand, electrification, AI infrastructure, energy grids and supply constraints**. The same macroeconomic factor can therefore be negative for gold without being strong enough to stop copper’s rise.

## Rotation is happening within markets, not only between sectors

Today’s signal is therefore not simply “crypto out, metals in.” The market is far more selective.

Within metals, we have **copper at an all-time high and gold under pressure**. Within crypto, we have weaker short-term price action but continued institutional inflows through U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs. In technology, AI and semiconductors remain one of the dominant investment themes, but part of the same story is now spreading toward the companies and resources required to physically build the infrastructure behind it.

That may be a more important sign of capital rotation than the movement of any single index or asset class. Capital does not need to abandon an entire sector for market leadership to change. It is enough for investors to increasingly favor segments with a better combination of **demand growth, constrained supply and confirmed price momentum**.

## The AI trade is no longer only a technology trade

If the current trend continues, one of the more interesting investment narratives in the next phase of the AI boom could be the shift from digital to physical infrastructure.

The first phase was the race for GPUs. The second was the massive buildout of data centers. The next phase is increasingly about **where the electricity will come from, how it will be transmitted and which materials will be required to build all of that infrastructure**.

Copper has a particularly clear role in this story. Every new data center, power grid, transformer, electric vehicle and large energy project increases demand for a metal whose production cannot be expanded quickly or easily.

That is why today’s copper record may be signaling something broader than just another high in the commodities market: **the AI boom is beginning to reshape physical-resource markets as well.**

## Our take

* **Copper reached a new all-time high of around $14,533 per metric tonne on the London Metal Exchange on September 7.**
* Since the beginning of 2026, copper has gained approximately **16%**, supported by long-term demand from electrification, power grids, electric vehicles, data centers and AI infrastructure.
* Global copper mine production fell by around **1.1% in the first half of the year**, adding to concerns about future supply.
* **Bitcoin has not yet produced a comparable price breakout** and is once again trading around the $79,000–$80,000 area.
* That does not mean institutional capital is leaving crypto: U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately **$770 million in total net inflows during the first four trading days of September**.
* **Gold is not participating in the same rally as copper**, showing that this is not a broad capital move into the entire metals sector.
* The key divide is currently between **industrial metals**, supported by physical demand and supply constraints, and **precious metals**, which are more sensitive to interest rates and monetary policy.
* The broader signal is that the **AI investment cycle is expanding from chips and software toward power infrastructure, energy grids and the raw materials needed to build them**.
