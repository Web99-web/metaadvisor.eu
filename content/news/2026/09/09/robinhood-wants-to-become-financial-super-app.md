# Robinhood is no longer just a trading app: it wants to become a financial super app

**Robinhood has once again drawn strong attention from Wall Street in recent days, but the growing interest in HOOD is not only about rising markets or crypto. The company is simultaneously expanding prediction markets, entering IPO underwriting, developing AI agents that can trade for users, building its own blockchain and trying to bring an increasingly large part of its customers’ financial lives onto one platform.**

What looked a decade ago like a simple mobile app for commission-free stock trading increasingly resembles an attempt to build a **financial super app**. Robinhood now brings together stocks, options, crypto, futures, prediction markets, retirement accounts, credit cards, wealth management, AI trading, tokenized assets, DeFi and even IPO underwriting.

## Robinhood already has something banks struggle to buy – young users

One of Robinhood’s biggest advantages is not a new feature but the structure of its user base. According to company data from the end of 2025, the **median age of a Robinhood user is just 36**, while **76% of funded customers are 45 or younger**; about 29% are 29 or younger, while another 47% are between 30 and 45.

That is an extremely valuable position because traditional banks, brokers and wealth-management firms have spent years trying to attract younger customers before they begin earning more and building meaningful wealth. Robinhood already has that audience on its platform and had around **28.5 million funded customers** and approximately **$355 billion in total platform assets** by the end of July 2026.

## The strategy is simple: grow up with the customer

Robinhood no longer wants to be the app someone opens at 20 or 25 to buy a first stock, a few hundred dollars of Bitcoin or a few options contracts. The company wants that same user to remain inside the Robinhood ecosystem decades later, when they begin using retirement products, larger investment accounts, credit cards, wealth management and other more sophisticated financial services.

That is why Robinhood does not need to sell every new product to an entirely new audience. It can offer them directly to tens of millions of existing users who already have accounts, know the app and often belong to a generation that is only now entering its higher-income and wealth-building years.

## Wall Street has started taking HOOD more seriously

A wave of positive analyst actions arrived in early September. **Morgan Stanley upgraded Robinhood from Equal Weight to Overweight and raised its price target from $124 to $150**, Piper Sandler raised its target to $145, Scotiabank initiated coverage with an Outperform rating and a $136 target, while Goldman Sachs later increased its own target from $124 to **$142**.

The market reacted strongly, with HOOD jumping around **16.6% in a single day on September 3 and closing at $124.72**. Part of the move was tied to a broader recovery in crypto, but analysts are increasingly pointing to product expansion, growth in prediction markets, Gold subscriptions, lending, credit products and wealth management as reasons for optimism.

## Prediction markets have become one of Robinhood’s biggest new businesses

One of the most striking parts of Robinhood’s expansion is **prediction markets**, where users trade contracts tied to the outcome of events. In the second quarter of 2026 alone, Robinhood processed a record **13.6 billion event contracts**, more than ten times the level from a year earlier, while the segment generated **$156 million in revenue**.

That means event contracts produced more Q2 revenue than crypto trading, which generated around $100 million, and more than equities trading at $129 million. Prediction markets are no longer an experimental add-on inside the app but one of the more important revenue streams within Robinhood’s trading business.

## The new Crypto.com and OG.com deal expands the market further

Robinhood strengthened that segment again on September 8 through a partnership with **Crypto.com and OG.com**, under which part of its football event contracts will be routed through their regulated infrastructure. Robinhood will also receive **equity stakes in Crypto.com and OG.com** as part of the multi-year agreement, showing that it does not want to act only as a distribution channel for prediction markets but also wants financial exposure to the infrastructure behind them.

Robinhood already uses several platforms for event contracts, including Kalshi, ForecastEX and Rothera, a CFTC-licensed exchange and clearinghouse launched through a joint venture with Susquehanna. Goldman Sachs cited the rapid growth of Rothera and the broader prediction-market business as one reason for increasing its valuation of Robinhood.

## Rothera shows how fast a new business line can scale

Rothera launched only in June but quickly became an important part of Robinhood’s prediction-market infrastructure. Goldman Sachs estimated in early September that Rothera had already reached around **$150 million in annualized revenue**, an unusually rapid start for a new platform.

Prediction markets also give Robinhood another way to drive user activity without relying only on stock or crypto price movements. Football seasons, elections and major global events can generate entirely new waves of trading even when traditional financial markets are relatively quiet.

## Robinhood is entering IPO underwriting for the first time

Almost at the same time as the Crypto.com deal, Robinhood made another important move and secured its **first official role as an IPO underwriter**. The company is participating in the planned Nasdaq listing of smart-ring maker **Oura**, alongside traditional investment banks including Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley.

For Robinhood, that is a highly symbolic shift because it is no longer only a platform where retail investors can buy shares after a company goes public. It is now trying to participate in the process that brings companies to the public market itself, while CEO Vlad Tenev has openly discussed giving ordinary investors greater access to IPO allocations that have traditionally been dominated by institutions.

## AI agents can already trade for users

Robinhood launched **Agentic Trading** in May, allowing AI agents to be connected to a dedicated trading account. The user defines capital, strategy and safety limits, while the agent can analyze data and execute orders without requiring manual confirmation for every transaction.

By the end of the second quarter, nearly **100,000 users had opened Agentic Trading accounts**, holding more than **$100 million in assets under custody**. Robinhood then expanded the concept toward crypto as well, with plans to let selected AI models connect to platform data and trading tools.

## AI trading introduces a new type of risk

Robinhood is also clear that an AI agent can make mistakes, misinterpret instructions, react to incomplete data or execute a strategy that creates large losses under certain market conditions. Agentic Trading is therefore not a conventional chatbot that simply answers questions, but a system that can be given the ability to execute real financial transactions.

That makes it one of the more interesting examples of where retail trading is heading. Tools that were once largely associated with professional quantitative trading teams are becoming available to ordinary users through a mobile app, but greater automation also creates a much stronger need for risk controls.

## Robinhood now has its own blockchain

The company moved even further away from the role of a traditional broker in 2026 by launching **Robinhood Chain**, its own Layer 2 blockchain built on Arbitrum technology. The public mainnet launched on July 1, and Robinhood is positioning it as infrastructure for financial services, DeFi and tokenized real-world assets.

At the same event, Robinhood introduced a new generation of **Stock Tokens**, available through Robinhood Wallet in more than 120 countries depending on local rules. These tokens provide economic exposure to securities and enable 24/7 blockchain trading, but they do not represent the same legal ownership or shareholder rights as directly holding the underlying stock.

## Crypto is no longer just about buying Bitcoin in the app

Robinhood is now developing its own blockchain, Stock Tokens, DeFi lending through Robinhood Earn, staking, perpetual futures and a non-custodial wallet at the same time. In Europe, it has also expanded perpetual futures beyond crypto to instruments linked to gold, silver, oil, ETFs and foreign-exchange pairs, while Robinhood Chain is designed as infrastructure where traditional and decentralized finance can increasingly overlap.

That is a very different strategy from Robinhood’s early years, when crypto was simply another category inside the trading app. The company is now trying to control more of the financial stack – from the app and customer account to trading infrastructure, blockchain and tokenized assets.

## Financial results show the expansion is already making money

Robinhood generated record revenue of **$1.31 billion in the second quarter of 2026**, up 32% from a year earlier, while net income rose 48% to $573 million. Diluted EPS reached $0.62, net deposits hit a record $22 billion, and Robinhood Gold subscribers climbed to a record **4.8 million**.

Even more importantly, the company now says it has **13 different business lines that have each reached at least $100 million in annualized revenue**. That shows Robinhood is gradually reducing its dependence on how much retail customers happen to trade stocks or crypto in any particular month.

## Robinhood wants to be a broker, bank and tech platform at the same time

Taken together, Robinhood now spans equities and options trading, crypto, futures, prediction markets, retirement accounts, Gold subscriptions, credit cards, cash management, advisory, private markets, AI agents, tokenized assets, blockchain, DeFi and IPO underwriting. That is a much broader business model than the one the company had when it went public in 2021.

The advantage of that model is that one user can conduct a growing share of their financial activity without leaving the platform. The downside is that Robinhood is simultaneously moving into more regulatory-sensitive areas, from prediction markets and derivatives to tokenized securities and AI agents that can execute trades.

## The biggest advantage may not be the products, but the users

Robinhood’s greatest long-term value may not come from any single new product. The company already has a huge base of relatively young investors and is now trying to increase the number of services those same users consume as their incomes, investments and overall wealth grow over time.

If that strategy works, a user who originally opened Robinhood for commission-free trading or a few hundred dollars in crypto could ten or twenty years later hold a retirement account on the same platform, use a credit card, receive wealth-management services, trade through AI agents and access private markets. That is why Robinhood increasingly looks like an attempt to build a **financial operating system for a new generation of investors**.

## The next major product wave arrives at the end of September

Robinhood has already announced **HOOD Summit '26 for September 29 and 30 in Houston**, where Vlad Tenev and other company executives are expected to unveil another round of products and updates for active traders. After the number of launches already seen this year, that suggests the current expansion is far from finished.

The key question is therefore no longer whether Robinhood can add another feature to the app, but whether it can turn dozens of different products into a sustainable and regulatorily manageable ecosystem. Wall Street increasingly appears to believe that it can, but the breadth of that ambition also means that regulatory setbacks, weaker trading activity or problems with new products could have a growing impact across the entire platform.
