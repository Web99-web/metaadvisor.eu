# Apple unveils iPhone Duo: is the market finally ready for a foldable iPhone?

**Apple unveiled the iPhone Duo on September 9, its first foldable iPhone and one of the biggest changes to the iPhone’s physical design since the original model was introduced. Closed, it has a 5.4-inch outer display, while opening the device reveals a 7.6-inch inner display designed for multitasking, video, gaming, apps and Apple Intelligence features. The starting price in the United States is $1,999.**

The iPhone Duo presentation was also the first major Apple product event under **John Ternus**, who took over as CEO from Tim Cook on September 1. Just days after taking charge of the company, the new Apple chief introduced one of the most important new product formats the company has launched in years — its first foldable iPhone. <a href="https://metaadvisor.eu/news/john-ternus-ceo-leads-apple-from-september-1-2026/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">John Ternus CEO leads Apple from 1 September 2026</a>

Pre-orders begin on October 16, with sales starting on October 23 in more than 70 markets. Apple will expand availability to another 28 countries and regions on October 30. The iPhone Duo is available in Star White and Night Sky, with 256 GB, 512 GB, 1 TB or 2 TB of storage.

## Two displays, but the same content format

When opened, the iPhone Duo features a **7.6-inch Super Retina XDR OLED display** with a resolution of 1878 x 2670 pixels, while the outer Super Retina XDR display measures 5.4 inches with a resolution of 1398 x 2034 pixels.

Apple designed both displays with the same aspect ratio so content can scale proportionally when the device is opened or closed without major changes to the layout. Both support ProMotion with an adaptive refresh rate of up to 120 Hz, Always-On display functionality and outdoor peak brightness of up to 3,000 nits.

The inner display has a nano-texture finish designed to reduce reflections and make the crease less noticeable. Apple has also hidden the FaceTime camera beneath the inner display, while support for Apple Pencil with USB-C is expected later in the year.

## Very thin when open, noticeably thicker when closed

Fully opened, the iPhone Duo measures 164.6 mm wide, 117.8 mm high and just **5.2 mm thick**, which Apple describes as making it the thinnest iPhone to date.

When closed, however, it is 84.1 mm wide and **11.3 mm thick**, while the total weight is **254 grams**. That makes it a very different device from a conventional iPhone when used with one hand.

For comparison, the standard iPhone 16 is 71.6 mm wide, 7.8 mm thick and weighs 170 grams. The iPhone Duo is therefore 84 grams heavier — almost **50% heavier than the iPhone 16** — and more than a centimetre wider when closed.

Apple says the closed Duo is designed for one-handed use and has dimensions similar to a passport. How noticeable its width, weight and thickness feel in everyday use will be one of the more interesting questions for hands-on testing.

## A20 Pro and iOS built for the larger screen

The iPhone Duo is powered by the new **A20 Pro**, the same chip Apple uses in the iPhone 18 Pro models. Built on a 2-nanometre process, it combines a 6-core CPU, 7-core GPU and dual 16-core Neural Engine for on-device AI processing.

Apple claims up to 20% faster CPU performance and a GPU that is up to 40% faster and more efficient than the A19 Pro, while Neural Engine compute performance has doubled. Because of the thin chassis, Apple has also introduced a new cooling system with a specially designed vapor chamber.

A larger display would have limited value without changes to the software. **iOS 27 has been specifically adapted for the iPhone Duo** and introduces Split View, allowing two apps to be displayed side by side on an iPhone for the first time.

Users can also open two windows of the same app, such as two Safari pages, quickly move apps between the two sides of the display and save app pairs for later use. Apple’s own apps have been adapted for the new format, while third-party apps already taking advantage of the larger display include Netflix, Zoom and Slack.

## Siri AI and Apple Intelligence get more room

Apple is also using the larger inner display for the latest generation of Apple Intelligence features. Siri AI can remain open on one part of the screen while the user continues viewing content or working in another app.

The new Siri uses personal context, information currently visible on the screen and content from messages, emails, photos and other sources to answer questions or carry out tasks. Apple Intelligence also adds new photo-editing, image-generation and Safari webpage-change monitoring capabilities.

Apple continues to emphasise on-device processing and Private Cloud Compute as key parts of its privacy strategy for AI features.

## The cameras take advantage of both displays

The iPhone Duo features a **48 MP Fusion Main camera** capable of shooting images at up to 48 MP, a standard 24 MP mode and a 2x telephoto option in optical quality. It is joined by a 48 MP Fusion Ultra Wide camera with macro photography support.

The foldable format enables several functions that are not possible on a conventional iPhone. Users can take selfies with the main rear camera while framing the shot on the outer display, while Duo Preview lets the person being photographed see the composition on the external screen.

The phone can also be partially opened and placed on a flat surface for photography or video without an additional tripod. The main camera supports **4K video at up to 120 fps in Dolby Vision**.

Apple is also introducing Smart Take, where on-device AI models analyse the scene and attempt to capture an image automatically when everyone is ready, along with several new FaceTime modes that make use of both displays.

## StandBy no longer requires a charger

The foldable design also brings an expanded StandBy mode. When the Duo is positioned on a surface, it can display the clock, photos, weather, calendar or widgets on either the inner or outer display even when it is not connected to a charger.

The ability to position the device at different angles can be useful for video calls, watching content, taking photos and videos or keeping information visible on a desk.

Touch ID is integrated into the side button, while the phone can also be unlocked with an Apple Watch. Apple retains Camera Control, while the new Siri Camera mode allows Siri to analyse what the camera sees and answer questions or initiate actions based on that information.

## Two batteries working as one

Because of the foldable construction, Apple has placed one battery in each half of the phone, but the software manages them as a single battery system.

Apple claims up to **31 hours of video playback using the inner display**, up to 44 hours using the outer display and up to 24 hours of overall use per charge when both screens are used roughly equally.

Wired charging can take the battery to around 50% in approximately 20 minutes, while MagSafe or Qi2 wireless charging reaches 50% in about 30 minutes.

The Duo is **eSIM-only worldwide**, with no physical SIM card slot. Apple’s new N1 chip adds Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread, while the C2 modem handles cellular connectivity.

## Is 256 GB enough for a $1,999 phone?

The entry-level iPhone Duo comes with **256 GB of storage**, while higher-priced versions offer 512 GB, 1 TB or 2 TB.

For many users who rely heavily on iCloud and streaming, 256 GB may be sufficient. At the same time, Apple is positioning the device for 4K120 Dolby Vision video, large games, photography, AI applications, multitasking and content creation.

In that context, 256 GB feels relatively limited for a device starting at **$1,999**. Users who shoot large amounts of photos and video or prefer to keep more content stored locally may find 512 GB a much more practical starting point.

## Apple enters a market that has existed for seven years

Apple is not the first company trying to convince users that a smartphone opening into a much larger display makes sense. **Samsung introduced the first Galaxy Fold in 2019, almost seven years before the iPhone Duo.**

The beginning of the category was not trouble-free. After some early Galaxy Fold review units experienced problems involving the display, hinge area and protective layer, Samsung postponed the planned launch in April 2019 to reinforce the construction and protection of the device.

Sales began later that year, and Samsung continued refining the hinge, displays, dimensions, cameras and software through subsequent generations. Honor, Motorola, Google, Xiaomi and other manufacturers have since entered the foldable smartphone market as well.

Apple is therefore not creating a new category. It is entering a market where other manufacturers have spent years solving the problems that naturally arise when an OLED display, batteries and an entire smartphone structure need to fold thousands of times.

## Foldables are growing but still represent only 1.6% of the market

Despite years of development and significant media attention, foldable smartphones still account for only a very small part of the overall smartphone market.

According to Counterpoint Research, around **19.6 million foldable smartphones were shipped globally during the full year 2025**, an increase of 18% from the previous year.

However, those 19.6 million devices represented only **1.6% of all global smartphone shipments in 2025**.

That number is important when assessing Apple’s entry. Foldable smartphones are no longer prototypes or technology experiments, but almost seven years after the first Galaxy Fold they still have not become the standard choice for most buyers.

There are signs that interest is increasing. Samsung’s **Galaxy Z Fold7** exceeded 250,000 units sold in Western Europe during its first four weeks on the market. That was more than double the Fold6 in the comparable period and almost 70% higher than Samsung’s previous foldable record holder, the Fold4.

## Can Apple finally shake up the foldable market?

That is why Apple’s arrival could matter to the wider market far more than the sales of a single new iPhone model.

Apple is entering at a time when foldable displays are thinner and more reliable, hinges are more advanced, software is better adapted to larger screens and manufacturers already have several generations of experience with problems that the first devices were only beginning to uncover.

Counterpoint Research expects global foldable shipments to rise from 19.6 million units in 2025 to around **38.6 million by 2029**, with Apple’s entry seen as one of the major potential catalysts for broader adoption of the category.

The iPhone Duo therefore arrives in an established market — but one that has still failed to make the jump from a relatively small niche into the true mainstream.

Apple no longer needs to prove that a functional foldable smartphone can be built. Samsung and other manufacturers have been doing that for years.

The more important question is: **can Apple convince millions of existing iPhone users that the technology is now mature enough, and that the larger screen justifies the extra weight, thickness and a starting price of nearly $2,000?**

## Our view

* **The iPhone Duo represents one of the biggest changes to the iPhone format since the original device was introduced.**
* Its main advantage is the **7.6-inch inner display**, which folds into a considerably more compact device with a 5.4-inch outer screen.
* Open, it is only **5.2 mm thick**, but closed it measures 11.3 mm and weighs **254 grams**, almost 50% more than the iPhone 16.
* Apple has not simply folded an existing iPhone: **iOS 27, Split View, two windows from the same app, Siri AI and adapted applications** are designed to make real use of the larger workspace.
* The two displays also open new possibilities for photography, video calls, StandBy and shooting without a separate tripod.
* **256 GB of base storage feels limited for a $1,999 device**, particularly with 4K120 video and its positioning at the very top of the premium market.
* Apple is entering the foldable smartphone market almost seven years after the first Samsung Galaxy Fold, whose original launch had to be postponed following problems discovered in early devices.
* After several generations of development, foldable technology is significantly more mature, but the entire category still accounted for only **1.6% of global smartphone shipments in 2025**.
* Global foldable shipments nevertheless grew 18% in 2025, while stronger sales of newer Samsung models suggest that consumer interest is gradually increasing.
* **The biggest question surrounding the iPhone Duo is therefore not whether Apple can build a good foldable phone, but whether Apple can turn a technological niche into a device category desired by a much broader premium audience.**
