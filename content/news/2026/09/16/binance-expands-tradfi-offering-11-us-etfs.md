# Binance Goes Deeper Into TradFi With a New Wealth Service Offering 11 US ETFs

**Binance is continuing to expand beyond cryptocurrencies. The platform has launched a new wealth management offering inside Binance Earn that gives eligible users access to 11 ETFs listed on US exchanges, primarily focused on short-term US government bonds and higher-quality fixed-income instruments. The key difference from many crypto products is that users are not buying tokenized versions of ETFs, but real ETF shares through traditional brokerage infrastructure.**

The new service further shows how the line between major crypto exchanges and traditional investment platforms is becoming increasingly blurred. Binance already offers access to thousands of US stocks and ETFs and is now trying to present part of that offering through an interface that looks more like wealth management and portfolio building.

## Binance Groups the 11 ETFs by Investment Horizon

The new offering sits inside Binance Earn, but it does not work like a traditional crypto Earn product with a fixed or prominently displayed yield. The ETFs are divided into three categories: **Cash Management**, **Steady Income** and **Yield Enhancement**, depending on the expected investment horizon.

Cash Management is aimed at shorter investment periods, generally up to six months, Steady Income at roughly six to twelve months, while Yield Enhancement is designed for periods longer than one year. The focus is on short-term US Treasury instruments, investment-grade bonds and other products that generally carry lower volatility than stocks or cryptocurrencies.

## These Are Not Tokenized ETFs

The most important detail of the new offering is how the ETFs are purchased. Binance does not issue its own token that merely tracks the value of a fund, and users do not receive a synthetic version of a US ETF. According to Binance, users are buying real ETF shares through its Stock Trading system.

That means the user receives the economic benefits associated with the underlying security, including changes in market value and any applicable cash distributions. Binance primarily acts as the user interface through which the order is placed.

## Nest Trading Routes Orders to Alpaca Securities

Behind the Binance interface is traditional brokerage infrastructure. According to Binance documentation, Nest Trading routes securities orders to Alpaca Securities, a US broker that handles order execution as well as clearing, settlement and custody.

This means the ETF shares do not exist merely as entries inside Binance's crypto infrastructure. The traditional securities remain within regulated brokerage and custody systems, while Binance allows users to access them from an app they may already use for crypto.

This structure also matters when it comes to investor protection. However, users should not automatically assume that every protection available with a direct brokerage account applies in exactly the same way to every Binance Stock Trading account, because specific rights depend on the legal account structure, the user's country and the applicable product terms.

## Binance Already Offers More Than 7,000 Stocks and ETFs

The new wealth offering with 11 ETFs does not mean Binance is only now entering traditional securities markets. During 2026, the platform significantly expanded its Stock Trading service and says eligible users can access more than **7,000 US stocks and ETFs**.

Fractional shares are also available, allowing users to buy only part of a stock or ETF share and invest smaller amounts. During the year, Binance also expanded its TradFi offering into other products, including options on US stocks and ETFs and recurring investment plans.

The new group of 11 ETFs is therefore primarily a curated selection inside Earn. Instead of searching through thousands of securities, users are presented with products associated with cash management, bonds and generating returns through traditional financial markets.

## Availability in the EU Is Not Automatic

The fact that Binance offers US ETFs globally does not mean every one of those products is available to users in every European Union country. Binance states that Stock Trading is only available to users who meet its eligibility requirements and are located in supported jurisdictions.

European retail investors also face an additional hurdle under **PRIIPs** rules, which require investment products offered to retail clients to provide specific documentation, including a Key Information Document. This is one of the main reasons many US-domiciled ETFs have long been unavailable to ordinary European retail investors through traditional brokers.

The actual availability of individual Binance ETF products therefore has to be checked for each account and country separately. Being able to see a product in the app does not automatically mean a user is allowed to buy it in their jurisdiction.

## Cash Management Does Not Mean Risk-Free Savings

Labels such as Cash Management or Steady Income may sound similar to a savings product, but an ETF remains a market-traded security. Its price can change, returns are not guaranteed, and investors may be exposed to interest-rate, credit and currency risk.

ETFs that invest in very short-term US government bonds generally fluctuate far less than stocks or cryptocurrencies, but that does not make them equivalent to a bank deposit. More complex bond strategies may also involve additional risks that cannot be understood simply from the Binance category in which a product appears.

## Binance Wants to Be More Than a Crypto Exchange

The most interesting part of this story is therefore not simply the number of 11 ETFs. Binance is increasingly trying to bring crypto and traditional finance together inside a single app. On the same platform, a user can hold Bitcoin and stablecoins, trade cryptocurrencies and, in supported countries, access US stocks, ETFs, options and other traditional financial instruments.

This is different from earlier visions in which blockchain was expected to completely replace traditional brokerage infrastructure. Binance is now doing almost the opposite: keeping traditional infrastructure in the background while giving crypto users a simpler interface for accessing conventional markets.

If this trend continues, the distinction between a major crypto exchange, an online broker and a wealth management app could become increasingly difficult to draw.
