# ChatGPT under the EU’s strictest DSA oversight: why it is now treated as a search engine

The European Commission officially designated ChatGPT on **August 31, 2026** as a **Very Large Online Search Engine (VLOSE)** under the **Digital Services Act (DSA)**.

On the same day, **Reddit** and **Roblox** were designated as **Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs)**. All three services reported at least **45 million average monthly active users in the European Union**, crossing the threshold that triggers the strictest additional obligations under the DSA.

The ChatGPT decision is especially interesting. The European Commission is no longer treating it only as an AI chatbot or text-generation tool. For DSA purposes, ChatGPT is now officially classified among **very large online search engines**, alongside traditional services such as Google Search and Bing.

## Why ChatGPT has been classified as a search engine

At first glance, the classification may seem unusual. Users do not open ChatGPT in the same way they open Google Search, and many still do not think of it as a traditional search engine.

However, the way people use generative artificial intelligence has changed rapidly. ChatGPT is no longer used only for writing, translation or coding. A growing number of users ask it questions they would previously have typed into a search engine: they look for news, products, explanations, comparisons, recommendations and information about companies, people, products or events.

When a service becomes one of the main ways millions of people find information, the regulatory question is no longer only how the AI model was trained. It also becomes important **how information is found and ranked, which sources are used, what kinds of errors can occur and what impact the answers may have on users**.

That is why the VLOSE status matters. The European Commission is no longer viewing a generative AI answer only as content produced by a model, but also as a way users find information on the internet.

## ChatGPT, Reddit and Roblox were all added on August 31, 2026, but did not receive the same status

It is particularly interesting that **ChatGPT, Reddit and Roblox were all added on the same day**, but did not receive the same regulatory designation.

ChatGPT was classified as a **Very Large Online Search Engine (VLOSE)**, while Reddit and Roblox were designated as **Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs)**.

The difference is more than terminology. A VLOP is a very large online platform through which users publish, share, sell, communicate or consume content and services. A VLOSE is a very large online search engine whose core function is to help users find information.

That is one of the most important messages of the entire decision. **ChatGPT is no longer being treated only as a chatbot from a regulatory perspective.** The EU is treating it as a service that has increasingly taken on the role of internet search.

## Why they are coming under the DSA now

The reason is not an arbitrary political decision, but a user threshold. The DSA provides a special regulatory regime for services that reach at least **45 million average monthly active users in the EU**.

According to the European Commission, ChatGPT reported around **159.1 million average monthly users in the EU**, Reddit around **57.2 million**, and Roblox around **46.6 million**. All three therefore crossed the regulatory threshold. After the official designation, they have four months, until **January 2027**, to fully comply with the additional obligations imposed on VLOPs and VLOSEs.

## What OpenAI will now have to do

For OpenAI, the new designation is not merely an administrative formality. ChatGPT will have to meet additional requirements related to the **assessment and mitigation of systemic risks**. These include risks linked to illegal content, protection of minors, users’ fundamental rights, physical and mental well-being, electoral processes and public security.

OpenAI will have to show that it not only identifies such risks but also takes measures to reduce them. These may include changes to algorithms, additional safety mechanisms, stronger oversight, greater transparency, risk-management processes and other technical or organizational measures.

Very large services are also subject to enhanced oversight by the European Commission. This means the regulator can more directly examine how a company complies with its obligations and manages systemic risks on its service.

## X is on the list, but Grok is not — Google is on the list, while Gemini is not

Another interesting distinction shows that the EU does not automatically treat every product from the same company in the same way.

**X is officially designated as a Very Large Online Platform (VLOP)** and is already under enhanced DSA oversight. However, **Grok is not separately listed as either a VLOP or a VLOSE**. The fact that Grok is integrated into X does not automatically give Grok its own status as a very large platform or search engine.

A similar situation exists **with Google**. Google Search is a VLOSE, while Google services such as YouTube, Google Play, Google Maps and Google Shopping are separately covered as major digital services. **Gemini, however, is not currently listed separately as either a VLOP or a VLOSE.**

This matters because it shows that the European Commission does not take the category “AI” and automatically place every major model into it. It looks at **the specific service, its function, its number of users and how the service is used**.

## That is why ChatGPT is a special case

The comparison with Grok and Gemini makes the importance of ChatGPT’s designation clearer.

X is covered by the DSA, but Grok is not separately designated. Several major Google services are on the list, but Gemini is not. ChatGPT, however, has received its own designation as a **Very Large Online Search Engine**.

That makes ChatGPT the first major generative AI chatbot formally placed by the European Commission into the strictest DSA regime as an online search engine.

From a regulatory perspective, the line between a traditional search engine and a generative AI assistant has started to blur.

## What changes for ordinary users

For users, there will not be a dramatic overnight change. ChatGPT will not suddenly look different because of the new designation, and normal use of the service will not disappear. Most changes will happen in the background. OpenAI will have to document risks more thoroughly, show regulators how it mitigates them and meet stricter requirements for transparency and accountability.

For users, issues such as clearer explanations of certain decisions, complaint procedures, moderation practices, protection of minors and the way algorithmic systems affect what people see may become more important over time.

### But does the DSA really protect the individual user?

This is where the more important question appears. **Facebook has already been under the strictest DSA oversight for years as a Very Large Online Platform (VLOP)**, but that status alone does not mean that a user who loses access to an account will necessarily get to speak with a human being, receive a clear explanation of the decision or get a quick and effective solution.

Metaadvisor.eu is not the only outlet that has written about users whose Facebook and Instagram accounts were blocked or permanently disabled. International media including **CBS News, Nine Australia and Radar PH** have also reported on waves of suspensions, users who say their accounts were disabled without a clear reason and difficulties recovering access, while Meta’s accountability and the effectiveness of regulatory oversight have also been recurring topics in outlets such as **The Guardian**.

A common problem in many such cases is not only the suspension itself, but what follows: communication is often reduced to automated forms, generic replies or processes in which it is extremely difficult to reach an actual human being. Even turning to bodies and mechanisms such as the **Appeals Centre Europe**, an out-of-court dispute settlement body under the DSA, does not necessarily mean that a problem will be resolved quickly or that a user will finally receive a concrete explanation of what happened.

That is why an uncomfortable question is worth asking: **what is the DSA for if the largest platforms can be fined hundreds of millions of euros while an individual user can still spend months trying to find out why an account was disabled and whether any human being reviewed the case at all?**

Large fines against major platforms generate headlines and demonstrate that the European Commission has enforcement powers. But the real value of the DSA should not be measured only by the size of the fines collected. It should also be measured by **whether an ordinary user can receive a clear explanation, a genuine right of appeal and effective protection when an automated system gets something wrong.**

The DSA does not mean that every individual user problem will end up before the European Commission. It does mean, however, that ChatGPT is no longer simply a product whose rules are determined only by the company that built it. It is now a service subject to a specific European regulatory framework.

In other words, the issue is no longer just a handful of isolated user complaints, but a pattern appearing across different markets and being reported by media in multiple countries. That is why this decision is more than another administrative entry on a regulatory list. It raises a broader question: **will enhanced DSA oversight actually mean something for individual people with real problems, or will its effectiveness continue to be measured mainly through regulatory proceedings and large financial penalties?**

## Why the decision matters right now

Generative artificial intelligence has moved in just a few years from experimental technology to an everyday tool for work, education, programming, research, shopping and finding information. The larger a service becomes, the more important the consequences of its systemic errors become.

A wrong answer from a small tool may affect only a few users. But if a system used by more than one hundred million people in the EU systematically produces incorrect, manipulative or harmful results, the consequences are no longer only a technical problem. They become a regulatory issue.

That is why the DSA is designed so that the largest digital services do not wait for a major incident, but instead assess systemic risks in advance and show what they are doing to reduce them.

## ChatGPT is no longer just a chatbot

The most important part of the European Commission’s decision may not be simply that ChatGPT has received another regulatory status. What matters more is **how the EU has classified it**.

It was not merely placed among very large digital platforms. It was classified as a **Very Large Online Search Engine (VLOSE)**.

That is a form of regulatory recognition of a change that is already happening among users: more and more people no longer begin their search for information by typing a few keywords into Google, but instead ask an AI assistant a full question and expect a ready-made answer.

ChatGPT is therefore no longer only a tool that generates text. For the European Commission, it is now also **one of the largest ways people in Europe find information**.
