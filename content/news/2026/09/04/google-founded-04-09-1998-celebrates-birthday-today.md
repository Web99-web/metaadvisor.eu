# Google was founded 28 years ago: it all began with a $100,000 check for a company that did not yet exist

If there is a startup story that sounds as if it could only have happened in America, it is difficult to find a better example than the beginning of Google. Exactly 28 years ago, on **September 4, 1998**, Google Inc. was officially founded, creating a company that would later fundamentally change the way we search for information, use the internet, watch video, read email and, today, interact with artificial intelligence.

But the story before that September 4 may be even more interesting than everything that came later. Google did not have a huge campus, billions of users or a market value measured in trillions of dollars. In fact, just days earlier, it did not even have a company or a corporate bank account.

What it did have was two Stanford students, a search engine that worked differently from much of what existed at the time, and an investor who, after a short demonstration, walked to his car, took out his checkbook and wrote a check for **$100,000**.

There was only one problem: the check was made out to **Google Inc.**, and Google Inc. did not yet exist.

## Before Google, there was BackRub

The story begins at Stanford University. **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** met in the mid-1990s and, according to Google's own account of its history, reportedly disagreed about almost everything when they first met. By the following year, however, they had started working together on a project that would fundamentally change the internet.

Their first search engine was called **BackRub**. Instead of primarily counting how many times a particular word appeared on a web page, the system tried to evaluate the importance of a page based on the links pointing to it. The idea was simple but revolutionary: a link from one website to another could be treated as a kind of recommendation, while a recommendation from an important website should carry more weight.

That research eventually led to PageRank and a search engine that began producing significantly more useful results than many of its competitors. BackRub soon received a new name – Google, derived from the mathematical term **googol**, meaning the number 1 followed by 100 zeros.

The google.com domain was registered in 1997, but **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** still did not have the company we now associate with that name. They were working from university rooms, developing the technology and trying to find a way to finance the project and turn it into a real business.

## A $100,000 check for a company that did not exist

The decisive moment came in August 1998. Stanford professor **David Cheriton** arranged a meeting where **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** could demonstrate their project to **Andy Bechtolsheim**, a technology entrepreneur, investor and one of the co-founders of Sun Microsystems.

**Andy Bechtolsheim** was not simply a random investor being shown a polished presentation by two students. He was an experienced engineer and entrepreneur who understood networks, computers and the way technology products could scale. When Google was demonstrated to him, he quickly recognized the potential of the system.

According to Stanford's account, **Bechtolsheim** listened to how the business model could work through relevant advertising alongside search results, ran the numbers and concluded that the project made sense. He did not spend months conducting due diligence or wait for the two founders to build a perfect corporate structure first.

He walked to his car, returned with his checkbook and wrote **$100,000 to “Google, Inc.”**

The problem was almost comical. **Google Inc. had not yet been incorporated.** There was not even a corporate bank account where the check could be deposited.

That check became one of the reasons **Page** and **Brin** now had to move quickly and do something they had not yet done: formally create the company.

## They celebrated the investment with breakfast at Burger King

Today it is difficult to imagine the founders of one of the world's largest technology companies holding a $100,000 check that they could not even cash. Even better is the detail of how they celebrated the fact that one of the best-known technology investors of the era had just decided to finance their project.

**Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** wanted to celebrate the $100,000 check, so they went to Burger King for breakfast. According to **Page's** later recollection, they wanted something tasty, unhealthy and cheap, and Burger King seemed appropriate. There was no luxury restaurant, corporate event or champagne photo opportunity. There were two young researchers, a check that could not yet be cashed and breakfast at a fast-food restaurant. That is precisely why the episode captures the early Silicon Valley so well. The money was not a reward for an already proven company. It was a bet on an idea, technology and the people who had built it.

The check had to wait until Google had a legal structure and a bank account. Only then could the $100,000 actually become capital for the company that was just beginning to take shape.

## Why is September 4 Google's real founding date?

**Google Inc. was incorporated on September 4, 1998.** Google itself gives this date when discussing its history. That is why September 4 can be considered the company's actual legal founding date.

The investment from **Andy Bechtolsheim** arrived shortly beforehand and became one of the key reasons **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** formalized the project they had been developing at Stanford. Once the company existed, they could open an account, deposit the check and begin building something that was no longer simply a university project.

Soon afterward, the team moved from university rooms into its first real office – a garage in Menlo Park rented to them by **Susan Wojcicki**. She would later become one of Google's first employees and, many years later, CEO of YouTube.

The garage eventually became one of the best-known symbols of startup culture. Interestingly, however, neither the garage nor September 4 became the date Google publicly celebrates today as its traditional birthday.

## For years, Google did not seem to know when its birthday was

This is where the story becomes unusually entertaining: **Google itself was not consistent during its early years about when its birthday actually was**.

Google's own Doodle archives show an almost unbelievable sequence of different dates. Its fourth birthday was celebrated on **September 27, 2002**, the fifth on **September 8, 2003**, the sixth on **September 7, 2004**, and the seventh on **September 26, 2005**.

From 2006 onward, Google began consistently using **September 27**, and over time that date became the traditional official day of its birthday celebrations.

Google later acknowledged just how unusual the history of its birthday is. In a post published on September 4, 2018, the company stated that Google Inc. had been incorporated on September 4 but that it had already been celebrating its birthday on September 27 for more than a decade. Even the title of that post referred to “20-ish years of Google,” effectively acknowledging that even the company that built the world's dominant search engine does not have a completely simple answer to the question of when its own birthday is.

So Google has one date on its incorporation documents and another date when the birthday cake appears.

**September 4, 1998** is the date the company legally came into existence. **September 27** became the traditional date of the public celebration.

## Is Yahoo the reason for September 27?

One of the most common explanations for the choice of September 27 takes us back to a time when the internet looked completely different from today.

Younger users may find it difficult to imagine now, but **Yahoo** was once one of the biggest internet brands in the world. Yahoo was not simply a website people occasionally opened for email. It was a major portal, a gateway to the internet for millions of users and one of the leading players in online search.

In 2005, competition over the size and quality of search indexes was an important part of the battle between search engines. In September of that year, as part of the celebrations for its seventh birthday, Google announced a major expansion of its web index.

The company said the new Google index was **1,000 times larger than its original index** and **more than three times larger than that of any other search engine**.

That moment is often associated with the later establishment of September 27 as Google's birthday. According to this interpretation, the date began to symbolically represent the moment when Google demonstrated just how far it had moved ahead of its competitors, including the then enormously important Yahoo.

However, it is important to distinguish between an interesting historical connection and a confirmed fact. **Google has never officially stated that it chose September 27 specifically because it had beaten Yahoo in the search-engine battle.** It is therefore more accurate to say that the choice of the date is often associated with the major search milestone of 2005, while the official explanation for why September 27 permanently became Google's birthday remains unclear.

## In 2015, Google received a new parent company – Alphabet

The Google that **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** founded in 1998 eventually expanded far beyond an internet search engine. The company developed Gmail, Google Maps, Chrome, Android and Google Cloud, acquired YouTube and began investing in projects increasingly removed from its original internet business.

That is why the founders carried out a major corporate restructuring in 2015 and created **Alphabet Inc.** as the new parent company. Alphabet replaced Google Inc. as the publicly traded company, while Google became its wholly owned subsidiary. The idea was to separate Google's core businesses – Search, advertising, YouTube, Android, Chrome and other major products – from other projects and companies such as Waymo, Calico and experimental technology ventures. **Sundar Pichai** became CEO of Google, while **Larry Page** became CEO of Alphabet. **Pichai** later took over the leadership of both Google and Alphabet.

## From a garage to a company that changed the internet

After the incorporation, things began moving quickly. **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** now had capital, Google moved from university rooms into the garage rented by **Susan Wojcicki**, and its new method of ranking websites began attracting more and more users.

What is particularly striking is how little today's Google and the wider Alphabet group resemble the project in which **Andy Bechtolsheim** invested the first $100,000. There was no Gmail, Google Maps, Android, Chrome, YouTube under Google, Google Cloud or Gemini. There was no Alphabet parent company either – that would only appear 17 years later. What existed was primarily one idea: the internet would become so large that finding the right information had to become better.

At a time when many internet portals were trying to keep users on their own websites for as long as possible, Google took a different approach. Its job was to show users, as quickly as possible, where the information they were looking for could be found. That difference would later become the foundation of one of the most valuable business models in internet history.

## Above all, an American story

The story of **Larry Page**, **Sergey Brin** and **Andy Bechtolsheim** almost perfectly captures what has long been described as the American Dream, particularly in the world of technology and Silicon Valley. Someone has an idea that has not yet become a large company, and someone else recognizes its potential quickly enough to be willing to risk their own money immediately.

**Bechtolsheim** did not wait for Google to have years of financial results, massive sales, hundreds of employees or proven market dominance. He saw the product, understood the problem **Page** and **Brin** were trying to solve and wrote a check for $100,000.

Even more remarkably, he made it out to **Google Inc.**, a company that did not yet exist.

That single scene contains almost the entire classic American startup story: two young people have an idea, manage to show it to the right person, and the investor does not say, “come back when you're bigger,” but decides to invest immediately.

Twenty-eight years later, that detail may still best illustrate just how far Google's beginnings were from the company we know today. They could not even immediately cash their first major investment check because the company named on it had not yet been incorporated. And **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** celebrated the whole thing with breakfast at Burger King.

## Our take

* **Google Inc. was officially incorporated on September 4, 1998**, making today's date the company's actual legal founding date.
* Just days earlier, **Andy Bechtolsheim** wrote **Larry Page** and **Sergey Brin** a **$100,000 check made out to “Google, Inc.”**, even though no company by that name yet existed.
* They could not immediately cash the check because Google had neither a legal company structure nor a corporate bank account, and the investment helped accelerate the formal incorporation.
* The meeting was arranged with the help of Stanford professor **David Cheriton**, and **Bechtolsheim** decided to invest very quickly after seeing the demonstration.
* **Page** and **Brin** celebrated their first major investment check with breakfast at Burger King.
* After incorporation, Google moved into the Menlo Park garage rented by **Susan Wojcicki**, which became the company's first real office.
* Google celebrated its birthday on several different dates during its early years – **September 27, 2002; September 8, 2003; September 7, 2004; and September 26, 2005**.
* From 2006 onward, **September 27** became the traditional date of Google's birthday celebrations, even though the company itself later confirmed that it had been incorporated on September 4.
* The choice of September 27 is often associated with Google's major 2005 search-index expansion and its rivalry with competitors such as Yahoo, but **Google has never officially confirmed that this was the reason the date was chosen**.
* Perhaps the most important part of the story is what happened before the success: **two students, one new idea and an investor willing to write a $100,000 check for a company that did not yet exist.**
