# Brent Nears $100 as WTI Surges and Saudi Attacks Raise Supply Fears

**Oil prices are rising sharply again. Brent reached an intraday high of $99.46 per barrel on Tuesday, September 8, while U.S. benchmark WTI climbed to $94.73. Around noon Central European time, Brent was trading near $98.39, up 1.43% on the day, while WTI stood at approximately $93.73, or 2.46% above the previous close.**

## Brent Tests the $100 Level Again

For Brent, the key focus is now the psychologically important **$100 per barrel level**. The intraday high of $99.46 pushed the European benchmark to its highest level since July 24, while WTI at $94.73 reached its highest level since June 8.

The move shows that the market is once again pricing a larger geopolitical risk premium into oil. In addition to actual disruptions affecting part of global supply, traders are increasingly paying for the risk that the conflict in the Middle East could escalate further.

## Attacks on Saudi Energy Facilities Increase the Risk

Fresh upward pressure followed attacks by **Iran-aligned Houthis on energy facilities and other targets in Saudi Arabia**. Parts of the energy infrastructure were temporarily disrupted, while Saudi authorities described the attacks as a serious escalation.

At the same time, tensions between the **United States and Iran** remain elevated following new strikes involving tankers and military targets. The combination of physical disruptions and the possibility of a broader conflict has once again become the main driver of oil prices.

## The Strait of Hormuz Remains the Critical Chokepoint

The situation in the **Strait of Hormuz** remains particularly important. Before the conflict, roughly one-fifth of global daily oil and LNG flows passed through the waterway.

Shipping traffic has slowed again following Iranian threats of retaliation for new U.S. strikes. As long as tanker flows remain well below normal levels, the market is unlikely to fully remove the geopolitical risk premium currently embedded in Brent and WTI prices.

## Why Has Brent Not Broken Above $100 Yet?

Despite significant disruptions, part of the global market has so far been able to compensate for missing supply. Producers outside OPEC, particularly the **United States, Canada and Guyana**, are increasing output, while Gulf countries are using alternative export routes to bypass some of the problems affecting Hormuz and the Red Sea.

At the same time, weaker demand, particularly from China, is limiting the pace of the price increase. This combination of additional supply and softer demand has so far kept Brent below $100, even as the physical oil market shows clear signs of tightness.

## The $100 Level Is Becoming a Key Market Signal

The next major question is whether Brent can **break decisively above $100 and remain there**. A confirmed move above that level would no longer look like a short-lived response to a single headline. It could instead signal that the market expects global supply disruptions to persist for longer.

Further oil price increases could also revive inflationary pressures, increase transport and energy costs and make interest-rate decisions more difficult for central banks.

## Our View

* **Brent reached $99.46 per barrel, placing it just below the psychologically important $100 level.**
* **WTI reached $94.73**, its highest level since early June.
* New attacks on **Saudi energy facilities** have increased geopolitical risk.
* Traffic through the **Strait of Hormuz remains well below normal levels**, keeping the physical oil market tight.
* Additional production from the United States, Canada and Guyana, together with alternative export routes, has so far prevented an even sharper rise.
* **A sustained move in Brent above $100** would be an important new signal for energy markets, inflation and broader financial markets.
