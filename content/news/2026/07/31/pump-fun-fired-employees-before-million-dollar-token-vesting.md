# Pump.fun Fired Employees Before Million-Dollar Token Vesting

Employees of the Pump.fun platform were reportedly fired only months before they were due to become entitled to PUMP tokens worth millions of dollars. At least one person was allegedly allocated an amount of tokens valued in the seven-figure range.

The story was first published by crypto outlet Sandmark, which cited token allocation documents, emails and recordings from an internal meeting. Cointelegraph and other crypto publications later reported the main claims from Sandmark’s investigation.

## Layoffs Two Months Before the First Vesting Date

According to Sandmark, some Pump.fun employees signed agreements in mid-June 2025 under which the first 25% of their allocated PUMP tokens would unlock after one year.

However, the employees were reportedly fired in early April 2026, approximately two months before they were due to become entitled to the first portion of the tokens.

Pump.fun co-founder Noah Tweedale reportedly said during an internal meeting that the company had grown too quickly and could no longer operate fast and efficiently enough. The exact number of employees affected by the first round of layoffs was not disclosed.

According to the report, dismissed employees were offered severance equal to one week of salary for every month worked at the company. However, the termination of their employment meant they lost rights to tokens that had not yet vested.

## One Allocation Was Reportedly Worth More Than $1 Million

Sandmark claims that at least one employee had been allocated an amount of PUMP tokens that would have been worth more than $1 million at the market price at the time of reporting.

That does not necessarily mean the employee already legally owned the tokens. Vesting agreements commonly make token transfers conditional on remaining employed until a predetermined date. If an employee leaves or is dismissed before that date, the unvested portion is usually forfeited.

Still, the timing of the layoffs raises questions about whether business reasons were the only motive for ending the employment relationships, or whether the company avoided transferring extremely valuable tokens to employees.

Pump.fun and its co-founders have not publicly responded to the allegations.

## Former Employees Report Another Round of Layoffs

A profile appeared on X claiming to represent more than 40 former Pump.fun employees. According to their statements, another round of layoffs took place in mid-July, around the beginning of a broader PUMP token unlock.

One person claims they were fired only one day before their tokens were due to begin vesting.

These claims have not been independently confirmed. Sandmark explicitly stated that it could not verify the identities of all individuals behind the profile or every specific claim regarding dismissal and vesting dates.

It is therefore important to distinguish between documented information relating to the first round of layoffs and later allegations made by former employees on social media.

## PUMP Tokens Began Unlocking in July

Pump.fun began its first major token unlock for the team and early investors in July 2026, after the one-year lock-up period expired.

According to figures reported by crypto media, approximately 57.3 billion PUMP tokens were distributed to 121 wallets on July 15. Their estimated value at the time was around $86.5 million.

That did not necessarily mean all the tokens would be sold immediately, but it illustrates how valuable allocations linked to vesting could have been.

For that reason, a difference of several months, weeks or even a single day in the date an employment relationship ends can determine whether an employee receives tokens worth hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars.

## Company Reportedly Failed to File Financial Accounts on Time

Sandmark also reports that Baton Corporation Ltd, the UK legal entity linked to Pump.fun, did not file its financial accounts for the period ending September 30, 2025, by the required deadline.

According to the report, the filing deadline was June 30, 2026, but the documents were still not available in the UK company register at that time.

This alone does not prove financial problems or irregularities connected to the layoffs. However, it draws additional attention because the company is simultaneously generating very high revenue, unlocking large amounts of PUMP tokens and facing allegations from former employees about dismissals immediately before vesting.

## Pump.fun Already Faces Legal Problems

Pump.fun is not facing serious allegations for the first time. Lawsuits have previously been filed against the platform over how memecoins are created and promoted, alleged market manipulation and practices said to have harmed retail investors.

In 2024, the platform also suffered an incident in which a former employee abused privileged access to its systems and extracted approximately $1.9 million.

Pump.fun confirmed at the time that the attacker was a former employee and announced compensation for affected users.

The latest case, however, does not concern a technical attack. It concerns the company’s treatment of its own employees and the valuable tokens allegedly promised to them under their agreements.

## Layoffs or Avoidance of a Million-Dollar Obligation?

There is currently no evidence that Pump.fun breached contracts or violated the law. The key issue will be what the token allocation agreements specifically stated, whether employees could be dismissed without cause and whether any company obligations continued after the employment relationship ended.

However, if it is confirmed that a larger number of people were dismissed immediately before vesting, it will be difficult to avoid the question of whether the restructuring was used to keep tokens that would otherwise have gone to employees.

The story is particularly sensitive because Pump.fun did not cite financial difficulties as the reason for the layoffs. The explanation that the company had “grown too quickly” comes at a time when tens of millions of dollars in PUMP tokens are being transferred to the team and early investors.

Vesting is intended to retain employees and link their compensation to a company’s long-term success. However, the same mechanism gives companies significant power: dismissal only days before the deadline can erase compensation accumulated over months or years.

That may be permitted under the contract, but it does not automatically make it fair.

In Pump.fun’s case, it has not yet been confirmed how many people lost their tokens, what the total value was or whether the layoffs were connected to the approaching vesting dates. Until the agreements are published or the parties involved respond, the story should be treated as a serious but still unresolved allegation.

If employees were indeed dismissed immediately before they were due to receive million-dollar allocations, Pump.fun will have to explain not only why it reduced staff, but why it did so at that exact moment.
