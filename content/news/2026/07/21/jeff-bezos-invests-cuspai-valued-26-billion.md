# Jeff Bezos Invests in CuspAI Valued at $2.6 Billion

Jeff Bezos and the UK government have invested in CuspAI, a British startup that uses artificial intelligence to discover new materials needed to manufacture chips, batteries and energy systems.

The Cambridge-based company raised $450 million, after which it was valued at approximately $2.6 billion.

## AI searches for materials that do not yet exist

Developing a new industrial material can take years. Scientists must test a large number of combinations before finding a material that is strong, lightweight, heat-resistant or able to conduct electricity more efficiently.

CuspAI wants to use artificial intelligence to significantly accelerate that process. Instead of lengthy manual testing, AI can analyze a large number of possible structures and suggest materials that may have specific desired properties. The company describes its system as a kind of search engine for materials. A manufacturer could define the type of material it needs, and the AI would attempt to find or design a suitable solution.

## Why are new materials important?

The production of modern chips, batteries and other technology products depends on rare and expensive metals that are available only in limited quantities. These include **iridium and ruthenium**, which are used in semiconductors, electronics and energy technologies.

If CuspAI can find cheaper and more widely available alternatives, manufacturers could reduce costs and their dependence on uncertain global supply chains. New materials could also enable more powerful chips, more efficient batteries and energy grids with lower electricity losses.

## Nvidia, Meta and Hyundai are involved

CuspAI has launched the AI Materials Foundry, a project bringing together more than 48 technology companies, industrial businesses and research institutions.

The companies involved include **Nvidia, Meta and Hyundai**. Their interest is linked to the need for more powerful chips, more efficient data centers, better batteries and new manufacturing technologies.

CuspAI plans to combine artificial intelligence, computer simulations and laboratory testing to shorten the path from an idea to a material that can be used in real-world production.

## Jeff Bezos did not invest $2.6 billion

The $2.6 billion figure represents the estimated value of the company following its latest funding round.

CuspAI raised $450 million in that round from a number of investors, including Jeff Bezos and the UK government’s sovereign AI fund. The individual amounts invested by Bezos and the UK government have not been disclosed. CuspAI plans to use the new capital to develop its technology and expand in Europe, the United States and the Asia-Pacific region.

## Big promises still need to be proven

The idea that AI can design materials that do not yet exist sounds like a major technological breakthrough. However, a computer-generated proposal is not the same as a material that can be manufactured safely, reliably and cheaply at scale.

Every material suggested by artificial intelligence must still undergo laboratory testing and prove that it can withstand the conditions of real industrial production.

CuspAI has attracted major investors and well-known technology companies, but it still needs to show that it can turn its AI promises into real solutions for chips, batteries and the energy sector.
