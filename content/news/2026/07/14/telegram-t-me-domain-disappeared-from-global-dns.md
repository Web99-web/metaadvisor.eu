# Telegram’s t.me domain disappeared from the global DNS

Telegram’s short domain `t.me`, used to open links to Telegram channels, groups, profiles and bots, disappeared from the global DNS overnight. Users who tried to open `t.me` links in a web browser encountered errors, while the Telegram app itself largely continued to function.

The incident quickly caused a stir across social media. Various theories emerged about why one of Telegram’s most important domains had suddenly stopped working, but at the time of publication no official explanation was available. The issue developed overnight in European time, before the usual morning working hours had begun in Montenegro.

## What happened to the t.me domain?

Publicly available records showed that the domain `t.me` had been assigned the status `serverHold`. This status is applied by the operator of the relevant domain extension and results in the domain being removed from its DNS zone.

That does not mean the domain was deleted or that Telegram lost ownership of it. The domain may still be registered, but while `serverHold` remains active, the DNS system does not receive the information needed to direct users to the correct servers.

In simpler terms, when someone clicks a link beginning with `t.me`, the browser no longer knows where that address is located.

The problem affected links to public channels, groups, user profiles, bots, invitations and other Telegram services accessed through the `t.me` domain. Messaging inside an already installed app does not necessarily depend entirely on that domain, which is why Telegram did not completely disappear or stop working altogether.

## Durov publicly asked for an explanation

Telegram founder Pavel Durov contacted the official `.ME` Registry account on the social network X, writing that `t.me` links had stopped working and asking the registry to look into the issue.

His brief public message prompted even more discussion because it suggested that Telegram may not have been informed about the problem in advance. However, the message alone does not reveal whether the cause was a technical error, an administrative issue, an automated security measure or something else.

While users waited for a response from the registry, social media filled with speculation, but none of the theories had been officially confirmed at that point.

## What does Montenegro have to do with it?

The most interesting part of the story is hidden in just two letters: `.me` — and in a fact that many people do not know.

**`.me` is the official national internet domain of Montenegro, also known as the country-code top-level domain, or ccTLD, of the country.** Montenegro received the international code `ME`, derived from the name Montenegro, and the national `.me` domain was introduced after the country became independent.

This means that `t.me` is registered within Montenegro’s national domain zone.

It is important to distinguish between the owner of an individual domain and the operator of the entire domain extension. Telegram uses and controls the address `t.me`, while the `.me` extension belongs to Montenegro’s national internet domain and is managed by an authorised registry.

Montenegro does not control Telegram’s content, messages or app. However, the operator of the `.me` registry technically controls whether an individual domain within the `.me` zone is published in the DNS.

## Why did Telegram choose a Montenegrin domain?

Telegram does not have a special business or organisational connection with Montenegro simply because it uses `t.me`. The domain was chosen because it is extremely short, easy to remember and convenient to share.

Montenegro’s national domain became popular worldwide because the word “me” has a clear meaning in English. Montenegro opened registration to users and companies around the world, and many international brands began using `.me` domains.

WhatsApp uses `wa.me`, Messenger uses `m.me`, Facebook uses `fb.me`, WordPress uses `wp.me`, Proton Mail uses `pm.me`, and PayPal is well known for addresses within its PayPal.Me service.

The global popularity of the `.me` extension turned Montenegro’s national domain into a valuable digital export product. At the same time, the Telegram incident reveals the other side of that choice: a global company can become dependent on the registry of another country’s national domain.

## Why does serverHold matter?

The `serverHold` status is different from an ordinary web hosting problem or an issue affecting a single server. According to ICANN, it is applied by the registry operator, after which the domain is no longer activated in the DNS.

Such a measure can be used for different reasons, including data verification, a security incident, suspected abuse, an administrative problem or a procedure triggered in error. The status itself, however, does not reveal why it was applied.

For that reason, it was not possible at the time of publication to say with certainty why `t.me` had been removed from the DNS. What happened technically was known, but the cause of the incident was not.

## Our view

- Telegram did not stop working completely, but it temporarily lost one of its most important public access points.
- `t.me` is not merely a shortened link. It is part of the infrastructure through which users access channels, groups, bots and other Telegram services.
- `.me` is Montenegro’s official national domain, while the individual domain `t.me` is registered and used by Telegram.
- The incident shows how even the world’s largest platforms depend on domain registries, the DNS system and infrastructure they do not fully control themselves.
- Social media quickly filled with various theories, but until the `.ME` Registry or Telegram releases further information, the actual cause remains unknown.
