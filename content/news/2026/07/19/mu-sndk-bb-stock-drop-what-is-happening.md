# MU, SNDK and BB Fell Hard: What Is Happening to These Stocks?

Micron, SanDisk and BlackBerry recorded sharp declines in recent days, and by the close of the U.S. market on Friday, July 17, their prices had not meaningfully recovered. Although all three instruments came under selling pressure at roughly the same time, there was no single negative development affecting them all. Micron and SanDisk were caught in a broader selloff involving memory, semiconductor and AI infrastructure stocks, while BlackBerry’s decline appears more closely linked to profit-taking after a strong previous rally.

## Micron and SanDisk fell with the memory sector

Micron and SanDisk benefited strongly in recent months from investment in artificial intelligence, data centers and the storage of rapidly expanding volumes of data. Micron produces DRAM, NAND and HBM memory used in AI accelerators and advanced data centers, while SanDisk is heavily exposed to NAND flash, SSDs and data-storage systems.

Both stocks had risen very quickly before the correction. When investors began questioning whether their prices had moved too far ahead of actual business growth, some market participants decided to lock in profits. The selling then spread to other memory, storage and semiconductor companies, putting pressure even on businesses that had not released any new negative corporate news.

## Why did Micron fall despite strong results?

Micron reported record results in its latest quarter, with strong growth in revenue, profit and cash flow. Demand for HBM memory, one of the essential components in advanced AI systems, remains high, and the company also issued a strong outlook for the next reporting period.

The decline therefore was not caused by a sudden deterioration in Micron’s operations, but by changing market expectations. After the enormous share-price increase, investors are asking how much future demand is already reflected in the valuation and whether high memory prices can last. Additional uncertainty comes from planned capacity expansion, the possibility of greater supply and the question of whether major technology companies will continue spending as aggressively on AI data centers.

The memory market is known for pronounced cycles. High prices encourage producers to expand output, while a later oversupply can push down prices and margins. Fear of another cyclical reversal is one of the main reasons investors are selling even though current business performance remains strong.

## Why did SanDisk fall even more?

SanDisk was among the market’s biggest winners before the correction. After rising by several hundred percent, the stock became especially vulnerable to profit-taking, shifts in sentiment and any doubts about a continued shortage of NAND memory.

When the selloff in memory stocks began, SNDK fell more sharply than Micron because it had also risen far more aggressively beforehand. Momentum works in both directions with stocks like these: while the price is rising, it attracts new buyers, but once the trend turns, the same investors can close positions very quickly.

SanDisk’s latest financial results did not point to a collapse in the business. Revenue and profit grew strongly, particularly in the data-center segment. The problem is that, after such a large increase in the share price, the market now expects almost flawless execution. Any slowdown in demand, increase in supply or pressure on NAND pricing could therefore trigger an outsized reaction.

## Analysts have not given up on MU and SNDK

Most analysts remain broadly positive on Micron and SanDisk. Their optimism is based on expectations that AI data centers, cloud infrastructure and the growing need for data storage will continue to increase demand for advanced memory technology.

For Micron, HBM is a particular focus because the most advanced AI processors cannot deliver their expected performance without it. For SanDisk, analysts point to NAND memory, SSD systems and rising data-center demand. However, the wide gap between low and high price targets shows how difficult it is to estimate the duration of the current memory cycle. Positive forecasts do not mean the shares cannot fall further before any recovery.

## BlackBerry is a different story

BlackBerry does not manufacture memory chips, so its decline does not have the same business background as the moves in MU and SNDK. The company now focuses mainly on QNX software for vehicles and industrial systems, as well as secure communications solutions for businesses and government institutions.

Its latest results showed improvements in revenue, profitability and the QNX segment. Even so, BB fell after a very strong previous increase. The most likely explanation is profit-taking, because the share price had risen much faster in a short period than the company’s revenue and profit.

Analysts are more cautious on BlackBerry than on Micron and SanDisk. The consensus remains largely at “Hold,” while average price targets do not suggest the same level of upside. A further recovery in BB is therefore more likely to depend on new QNX contracts, higher revenue, stronger cash flow and additional guidance upgrades than on a general rebound in the AI and semiconductor sector.

## Three instruments, but two different reasons for the decline

MU and SNDK fell as part of a broader correction in memory, semiconductor and AI infrastructure stocks after an exceptionally strong previous run. Investors are not saying that demand for memory has disappeared. Instead, they are questioning whether share prices had already priced in almost all of the expected growth.

BB fell for different reasons. Its business is gradually improving, but the stock had risen sharply before the correction while analyst expectations remained more moderate. What links all three is high volatility and the presence of momentum investors who enter quickly while prices rise but may leave just as quickly when the trend changes.

## What could happen when the market reopens?

The U.S. stock market reopens on Monday, July 20. After the sharp decline, a technical rebound is possible if some investors decide prices fell too quickly. However, one positive trading day would not prove that the correction is over.

If the Nasdaq and the semiconductor sector remain under pressure, MU and SNDK could weaken again. SanDisk is more volatile and may therefore record larger moves in either direction. Micron has a stronger and more stable fundamental story tied to HBM memory, but it is still not protected from a broad sector selloff.

BlackBerry is more likely to move according to its own news, results and contracts. Without a new and powerful business catalyst, the stock could remain under pressure after its previous strong rally.

## Derivatives carry greater risk than ordinary shares

Investors holding MU, SNDK and BB through perpetual derivatives do not own actual shares in the companies. The derivative only tracks the stock price and may involve leverage, funding fees, liquidation risk and deviations from the price of the underlying share.

These instruments may continue moving outside regular U.S. market hours. Liquidity is often lower during those periods, spreads may be wider and the derivative price can temporarily diverge from the official stock price. With leveraged positions, even a relatively small additional decline can have a much larger effect on the capital invested.

For that reason, it is not enough to consider only whether MU, SNDK or BB may recover over the long term. Investors also need to monitor leverage, funding costs, liquidation levels and how long a position can withstand further short-term volatility.
