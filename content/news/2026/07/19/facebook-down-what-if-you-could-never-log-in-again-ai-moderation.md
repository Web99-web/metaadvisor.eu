# Facebook Down Again: What If You Could Never Log In Again?

Facebook suffered another major global technical outage today, leaving millions of users around the world unable to access their accounts, load the News Feed or use certain Meta services. For most people, the problem lasted only a few hours.

But now imagine Facebook coming back online for **everyone except you**.

Imagine that tomorrow, the day after tomorrow or even a month from now, you still cannot log in.

You never see your photos, messages, contacts, groups, pages or Business Suite again.

Messenger does not return.

Your photos do not return.

Your business pages do not return.

Your customers and advertisements do not return.

You lose your main communication channel and part of your income overnight because you run your business through Facebook or Instagram.

There is no telephone number you can call.

There is no email address where you can send an appeal.

There is no person who will explain what you supposedly did.

This is exactly how a growing number of users have described their digital lives over the past year and a half. They say they permanently lost access to their Facebook and Instagram accounts after decisions made by automated AI moderation systems. Many claim they were never given a clear explanation of why they were removed from the platform or a genuine opportunity to have their case reviewed by a human being.

For most users, today’s global Facebook outage will be forgotten within a few hours. For others, that same feeling has become permanent and appears irreversible because Meta seemingly has no intention of allowing them back onto the **“social network.”**

## Facebook once again unavailable to users worldwide

According to Downdetector data and numerous user reports, the problems began during the day, with tens of thousands of outage reports recorded within a short period across Europe, North America, Asia and other parts of the world.

Users reported being unable to log in to Facebook, seeing the message **“Account Temporarily Unavailable,”** being unable to load the News Feed and experiencing problems accessing Facebook pages and groups. Some also reported issues with Messenger, while Instagram was affected for part of the user base.

Unlike a problem with an internet connection or an individual account, this was a global incident that affected a large number of users at the same time.

## “Account Temporarily Unavailable” triggered panic

The message **“Account Temporarily Unavailable”** caused particular concern.

Many users immediately feared that their accounts had been hacked or permanently suspended.

That reaction is understandable because similar messages have been seen in recent months by many users whose Facebook or Instagram accounts were permanently disabled after decisions made by automated moderation systems.

In today’s case, however, the problem was a technical issue on Meta’s side rather than an individual account suspension.

## CBS News had already warned about similar cases

Major international media outlets have also reported on the problems caused by automated moderation in recent months.

CBS News published a report about users who say their Facebook and Instagram accounts were locked without a clear explanation and that their appeals were rejected within only a few minutes.

One of the best-known cases involves Eric Cunningham, a teacher from Chicago. He said Meta never explained why his account had been disabled and that the appeal process ended so quickly that he believes his case could not have been reviewed by a real person.

## Meta has not yet revealed the cause of today’s outage

At the time of writing, Meta had not published a detailed technical explanation for today’s global incident.

Based on the information currently available, it appears to have been a technical problem within Meta’s infrastructure. There is no confirmation that the outage was caused by a security incident or cyberattack.

## When a platform becomes your digital life

Facebook and Instagram are no longer simply social networks.

For many people, they serve as a photo album, address book, message archive, business tool, advertising platform, online store and primary communication channel with customers. Losing an account is therefore no longer merely a technical problem. It can mean losing memories, business contacts and income. Even more frightening is the loss of years of work — literally within a single second.

Today’s global outage, which has already lasted for several hours, is temporary for most users. For many people who say they were wrongly removed from the platform, the feeling of loss, permanent damage and injustice has already continued for months or even years.

## Our view

* Today’s Facebook outage showed how quickly panic can spread when people lose access to their accounts, even when it lasts for only a few hours.
* Many users have described exactly that feeling for more than a year after, according to their own accounts, permanently losing access to Facebook and Instagram because of decisions made by automated AI moderation.
* When Meta goes down, everyone talks about it.
* When Meta permanently removes an ordinary user from the platform, it often remains a personal tragedy that nobody reports.
* A removed user is, in a way, silenced because they no longer have the “social network” where they could share what happened. Often nobody knows, and friends may simply assume that the person has become less active online.
* Today’s global outage may be the clearest reminder that access to our digital lives should not depend entirely on an algorithm or on a decision that nobody is able to explain clearly.
