# ChatGPT materials are not automatically protected in court

A court in the US state of Massachusetts has ruled that materials created with ChatGPT are not automatically protected from disclosure to the opposing party simply because they are connected to court proceedings.

In this case, the documents were created by the romantic partner of one of the parties involved in the proceedings. The court concluded that it had not been proven that she prepared the materials under the instructions of an attorney or as a member of the legal team. As a result, attorney work-product protection could not automatically be applied to the documents.

## What is attorney work-product protection?

The US legal doctrine known as *work product* protects certain documents, notes, legal strategies and analyses prepared in anticipation of litigation.

The purpose of this protection is to allow attorneys and their assistants to prepare a case without being required to disclose their assessments, plans and legal strategy to the opposing party.

However, the protection does not apply to every document connected in some way to a legal dispute. A court may consider who created the document, why it was created, whether it was prepared under an attorney’s instructions and whether its true purpose was to prepare for litigation.

## A partner or family member is not automatically part of the legal team

In this case, the important issue was not only the use of ChatGPT, but also the person who used the tool. The materials were not created by an attorney, a person representing themselves in court or someone proven to have been working under the direction of legal counsel.

The fact that a partner, friend or family member helps prepare arguments does not automatically make that person part of the legal team. Their documents therefore do not automatically receive the same legal protection as notes prepared by an attorney while developing a litigation strategy.

## ChatGPT is not an attorney

A conversation with ChatGPT is not the same as a confidential conversation with an attorney. An AI system can help organise information, summarise documents or draft text, but there is no attorney-client relationship between the user and the artificial intelligence system.

For that reason, conversations with a chatbot are not automatically covered by attorney-client privilege. A user who enters details of a legal dispute into an AI tool cannot assume that every prompt, response, draft and uploaded document will be legally protected.

This does not mean that no material created with ChatGPT can ever be protected. Courts in other US cases have already reached different conclusions depending on who used the AI, whether the person was representing themselves and whether the materials were created solely in preparation for litigation.

## US courts do not yet have a uniform answer

During 2026, several US courts considered whether conversations with generative artificial intelligence could qualify as protected work product.

In some cases, courts protected the ChatGPT conversations of people representing themselves, finding that the prompts and responses reflected their personal thoughts, research and preparation of legal strategy.

In other cases, protection was denied because the AI materials were not created under an attorney’s instructions, did not represent communication with an attorney or were not produced in circumstances that justified a reasonable expectation of confidentiality.

It is therefore not enough to say that a document was created “for court”. Each case depends on the specific circumstances in which the material was created and used.

## What should users avoid entering into AI tools?

Particular caution is needed when entering medical records, personal data, trade secrets, contracts, internal communications and details of ongoing legal proceedings.

Users often treat a conversation with a chatbot as a private space for thinking. From a legal perspective, however, such communication does not automatically have the same status as a conversation with an attorney.

Even when AI is used only to edit or improve a text, users should consider whether the material contains information that the opposing party should not receive or whose disclosure could harm the legal proceedings.

## Our view

- Materials created with ChatGPT are not automatically confidential or legally protected.
- Courts consider not only which tool was used, but also who created the document, for what purpose and under whose instructions.
- A partner, friend or family member does not become part of the legal team simply by helping prepare a case.
- A conversation with artificial intelligence is not equivalent to confidential communication between an attorney and a client.
- US court decisions so far are not fully consistent, meaning protection may depend on the circumstances of each individual case.
- Sensitive personal, business or legal information should not be entered into ChatGPT or other AI tools without careful consideration.
