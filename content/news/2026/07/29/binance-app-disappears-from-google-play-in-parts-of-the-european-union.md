# Binance App Disappears From Google Play in Parts of the European Union

Binance’s Android app is no longer appearing in the Google Play Store in some European Union countries while the company works with Google on a solution.

So far, the app’s disappearance has been confirmed by users in Spain and Latvia. There is no confirmation that it has been removed from every EU member state, and availability may vary depending on the country and the local version of the Google Play Store.

Binance said it was aware of changes to Google Play policies affecting the availability of crypto apps and their updates in certain markets. The company did not publish a complete list of affected countries or specify which policy led to the restriction.

## App Not Visible in Spain and Latvia

Users in Spain and Latvia confirmed that they could no longer find the Binance app through Google Play search.

Cointelegraph initially mentioned Poland in an earlier version of its report but later removed that reference because it could not be independently verified. This suggests that the restriction is not being applied uniformly across the European Union.

The Binance app page may still remain accessible through a web browser, but that does not mean users in every country can install or update it. Google can restrict an app’s regional visibility without removing it entirely from the global store.

## Google Play Requires Compliance With MiCA Rules

Google Play policies for cryptocurrency exchange and software-wallet apps in the European Union require providers to comply with applicable regulatory conditions.

For crypto platforms, this effectively includes appropriate authorisation under the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, known as MiCA, and status as a crypto-asset service provider, or CASP.

If a company does not hold the required approval for a specific market, Google may restrict the app’s visibility, new installations or the availability of updates in that country.

Google has not issued a separate statement explicitly confirming that Binance was removed for breaching MiCA rules. However, the availability changes appeared at a time when crypto platforms in the EU are being required to obtain a new regulatory status.

## Binance Has No Publicly Confirmed MiCA Authorisation

Binance withdrew its application for MiCA authorisation in Greece in June and said it planned to apply in another EU member state.

The company says it intends to remain active in the European market. However, by the end of July, Binance had not publicly announced that it had obtained full MiCA authorisation allowing it to offer regulated services across the European Union.

Rules for crypto platforms became stricter after the transitional period ended on 1 July. Providers without the necessary authorisation can no longer offer and promote regulated services to EU users in the same way as before.

The app’s disappearance in certain countries is therefore being linked to Binance’s regulatory status, although neither Google nor Binance has confirmed that MiCA is the sole and direct reason for the restriction.

## Binance Is Not Banned Across Europe

The removal of an app from a local Google Play Store does not automatically mean that Binance has been completely banned in that country.

Existing user accounts are not automatically deleted, and funds held in those accounts do not disappear. An already installed app may continue to work, while the main problem affects new installations, reinstallation or future updates.

A user who changes phones or deletes the app may find that it can no longer be downloaded again from Google’s official store.

Binance’s web platform may also remain available, depending on the country, local restrictions and which services the exchange is permitted to provide there.

## EU Users May Request Services Only in Limited Circumstances

MiCA does not create a simple ban preventing an EU citizen from ever using a platform without European authorisation.

There is a narrow exception known as reverse solicitation. It allows a user, acting on their own exclusive initiative, to request a particular service from a platform outside the EU, provided that the platform had not previously targeted, advertised to or actively attracted that user.

However, a company cannot use this exception as a general business model for serving European customers. It cannot actively advertise in the EU, run local campaigns and then claim that every user arrived entirely on their own initiative.

A disclaimer stating that a user is proceeding at their own risk is also not sufficient to bypass regulatory requirements.

## A Platform Without MiCA Approval Is Not Automatically a Scam

The absence of MiCA authorisation does not automatically mean that a platform is fake, unsafe or designed to steal customer funds. Equally, a MiCA licence does not guarantee that users will not lose money because of falling crypto prices or poor investment decisions.

MiCA mainly regulates how crypto companies operate, safeguard client assets, manage risks, handle complaints and submit to regulatory supervision.

Users of a platform without EU authorisation may have less regulatory protection and fewer legal options if something goes wrong.

The difference between regulated and unregulated platforms should therefore be explained clearly without creating the impression that every unauthorised platform is automatically fraudulent.

## App Removal Increases the Risk of Fake APK Files

One of the biggest security risks emerges when users can no longer download the official app from Google Play.

They may begin searching for APK installation files through search engines, Telegram, social media, advertisements or unofficial app stores. Scammers can exploit this by distributing fake Binance apps designed to steal passwords, login credentials, verification codes or crypto assets.

Users should not install the Binance app through links received in messages, emails or advertisements.

Even if Binance officially provides an installation file outside Google Play, the domain, security certificate and source of the file should be carefully checked before installation.

## What Does Binance Say?

Binance confirmed that it was aware of changes affecting app availability in certain markets and that it was working with Google on a solution.

The company did not say how many countries were affected, when it expected the app to return or whether it first needed to resolve its regulatory status in those countries.

It is also unclear whether the issue affects only new installations or whether it will also limit future updates for users who already have the app installed.

Until Binance and Google release more detailed information, it is not possible to say with certainty how broad the restriction is or how long it may last.

## Google Play Is Becoming a Regulatory Gatekeeper for Crypto Exchanges

The case shows how strongly app stores can influence access to financial and cryptocurrency services.

Google is not a financial regulator, but through its store policies it can effectively determine whether an app is available to millions of users in a particular country.

When regulatory authorisation becomes a condition for app distribution, the impact of MiCA is no longer limited to licences and legal documents. It directly affects whether users can find, install or update an app on their phones.
