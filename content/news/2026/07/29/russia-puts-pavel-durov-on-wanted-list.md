# Russia puts Pavel Durov on wanted list

Russia’s Federal Security Service, the FSB, has accused Telegram founder Pavel Durov of allegedly facilitating terrorist activity and announced that an international search for him has been launched.

According to Russian authorities, Telegram failed to remove channels, chats and bots allegedly used by Ukrainian intelligence services, terrorist groups and extremist organisations to coordinate attacks, recruit operatives and conduct cyber fraud.

## Interpol notice has not been confirmed

Although Russian authorities claim that Durov has been placed on an international wanted list, there is currently no confirmation that Interpol has issued a Red Notice for him.

At the time of publication, Durov was not listed in Interpol’s publicly accessible database of wanted persons. Russian authorities have also not specified which international mechanism is being used to pursue him.

Claims that an international arrest warrant has been issued should therefore be treated cautiously. It is more accurate to say that Russia has launched an international search, while any possible Interpol notice has not been publicly confirmed.

## Russian case began in February

The latest announcement does not mark the beginning of the investigation into the Telegram founder. Russian authorities opened a criminal case against Durov in February 2026. At the time, Durov said Moscow was using the investigation as a new form of pressure against Telegram and as justification for restricting access to the app in Russia.

He accused Russian authorities of repeatedly creating new pretexts to restrict citizens’ rights to privacy and freedom of speech.

The latest development means that the FSB has now announced a formal charge and the launch of an international search.

## Telegram responds with provocative post

Telegram’s official account on X responded to the new allegations by sharing a photograph of Pavel Durov giving Russia the middle finger.

Durov has not yet published a more detailed personal response to the latest charge.

His earlier statements nevertheless make Telegram’s position clear: it does not view the Russian case as an ordinary criminal investigation, but as an attempt by the state to gain greater control over users’ private communications.

## Telegram’s long conflict with Russia

Pavel Durov left Russia in 2014 following a dispute with authorities over demands to hand over user data from VKontakte, the social network he also founded.

Russia attempted to block Telegram in 2018 after the company refused to provide encryption keys for user communications. The ban was lifted in 2020, but relations between Telegram and the Russian authorities never fully normalised.

Telegram has since become one of the most important communication platforms in Russia. It is used by ordinary citizens, opposition groups, government officials, the military and numerous pro-war channels.

Because of its widespread use, completely blocking Telegram would not be a simple move even for the Russian authorities.

## FSB targets Telegram over channels left online

The FSB claims Telegram failed to remove content allegedly used by Ukrainian intelligence services, terrorist groups and extremist organisations. According to the Russian authorities, the channels, chats and bots were used to coordinate attacks, recruit operatives, organise sabotage and conduct cyber fraud. No independently verified evidence has yet been published showing that Durov personally facilitated such activities.

It is also unclear whether the charge relates to his own conduct or to Telegram’s refusal to remove certain content or provide specific data to the authorities.

## Separate investigation against Durov in France

Durov is also facing a separate criminal investigation in France. French authorities are investigating alleged criminal activity on Telegram, insufficient content moderation and the company’s cooperation with law enforcement.

The French case is not connected to the new Russian allegations. Although Durov’s travel restrictions were previously eased and he was allowed to leave France, the criminal investigation was not automatically closed.

The new Russian charge comes only around two weeks after Telegram’s `t.me` domain temporarily disappeared from the global DNS. According to the available information, that incident was not connected to Russia, but to a decision by the `.ME` registry related to compliance with US sanctions rules. However, both cases show how exposed Telegram is to political and infrastructure-related pressure outside the company’s full control.

## International search is not a Red Notice

The terms international search, international arrest warrant and Interpol Red Notice are often used as though they mean the same thing, but there are important differences.

A country can announce that a person is internationally wanted and ask other states for assistance. This does not automatically mean that Interpol has accepted the request or issued a Red Notice.

A Red Notice is not an international arrest warrant that directly requires every country to detain a person. It is a request to member countries to locate and provisionally arrest someone in accordance with their own national laws.

Interpol also reviews requests to determine whether they comply with its rules, including its prohibition on cases that are predominantly political in nature.

## Will Durov still be able to travel?

Until it is confirmed which channels Russia is using to pursue Durov internationally, it is impossible to say with certainty how the charge will affect his ability to travel. If Russia is only maintaining its own international wanted record and sending bilateral requests to individual countries, the impact will depend on those countries’ relations with Moscow.

Should Interpol approve and circulate a Red Notice, Durov could face a greater risk of provisional detention when crossing borders.

However, each country decides independently how to act on such a notice. The political nature of the conflict between Russia and Durov could also influence how any potential extradition request is assessed.

## Security concern or political pressure?

Russia claims Telegram has not done enough to prevent the platform from being used to organise attacks and other criminal activity.

Telegram and Durov argue that Russian authorities are using security allegations as a form of political pressure and as an attempt to control private communications.

Similar questions are increasingly being raised in other countries: can the founder of a communication platform be held criminally responsible for the content and activities of its users?

The answer may depend on whether the platform was notified about specific unlawful content, how it responded to those reports and whether authorities were requesting the removal of content or access to users’ private messages.
