# YGG shuts crypto gaming arm: play-to-earn pioneer turns to AI data

Yield Guild Games, better known as **YGG**, is shutting down its crypto game publishing arm **YGG Play**, laying off **35 employees** and redirecting resources toward a new area: the **AI data economy**. For a project that was for years one of the better-known names in the play-to-earn and Web3 gaming scene, this is not a small change of direction. YGG does not present the decision as a problem with one product or one game. Its explanation points to a market that has not returned to previous levels for Web3 games, prolonged pressure in the crypto sector and a game publishing market that has itself become difficult. The company says **YGG Play is no longer commercially sustainable**.

A particularly important detail relates to **October 10**. YGG says the major market crash on that day “fundamentally changed retail market psychology” and that it does not expect the crypto consumer market or the Web3 game publishing market to recover sufficiently in the near term.

That sentence may be the most important part of the entire announcement. YGG is effectively acknowledging that this is not only a temporary decline in interest, but a change in user behavior. Retail audiences are no longer entering Web3 games with the same expectation that tokens, rewards and NFT assets will rise as they did in earlier crypto cycles.

## Who is YGG and why does this news matter?

Yield Guild Games was not a small unknown gaming project disappearing after one failed attempt. YGG was one of the symbols of the **play-to-earn** wave, especially during the period when **Axie Infinity**, NFTs and blockchain games looked like a possible new model of the digital economy. The project developed through the Web3 gaming guild model. These guilds brought together players, digital assets, NFT assets and blockchain games. During the strongest phase of the market, they bought or managed the digital assets needed to play and then provided them to players who tried to earn through gaming.

That model carried particular weight in Southeast Asia, including the Philippines, where play-to-earn was at one point not only a crypto experiment, but also an additional source of income for some players. For the crypto industry, YGG and similar projects were proof that NFTs and blockchain games could have a concrete use beyond pure speculation. That is why the shutdown of YGG Play is not an ordinary cost-cutting story. When a project tied to the very core of the play-to-earn narrative closes its publishing arm, it says something broader about the state of Web3 gaming.

## What exactly is being shut down?

YGG Play was the part of the business focused on publishing and promoting crypto games. Now the **YGG Play website**, the web app or game launchpad, the community rewards platform and marketing support for third-party games are being shut down.

The games **LOL Land** and **Waifu Sweeper** are also being removed. According to available information, the Web3 versions of **GIGACHADBAT** and **Ragnarok Breaker** are expected to continue operating.

According to industry reports, the YGG Play platform is expected to stop operating at the end of July, or no later than around **August 1**. That means the company is not just shutting down one internal team, but an entire part of the infrastructure that was supposed to serve as a publishing and distribution channel for Web3 games.

What makes this especially interesting is that YGG Play was not a project with no revenue. According to figures cited by crypto media, YGG Play generated around **$9 million in lifetime revenue** by the first quarter of 2026. The problem is that, under the new market conditions, that revenue is no longer enough for the business to be considered commercially sustainable. That is an important distinction. This is not an empty idea that never had users or revenue. It is a business that built something, but whose growth model is no longer supported by the market.

## YGG has money, but it is changing direction

Another important detail is the company’s financial position. After the restructuring, YGG does not look like a project shutting down because it has run out of money. According to the official announcement, its treasury stood at **$20.6 million** at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Of that, around **$6.2 million** was held in stablecoins, T-bills and large-cap tokens.

The company says the shutdown of YGG Play and the cost reductions will extend its operating runway to around **four years**. In other words, YGG is not closing YGG Play just to survive the next few months. The decision looks more like a strategic cut: stop spending capital on a part of the business that management believes will not recover quickly enough, and redirect resources into an area with greater potential.

That area is now artificial intelligence, or AI.

## From play-to-earn to AI data

YGG’s new direction is the **AI data economy**, meaning the market for data used by artificial intelligence. The company wants to use its global gaming community to create gaming datasets that AI models can use for learning. The idea is that players can generate valuable data simply by playing. This is a major break in the story. The old Web3 gaming narrative was simple: play, own digital assets, earn tokens. The new narrative sounds different: play, generate behavioral data, and that data may become valuable to AI companies.

Gaming data can be interesting because games generate huge amounts of human behavior in digital environments. Players make decisions, test strategies, react to obstacles, cooperate, compete and often find ways to use systems differently than developers expected. For AI models that need to learn from complex digital worlds, such data can be valuable. YGG is now trying to use what it already has: a global gaming community, experience in coordinating online communities and infrastructure built around digital economies. Only the definition of what represents the main value is changing. In the play-to-earn phase, it was tokens and NFT assets. In the new phase, it could be data.

## What does this decision say about Web3 gaming?

YGG’s move does not mean Web3 gaming is dead. But it does show that the old model can no longer be sold as easily as it was during the NFT boom. Play-to-earn worked while users believed they were entering a growing economy. When that trust breaks, games that depend on tokens, rewards and a constant inflow of new users become much harder to sustain.

The problem is not only the crypto market. Traditional game publishing is also difficult, competitive and expensive. Add weaker retail interest in Web3 products, a smaller NFT hype cycle and more cautious users after major market crashes, and Web3 game publishing becomes even more challenging. That is why YGG’s sentence about October 10 matters. YGG is not only saying that prices fell. It is saying that user psychology changed. That is a bigger problem than short-term volatility, because it suggests that the audience no longer behaves the way it did in an earlier bull market.

## Our view

- YGG matters because it was one of the better-known symbols of the play-to-earn and Web3 gaming wave.
- The shutdown of YGG Play shows that the crypto gaming market has not recovered in the way many projects expected.
- The statement that the **October 10** market crash changed retail user psychology is especially important.
- YGG Play generated around **$9 million in lifetime revenue**, but that was no longer enough to keep the business commercially sustainable.
- After the restructuring, YGG still has around **$20.6 million in treasury**, including about **$6.2 million** in stablecoins, T-bills and large-cap tokens.
- The company says the restructuring gives it a runway of around **four years**, which shows this is not only a shutdown, but an attempt at strategic survival.
- The most interesting part is the shift toward the **AI data economy**: from “play and earn tokens” to “play and generate data for AI.”
- For Web3 gaming, this is a warning that tokens and NFT assets are no longer enough if the game, the economy and user demand are not sustainable.
- For the AI market, it is another sign that gaming data could become more interesting as raw material for training models.
