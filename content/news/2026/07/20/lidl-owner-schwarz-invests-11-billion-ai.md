# Lidl Owner Schwarz Invests €11 Billion in AI

Most customers associate Lidl and Kaufland with groceries, low prices and thousands of stores across Europe. Their owner, Schwarz Group, no longer wants to be only a retail giant. Through Schwarz Digits, the group is rapidly expanding its own cloud, cybersecurity and AI infrastructure and aims to become a European alternative to U.S. technology companies such as Amazon, Microsoft and Google.

At the center of this strategy is a new data center campus in Lübbenau, in the German state of Brandenburg. The project, valued at around €11 billion, is the largest single investment in Schwarz Group’s history. It is intended to provide data storage, cloud services and the computing power required to develop and operate artificial intelligence systems.

## From supermarkets to one of Europe’s largest technology projects

Schwarz Group owns Lidl and Kaufland, but its business has long extended far beyond retail. The group also operates its own food production, logistics, the recycling company PreZero and the digital division Schwarz Digits.

Schwarz Digits develops the STACKIT cloud platform, cybersecurity solutions, communication tools and artificial intelligence applications. In the 2025 financial year, the digital division generated around €2.2 billion in revenue, approximately 16% more than a year earlier. Growth is being driven by rising demand from European companies and institutions for cloud services that store data in Europe and operate under European rules.

This means Lidl and Kaufland’s parent company is no longer developing IT systems only for its own stores. It is now also offering its technology to other companies, institutions and the public sector.

## A 200-megawatt data center campus

The new data center campus in Lübbenau is planned with a total capacity of 200 megawatts. It will cover approximately 13 hectares of a former industrial site and is expected to consist of six separate data center modules.

The first phase, comprising three modules, is scheduled for completion by the end of 2027. Once the entire project is finished, the infrastructure could support up to 100,000 graphics processing units, or GPUs, used to train and operate large AI models.

That capacity shows that this is not merely a facility for storing Lidl and Kaufland business data. Schwarz Group is building infrastructure that could provide substantial computing power to external users, including companies, government bodies and other organizations that want their data to remain under European control.

## Why does Lidl want to compete with Amazon and Microsoft?

Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud dominate the global cloud computing market. European companies and institutions therefore entrust a large share of their data and digital operations to U.S. providers.

Schwarz Digits wants to offer a European alternative. The company places particular emphasis on digital sovereignty, meaning that data can be stored and processed in Germany and Europe, under European regulations and without complete dependence on U.S. technology companies.

The goal is not to remain a small regional cloud provider. Schwarz Digits’ leadership has spoken about the ambition to build a European hyperscaler capable of delivering cloud storage, computing power, AI tools and other digital services at very large scale.

## A new technology campus for 3,500 employees

Alongside the data center project, Schwarz Digits is opening a new technology campus designed for around 3,500 employees. The complex includes office space, green areas, restaurants, fitness facilities and a childcare center, and is intended to provide a modern working environment for specialists in cloud computing, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence.

The official opening of the campus is planned for July 21, 2026. The message is clear: the group known for supermarkets also wants to present itself as a serious technology company capable of attracting thousands of IT professionals.

## Lidl’s internal systems became a product for external customers

Schwarz Group’s digital infrastructure was originally developed to support Lidl, Kaufland and other companies within the group. Thousands of stores, warehouses and suppliers, together with enormous logistics and sales systems, required reliable in-house IT infrastructure.

The group later realized that it could offer the same cloud and security services to other organizations. STACKIT now positions itself as a European cloud platform, while Schwarz Digits also provides cybersecurity, communication tools and AI solutions through its other companies.

Existing and potential customers include companies, public institutions and organizations for which the protection of sensitive data is especially important. Schwarz Group is also working with German institutions on sovereign cloud solutions for public administration.

## An AI gigafactory with Deutsche Telekom would be a separate project

There have also been reports of a possible collaboration between Schwarz Group and Deutsche Telekom on a European AI gigafactory. Such a facility would be specifically designed to provide the enormous computing power required to develop advanced AI models.

This potential joint project should not be confused with Schwarz Digits’ confirmed data center campus in Lübbenau, which is already under construction. An AI gigafactory with Deutsche Telekom would be an additional project, but no final agreement or complete set of details has been officially confirmed.

Schwarz Group therefore already has its own major cloud and AI investment, while cooperation with Deutsche Telekom could represent the next step in its expansion into the European AI infrastructure market.

## Lidl and Kaufland revenue finances the technology expansion

Schwarz Group generated total revenue of approximately €185.6 billion in the 2025 financial year. Lidl contributed around €140.2 billion, while Kaufland generated approximately €36.7 billion.

The strong and stable retail business allows the group to make investments that most European technology companies could not afford. Revenue from millions of daily purchases at Lidl and Kaufland is now helping to finance one of Europe’s most ambitious cloud and AI projects.

This gives Schwarz Group a major advantage. Unlike technology startups, it does not have to rely solely on external investors, public markets or a single digital product that has not yet become profitable. Its technology expansion can be supported by revenue from its enormous retail business.

## Can a retail giant really compete with Big Tech?

Schwarz Digits is still significantly smaller than AWS, Microsoft Azure or Google Cloud. The U.S. companies operate global networks of data centers, have decades of experience, large partner ecosystems and far greater technology budgets.

Schwarz Group nevertheless has several important advantages. It is financially strong, takes a long-term approach, already manages a huge number of its own users and digital systems, and can position itself as a European provider for organizations that do not want their most sensitive information to depend entirely on U.S. platforms.

An €11 billion data center campus, capacity for up to 100,000 GPUs and a technology campus for thousands of specialists do not mean that Schwarz Digits will soon overtake Amazon or Microsoft. They do show, however, that the owner of Lidl and Kaufland no longer intends to remain only their customer.
