# Allbridge Hacked: $1.65 Million Lost

Allbridge temporarily paused its cross-chain protocol, Allbridge Core, following a security incident on Solana. According to estimates from blockchain security firms, the total loss was approximately $1.65 million.

The attack affected Allbridge’s USDC/USDT liquidity pool. The stolen funds were later transferred from Solana to Ethereum, while part of the money was reportedly moved to protocols designed to make transactions more difficult to trace.

## How was the attack carried out?

According to Onchain Lens, the attacker took out a flash loan of approximately 1.12 million USDC through the Kamino protocol.

The attacker then used a series of rapid swaps between USDC and USDT to disrupt the asset ratio and exchange rate inside Allbridge’s liquidity pool. After manipulating the pool, the attacker withdrew liquidity at a more favorable rate, repaid the flash loan and kept the difference.

The $1.12 million flash loan was therefore not the attacker’s profit. It was a short-term loan that had to be repaid within the same blockchain transaction.

## How much money was actually stolen?

The final amount has not yet been officially confirmed. Some on-chain analyses have directly identified more than $1.1 million in withdrawn funds.

PeckShield and CertiK estimate the total loss from the incident at approximately $1.65 million. That figure may include losses from the liquidity pool and additional arbitrage transactions carried out after the exchange rate became distorted.

It is therefore not yet possible to say with certainty that the main attacker personally kept the entire $1.65 million.

## Allbridge paused the protocol

Allbridge confirmed the security incident and said the protocol had been paused as a precaution while the investigation continues.

The company also asked users who benefited from the temporary arbitrage opportunity created by the pool imbalance to voluntarily return their profits.

According to Allbridge, any returned funds would be used to compensate affected liquidity providers.

## Allbridge has been attacked before

This was not the first flash loan attack targeting Allbridge. In April 2023, the protocol suffered a similar incident on BNB Chain in which more than half a million dollars was stolen.

The attacker also manipulated the price ratio inside a liquidity pool during that incident. Most of the stolen funds were later returned after Allbridge offered a reward and the possibility of treating the attacker as a white-hat hacker.

The latest incident once again shows how cross-chain bridges and DeFi liquidity pools can be vulnerable to manipulation using large amounts of capital borrowed through flash loans.
