# Trump memecoin falls 98%, investors lose $3.8B

**Almost one million accounts have lost a combined total of around $3.8 billion after buying the $TRUMP memecoin, according to an analysis by blockchain analytics firm Nansen, reported by TechCrunch and The New York Times. The token has fallen almost 98 percent from its peak, but the story is not only about one memecoin. The entire crypto market has gone through a sharp loss in value.**

According to Nansen’s analysis, by the end of June 2026, a total of **988,905 accounts** were in the red on the $TRUMP memecoin. That means around two-thirds of buyers of the token had lost money.

The most important market figure, however, is the price. $TRUMP was trading on Sunday at around **$1.69**, almost **98 percent below** its peak of **$75.35**.

That is an extreme decline, but it did not happen in a vacuum. Bitcoin has lost roughly half of its value from its peak. Ethereum has fallen even more sharply. Solana is far below its all-time high, and Cardano is also more than 90 percent below its ATH level. The difference is that $TRUMP was a memecoin with very strong hype, a famous name and a large number of retail investors who entered only after the story had already exploded.

## It was not only $TRUMP that fell

When discussing the fall of the $TRUMP memecoin, it is easy to isolate that token and present it as a standalone case. But the broader crypto picture shows that even the largest tokens have lost a major part of their value compared with their peaks.

Bitcoin previously reached levels above **$120,000**, before falling back toward the area around **$60,000**. That means even the best-known crypto asset lost around half of its value from the peak.

Ethereum was close to **$5,000**, while it now trades around **$1,600**. Solana reached around **$293** at its all-time high and is now much lower, around **$77**. Cardano reached around **$3** in a previous cycle, while today it is far below that level, at only around **$0.179**.

In other words, $TRUMP is not the only token that has fallen sharply. But $TRUMP is a more extreme example because it is a memecoin that rose very quickly, attracted a large number of buyers and then lost almost all of its market value compared with the peak.

## From viral launch to a nearly 98 percent drop

The $TRUMP memecoin was publicly introduced in January 2025, shortly before Donald Trump’s inauguration. Trump promoted it at the time on Truth Social and X as the “Official Trump Meme”, while CBS News wrote in its report that Trump had launched his own $TRUMP meme cryptocurrency.

That initial interest quickly pushed the token’s price higher, but it later became clear that many buyers had entered too late. After peaking at **$75.35**, the token fell to around **$1.69**.

In other words, anyone who bought near the top is now exposed to an almost complete loss of value.

This is a common pattern with memecoins. First comes major hype, then a sharp rise, then broader public entry, followed by fading interest and a strong price decline. Early buyers and fast traders can make profits, while later investors often remain trapped in the drop.

## Nansen analysis: almost one million accounts in the red

Nansen’s analysis is especially important because it is not based only on market sentiment, but on blockchain data. Blockchain transactions are publicly visible, so analytics firms can estimate how many accounts bought a token, at which price levels, and how many of them ended up at a loss.

According to data reported by TechCrunch and The New York Times, almost **988,905 accounts** had lost money on the $TRUMP memecoin by the end of June. That is around two-thirds of all buyers. Total losses are estimated at around **$3.8 billion**. That ratio shows how brutal the memecoin market can be for retail investors. On paper, a token can look like a “viral opportunity”. In reality, many people enter only after the biggest rise and the biggest gains have already passed.

## Why memecoins are especially risky

Memecoins often do not have classic business value behind them. They do not have revenue, a product, clear economics or long-term utility that would support the price. Their value usually depends on market attention, virality, a famous name, community and short-term sentiment. That does not mean money cannot be made on memecoins. It can. But the risk is extremely high because the price often moves faster than the average investor can process information.

With the $TRUMP token, the additional issue is that the project had a strong brand and enormous visibility from the start. That may have attracted people who do not usually understand the crypto market, but reacted to the name, media attention or the feeling that “something big is happening”. In such situations, the price often does not rise because the project has fundamental value, but because attention is rising. And when that attention disappears, the market can turn very quickly.

## The big difference between early and late buyers

With memecoins, the timing of entry is often decisive. Those who enter very early can make large gains if the hype spreads. But those who buy after a token has already exploded often take on the greatest risk.

The Trump memecoin $TRUMP is a good example of that problem. The token attracted enormous attention, reached a high price and then fell almost 98 percent from its peak. That means later buyers did not just lose part of the value. In many cases, they were left with a token worth only a small fraction of its earlier price. That is why buying a memecoin only because “everyone is talking about it” is especially dangerous. When something has already become major news, a large part of the rise is often already over.

This does not mean that every memecoin has to end the same way. Dogecoin, Shiba Inu and Pepe have shown that some older or more recognizable memecoins can survive several market cycles and maintain significant trading volume. Newer tokens such as Bonk, Pengu or Dogwifhat also show that interest in the meme segment has not disappeared.

But that does not change the basic risk. The memecoin market is not the same as investing in a project with a product, revenue or a clear business model. Value is often based on community, virality, liquidity and market attention. When that attention weakens, the decline can be very fast. That is why retail investors need to distinguish between memecoins that have already built broader recognition and liquidity, and short-lived hype projects that depend on a single wave of attention. The Trump memecoin shows how risky it can become when a famous name, strong hype and late retail entry come together in the same story.

## What this story says about the crypto market

This analysis matters not only because of one token. It shows a broader problem in the crypto market: how hype, famous names and social media can turn into a very risky financial product.

At the same time, it is fair to say that the entire crypto market has lost a large part of its value from previous highs. Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana and Cardano are also far from the levels they once had. That is why $TRUMP should not be seen as the only problem in the crypto market, but as an extreme example of what happens when a broader market decline meets memecoin hype.

With Bitcoin and Ethereum, investors can at least discuss the network, infrastructure, ETFs, institutional interest and their long-term role in the crypto ecosystem. With memecoins, the foundation is often much thinner: attention, brand, virality and the feeling that money can be made quickly. When that attention disappears, the price can fall much faster than with larger crypto projects.

## A 98 percent drop changes the math of recovery

A 98 percent drop does not mean that the price only has to rise 98 percent to return to its old level. The math is much harsher. If a token falls from $75 to around $1.50 or $2, it needs to rise many times over just to come close to the old peak. That is why large crashes are especially dangerous for late investors.

Many buyers look only at the nominal price and think a token is “cheaper” because it has fallen. But after an extreme decline, the key question is not only how low the price is, but whether there is any reason for demand to return. With memecoins, that reason often depends on a new wave of hype. And hype is difficult to predict and even harder to sustain over the long term.
