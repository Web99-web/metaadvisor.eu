# Meta quietly launches Pocket: Gizmo returns under Meta’s name

**Meta has quietly launched a new app called Pocket, a platform that lets users create small interactive apps and games using AI prompts. But the most interesting part of the story is not just the new app. Pocket looks like a continuation of the Gizmo project, a vibe-coded gaming platform whose team Meta acquired earlier this year.**

Gizmo already had a very similar idea: the user writes what they want to create, and AI turns that into a small interactive game, effect, mini-app or digital experience. Pocket now looks like Meta’s version of that concept, with a focus on creating and sharing so-called “gizmos”.

In other words, Meta is not just testing another gaming app. It is testing a new format for social content. Instead of users posting only text, images or video, Pocket tries to enable small interactive posts that other people can launch, use, play with and share.

## Gizmo under Meta’s roof

According to TechCrunch, Pocket is the result of Meta’s acquisition of the team behind Gizmo, a platform focused on exactly this kind of “vibe-coded” gaming experience. Gizmo allowed users to create small interactive pieces of content using written AI prompts, and it also had a discovery feed where users could try experiences made by others.

The similarities between Gizmo and Pocket are therefore not accidental. Pocket describes itself as a creative platform for making and sharing “gizmos”. These “gizmos” can be small games, effects, tools or other forms of interactive content. Users can create them with a prompt, edit them and then share them in a feed. That is an interesting shift. Meta is not only trying to follow the trend of AI images and AI videos, but is looking toward AI tools that create functional digital experiences.

## From AI images to AI mini-apps

Meta has been pushing AI into its products aggressively for some time. The company has added AI features to its social platforms, developed the Meta AI app, experimented with AI-generated images and videos, and introduced Vibes, an app for AI video content.

Pocket fits into the same direction, but it goes one step further. Here, AI does not only create an image we look at or a video we scroll through. AI tries to create something that can be launched and used. That could be a small game, an interactive effect, a simple tool or a creative experiment that reacts to the user.

Business Insider describes Pocket as a social AI app, not just a gaming tool. That is an important difference. If Pocket remains only a mini-game generator, it is an interesting but niche experiment. But if Meta tries to turn it into a new feed, the story becomes much bigger.

Then Pocket is no longer just an app for games. It becomes a test for one question: can the next generation of social networks be interactive?

## What is vibe coding?

Pocket is built around a trend increasingly known as “vibe coding”. In traditional programming, the user needs to know how to write code. In vibe coding, the user describes what they want, and AI tries to create a functional result from that description. Instead of technical commands, the starting point is an idea, a feeling and a description.

For example, a user could write that they want a small game with flying objects, a quiz for friends, a virtual drawing tool or an interactive effect that reacts to touch, sound or the movement of a phone. AI then tries to turn that idea into something that can be tested immediately.

In the developer world, vibe coding is already used for quickly prototyping apps, websites and tools. Pocket tries to bring that logic down to the level of ordinary users. That may be the most interesting part of the whole story. Meta is trying to turn something that has so far been mostly a developer trend into a mainstream social product.

## A new feed or just another experiment?

The app was publicly spotted by reverse engineer Alessandro Paluzzi, who often finds new apps and features before official announcements. According to data cited by TechCrunch, Pocket was first published on June 29, 2026, on the App Store and Google Play, but Meta has not loudly promoted it so far. That suggests this is a quiet test rather than a major global launch.

That is typical for large technology companies. First, they test user interest, measure how many people create content, how often it is shared and whether users return to the app. Only after that do they decide whether the project will receive stronger support. But even if Pocket remains a small experiment for now, the signal is important.

Meta clearly does not want AI to be only an add-on feature inside existing apps. It wants to see whether AI can create a completely new content format. After status updates, photos, Stories, Reels and AI videos, the next step could be a feed full of small AI apps and games.

## Why Pocket matters

Pocket matters because it shows how AI is moving from generating content toward generating experiences. Until now, most mainstream AI tools have created something passive: text, image, video, voice or music. Pocket tries to create something interactive. This is content that is not only viewed, but used.

If this format works, it could change how creators, brands and ordinary users think about posts. Instead of a video, someone could post a mini-game. Instead of a static campaign, a brand could publish a small interactive tool. Instead of a regular meme, users could share small AI experiments that others can change or build on.

That opens interesting possibilities, but also new problems. If AI is already creating huge amounts of text, images and video, interactive AI content could flood feeds even further. Platforms will have to decide how to filter quality, prevent spam, moderate interactive apps and protect users if such content uses the camera, sound, photos or device sensors.

With a regular image, the risk is smaller. With a mini-app that reacts to a device, privacy and security become much more serious issues.
