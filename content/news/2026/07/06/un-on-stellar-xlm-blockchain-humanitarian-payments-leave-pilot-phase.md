# UN on Stellar XLM blockchain: humanitarian payments leave pilot phase

**UNDP, the United Nations Development Programme, is expanding the use of blockchain payments on the Stellar network, which is connected to the XLM token. After pilot projects in several countries, UNDP now wants to use blockchain payments more broadly in humanitarian and development programs.**

This is not a story about crypto speculation or the price of XLM. It is about aid payments, lower costs and more resilient infrastructure in countries where traditional financial systems often do not work quickly or reliably enough. According to Cointelegraph, UNDP signed a new agreement with the Stellar Development Foundation after 16 months of research and pilot programs in **Haiti**, **Syria**, **Kenya**, **Guatemala** and **The Gambia**. Additional projects were also carried out in **Colombia** and **Papua New Guinea**.

The payments use the Stellar blockchain, the network connected to the **XLM** token. Stellar has long been known as blockchain infrastructure for fast and low-cost payments, especially in the context of cross-border transfers, financial inclusion and payouts in regions where access to the banking system is difficult.

## Distribution costs reduced from 10 to 2 percent

The most important figure from the UNDP pilots comes from **Syria**. In a Cash for Work program, where payments were recorded on-chain, **distribution costs were reduced from 10 to 2 percent**. That is a major difference for humanitarian programs. Every percentage point spent on distribution means less money for the final recipients. If payment costs can be reduced, a larger share of aid can reach the people it is intended for.

Another important example comes from **Haiti**, where a pilot project continued processing payments even during a mobile network outage. That is especially important in crisis areas, where infrastructure outages are not an exception but a frequent problem. This is why the news is interesting: blockchain is not being used here as a marketing term, but as a tool for solving a concrete problem in payments.

## The UN already has experience with Stellar through Ukraine

This is not the UN’s first contact with Stellar infrastructure. During the war in **Ukraine**, UNHCR launched a blockchain pilot for aid payments to displaced and war-affected people, also using Stellar infrastructure.

That earlier pilot showed how blockchain can be used in crisis situations when people are displaced, access to banks is difficult and aid needs to arrive quickly and transparently.

Now UNDP is going one step further. After tests in **Haiti**, **Syria**, **Kenya**, **Guatemala** and **The Gambia**, the goal is to establish a process through which UNDP country offices could use blockchain payments across a wider range of programs.

In other words, blockchain payments are no longer just a one-time test. The UN is now trying to see whether the same model can be used in different countries and different humanitarian situations.

## Why Stellar XLM?

Stellar is a blockchain network connected to the **XLM** token. In this story, XLM is not important as an investment theme, but as part of the payment infrastructure. UNDP is not talking about buying XLM or about the token price. It is talking about using the Stellar network for aid payments and development programs. That is an important difference. The crypto market often focuses on token prices, but here the more important question is whether the network can enable faster, cheaper and more resilient payments to users on the ground.

If Stellar infrastructure can be used in humanitarian programs, the project gains a different context from an ordinary crypto exchange story.

## From pilot phase to broader use

UNDP now wants to define how its country offices could use blockchain payments in different programs. That does not mean blockchain will immediately replace all existing payment methods, but it does mean it is no longer being viewed only as an experiment.

Humanitarian payments are sensitive. The system must be secure, aligned with local rules, simple for users and reliable enough in crisis conditions. That is exactly why these pilots matter. If technology can help with real problems such as distribution costs, network outages and limited access to banks, blockchain gains a much more concrete role.
