# Coca-Cola Makes Milk: Ransomware Halts Production at Fairlife

Most people associate Coca-Cola with carbonated drinks, water and juice. Far fewer know that the company also owns Fairlife, a major US producer of ultra-filtered milk and protein drinks.

That part of Coca-Cola’s business has now been hit by a ransomware attack. Fairlife temporarily suspended production across its facilities in the United States after an unauthorised third party gained access to part of the company’s systems, including systems connected to production.

Fairlife’s Canadian operations have not been affected and continue to operate.

## What happened at Fairlife?

Coca-Cola said Fairlife detected unauthorised access to part of its information systems as part of a ransomware incident. After discovering the attack, the company activated its incident-response and business-continuity plans, launched an investigation with the assistance of external cybersecurity experts and notified law enforcement.

The full scope, nature and possible consequences of the attack are still being investigated. Coca-Cola has not disclosed the name of the ransomware group, whether a ransom demand was made or how long a complete system recovery may take.

Production in the United States has been temporarily suspended while the company works to restore the affected systems. At the time of publication, it was not known when the production lines would restart.

## Fairlife product safety has not been affected

Coca-Cola stressed that the cyberattack did not affect the quality or safety of Fairlife products. There is currently no official warning telling consumers to avoid products already available in stores.

The suspension concerns production operations and affected technology systems, not a confirmed problem involving milk, protein drinks or other products that have already been manufactured.

If the interruption continues, it could affect the availability of some products. Coca-Cola has not yet published an estimate of possible shortages or the financial impact of the incident.

## What is Fairlife, and why does Coca-Cola make milk?

Fairlife is a US company specialising in lactose-free ultra-filtered milk, Core Power protein drinks and other dairy products with a higher protein content.

Unlike conventional milk, Fairlife products go through an ultra-filtration process that changes the balance of protein, sugar and calcium. The brand is particularly popular among consumers looking for protein drinks, lactose-free products and functional nutrition.

Coca-Cola initially developed Fairlife through a partnership and took full control of the company in 2020. Fairlife is therefore not merely a brand distributed by Coca-Cola, but a wholly owned subsidiary.

That may sound unusual to European consumers because Fairlife does not have the same market presence in Europe. In the United States, however, it is a large and rapidly growing business.

## Fairlife is Coca-Cola’s billion-dollar dairy brand

Fairlife exceeded $1 billion in annual US retail sales as early as 2021. Coca-Cola then began describing it as its new billion-dollar retail brand.

Its growth has been driven by rising demand for protein drinks, lactose-free products and dairy products with lower sugar content.

The ransomware attack therefore did not hit a small side business. It affected one of the important growing areas within Coca-Cola’s portfolio.

## Attack comes amid a $650 million investment

The ransomware attack occurred during a period of major expansion for Fairlife. Coca-Cola announced a $650 million investment to expand Fairlife’s production facility in Coopersville, Michigan.

The project includes two new production lines, a substantial expansion of the existing facility and the creation of approximately 150 new jobs. Commercial production on the new lines is scheduled to begin in 2028.

The company also announced plans to launch a new Fairlife production facility in Webster, New York, during 2026.

The attack therefore hit the brand at a time when Coca-Cola is investing hundreds of millions of dollars to increase production capacity and meet growing demand.

## How can ransomware stop milk production?

Modern food and manufacturing companies depend on computer systems that manage orders, inventory, production lines, quality control, logistics and distribution.

When ransomware affects part of that infrastructure, a company may stop production even if the physical machinery has not been damaged. Without reliable digital systems, it may not be possible to operate facilities safely, monitor the production process or confirm that all components are functioning correctly.

A temporary shutdown may also be a security measure intended to prevent the attack from spreading to other parts of the company’s network.

Previous ransomware attacks on food and beverage manufacturers and major distributors have caused production disruptions lasting several weeks, order-processing problems and empty store shelves. It is not yet known how long the effects of the Fairlife attack will last.

## What remains unknown?

Coca-Cola has not disclosed which ransomware group is behind the incident, whether a ransom was demanded or whether the company is in contact with the attackers.

It is also not known how many individual US facilities were directly affected, when the attack was first discovered or when production will resume.

The company has confirmed unauthorised access to part of Fairlife’s systems, disruption to production-related systems, the temporary suspension of US production and the continuation of Canadian operations.

While the investigation continues, there is not enough confirmed information to draw conclusions about the attack’s full scale or long-term consequences.
