# Hacker Deletes Romania’s Land Registry?

Romania’s real estate market faced serious disruption following a cyberattack on the country’s national land registry and cadastral system.

The attack took the e-Terra platform offline. Notaries, surveyors, lawyers, banks and government agencies use the system to verify property ownership, mortgages and land records.

Without access to the platform, land registry extracts could not be issued normally, property sales could not be confirmed and new mortgages could not be registered.

## Hacker claims the database was deleted

The attacker, who uses the name ByteToBreach, claims to have breached the systems of Romania’s National Agency for Cadastre and Land Registration, known as ANCPI.

According to information published by security researchers, the hacker allegedly stole databases, internal documents, employee credentials and part of the system’s source code.

Following a failed extortion attempt, the main database was reportedly deleted. The attacker also claimed to have tried to destroy backups to make the system more difficult to restore.

## Did the entire land registry really disappear?

The claim that Romania’s entire land registry was deleted is currently based mainly on statements made by the attacker and companies monitoring dark web activity.

Romanian authorities say critical legal and technical records concerning ownership, land parcels and mortgages were not permanently lost.

ANCPI said the systems were being restored from backups stored in multiple locations. This could mean the active database was deleted or disabled, but not necessarily that every record disappeared permanently.

## The property market remained blocked

Even when backups exist, the real estate market cannot operate normally until reliable access to official records is restored.

Without e-Terra, notaries cannot confirm who owns a property, whether a mortgage exists or whether another registration procedure has already been initiated.

As a result, numerous property sales, home loans, ownership registrations and other transactions were temporarily stopped or significantly delayed.

## Did Romania refuse to pay the ransom?

Some reports claim the hacker attempted to extort money from the Romanian agency and deleted the database after the ransom was not paid.

However, Romanian authorities have not publicly disclosed the amount demanded or officially confirmed the entire sequence of events.

What has been confirmed is a cyberattack, an outage of the national platform and an alleged theft of data. Details regarding the ransom demand and the final level of damage remain under investigation.

## The agency’s most serious incident

ANCPI described the attack as the most serious technical incident in the institution’s history.

The case shows how heavily an entire property market can depend on a single government digital system. When that system fails, the consequences extend beyond public offices and affect citizens, banks, buyers, sellers and the wider construction industry.

Romania continues to restore the affected systems and investigate the full scope of the attack. The amount of data actually stolen or deleted has not yet been conclusively confirmed.
