**AI agents are moving beyond recommending products to completing payments on behalf of users. Animoca Brands has tested that capability with Visa in Hong Kong, while the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) has launched Project SAGE to explore agent identity, delegated permissions and accountability for financial transactions. At the center is KYA, or Know Your Agent: how can a financial system establish which software agent is acting and who authorized it?**

Until recently, people could ask an AI assistant to compare prices, find card rewards or recommend a purchase, but usually still had to complete checkout themselves. The emerging model known as *agentic commerce* brings product discovery, selection and execution closer together. It also introduces a new set of risks when software misunderstands an instruction or makes a purchase the customer did not intend.

## Animoca Brands and Visa pilot agent-led shopping

On July 8, 2026, Animoca Brands announced a live pilot of two capabilities developed with Visa for its Minds platform. One helps users discover relevant benefits and rewards associated with eligible Visa cards. The other enables AI agents to complete purchases at selected Hong Kong merchants on users' behalf, beginning with the Bruce Lee Club Ltd eShop.

Instead of merely suggesting a product, an agent operating under predefined permissions can proceed to checkout using the Visa Intelligent Commerce framework. This does not mean autonomous purchasing is already available to every cardholder or across all online stores. The announcement described a controlled pilot involving particular capabilities and merchants.

## How does an agent pay without exposing card details?

Animoca says the pilot combines tokenized payment credentials, authentication and transaction controls. Tokenization reduces the need to expose actual card numbers throughout the payment process, while users retain control over when and under what conditions an agent may act. The relevant distinction is between giving software unrestricted access and authorizing a defined financial task.

For example, a future user might allow an agent to purchase a specified item below a spending limit or only from an approved retailer. That is an illustrative scenario rather than confirmation that every such option is available in the current pilot. Tokenization also does not eliminate the risk of incorrect orders, disputed charges or fraud.

## Hong Kong launches Project SAGE: what is KYA?

On November 2, 2026, the HKMA launched Project SAGE, short for *Securing Agentic Finance with Governance and Enablement*. The initiative is intended to support responsible adoption of agent-driven financial services while developing an appropriate governance framework. An expert group brings together participants from financial institutions, technology companies, professional services and academia.

One of the project's four focus areas is **KYA (Know Your Agent)**, including ways to verify an AI agent's identity and the authority delegated to it by a person or organization. Financial institutions already need to establish who their customers are. Agentic finance adds another question: which application is initiating a payment, on whose behalf and within what limits? The HKMA is exploring approaches to these questions, not announcing a blanket legal requirement for every AI agent to register.

The other areas cover practical use cases, interoperability standards and legal or regulatory responsibilities. The HKMA specifically mentions consumer commerce, business procurement and treasury activity, as well as machine-to-machine payments for usage-based digital services.

## Who is responsible if an AI agent gets a purchase wrong?

Consider an agent instructed to book a flight under a given budget that instead selects a nonrefundable ticket or overlooks extra fees. A payment can be technically valid even when the outcome conflicts with the customer's intentions. Responsibility could depend on authorization terms, applicable law and the actions of the user, agent provider, merchant and payment intermediaries.

There is also a security concern when hostile content on an external website attempts to redirect an AI agent. Such *prompt-injection* attacks become especially consequential when the agent can spend money rather than simply produce text. Spending caps, merchant verification, detailed audit records and the ability to stop transactions therefore matter as much as improvements in the AI model itself.

## Could AI agents change how online stores sell?

Agent-led commerce could alter the way merchants reach customers. Instead of relying solely on attracting human clicks through ads and design, retailers may need to provide reliable, machine-readable information about pricing, availability, delivery and return policies. An agent can compare those details systematically, although brand trust and personal preference are unlikely to disappear from shopping.

Business applications could go further. Project SAGE identifies procurement and machine-to-machine payments as areas for exploration. An agent could eventually pay for a software subscription, an API request or another digital service under a preapproved budget. These are potential applications, not evidence that fully autonomous business payments are already the industry standard.

## Our take

- **From advice to action:** The major shift occurs when software can execute a real financial transaction rather than merely recommend one.
- **KYA and accountability:** Verifiable agent identity and delegated authority may be essential to trust.
- **Spending controls:** Clear limits on amounts, merchants and permissions are more useful than vague promises of full autonomy.
- **Tokenization is not a cure-all:** It can protect card credentials but cannot prevent every unwanted transaction.
- **A business opportunity:** Online merchants and digital service providers may increasingly need agent-readable offers and terms.
- **Rules are still emerging:** Project SAGE explores possible frameworks; it is not a completed regulatory regime for all AI agents.

**The question is no longer only whether an AI agent can find the best offer. It is whether a merchant, bank or payment provider can reliably establish who authorized the agent and exactly what it is allowed to pay for. Hong Kong's pilot shows the technology beginning to work, while Project SAGE addresses the safeguards needed before wider adoption.**
