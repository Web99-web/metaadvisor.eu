**US technology stocks staged a strong recovery on November 11, 2026, but Bitcoin failed to match their gains. The Nasdaq finished approximately 1.6% higher while the world's largest cryptocurrency hovered around $74,000 during the day, after falling toward $72,700 in the previous session. Four consecutive trading days of US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows, stronger American inflation and expectations of higher interest rates continue to pressure crypto markets.**

## Wall Street rebounds while Bitcoin lags

US stocks recovered on Wednesday, supported primarily by renewed interest in artificial intelligence companies. The Nasdaq Composite gained around 1.6%, the S&P 500 rose roughly 1.3%, and the Dow advanced about 0.5%. Concerns about technology valuations and the sustainability of AI infrastructure spending had weighed on shares previously.

AI cloud provider CoreWeave reported strong quarterly revenue growth and raised its full-year outlook, lifting sentiment across the sector. Bitcoin, however, stayed near $74,000 during the earlier trading period after dipping toward $72,700 on Tuesday. Although the price partially stabilized, it did not stage a rebound comparable to the technology sector. Bitcoin and technology shares sometimes respond similarly to changes in risk appetite, but their movements are not permanently linked.

## Four days of Bitcoin ETF outflows

US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded four consecutive trading days of net withdrawals, highlighting continued investor caution. These funds allow exposure to Bitcoin through traditional financial products.

* November 5: approximately $430 million
* November 6: approximately $412 million
* November 9: approximately $201 million
* November 10: approximately $95 million

Combined net outflows over the four days reached approximately $1.14 billion. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) recorded around $78 million in net withdrawals on November 10 alone. The pace of daily outflows has been declining, which could suggest that ETF-related selling pressure is easing. However, it does not yet demonstrate a return of institutional demand, and ETF flows do not explain every Bitcoin price movement.

## US inflation adds pressure to crypto

Consumer prices in the US rose 0.4% month over month in October, above expectations, according to data released November 10. The surprise strengthened expectations that the Federal Reserve might raise interest rates again. Traders assigned an approximately 90% probability to a December increase in some market snapshots. This was a market expectation, not a decision already taken.

Higher interest rates can make certain fixed-income assets more attractive, raise financing costs and weigh on riskier investments. Although some investors view Bitcoin as a long-term hedge against monetary risks, over short periods it often behaves as a risk-sensitive asset. Stronger inflation therefore does not automatically lift Bitcoin prices.

## Liquidations add further stress

Across the broader cryptocurrency derivatives market, liquidations reached approximately $725 million during one observed 24-hour period, with leveraged LONG positions heavily affected. When prices move against traders and remaining margin becomes insufficient, exchanges may automatically close positions. Those closures can add selling pressure and accelerate a decline, even without a new negative event involving Bitcoin itself.

## Why is Bitcoin not following AI stocks?

AI shares had a specific catalyst in CoreWeave's financial results and renewed demand for computing infrastructure. Bitcoin instead faces ETF withdrawals, weaker demand indicators and the effects of recent liquidations.

Unlike individual companies, Bitcoin does not report quarterly earnings or corporate revenue that can prompt an immediate valuation change. Its short-term price depends heavily on supply and demand, liquidity, institutional capital flows and monetary-policy expectations. This does not mean its broader relationship with technology stocks has disappeared, only that the markets can recover at different times.

## Which Bitcoin price levels matter?

After the decline toward $72,700, traders have focused on the region around $73,000 to $74,000. Some technical analyses highlight the 200-day moving average near $73,200 and the 365-day average near $76,400 as reference levels. None of these figures guarantees a future price direction.

The options market also signals caution. A substantial share of actively traded contracts expiring in November are put options, which can be used to hedge existing exposure or speculate on a further decline. Their popularity alone is not proof that all market participants expect lower prices.

## Is easing selling pressure the start of a recovery?

Smaller daily ETF outflows and stabilization after a sharp drop could be early signs of calmer conditions. A more sustainable recovery would require evidence of renewed ETF inflows, fewer forced liquidations and stronger spot-market demand. Short-lived rebounds can also result from traders closing SHORT positions without changing the broader trend. A Wall Street rally is therefore insufficient evidence that Bitcoin will automatically follow.
