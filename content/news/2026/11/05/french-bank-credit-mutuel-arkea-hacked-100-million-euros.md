**French banking group Crédit Mutuel Arkéa has confirmed a major cyberattack in which attackers managed to transfer approximately €100 million from an account belonging to the bank itself to accounts at other financial institutions. According to official statements, customer accounts were not affected, but the incident has raised serious questions about the security of Europe's banking system. Particularly concerning is the fact that the attackers gained access through a platform operated by an external service provider, while some of the stolen money may have reached cryptocurrency exchanges. The incident comes just days after the Binance hack, in which digital assets worth approximately $6.34 billion were stolen.**

## How did hackers steal money from the French bank?

Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a banking group headquartered in Brest, France, officially confirmed on November 4, 2026, that it had been the victim of a sophisticated cyberattack. According to information released by the bank that day, the attackers exploited the platform of an external service provider to gain access to the banking system and initiate unauthorized financial transactions.

Unlike common online fraud schemes in which criminals attempt to steal money from individual customer accounts, the target in this case was an account belonging to the bank itself. The attackers used that account to transfer funds to other banking institutions and may subsequently have converted the money into cryptocurrencies through specialized platforms.

The bank has not publicly identified the compromised service provider or explained in detail which technical method the attackers used to gain access. It confirmed that the attack originated externally and ruled out ordinary human error by its employees as the cause. Investigators must now determine whether the attackers exploited stolen credentials, a vulnerability in the partner's system or another method of access.

## Around €100 million missing, but the final amount remains unknown

According to information published on November 5, the value of the missing funds is approximately €100 million, equivalent to around $112.6 million. Initial media reports also mentioned €118 million, but Crédit Mutuel Arkéa did not confirm that figure. The bank acknowledged the approximate scale of the loss while emphasizing that the final financial consequences were still being assessed.

Some of the funds were successfully blocked, while efforts to recover the remainder are continuing. At this stage, it has not been confirmed how much money will ultimately be recovered, how much has already moved outside the traditional banking system or whether the attackers succeeded in converting any of the funds into digital assets.

It is important to distinguish the total value of unauthorized transactions from the bank's final financial loss. If some transfers are stopped or subsequently reversed, the actual loss could be smaller than the amount currently being reported. The bank also stressed that the incident does not threaten its financial stability, although it represents an exceptionally large theft from a European financial institution.

## Why were customer accounts not affected?

This case differs from many other banking fraud incidents because the attackers managed to steal money belonging to the bank itself, while its customers' accounts were not affected, according to official information. This raises questions about which parts of the banking system the attackers were actually able to access and which security barriers may have remained intact.

One possible explanation is that the attackers failed to gain access to the systems protecting individual customer accounts. However, this has not been confirmed. It is equally possible that they deliberately targeted the bank's own account from the beginning because it offered an opportunity to transfer larger amounts of money. The investigation must still establish the actual extent of their access privileges.

After detecting the attack, Crédit Mutuel Arkéa temporarily suspended certain transactions to prevent further unauthorized transfers. These precautionary measures caused delays in the execution of some payments and money transfers. On November 4, the bank announced that almost all operations had resumed following additional security checks.

The bank specifically denied reports suggesting that the attack had been carried out through its online banking subsidiary, Fortuneo. According to its official explanation, Fortuneo was not the entry point, and its customers' funds were not at risk. Nevertheless, the bank warned customers about potential follow-up phishing attacks, as criminals frequently exploit widely reported security incidents to distribute fraudulent security notifications.

## Did the stolen money reach cryptocurrency exchanges?

One of the most interesting aspects of the case concerns the possible conversion of stolen funds into cryptocurrencies. Crédit Mutuel Arkéa confirmed that the attackers transferred money to other banks, potentially allowing them to move the funds further toward digital asset trading platforms.

According to media reports, cryptocurrency exchange Kraken detected unusually large transfers associated with a European bank and stopped some suspicious transactions. Blockchain researchers also tracked attempts to move funds across cryptocurrency platforms. These details are based on media investigations and have not been fully confirmed in the bank's official statements.

It is important to emphasize that the money was not stolen from a cryptocurrency system. Based on the information confirmed so far, the funds were traditional bank assets that were transferred without authorization through conventional financial infrastructure. Cryptocurrencies appear only as a possible subsequent step in attempts to conceal the origin of the money.

Such a strategy can allow criminals to move funds rapidly across multiple platforms and jurisdictions. However, public blockchains also leave transaction records that can be traced, helping specialized investigators and cryptocurrency exchanges identify and freeze suspicious assets.

## French authorities launch an investigation

The Paris public prosecutor's office confirmed that it opened an investigation following the bank's complaint on November 4. The case concerns an organized attack against an automated data processing system, with the investigation being conducted by French authorities specializing in cybercrime.

Crédit Mutuel Arkéa also notified the European Central Bank and the relevant French regulatory authorities. The involvement of European supervisory institutions indicates that the incident is being treated not merely as an isolated financial fraud case but also as a serious security issue affecting banking infrastructure.

The identity of the attackers has not yet been publicly established. There is no confirmed evidence linking the incident to the North Korean groups mentioned in connection with the Binance hack, nor has it been demonstrated that the same criminal organization or security vulnerability was responsible for both attacks.

Investigators will need to answer several important questions: how the external platform was compromised, which permissions the attackers managed to exploit, why unauthorized transactions could be executed and how much time passed before the activity was detected.

## Why is an attack on a bank particularly concerning?

European banks operate in a highly regulated environment and are required to maintain strong security standards for their financial systems. That is precisely why the Crédit Mutuel Arkéa case has attracted particular attention. Although attempted cyberattacks against banks occur every day, the successful digital transfer of tens of millions of euros from a major financial institution is considerably less common.

A particular concern is banks' dependence on external technology partners. Modern financial institutions use numerous interconnected platforms for payment processing, infrastructure management, communication and automation. Each system may be individually protected, but its connection to the bank creates additional opportunities for attackers.

If an external service provider has permissions that allow financial transactions to be initiated or approved, compromising its systems can have direct financial consequences. As a result, a bank's security depends not only on its own employees and computers but also on the quality of protection throughout its network of technology partners.

The incident also raises questions about controls over exceptionally large transactions. Financial institutions use various systems to detect unusual activity, but investigators must still establish why the safeguards in this case did not prevent every unauthorized transfer. The fact that the bank subsequently managed to stop some of the funds shows that certain controls worked, but it does not reveal where the initial security failure occurred.

## CZ warned that banks are vulnerable to cyberattacks

The Crédit Mutuel Arkéa attack comes as the global financial industry continues to examine the theft of approximately $6.34 billion from Binance on October 29. In that case, attackers spent months preparing their operation, including fake job interviews, compromised coding assignments and the misuse of internal technology tools.

Interestingly, Changpeng Zhao, widely known as CZ and the founder of Binance, publicly warned about security risks facing traditional banks on November 3. In an interview with CoinDesk, he argued that banks were, in his opinion, even more vulnerable to cyberattacks than cryptocurrency exchanges. He also emphasized that advances in artificial intelligence were expanding the capabilities available to cybercriminals.

His remarks attracted additional attention after the theft at Crédit Mutuel Arkéa became public. However, Zhao's statement represents his personal assessment rather than proof that banks are generally less secure than cryptocurrency exchanges. Two major incidents occurring within days of each other demonstrate that both types of financial institutions can face serious attacks, but they do not provide a reliable comparison of their overall security.

In the French bank's case, a different entry point has been confirmed: a platform operated by an external service provider. Although there is no evidence of a direct connection between the two attacks, both incidents highlight the risks created when attackers exploit legitimate access to systems with extensive financial permissions.

Following the Binance incident, US regulators contacted banks to examine potential security vulnerabilities. This demonstrates that cyber risks affecting the cryptocurrency industry and traditional banking are increasingly being treated as interconnected security challenges.
