**Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, is facing one of the biggest security tests in the history of digital assets. Following a cyberattack in which approximately $6.3 billion was stolen, customers have withdrawn a net $4.72 billion since withdrawals resumed. Nevertheless, that amount represents less than 3% of the exchange's reported assets, while Bitcoin held near $78,000. The question is not only how much money has left the platform, but also how much confidence remains.**

## From $3.64 Billion to $4.72 Billion in Net Outflows

Following the attack on October 29, 2026, Binance restricted withdrawals for approximately 48 hours. On Sunday, November 1, the exchange began gradually restoring withdrawal services for selected cryptocurrencies and networks. During the first nine and a half hours, customers withdrew approximately $4.65 billion, while deposits amounted to roughly $1.01 billion, resulting in an initial net outflow of around $3.64 billion.

The figure increased during Monday. According to more recent reports, Binance recorded approximately $7.4 billion in gross withdrawals and $2.7 billion in deposits after reopening its withdrawal services. The net difference reached **$4.72 billion**, equivalent to approximately **2.8%** of assets held on the platform. This is a more recent figure than the $3.64 billion reported earlier in the morning, not an additional outflow that should be added to the original amount.

Large figures require context. In December 2022, following the collapse of FTX, Binance processed approximately $6 billion in net withdrawals over 72 hours. The current outflows therefore indicate considerable anxiety among customers, but they do not, by themselves, prove that Binance is experiencing the same scenario as FTX. Reuters reported that some analysts considered the present proportion of withdrawals relatively moderate compared with the exchange's overall size.

## What Was Stolen, and How Is Binance Covering the Losses?

The attack affected digital assets valued at approximately **$6.3 billion**. The US Federal Bureau of Investigation attributed the theft to actors linked to North Korea, specifically the TraderTraitor group. Investigations and efforts to trace the stolen assets are continuing. Public attribution of the attack does not mean that all funds have been recovered or that every technical aspect of the incident has already been clarified.

In its <a href="https://www.binance.com/en-GB/support/announcement/security-incident-recovery-update-4-withdrawals-resume-in-phases-2026-11-01-73911fb819b240f88b88256bdd318eda" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official update published on November 1</a>, Binance announced that it had secured replacement assets covering approximately **95% of the affected amount**. According to the exchange, major customer balances, including BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL and USDC, are once again fully backed. The remaining 5% primarily involves less liquid tokens that are more difficult to acquire in large quantities without causing substantial price movements. Binance states that it will bear the financial burden itself, using corporate funds and available protective reserves.

This highlights an important distinction between financial reimbursement and the complete resolution of a security incident. Securing replacement assets does not prove that the originally stolen tokens have been recovered or that every security risk has been eliminated. The exchange has announced that it will publish an incident report once the investigation is complete and continue gradually restoring the remaining withdrawal services.

## Withdrawing Crypto From an Exchange Is Not the Same as Selling Bitcoin

Net outflows represent the difference between assets leaving an exchange and assets entering the platform. However, this figure **does not, by itself, reveal whether a customer has sold cryptocurrency**. An investor may transfer Bitcoin to a private wallet and continue holding it for years. Another customer may move USDT to a competing platform, while a third may sell tokens and withdraw money from the cryptocurrency ecosystem entirely. The effects on market prices differ considerably between these scenarios.

To assess actual selling pressure, it is necessary to examine the composition of withdrawals, market orders, liquidity, stablecoin flows and leveraged positions. Reuters reported on Monday that the total market capitalization of stablecoins had declined since Friday, potentially indicating that some capital had moved into cash or traditional assets. However, this is an indication rather than proof of where every withdrawn dollar ultimately went.

Bitcoin remained near **$78,000** on Monday after touching its lowest level in approximately six weeks on Friday. Ethereum partially recovered from a two-month low. This indicates that the immediate market reaction was not proportional to the nominal value of the theft, but it does not guarantee that volatility has ended. Prices change throughout the trading day, so these levels represent a market snapshot rather than the closing prices for November 2.

## ETF Outflows Add Further Pressure to Market Sentiment

The security incident occurred at a time when institutional demand was already showing signs of weakness. According to Farside Investors data for Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs, US spot Bitcoin funds recorded **$643.2 million in net outflows** on Friday, October 30, while Ethereum funds experienced an additional **$155.7 million** in withdrawals. Combined daily net outflows therefore amounted to nearly **$798.9 million**.

Friday's withdrawals from Bitcoin ETFs erased approximately $440.8 million in net inflows accumulated over the previous four trading sessions. As a result, the entire week from October 26 to October 30 ended with approximately $202.4 million in net outflows from Bitcoin funds and around $431.4 million from Ethereum funds. Ethereum ETFs recorded five consecutive trading days of negative flows. However, it would be incorrect to automatically attribute all these withdrawals to the Binance attack: the data show a coincidence in timing and negative market sentiment, but they do not reveal the motivations of individual investors.

At the time this article was prepared, final aggregated data for all US spot ETFs covering November 2 were not yet available. Therefore, Friday, October 30, remains the latest completed and confirmed comparison period, rather than relying on estimates of Monday's fund flows.

## Why Is Trust Becoming More Important Than a Single Price?

Centralized cryptocurrency exchanges play an important role in trading, connecting buyers and sellers, and providing access to liquidity. However, customers also transfer some control over the custody of their assets to these platforms. Even when an exchange has sufficient reserves to compensate for a security incident, a suspension of withdrawals highlights an operational risk that does not exist in the same form when users directly control their private keys.

On the other hand, self-custody of cryptocurrencies introduces different risks, including the loss of private keys, phishing, transfers to incorrect addresses and technical mistakes. Therefore, the response to a security incident should not be a universal recommendation for all customers to immediately withdraw every asset from exchanges. What matters more is understanding who controls the private keys, which account protections are in place, whether reliable proof of reserves is available and what options customers have when a service becomes temporarily unavailable.

The current crisis also tests Binance's ability to finance reimbursements, conduct security checks and maintain sufficient liquidity at the same time. These are separate challenges. The exchange's official claim that approximately 95% of the affected assets have been replaced represents a positive step. However, the long-term restoration of confidence will also depend on the transparency of the investigation, the full resumption of services and whether withdrawal volumes continue to decline.

## Our Analysis (Metaadvisor)

* **Nearly $5 billion in outflows does not automatically mean $5 billion in sales.** It is essential to distinguish between transferring assets away from an exchange and withdrawing capital from the entire cryptocurrency market.
* **The approximately 2.8% share is just as important as the absolute amount.** A large nominal outflow reflects customers' reactions, but without comparing it with the platform's total assets, it can create a misleading impression of the scale of financial risk.
* **Compensating customers is not the same as resolving the security problem.** Binance's ability to secure replacement assets reduces the immediate financial damage, but questions about the causes of the attack and future protective measures still require answers.
* **ETF flows indicate broader caution.** Withdrawals from US funds occurred before the security incident was fully resolved and demonstrate that cryptocurrency sentiment also depends on macroeconomic conditions, liquidity and investors' willingness to take risks.
* **Binance has so far demonstrated an ability to absorb a major financial shock**, and withdrawals have not reached a level that would, by itself, prove that most customers have lost confidence in the exchange. However, the biggest test still lies ahead: Will Binance transparently explain the attack, fully restore its services and maintain liquidity without triggering another wave of panic? For the market, the combination of these signals matters more than a single day's Bitcoin price.
