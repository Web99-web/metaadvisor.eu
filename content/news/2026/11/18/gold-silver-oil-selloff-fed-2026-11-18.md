**Gold, silver and crude oil all fell sharply on November 18, 2026. Gold broke below $3,800 an ounce, silver approached $45 and U.S. WTI crude lost substantially more than Brent. The declines occurred together, but the drivers were not identical. A firmer dollar and expectations of higher U.S. interest rates weighed on precious metals, while oil faced additional pressure from inventory data and concerns about a potential supply surplus.**

## How far did commodity prices fall?

A later-session market snapshot showed gold near $3,736.52 per ounce, down approximately 2.93%, and silver around $45.02, a decline of roughly 4.97%. WTI crude traded near $81.66 a barrel, down about 5.06%, while Brent was around $91.19, approximately 2.09% lower. These figures are observations of different instruments at a specific point in time. They should not be interpreted as a uniform set of official exchange settlement prices.

For gold, the break below the $3,800 threshold was particularly significant. CME Group reported that December gold futures fell roughly 2.8% to about $3,757, the lowest level since September 2025. Silver fell faster. Unlike gold, silver also has substantial industrial uses, leaving it sensitive to both interest-rate expectations and concern about manufacturing demand.

## Why are precious metals under pressure?

The Federal Reserve released the minutes of its October policy meeting. Almost all participants considered further rate increases likely to be appropriate under their baseline economic outlooks. That is not a decision to raise rates in December, but CME FedWatch futures pricing showed a very high market-implied probability of a quarter-point increase during the session.

When expected interest rates rise, bonds and other interest-bearing assets become more competitive with physical gold and silver, which do not pay interest. A stronger dollar can also make dollar-denominated commodities more expensive to overseas buyers. Silver carries an additional cyclical risk because part of its demand depends on industrial activity.

## Oil faces a separate supply story

Crude prices were pressured by more than monetary policy. Evidence of rising U.S. petroleum inventories and expectations of growing global supplies weighed on the market on November 18. Slower demand growth would add to those concerns if tighter financing conditions persisted. Traders were also assessing the possibility of recovering Middle Eastern exports.

Preliminary industry estimates of U.S. inventories should be distinguished from the official weekly Energy Information Administration figures. WTI and Brent are also different crude benchmarks, so they can respond differently to regional logistics and supply conditions even during a broader selloff.

## Does a broad selloff signal more trouble?

When precious metals, oil and Bitcoin decline on the same day, it can signal widespread risk reduction or a need for liquidity. However, correlated moves do not prove a single shared cause. Gold is particularly sensitive to yields and the dollar, silver also reflects industrial expectations, and oil depends heavily on supply and demand. One session of selling cannot establish a reliable longer-term trend on its own.

## Our Perspective (Metaadvisor)

* **Gold is not immune to higher rates.** Its role as a defensive asset does not prevent short-term losses.
* **Silver is more volatile.** Demand comes from both investors and industrial users.
* **WTI fell more than Brent.** Regional factors matter even when the wider commodity market moves in the same direction.
* **The Fed has not decided on December.** Market-implied probabilities are not central-bank commitments.
* **One day does not define a trend.** Dollar strength, bond yields, inventories, growth data and liquidity will be key to follow.
