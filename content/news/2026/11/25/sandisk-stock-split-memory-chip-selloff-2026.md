**From over $1,000 to around $74? At first glance, the monthly chart of SanDisk’s perpetual contract on MEXC looks catastrophic. However, the company has just implemented an 8-for-1 stock split. Although this explains much of the visual decline, the actual sell-off is significant: SNDK shares lost 11.13% on Nasdaq on November 24, and weakness continued overnight.**

## How much has SanDisk actually fallen?

The stock closed at $87.71 on November 23 and at $77.95 on November 24. Its low during the regular trading session was $74.83. Yahoo Finance also recorded an overnight quote of $74.68.

On November 25 at 08:22 Central European Time, we recorded an SNDKUSDT perpetual contract price of **74.14 USDT** on MEXC, with a 24-hour low of 74.03 USDT. This is not the same quote as the actual share price on Nasdaq: it is a derivative with its own trading dynamics.

## Why does the monthly chart look catastrophic?

Since November 20, SanDisk shares have been trading on an 8-for-1 split-adjusted basis. One old share became eight new shares, without any change in proportional ownership solely as a result of the split.

For example, a pre-split price of $1,200 corresponds to a post-split price of $150. Only a change relative to that $150 represents a market move. The MEXC monthly chart we reviewed displays values from before and after the split and should therefore not be used to calculate returns without appropriate adjustment.

However, the split does not erase the actual loss. Trefis reports that SanDisk closed at $937.46 on November 12 before the split, equivalent to approximately $117.18 on the new basis. The November 24 closing price of $77.95 therefore represents a decline of roughly one-third relative to that adjusted price.

## Why is the memory chip market under pressure?

Benzinga cites concerns about future AI infrastructure spending, competition from Chinese memory manufacturers and the sustainability of high NAND and HBM memory prices. The pressure is not limited to one company; it affects the broader semiconductor sector.

SanDisk produces NAND flash memory and SSD devices that enable large volumes of data to be stored. Data centre expansion may increase demand for such products, but profitability also depends on how quickly supply grows and the prices agreed in customer contracts.

## AI demand does not guarantee permanently high margins

Memory manufacturing is cyclical. High prices encourage investment in additional capacity, and if supply subsequently grows faster than demand, prices and margins may weaken. This is a possible scenario, not confirmation that the NAND memory market is already oversupplied.

Contract prices, inventories, manufacturers’ capital expenditure and actual data centre orders will be important in assessing the next phase of the cycle. Growth in the number of AI projects does not necessarily mean equivalent profit growth for every chip manufacturer.

## What should perpetual contract traders distinguish?

A perpetual contract does not confer ownership of a share. In addition to the underlying instrument’s price, leverage, position funding, liquidity and exchange rules for corporate events such as stock splits can affect results. Historical charts may be visually misleading if earlier prices are not adjusted.

## Our view

- **A split is not a loss:** The nominal share price changes in proportion to the number of shares.
- **The sell-off is real:** The daily stock market decline of 11.13% cannot be explained by the split alone.
- **AI and the memory cycle:** Demand for data storage can grow while memory prices simultaneously come under pressure.
- **Different markets:** The price of a perpetual contract in USDT must be distinguished from the stock market price in USD.

**SanDisk illustrates why, during periods of substantial market volatility, it is important to distinguish corporate actions, actual losses and expectations of future earnings.**
