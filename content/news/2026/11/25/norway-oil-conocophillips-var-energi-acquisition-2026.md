**More than half a century after the discovery of Ekofisk, one of the foundations of Norway’s oil industry, US company ConocoPhillips has agreed to sell its Norwegian operations to Vår Energi. The transaction is valued at $4.425 billion. Meanwhile, Norwegian crude oil production increased by 8.6% in October compared with September. What does the change in ownership mean for the future of European energy supply?**

## What is being sold for $4.425 billion?

The agreement announced on November 23, 2026, covers interests in 13 producing fields on the Norwegian continental shelf, including the Ekofisk area, as well as associated transportation infrastructure. The transaction also includes a 40.25% interest in the UK’s Teesside oil terminal.

Completion is expected towards the end of the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals. The $4.425 billion figure represents the agreed value before anticipated adjustments, rather than the amount payable in full to the seller on the closing date. ConocoPhillips expects approximately $2.5 billion in net cash proceeds.

## Norwegian company strengthens its position

Vår Energi, whose largest shareholder is Italy’s Eni, is expanding its presence on the Norwegian continental shelf through the acquisition. Following completion of this transaction and the previously announced BlueNord transaction, the company expects combined pro forma production of around 550,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day for 2026.

Its long-term target has been increased to more than 500,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day by the mid-2030s. Oil equivalent includes natural gas, so this figure is not the same as crude oil production. It is a business target, not a guarantee of future results.

## Norwegian oil production rose 8.6% in October

According to preliminary Norwegian Offshore Directorate figures published on November 20, Norwegian crude oil production averaged 1.843 million barrels per day in October.

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---:|
| October 2025 | 1.901 million barrels per day |
| September 2026 | 1.697 million barrels per day |
| October 2026 | 1.843 million barrels per day |
| Change from September | +8.6% |
| Change from October 2025 | −3.1% |
| Difference from October forecast | +5.9% |

October production of all liquid hydrocarbons, including NGL and condensate, amounted to approximately 2.03 million barrels per day. Natural gas sales reached 11 billion standard cubic metres, 2.1 billion more than in September.

The monthly recovery should therefore not be interpreted as evidence of a long-term increase in crude oil production: October output was still lower than a year earlier.

## Why are mature oil fields still valuable?

Existing platforms, pipelines and terminals make it possible to use infrastructure that has already been built. A new owner can seek to improve reservoir recovery, connect smaller fields to existing facilities and extend their operating lives.

However, mature oil fields also have natural limitations. Production may decline over time, while infrastructure maintenance, safety investments and future decommissioning generate costs. The commercial viability of additional production also depends on future oil and gas prices.

## What does this mean for European energy security?

In yesterday’s MetaAdvisor analysis, we examined the decline in weekly crude oil exports from the Middle East. Norway’s story illustrates another part of the global energy picture: the importance of production and transportation infrastructure outside the Strait of Hormuz region.

A change in ownership of Norwegian fields will not itself create additional barrels of oil. However, the new owner’s investment in production and infrastructure maintenance may influence the long-term availability of regional energy supplies. Norwegian gas production is particularly important for European buyers connected to its pipeline network.

## Our view

- **Industry consolidation:** Vår Energi is expanding its operations by acquiring existing production capacity.
- **Infrastructure value:** Terminals, platforms and pipelines can be just as important as the fields themselves.
- **Monthly growth is not a long-term trend:** October production increased from September but remained below the previous year’s level.
- **Energy security:** Regional sources can contribute to supply diversification, but cannot automatically replace every disruption in global exports.
- **Price and investment risk:** Future production will depend on project economics, regulatory decisions and reservoir conditions.

**As ownership of Norwegian oil operations changes, the most important question for Europe remains how much energy existing fields can deliver over the next decade.**
