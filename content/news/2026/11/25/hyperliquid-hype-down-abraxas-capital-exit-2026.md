**HYPE fell nearly 8% over 24 hours and lost around 40% of its value from its all-time high. Meanwhile, blockchain analysts report that Abraxas Capital, a major institutional trader, has completely exited Hyperliquid after making more than half a billion dollars in profits. Is this simply profit-taking, or is something else behind the departure?**

On Wednesday, November 25, 2026, HYPE traded around $58 during the morning, well below its September peak of $97.96. While another token unlock is approaching, several other developments could help explain the unusually strong selling pressure.

## Abraxas Capital exits Hyperliquid

According to Lookonchain's November 25 analysis, Abraxas Capital withdrew its remaining capital from Hyperliquid. Analysts reported $12.49 billion in perpetual futures trading volume, estimated profits of $552.49 million, a final sale of 219,081 HYPE worth $13.25 million, and a purchase of 4,230 ETH. Blockchain researcher MLM estimated profits at approximately $530 million.

What makes the departure remarkable is that this was not an unsuccessful trader but a highly profitable institutional participant. These estimates concern tracked wallets, rather than audited financial statements of the fund.

## Did a security incident at a rival exchange trigger the withdrawal?

The withdrawal accelerated following a security incident at rival decentralized exchange Lighter on November 16. Blockchain analysts linked Abraxas to compromised API keys and an estimated loss of $10.39 million.

On November 16, the value of its Hyperliquid accounts fell by approximately $150 million. Two days later, positions worth around $200 million were closed and 109 million USDC was transferred to Binance. The process of unstaking HYPE also began. A reduction in account value is not equivalent to a trading loss.

The timing raises the question of whether the fund is reducing its exposure to decentralized exchanges because of security concerns. Abraxas has not publicly confirmed its reason for leaving.

## Hong Kong puts Hyperliquid on its regulatory alert list

On November 18, Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) added Hyperliquid to its list of suspicious virtual asset trading platforms. The regulator says the platform lacks the appropriate local licence. The warning is not a global ban or proof of fraud, but adds regulatory uncertainty.

## XMR1 loses 99.8% of its value in half an hour

On November 20, XMR1, a token representing Monero on Hyperliquid, fell from roughly $500 to $1 in around 30 minutes. According to Bitquery's analysis, token sales generated $13.51 million from 420 buyers. A representative of issuer Wagyu said the private key controlling token distribution had been compromised.

There is no confirmed compromise of the underlying Hyperliquid protocol. The incident concerns a separate project within its ecosystem.

## Why was the previous HYPE unlock different?

In early October, Metaadvisor.eu reported a planned OTC agreement under which one institutional buyer was expected to acquire all 3.75 million HYPE tokens, then worth approximately $329 million. A private transaction outside the public market can reduce immediate selling pressure.

Tokenomics.com's calendar indicates a potential unlock of around 14.18 million HYPE on November 29, worth approximately $822 million at $58 per token. Available calendars, however, disagree on the schedule. The figure covers different recipient categories and should not be equated solely with team allocations.

An unlock is not the same as a sale. Available information does not confirm a comparable OTC arrangement for this distribution.

## Liquidations amplify the decline

Available market data indicate approximately $2.13 million in HYPE long liquidations over 24 hours, while open interest fell around 22% over seven days. Forced closures of leveraged positions can amplify selling pressure.

## Institutional interest has not disappeared

On October 29, Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), owner of the NYSE, announced an agreement with TradeXYZ to develop regulated perpetual futures markets using Hyperliquid infrastructure. Interest in the technology does not necessarily translate into a higher token price.

## Our perspective

The departure of a major institutional trader, security incidents, regulatory pressure, forced liquidations and a potential increase in token supply are occurring almost simultaneously. It would be misleading to attribute the current HYPE decline solely to the unlock.

**Is Abraxas's departure an isolated decision by one fund, or the beginning of a broader institutional retreat from decentralized exchanges?** The answer could matter more for HYPE's future price than the unlock itself.
