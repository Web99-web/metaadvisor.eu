**Chinese technology giant Huawei has announced a new smartphone brand, OSTA, known as Xingmang in China, with its official unveiling scheduled for November 20, 2026. The move comes six years after Huawei sold its former Honor brand amid restrictions imposed by the United States on access to technology. The new project could mark a return to Huawei's multi-brand smartphone strategy while expanding its presence in devices featuring advanced artificial intelligence capabilities.**

## Huawei officially confirms the OSTA brand

Richard Yu Chengdong, chairman of Huawei's consumer business group, confirmed on the Chinese social platform Weibo on November 11 that the company will introduce its new OSTA brand, known as Xingmang in China, on November 20. The presentation is scheduled for 2:30 p.m. Beijing time, or 7:30 a.m. Central European Time. It will take place as part of a broader Huawei event involving products from its intelligent vehicle ecosystem.

An intriguing detail attracted particular attention: Yu posted his announcement from a device identified as the OSTA O1 Pro. This is the first direct indication that a device bearing that name already exists within Huawei's ecosystem, although its final specifications have not been officially unveiled. A day later, on November 12, the brand's official account published its first promotional video. The visual identity uses the slogan "Come As You Are," while Huawei emphasizes innovation, design and new technological possibilities.

## Why did Huawei sell Honor six years ago?

Honor was founded in 2013 as a Huawei brand primarily aimed at affordable and mid-range smartphones. It enabled the company to compete with other Chinese manufacturers, including Xiaomi, Oppo and Vivo, without directly changing the positioning of its premium devices.

The situation changed after the United States imposed severe restrictions on Huawei in 2019 and 2020. The measures made access to US technology, software and advanced semiconductors manufactured using certain American technologies more difficult. In November 2020, Huawei sold Honor to a consortium of Chinese distributors and other investors. At the time, Huawei stated officially that it would retain no ownership stake or management role after the transaction was completed.

The sale was intended to allow Honor to continue operating outside Huawei's ownership structure and to ease the consequences of supply-chain restrictions. Honor subsequently continued as a separate company and gradually expanded into more expensive devices. **OSTA is not a relaunch of Honor, nor does it mean Honor is returning to Huawei's ownership.** It is a new Huawei brand, while Honor remains an independent company.

## OSTA O1 Pro: What do we know about the first phone?

The OSTA O1 Pro name had already appeared in Chinese technology reports before the official announcement. After Richard Yu posted from a device bearing that label, the existence of the model became considerably more credible, but Huawei has still not confirmed its full technical configuration.

According to unconfirmed reports, the O1 Pro could feature an unusually wide display measuring approximately 6.39 inches, with an aspect ratio of around 16:9.5. Such a design would differ from the tall, narrow screens that dominate today's smartphone market. Rumored configurations include 12GB or 16GB of RAM, storage of up to 1TB and possible black, white and purple finishes. Some reports also mention a Huawei Kirin 9-series processor and the HarmonyOS operating system.

However, **the display, processor, memory, colors and price have not been officially confirmed as final product specifications**. It is also unclear whether Huawei will introduce only the O1 Pro or a standard OSTA O1 model as well. The official event on November 20 should clarify at least some of these questions.

## Artificial intelligence could be the key difference

Huawei offered another indication of the brand's direction on November 12. Richard Yu said the company wants to explore new possibilities for artificial intelligence through OSTA. The message comes as smartphone manufacturers attempt to move beyond conventional voice assistants toward systems capable of completing more complex tasks across different applications.

Traditional mobile AI assistants mostly answer questions, retrieve information or execute simple commands. A newer generation aims to connect several steps, such as searching for content, interpreting a user's request and carrying out actions in other applications. Huawei is already developing its own HarmonyOS operating system and the Celia AI assistant. Their integration could matter for future OSTA products, particularly if Huawei wants to deliver features built on close coordination among hardware, software and artificial intelligence.

Nevertheless, Huawei has not yet published a detailed list of AI features for future OSTA phones. It is therefore impossible to confirm whether the devices will offer advanced autonomous app control, what level of privacy they will provide or how substantially their capabilities will differ from existing Huawei models.

## A new brand does not necessarily mean cheaper phones

Honor's history has fueled speculation that OSTA could take over its former role as Huawei's more affordable brand. However, the available information does not support a firm conclusion. Huawei already operates several recognizable device ranges. The Mate and Pura series mainly target the premium segment, while the nova and Enjoy families serve other parts of the market.

OSTA could allow Huawei to segment its offering further, but it remains unclear whether the main distinction will be price, design, AI features or sales channels. Unofficial reports about the O1 Pro suggest the first device may not belong to the lowest price tier. A wide screen, substantial memory and advanced features could point to more ambitious positioning. Without official pricing and specifications, it is too early to describe OSTA as a direct successor to the original, lower-priced Honor brand.

## Why is Huawei launching a new brand now?

Huawei has gradually rebuilt its position in China's smartphone market in recent years, despite restrictions affecting its international supply chain. According to market research company IDC, Chinese smartphone shipments fell 15.4% year over year in the third quarter of 2026, to approximately 57.9 million devices.

Huawei, by contrast, increased its own shipments by 17.3% and captured approximately 21.1% market share, maintaining the leading position in its home market. That performance is particularly notable because many competitors experienced declining shipments during the same period. Still, growth by one manufacturer in an overall shrinking market does not guarantee the trend will continue. Chinese consumers are keeping their existing devices longer, competition remains intense, and rising costs for memory chips and other components are placing additional pressure on manufacturers.

Huawei could use a new brand to reach customer segments that its current portfolio does not serve as effectively. At the same time, OSTA could broaden the company's market reach without substantially changing the positioning of its established product lines.

## Huawei has been preparing the name for years

Trademark records suggest Huawei did not begin developing the new brand's identity only this November. The company had already filed an application in 2023 for a name associated with the Chinese designation Xingmang, while additional trademark applications were recorded during 2026. Filings connected with OSTA span several electronics categories, including smartphones, tablets, laptops, smartwatches, glasses, headphones and chips.

That scope may point to a longer-term possibility of building a broader device ecosystem. However, registering a trademark in a particular product category does not mean a company will actually manufacture everything listed. Businesses often protect names in advance to preserve options for future expansion. For now, the brand's upcoming introduction has been officially confirmed, while the precise scope of its future product range remains open.

## Can OSTA succeed outside China?

One of the most important unanswered questions is the international availability of the new phones. Huawei has a strong position in China, supported by its domestic app ecosystem, HarmonyOS and in-house technology capabilities. The situation outside China is more complicated, particularly in markets where Google's mobile services form an important part of users' expectations.

Success in Europe or other international markets would require more than a powerful processor and a good camera. App availability, service compatibility, repair networks, pricing and long-term software support all matter. Huawei has not yet confirmed which markets will receive OSTA phones or whether and when they could become available outside China. At this stage, it is therefore reasonable to view OSTA primarily as the next step in Huawei's domestic strategy, with the possibility of international expansion later.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

* **OSTA is an officially confirmed new Huawei brand.** Its presentation is scheduled for November 20, 2026.
* **It is not the return of Honor.** Huawei sold Honor in 2020, and Honor has operated independently since then.
* **OSTA O1 Pro is no longer just a rumored name.** The label appeared alongside a post from a senior Huawei executive, but final specifications remain unconfirmed.
* **AI is likely to play an important role.** Huawei has officially said the new brand will explore additional possibilities for artificial intelligence.
* **Pricing and market positioning remain unknown.** There is no confirmation that OSTA will directly replace Honor's former affordable range.
* **Huawei is growing despite a shrinking Chinese market.** IDC reports a 17.3% rise in Huawei shipments while the overall market declined by 15.4%.
* **Trademark filings suggest broader ambitions.** They do not establish that every listed device will actually be introduced.
* **International availability has not been confirmed.** It will be particularly interesting to see whether Huawei plans to offer OSTA devices to European customers.

**Six years after selling Honor, Huawei is once again creating room for a separate smartphone brand. Yet market conditions are now different: artificial intelligence is becoming a more important part of the mobile experience, manufacturers compete through their own software ecosystems, and consumers are increasingly careful about when they replace their existing phones. OSTA could become a significant part of Huawei's new strategy, but its actual role will become clearer only after the first products are unveiled.**
