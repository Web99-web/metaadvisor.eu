**Deutsche Bank confirmed on November 11, 2026, that its digital asset custody service had gone live for its first paying customers. The service targets institutional and corporate clients and initially supports selected digital assets, including Bitcoin and Ether. The launch moves the project beyond an earlier announcement into active operation.**

## From announcement to launch

The bank outlined plans for institutional digital asset custody in September, subject to regulatory approval. After obtaining approval from German financial regulator BaFin, the service began operating for initial clients. This marks an actual product launch, not simply a proposed future offering.

However, availability for paying institutions does not mean every retail customer can open a cryptocurrency custody account. Access depends on applicable contractual, regulatory and risk requirements.

## What does custody involve?

Institutional custody includes safeguarding digital wallets, managing cryptographic private keys and authorizing asset transfers. Private keys enable control over digital assets, and losing them or exposing them to unauthorized users may cause significant financial harm.

Deutsche Bank provides technical and organizational infrastructure intended to help professional customers meet security, access-control, reporting and compliance obligations. The service addresses operational needs rather than protecting asset values from market volatility.

## Bitcoin, Ether and selected stablecoins

The initial offering includes Bitcoin and Ether alongside selected stablecoins and electronic-money tokens, including USDC, EURC and EURAU. Stablecoins generally track traditional currencies, but their legal structures, reserve arrangements and risks can differ.

**Professional custody does not eliminate the risk of falling cryptocurrency prices.** Crypto-assets also differ from ordinary eligible bank deposits and do not benefit from an equivalent deposit-guarantee scheme.

## Tokenization and future services

Deutsche Bank is also examining potential applications involving tokenized financial instruments and digital transaction settlement. Further cooperation in asset management could create opportunities for additional services, but these depend on commercial decisions, demand and the regulatory framework.
