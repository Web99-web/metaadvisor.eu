**On November 21, 2026, Bitcoin traded for as little as C$24,250, approximately US$16,900, on Kraken's Canadian-dollar market while changing hands near US$57,700 on the same exchange's US-dollar market. The price disruption also affected other cryptocurrencies and even the USD/CAD trading pair. Kraken says orders were processed correctly, found no evidence of a security breach and will not reverse completed trades.**

An unusual market disruption unfolded on cryptocurrency exchange Kraken over several hours, rather than the few seconds associated with many well-known flash crashes. While Bitcoin traded at approximately $57,000 to $58,000 on global markets, transactions in Kraken's BTC/CAD pair occurred at prices equivalent to roughly $17,000 per Bitcoin. That gap does not mean Bitcoin lost 70% of its value worldwide; it means prices on one relatively narrow market diverged sharply from the broader market.

## How did Bitcoin fall to C$24,250?

Kraken said heavy selling pressure on Canadian-dollar trading pairs began around 20:00 UTC on November 20, equivalent to 21:00 in Croatia. Overnight, BTC/CAD suffered repeated steep drops and partial rebounds before touching C$24,250 on November 21, during the 15-minute interval beginning at 08:00 UTC. At approximately the same time, BTC/USD on Kraken was trading near US$57,700.

At an indicative exchange rate of C$1.4325 per US dollar, that low translated to around US$16,900. The difference from the same exchange's US-dollar market was approximately 71%, an extraordinary divergence between two pairs tracking the same asset. Kraken's data for the 15-minute interval containing the low show 976 trades totaling roughly 22.38 BTC, but do not establish how much Bitcoin actually changed hands at the very bottom.

## An even stranger reading: USDT fell to C$0.0022

Bitcoin was not the only affected asset. Kraken's publicly available market data for the relevant pairs show lows of C$796 for Ethereum, C$32 for Solana, C$0.4868 for USDC and as little as **C$0.0022 for USDT**. The USD/CAD pair registered a price of just C$0.30001 per US dollar, even though the reference exchange rate was approximately C$1.43.

The USDT figure is particularly striking because the stablecoin is designed to maintain a value close to one US dollar. However, **C$0.0022 was the recorded low in a specific Kraken trading pair, not evidence that USDT lost its dollar peg globally**. Nor do the lows in different pairs mean all the prices were reached at exactly the same moment or that large quantities could have been purchased at those levels.

The simultaneous dislocations across several markets instead suggest a disruption to price formation and available liquidity in Canadian-dollar pairs, rather than a wholesale collapse in the value of all these assets. The precise mechanism behind the incident has not been officially established.

## Kraken: No breach, executed trades stand

In a public statement on November 21, Kraken Support confirmed that Canadian-dollar trading pairs had experienced heavy selling pressure, pushing prices far below levels on other markets. The exchange said its platform had processed every order correctly and that security teams had found no evidence of a breach or compromised customer accounts. Its final message was concise: **“Executed trades stand.”**

That means Kraken does not currently intend to reverse completed purchases and sales simply because they occurred at prices far below those seen globally. For someone who sold Bitcoin, accidentally or automatically, during the steepest part of the decline, the consequences could be a substantial financial loss. Conversely, the buyer on the other side might have obtained an unusually favorable price. The number of affected customers and their actual gains or losses have not been established.

## Was someone automatically selling Bitcoin every 15 minutes?

An analysis of Kraken's public trade records published by Coindaro identified a series of market sell orders arriving at regular intervals. According to the analysis, some orders appeared hourly and later approximately every 15 minutes. It identified 28 orders involving around **363 BTC**, with an estimated gap of approximately C$10.9 million between the execution values and comparable values on the US-dollar market.

That amount is an analytical comparison of prices, **not a confirmed loss suffered by a single trader or a measured amount of market manipulation**. Public records do not identify who submitted the orders, whether they came from one account or several, or whether they formed part of an automated strategy. Regularly timed sales are a useful lead for further investigation, but do not prove that someone deliberately caused the crash.

## How could thin liquidity have made the plunge worse?

The large gap between Kraken's CAD and USD markets raises the question of whether buy orders and market participants who normally keep prices aligned disappeared at a critical moment. Blockstream CEO Adam Back suggested that a liquidity provider in Kraken's USD/CAD market might have run out of available funds. That remains his hypothesis; Kraken has not confirmed it as the cause.

When too few buyers remain in an order book, a market sell order can execute at progressively lower prices as it searches for counterparties. If arbitrage traders also cannot move funds quickly enough between markets, the same asset can temporarily trade at dramatically different prices. The incident demonstrates that even an established cryptocurrency exchange cannot guarantee deep liquidity in every trading pair at every moment.

## What happened to traders using stop-loss orders?

The incident is especially relevant to traders who use stop-loss orders to limit losses. Once a stop-market order is triggered, the resulting sale is filled against available bids, not necessarily at the trigger price. In a market with a thin order book, the actual execution price may therefore be much lower than expected.

That does not prove stop-loss orders caused this crash or that all affected traders used the same order type. Reaching such a conclusion would require account-level information about orders and executions that is not publicly available. Kraken's decision to uphold completed transactions nevertheless shows how severe the consequences can be when a market temporarily loses its usual depth.

## Prices recovered, but the cause remains unconfirmed

Kraken's BTC/CAD price gradually returned toward levels consistent with its US-dollar market. According to reports based on Kraken's data, the pair climbed back above C$80,000 during November 21. The rebound closed most of the gap, but did not answer who had placed the large sell orders or why several CAD pairs had diverged so dramatically.

No evidence has been made public establishing that Kraken deliberately manipulated the market, nor has a hack been confirmed as the cause. What is documented is an extraordinary divergence in executed market prices and the exchange's official decision not to undo those trades. A fuller account will require more information about order-book depth, liquidity providers and the exchange's market safeguards.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

- **One exchange, two prices:** Bitcoin briefly traded at the equivalent of roughly US$16,900 on BTC/CAD while remaining near US$57,700 on BTC/USD. Bitcoin did not fall to $17,000 globally.
- **USDT is another warning:** The recorded low of C$0.0022 shows how far an individual trading pair can diverge. It does not, on its own, establish a worldwide stablecoin depeg.
- **Liquidity matters:** Large sell orders can move prices much further when there are not enough buyers in the order book.
- **A stop-loss does not guarantee an execution price:** Automated orders do not eliminate the risk of poor fills during a sharp market move.
- **Manipulation has not been proven:** Regularly spaced sell orders warrant investigation, but reveal neither the identity nor the intent of the traders.
- **Accountability remains an open question:** Kraken says its systems worked correctly. The broader question is what protections exchanges should provide when one pair diverges dramatically from the rest of the market.

**For traders, the essential distinction is between the market price shown elsewhere and the price at which their own order can actually be executed. The Kraken incident demonstrated that under extreme conditions, the difference can run into tens of thousands of dollars per Bitcoin.**
