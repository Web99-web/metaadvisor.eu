**Hackers linked to the October Binance theft are active again. On November 19, 2026, they moved approximately 137,602 ETH, worth around $248 million, to 12 different crypto addresses in just 16 minutes. The transfers represent another movement of previously stolen assets, not a new attack. Some ETH moved earlier has already been exchanged for Bitcoin, but there is no confirmed sale of the $248 million transferred today.**

## What happened today?

Blockchain transaction monitoring shows that a wallet associated with the theft made **12 transfers** on November 19 between approximately 07:51 and 08:07 Central European Time. Eleven transactions involved 12,000 ETH each, while the final transfer contained approximately 5,602 ETH.

In total, **137,602 ETH**, worth approximately **$248 million** at the time of transfer, was moved. Since November 17, researchers have tracked the redistribution of approximately **290,898 ETH** linked to the same incident.

**Moving cryptocurrency is not the same as selling it.** Today's transactions confirm transfers between addresses. By themselves, they do not establish that the full amount was sold on an exchange, converted to Bitcoin or exchanged for cash.

## A continuation of the major October theft

The activity is linked to the attack on Binance's internet-connected wallets (*hot wallets*) in late October 2026. The value of the assets stolen at the time was estimated at approximately $6.3 billion to $6.4 billion.

Investigators have tracked the stolen assets across numerous addresses since the original breach. This latest wave shows that some of the stolen funds are still moving, even as legal and technical measures are being pursued to freeze them.

## Why is some ETH being converted into Bitcoin?

Monitoring of earlier transfers showed that part of the stolen Ethereum passed through decentralized services that exchange assets across blockchains, including THORChain.

This allows ETH to be exchanged for BTC and then transferred to new Bitcoin addresses. That makes it harder to reconstruct the route taken by the stolen assets, but it does not erase the digital trail entirely. Transactions remain recorded on public blockchains, where analysts can attempt to connect them.

Those earlier conversions **do not prove that today's 137,602 ETH has already been converted into Bitcoin**.

## Binance seeks to freeze the stolen funds

As the assets move between wallets, Binance and the relevant authorities continue efforts to restrict access to funds associated with the theft. According to reports on the case, approximately **$2.85 billion** has already been frozen or intercepted.

Freezing assets, however, is not the same as recovering them permanently. Their origin and ownership must be established, and the appropriate legal processes completed.

That makes the new destination addresses particularly important: subsequent transfers could show whether the attackers intend to hold, redistribute or exchange the funds.

## Could the transfers affect ETH's price?

The $248 million transfer attracted market attention because of its size. However, **a transfer between wallets does not automatically increase the supply of ETH offered for sale on exchanges**.

If the ETH remains at its new addresses, the immediate price effect could be limited. If large quantities are exchanged or sold, additional selling pressure could develop depending on liquidity and how the transactions are executed.

Ethereum's price is also affected by broader financial market conditions. It would therefore be unjustified to attribute today's, or any other, ETH price decline solely to activity in wallets linked to the theft.

## What are investigators watching next?

The key question is where the assets held at the 12 new addresses go next. Transfers to centralized exchanges, decentralized swap protocols or wallets on other blockchains would be especially relevant.

Further transactions could reveal whether the attackers are continuing to obscure the origin of the assets or attempting to convert them into funds that are easier to use. Until such activity is confirmed, the most accurate description remains **a transfer of stolen ETH**, not a sale.
