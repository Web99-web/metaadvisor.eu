**Taiwanese shares fell sharply on November 19, 2026, led by semiconductor makers and other technology companies. The benchmark TAIEX finished 4.4% lower, while Taiwan's over-the-counter market index plunged 7.22%. US spot Bitcoin and Ethereum exchange-traded funds had also recorded net outflows on the preceding day, as a stronger dollar and a more hawkish Federal Reserve weighed on investor sentiment.**

## Taiwan's TAIEX loses 1,768 points

The TAIEX closed at 38,426.89 points, down 1,768.02 points or 4.4%. It briefly fell further, to around 38,259.70, during the session. The closing level was below 40,000 and beneath the 240-day moving average, a measure followed by some investors as an indicator of the longer-term trend.

Selling was even heavier among smaller and medium-sized companies. The OTC index declined 7.22%, while Taiwan's semiconductor subindex lost 4.29%. The breadth of those declines shows that selling pressure extended beyond a single large company.

## TSMC falls 3.45%

TSMC, one of the world's most important advanced chip manufacturers, fell 3.45% to NT$2,100 per share. The NT$75 decline mattered beyond the company itself because TSMC accounts for more than 40% of Taiwan's stock-market capitalization. Its share price therefore has an outsized effect on the TAIEX.

The selloff unfolded against broader concerns about earnings and valuations across the AI industry. Alongside the expense of building data centers and purchasing chips, investors are watching US technology export restrictions on China and the risks they create for Asian supply chains.

Taiwan's financial regulator announced additional short-selling restrictions after the sharp market decline. The measures are designed to bar certain forms of short selling on the following trading day if an individual stock has fallen at least 3.5% in one session. The intervention seeks to limit selling pressure but cannot guarantee a price recovery.

## Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs record fresh outflows

Farside Investors' figures for **November 18, 2026**, show **$306.5 million in net outflows** from US spot Bitcoin ETFs. Among the biggest outflows were $148.7 million from IBIT and $69.3 million from FBTC.

On the same date, US spot Ethereum ETFs recorded **$39.8 million in net outflows**. These figures concern US investment funds on the preceding trading day. They do not prove that ETF redemptions caused Taiwan's stock-market decline, but they fit a broader pattern of investors reducing exposure to riskier assets.

## The Fed and dollar pressure multiple markets

The US dollar strengthened after more hawkish signals from Federal Reserve officials. The dollar index reached a new 19-month high on November 18, as markets reassessed the possibility of higher interest rates remaining in place for longer.

Higher expected rates can make interest-bearing dollar assets more attractive while weighing on richly valued technology stocks. Precious metals were under pressure too. On November 18, spot gold fell approximately 3.24% and silver dropped 4.98%. In that session, gold did not behave like a conventional haven from falling shares.

That does not mean all these assets must continue declining. Currencies, metals, chip stocks and cryptocurrencies have different drivers. One day's overlapping moves do not establish a lasting relationship.

## Anthropic's record IPO contrasts with infrastructure weakness

The contrast is particularly striking because Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on November 18 and raised approximately $81.31 billion in a record public offering. Demand for ownership in an AI model developer was exceptionally strong even as shares in some companies supplying the industry's hardware faced selling pressure.

These signals are not necessarily contradictory. Investors may believe in AI's long-term potential while questioning current chip-sector valuations, infrastructure spending and the time required to earn a return on those investments. A successful IPO does not automatically lift the entire technology sector.

## What does the market's SHORT signal mean?

A break below important moving averages, broad selling and pressure on smaller companies can be interpreted as a **bearish near-term market signal**. It does not prove that prices have reached a bottom or amount to a recommendation to open short positions. Taiwan's new short-selling limits also change the conditions for such trades.

The next indicators to watch are whether TSMC stabilizes, how broad any rebound becomes, movements in the dollar and US Treasury yields, further Fed statements and whether crypto ETF flows recover. A single positive session would not erase the significance of this selloff.

- **The OTC decline shows the breadth of the selloff:** A 7.22% loss points to weakness among smaller listed companies as well.
- **Crypto ETF outflows need separate interpretation:** November 18 redemptions support a cautious market narrative but do not demonstrate a causal link with Asian shares.
- **A record IPO does not solve profitability questions:** Demand for Anthropic shares does not remove the cost of chips and AI data centers.
