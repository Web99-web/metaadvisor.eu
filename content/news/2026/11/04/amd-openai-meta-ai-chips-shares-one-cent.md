**AMD has reported record quarterly revenue of $13.4 billion, but its agreements with OpenAI and Meta reveal an increasingly complex approach to financing the growth of artificial intelligence. The two technology companies have received the right to purchase a combined total of up to 320 million AMD shares at an exercise price of just one US cent per share, provided they meet contractual business obligations and other requirements. These arrangements raise an important question: how much of the AI industry's expansion reflects independent market demand, and how much is being driven by increasingly close financial relationships between chip manufacturers and their largest customers?**

## AMD reports record revenue, but warrant agreements affect earnings

US semiconductor manufacturer Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) released its financial results on November 3 for the third quarter of 2026, which ended on September 26. Total revenue reached a record $13.4 billion, representing a 45% increase compared with the same period a year earlier. Net income under US Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) was approximately $2.4 billion, while adjusted net income reached $3.5 billion. The strongest performance came from the Data Center segment, where revenue increased by 97% to a record $8.5 billion.

Behind these results is also a significant accounting impact associated with agreements involving major buyers of AI infrastructure. AMD recorded a negative impact of approximately $1.097 billion from changes in the fair value of liabilities related to stock purchase rights, known as warrants. In its more detailed regulatory filing, the company reported a total non-cash impact of approximately $1.2 billion associated with these instruments. This does not mean AMD paid that amount to OpenAI or Meta. Instead, it reflects a change in the accounting value of future rights arising from the agreements.

## OpenAI and Meta could acquire up to 320 million AMD shares

AMD entered into a multi-year agreement with OpenAI in October 2025, followed by another agreement with Meta in February 2026. According to published documents, each partner plans to deploy up to six gigawatts of infrastructure based on AMD graphics processing units. The agreements are linked to substantial future purchases of AI chips, but they also include additional financial incentives that could allow customers to participate in AMD's potential future increase in value.

Each company received a warrant allowing it to purchase up to 160 million AMD shares at an exercise price of $0.01 per share. Together, the agreements cover a potential 320 million shares, although these rights are conditional. The rights vest gradually based on agreed purchase volumes, market conditions and additional technical and commercial requirements. According to AMD's report, as of September 26, 2026, no shares under these agreements had vested or become exercisable.

## Why is a chipmaker offering equity incentives to customers?

AMD is seeking to strengthen its position in the AI accelerator market, which is dominated by Nvidia. Developing advanced artificial intelligence models requires enormous computing power, while long-term agreements with major customers help manufacturers plan production and the development of new systems more effectively. Securing demand from companies such as OpenAI and Meta could also accelerate the adoption of AMD's hardware and software infrastructure.

For customers, these agreements provide an additional advantage. OpenAI and Meta can reduce their dependence on a single supplier of AI processors while potentially gaining a financial interest in the future growth of an alternative manufacturer. If AMD increases its market share and corporate value, exercising these warrant rights could become extremely valuable for its strategic customers. The relationship between manufacturer and customer therefore becomes considerably more complex than a conventional purchase and sale of computing equipment.

## What does the billion-dollar accounting charge mean for AMD?

Warrants are valued using complex financial models that consider factors such as AMD's share price, expected volatility and the estimated probability of meeting contractual conditions. As the market price of AMD shares and expectations regarding future deliveries change, so does the estimated value of the liabilities the company must report in its financial statements. As a result, AMD can simultaneously generate record sales and recognize a substantial non-cash accounting charge.

Such an effect should not automatically be interpreted as a deterioration in business performance, but it should not be ignored either. According to AMD's regulatory filing, the warrant-related liability stood at approximately $1.2 billion at the end of the third quarter. At the same time, approximately $4.5 billion in unrecognized grant-date value remained associated with unvested warrant rights, which would be recorded as a reduction in revenue proportionally as future qualifying customer revenue is recognized. The financial consequences of these agreements could therefore remain an important component of AMD's results in the coming quarters.

## Existing shareholders face potential ownership dilution

One of the most significant risks associated with these agreements concerns existing AMD shareholders. If OpenAI and Meta satisfy the requirements and exercise their warrants, AMD could issue a substantial number of additional shares. Existing investors would retain their shares, but their percentage ownership of the company could decline. This process is known as dilution. It does not necessarily cause the market price of a stock to fall, but it changes how the company's future value is distributed among shareholders.

The potential 320 million new shares represent approximately 19.5% of the number of AMD shares outstanding at the end of September 2026. However, if all those additional shares were issued, they would account for approximately 16.3% of the increased total share count, assuming no other changes in outstanding shares. The actual outcome will depend on the fulfillment of contractual conditions, future share issuances and AMD's business performance. Investors must therefore consider both the potential revenue growth and the cost of the equity incentives being used to secure that growth.

## Is the AI industry increasingly financed through interconnected interests?

AMD's agreements with OpenAI and Meta are part of a broader trend in which infrastructure manufacturers and major artificial intelligence customers are becoming more closely connected. Technology companies are committing to long-term purchases of computing capacity, while suppliers offer financial incentives and potential ownership opportunities to secure major customers. This business model can accelerate infrastructure development and competition, but it also creates a network of interconnected economic interests.

Connections between customers and suppliers do not, by themselves, prove that revenues are artificially inflated or that a financial bubble exists. The underlying demand for computing power is growing, and AMD's financial results confirm strong demand for data center processors. Nevertheless, assessing the AI investment cycle increasingly requires a clear distinction between realized revenue and cash flows, long-term contractual commitments, future equity rights and expectations of continued growth.

## Can AMD seriously challenge Nvidia's dominance?

The growth of AMD's Data Center business demonstrates that the market is looking for additional suppliers of advanced AI processors. The company is developing a broader technology ecosystem that includes Instinct graphics processors, EPYC server processors and the ROCm software platform. Major agreements with OpenAI and Meta could help AMD strengthen that ecosystem, particularly if customers increase the actual deployment and use of its systems.

However, Nvidia's market dominance cannot be assessed solely through the value of announced contracts. Actual deliveries, energy efficiency, software support, profitability and manufacturers' ability to supply sufficient quantities of advanced chips are all essential factors. AMD has delivered an exceptionally strong quarter, but the ultimate success of its strategy will depend on how much of the contracted demand translates into sustainable revenue and profits over the coming years.
