**Bitcoin briefly plunged to around $55,100 on Saturday, November 21, after falling below the psychological $60,000 threshold the previous day. According to quotations from Binance's BTC/USDT market, the price fell to approximately $55,130 in the observed 24-hour period, before recovering toward $58,000 following the sharp sell-off. The new low raises the question of whether the market has found temporary support or whether a further decline toward $52,000 remains possible.**

## A new low, followed by a rapid rebound

Bitcoin had been losing ground alongside technology shares and other risky assets in recent days. During a period of exceptional volatility, it could lose several thousand dollars within a very short time and then recover some of those losses. That matters because the lowest price recorded tells us neither how long the market stayed at that level nor what direction the next major move will take.

Quotations from one exchange do not necessarily match those from another trading platform. A day's low, the price of the most recent transaction and the average price during the day are different measures. The figure of approximately $55,100 should therefore be understood as a recorded low rather than a price at which Bitcoin remained throughout the session.

## Why is money leaving Bitcoin?

The sell-off was not triggered by a single event. Investors are dealing with falling technology shares, questions over the sustainability of AI investment and uncertainty about monetary policy. Under these conditions, Bitcoin often behaves as a risk asset, especially when major market participants reduce exposure across several asset classes at once.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs also recorded substantial net outflows during November. Particularly notable was the roughly $541 million outflow from BlackRock's IBIT on November 19, while US spot BTC ETFs together recorded around $582 million in net redemptions. These flows point to weaker demand through institutional investment products, but they should not automatically be treated as simultaneous sales of an identical amount of Bitcoin on a single exchange.

## Liquidations accelerate price movements

Leveraged positions account for an important part of cryptocurrency trading. When prices drop sharply, some traders no longer have enough margin to maintain their LONG positions, which exchanges may forcibly close. Such liquidations add to selling pressure and can accelerate a decline already under way. Once some of those positions are closed, the market can also rebound quickly because the forced selling has diminished.

It is particularly important to distinguish extra money held in a trading account from additional margin actually allocated to protect a specific position. In isolated-margin trading, funds in the account do not necessarily protect an open position automatically. Protection depends on each exchange's settings, the leverage used and whether margin-management features have been activated.

## Could Bitcoin fall to $52,000?

After the test of $55,000, the $52,000–$53,000 area has become one possible scenario in the event of continued selling. A fall from the low of approximately $55,130 to $52,000 would represent a further decline of around 5.7%. Such a move would not be unusual for Bitcoin, but it is by no means inevitable. The key things to watch are whether buyers defend today's low, whether ETF outflows continue and whether Wall Street stabilizes.

If Bitcoin recovers and holds above $58,000–$60,000 for a sustained period, pressure could ease. If it loses the $55,000 area again, there would be more room for another test of lower levels. Neither development guarantees an outcome: a market can temporarily break an important level and then recover rapidly.

A more severe fall, for example toward $35,000, would require deeper and more sustained market stress. That level should not currently be presented as the base-case forecast, particularly amid large short-term price swings.

## Ethereum and altcoins follow market sentiment

The pressure is not limited to Bitcoin. Ethereum and a number of altcoins have also faced substantial volatility, with differences in liquidity potentially amplifying percentage moves. However, there is no rule that every altcoin must fall by the same proportion as Bitcoin or recover on the same day. Individual market data and trading conditions need to be assessed separately.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

- The area around $55,000 has actually been tested, but it is not a confirmed market bottom.
- The rebound toward $58,000 shows a buyer response, but does not prove the correction has ended.
- A move to $52,000 remains possible if selling resumes; it is not a certain forecast.
- ETF flows and technology shares are important for understanding overall market sentiment.
- Leverage can lead to liquidation even when a trader's longer-term directional assessment proves correct.

Bitcoin's fall shows just how quickly a price level that looked like a possible scenario yesterday can become a real market quotation. The most important question now is whether buyers remain active after the rebound or whether another wave of selling is approaching.
