**Deutsche Bank shares fell more than 5% on November 17, 2026, after an international investigative reporting project published findings based on more than 15 million leaked internal records. The investigation, titled “Inside Deutsche Bank,” raised questions about Germany's largest lender, its historical dealings with Russian oligarchs, US President Donald Trump and the late financier Jeffrey Epstein, and longstanding weaknesses in internal controls. Deutsche Bank says the issues date back many years, its oversight has improved and it does not expect material financial consequences. Investors nevertheless responded by selling the stock.**

## What triggered Deutsche Bank's share-price decline?

Deutsche Bank shares lost approximately 5.2% in early Frankfurt trading, with steeper losses also recorded during the session. Morning market reports put the stock at around €31.22, after a session low near €30.89. The move was a strong reaction to fresh reporting about the bank's past, not an announcement of a liquidity crisis or a confirmed inability to repay deposits. Prices may change during the trading day.

The trigger was “Inside Deutsche Bank,” an international investigation coordinated by the Barracuda Media consortium. More than 200 journalists analyzed millions of records, including emails, meeting minutes, internal risk assessments and client documentation. Participating outlets included the Financial Times, Der Spiegel, the New York Times and Le Monde. The journalists say the records reveal years of problems in the handling of high-risk clients and internal oversight. Reuters cautioned that it could not immediately independently verify every claim in the investigation.

## Trump, Epstein and Russian oligarchs under scrutiny

One of the investigation's most sensitive areas concerns the bank's historical relationships with politically exposed and controversial clients. Media reports contain new claims involving Donald Trump, Jeffrey Epstein and several Russian oligarchs. The Financial Times reported that in 2017 the bank allegedly sent a Trump tax document to another client's accountant by mistake and did not inform the US administration at the time. This is a claim in a journalistic investigation, not a court finding of liability.

In Epstein's case, investigators examined transactions and internal records raising further questions about how suspicious payments were handled. Journalists also allege that the bank considered expanding business with Russian clients during certain periods despite warnings from internal compliance teams. **Being named in leaked documents is not, by itself, evidence of criminal wrongdoing.** Media allegations must also be distinguished from findings by regulators or courts.

## Deutsche Bank: Some matters date back 20 years

In an official statement on November 17, Deutsche Bank said that most of the reported matters were historical, with some going back as far as two decades. The bank said relevant issues had been reviewed at the time and that it had cooperated with regulators. Under chief executive Christian Sewing, who has led the bank since 2018, Deutsche Bank says it has made extensive changes to its organization and oversight.

According to the statement, the bank invested approximately **€7 billion directly in its control environment between 2019 and 2025**. It also pointed to record profits in 2025 and positive results in recent quarters. Management does not expect material financial effects from the historical matters discussed in the leaked records. The market response nevertheless suggests investors are considering reputational risks and the possibility of renewed regulatory scrutiny.

## Why do 15 million documents matter?

Large international banks hold vast quantities of confidential information about clients, transactions and risk assessments. Internal records can show how decisions were made, what warnings senior managers received and whether control systems were effective. The reporting consortium describes this as one of the largest known leaks of internal banking documents. Even so, the authenticity of individual records, their context and any legal consequences require careful distinction.

**An internal-document leak does not prove that a bank is currently insolvent or that customer deposits are at risk.** The reports published so far concern corporate governance, compliance and possible responsibility for historical conduct. Further explanations from the bank and any decisions by regulators will matter to a final assessment.

## The bank's selloff comes amid broader European weakness

Deutsche Bank came under pressure as other European stocks were also falling. The STOXX 600 index declined by roughly 0.8% in early trading. Technology shares faced additional uncertainty over new US restrictions on advanced AI chip exports and expectations ahead of Nvidia's earnings. Weaker risk appetite may have amplified investors' reaction to the Deutsche Bank headlines.

Two questions should be kept separate: how serious the newly reported documents may be for the bank's reputation and future regulatory position, and how much of the share decline reflects broader European market weakness. There is currently no confirmed evidence that this incident has triggered a systemic banking crisis. Any new investigations, proceedings or lawsuits would matter more to the longer-term outlook than a single volatile morning of trading.
