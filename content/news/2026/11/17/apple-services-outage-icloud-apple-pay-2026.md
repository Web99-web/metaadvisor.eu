*The cover image is illustrative.*

**Apple confirmed disruptions to several online services on November 17, including iCloud, the App Store and Apple Music, while users also reported problems with Apple Pay. The disruption affected services relied upon daily by many iPhone and other Apple device owners. The company said an internal system update caused the outages and reported that many services had returned to operation during the day. There is currently no confirmation that a cyberattack caused this outage.**

## Which Apple services were affected?

According to Apple's system status notices and reporting by the Associated Press, the disruption affected the App Store, Apple Music and certain AppleCare services. Users also experienced problems signing in to iCloud accounts. These issues could interfere with downloading apps, accessing music and using services connected to an Apple account.

Meanwhile, Downdetector recorded an increase in user reports concerning Apple Pay and other Apple services. In the United States, Apple Pay reports began climbing sharply around 11:40 a.m. Eastern Time, or 5:40 p.m. in Zagreb. No reliable comparative breakdown has established which country or city experienced the most severe disruption. **Reports of Apple Pay issues do not mean every payment through the service failed.** It is important to distinguish outages officially acknowledged by Apple from issues reported by users.

## Apple points to an internal update

Apple said on Tuesday that many systems had returned after problems caused by an internal update. Reuters reported that the company was continuing its restoration work. At publication time, sufficient official figures were not available to determine the total number of affected users, the duration of each individual disruption or the financial impact.

**An internal update failure is not the same as a cyberattack.** A software change can produce a widespread outage without the involvement of an outside attacker. Apple's explanation currently points to a technical cause, although a complete public technical review has not yet been released.

## Is this connected to the Optimizely attack?

Earlier today, Metaadvisor.eu [reported on a separate attack involving the Optimizely platform](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/iphone-zoom-ikea-salesforce-hackers-optimizely/). Malicious scripts were distributed through websites associated with major organizations. Among the attack techniques were attempts to exploit vulnerabilities on **iPhones**.

The two stories are close in time, but **there is no confirmed evidence connecting them**. In the Optimizely incident, security researchers described the delivery of malicious scripts through third-party websites. For the disruptions to its own services, Apple identified an internal update as the cause. An attempted exploit against a vulnerable iPhone does not establish that Apple's infrastructure was hacked or that every visitor to an affected website was infected.

## Why does this matter beyond Apple's ecosystem?

iCloud supports data synchronization and sign-ins, the App Store distributes applications, and Apple Pay enables digital payments. When several of these services encounter problems at once, individuals, developers and businesses can experience disruption. Even a temporary incident highlights the extent to which everyday activities depend on a single technology provider.

The outage came amid a series of major technology disruptions in the United States. However, the close timing of separate incidents does not establish a shared cause. Apple's final explanation, the duration of individual outages and any follow-up measures will be important when assessing the resilience of its systems.

## What should users do?

Users still experiencing problems can consult Apple's official system status page and try again once a service is available. A failed sign-in, synchronization attempt or payment does not, by itself, mean an account has been compromised. Users should nevertheless remain cautious about unexpected messages requesting passwords or card details, because fraudsters may exploit outages to send fake support notices.
