**Bitcoin fell below $59,000 on Friday, November 20, 2026, while Ethereum slid toward $1,600 and selling spread to several major altcoins. At the same time, US technology stocks faced renewed pressure and investors continued withdrawing capital from spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds. On November 19 alone, US BTC ETFs recorded approximately $582 million in net outflows, including a record $541 million from BlackRock's IBIT. Against this backdrop, concerns are growing over whether Bitcoin can hold the area around $58,000 or whether the correction will extend to lower levels.**

## Bitcoin breaks below $59,000 as Ethereum approaches $1,600

During trading on November 20, Bitcoin slipped below $59,000 after having traded above $60,000 earlier in the day. Market quotes monitored at around 6:00 p.m. Zagreb time showed BTC at approximately **$58,400** and ETH at approximately **$1,615**. These were point-in-time quotes from individual markets, not official daily closing prices; actual values can vary by exchange and the time of observation.

The pressure was not limited to the two largest cryptocurrencies. During the same observation period, Avalanche (AVAX) traded at around **$6.477**, Injective (INJ) at around **$6.011**, and Chainlink (LINK) at around **$8.34**. These examples illustrate how broadly negative sentiment has spread among altcoins, but without a comparison with previous closing prices, it would be inappropriate to infer precise daily percentage declines from those figures.

## BlackRock's IBIT records a record outflow

Much of the attention is focused on US spot Bitcoin ETFs. According to reports covering November 19 trading, the funds collectively saw around **$582.2 million in net outflows**, with approximately **$541.1 million** withdrawn from BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust, known by its ticker IBIT. This was the fund's largest single-day outflow since its launch.

Total net outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs during November reached approximately **$3.47 billion**. This pattern suggests that market pressure is not restricted to leveraged traders: demand is also weakening through the products traditional investors use to gain Bitcoin exposure. However, a net ETF outflow is not the same as an immediate sale of an identical amount of Bitcoin on a single exchange, since its market impact depends on redemption mechanics and liquidity.

## Wall Street and AI stocks under pressure

Selling has also spread across US and global equities, particularly in the technology sector. On Thursday, November 19, the **S&P 500 lost about 2.0%, the Nasdaq about 2.3%, and the Dow Jones about 2.2%**. Negative sentiment continued during US trading on Friday, but markets had not yet closed when this article was prepared, so intraday movements should not be presented as final daily results.

Investors are increasingly reassessing valuations of companies linked to artificial intelligence and the sustainability of major investments in data centers, chips and computing infrastructure. Expectations about US interest rates, a stronger dollar and declining risk appetite add to the uncertainty. When large funds reduce exposure to both technology shares and cryptocurrencies, a correction in one market can further weaken sentiment in the other.

## Liquidations amplify price moves

Bitcoin's decline triggered forced closures of some leveraged positions. According to market summaries dated November 20, approximately **$719 million in crypto positions was liquidated** over one 24-hour period, predominantly positions betting on rising prices.

When the price falls below the level at which a trader can maintain a LONG position, an exchange may close it automatically. This adds selling pressure and can accelerate an existing decline. At the same time, after a large liquidation wave, the market may stage a sharp short-term rebound because some of the forced selling pressure has already been exhausted. For that reason, neither a sudden drop nor a single strong recovery proves that a new long-term trend has begun.

## Could Bitcoin fall toward $54,000 or $55,000?

Following the move below $59,000, the **$58,000–$59,000 area** has become important for assessing Bitcoin's short-term direction. If Bitcoin loses that range as well, while heavy ETF outflows and equity selling continue, a further test of lower prices is possible. **The $54,000–$55,000 range is one potential bearish scenario**, not a confirmed support level or a target that the market must reach.

A decline from $58,400 to $55,000 would represent an additional loss of approximately **5.8%**, while a move to $54,000 would amount to about **7.5%**. Such moves are not unusual during periods of elevated volatility, but the fact that prices have already fallen does not mean they must fall further. The key questions are whether capital continues to leave the market, how buyers respond and whether broader markets stabilize.

A much deeper decline, for example toward $35,000, would require a considerably more severe and prolonged deterioration. That level should not currently be presented as an immediate or base-case scenario. Conversely, a Bitcoin recovery above $60,000 could ease the pressure, particularly if accompanied by smaller ETF outflows and a rebound in technology stocks.

## What does the decline in Ethereum and altcoins mean?

Ethereum traded near **$1,615** during the observed period, making the psychological $1,600 threshold an important short-term reference point. For AVAX, INJ and LINK, their sensitivity to market sentiment means that further Bitcoin weakness could increase volatility. This does not mean all cryptocurrencies must follow the same pattern or lose the same percentage.

Previously reported movements of stolen ETH linked to a major security incident at Binance have also attracted attention. However, **there is no basis for attributing today's ETH decline to transfers of stolen funds alone**. Moving assets between wallets is not the same as selling them, and the current decline is taking place within a much broader market context.

## What could stop the sell-off?

For a more sustainable recovery, investors would want to see the Nasdaq and other major US equity indexes stabilize, Bitcoin ETF outflows slow and forced liquidations subside. Markets will also be watching Federal Reserve commentary, the direction of the US dollar and changes in government bond yields.

A rebound remains possible even after such a sharp decline. If sellers exhaust short-term pressure and buyers defend lower levels, a rapid counter-move could follow. The important distinction is between a temporary bounce and a more durable trend change, especially when equities and cryptocurrencies are moving lower together.
