**The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is examining potential accounting irregularities at Nvidia, according to people familiar with the matter. New details published on November 20 added to investors' concerns, while shares in the AI chipmaker lost more than 11% of their value during trading. The report comes as Wall Street scrutinizes artificial intelligence financing, elevated technology valuations and the transparency of business relationships across the sector. There is, however, no confirmation that Nvidia violated accounting rules or that the investigation will result in sanctions.**

## SEC examines Nvidia's accounting

Nvidia is facing renewed regulatory pressure following November 20 reports that the US SEC is examining certain aspects of its accounting practices. According to people familiar with the matter, investigators are considering possible issues in the company's financial reporting. The existence of an investigation was first reported on November 17, but its precise focus was not known at the time. The latest information matters because it points to accounting, a particularly sensitive issue for a company whose market value depends heavily on expectations of strong future growth.

The SEC has not publicly disclosed details of the investigation, and it is not known when the inquiry began, how far it has progressed or whether any irregularities have been identified. Nvidia did not immediately issue a public response to the latest media reports. **The existence of a regulatory investigation does not, by itself, mean that a company has broken the law.** Such inquiries can end without charges or penalties, although uncertainty about financial reporting alone can trigger substantial market reactions.

## Record revenue, but growing questions

Nvidia is one of the world's most important suppliers of chips and computing infrastructure used in artificial intelligence. Its processors power the training of large AI models, data-center operations and commercial AI services. Exceptionally strong demand has enabled sharp revenue and profit growth, but it has also raised investor expectations.

In earnings released on November 17, Nvidia reported approximately **$115.8 billion in revenue for the third quarter of fiscal 2027**, which it said represented year-over-year growth of 103%. The data-center business generated about **$107.6 billion in revenue**, while net income reached approximately **$65.3 billion**. These figures illustrate the scale of demand for AI infrastructure, while also raising questions about how sustainable such a pace of growth may be.

Investors are interested not only in how much revenue the company reports, but also when it recognizes that revenue, how quickly customers pay for delivered products and what financial obligations arise from relationships with customers and partners. When a business expands so rapidly, accounting transparency becomes as important as the headline results themselves.

## Singapore, geographic revenue and transparency

Attention has also turned in recent months to the way Nvidia reports revenue across geographic markets. The company changed its reporting methodology from one based on customers' billing addresses to one linked to customer headquarters. This makes direct comparisons between current and earlier regional data more difficult, particularly when a customer is based in one country but operates computing infrastructure in another.

Singapore has therefore become a particular focus for analysts. It previously featured prominently in Nvidia's sales figures, but substantial revenue associated with customers in Singapore does not necessarily mean the chips were physically delivered there. The issue is further complicated by US restrictions on exports of advanced AI chips to China and the possibility that intermediaries operate across multiple markets. **There is no confirmation that the geographic classification of revenue is itself the subject of the SEC accounting investigation.** It is a separate transparency question that investors are monitoring and should not be equated with proven accounting misconduct.

## Why has Nvidia not yet filed its 10-Q?

Wall Street has also noted that Nvidia did not file its detailed quarterly Form 10-Q alongside the November 17 earnings announcement. In previous reporting periods, the company often released the document on the same day; this time, it said the filing would follow in the coming weeks. **The statutory filing deadline had not passed at the time of the report**, so the timing alone does not establish a regulatory violation.

Investors are nevertheless awaiting the document because a 10-Q provides more extensive information on accounting policies, legal proceedings, business risks, financial commitments and other circumstances that may affect the company's value. In the current environment, additional disclosures about financial relationships and risks could be especially significant for the market.

## AI financing faces closer scrutiny

Wall Street has been debating the sustainability of enormous investments in data centers, chips and AI computing capacity for some time. Nvidia is among the biggest beneficiaries of that spending, but it also operates within a complex ecosystem in which infrastructure suppliers, data-center operators, investors and customers may have interconnected financial interests.

Particular attention is being paid to arrangements in which technology companies invest in AI projects or help finance infrastructure for customers who subsequently purchase their products. Such business models are not inherently improper, but they can make it harder to assess independent demand and the allocation of financial risk. Nvidia has also clarified approximately **$118 billion in guarantees related to data centers**, explaining that certain commitments become effective only once the relevant facilities are completed and ready for operation. This does not mean the company has an immediately payable debt of the same amount, nor is it evidence of wrongdoing. It does, however, show how large and complex financial relationships in the AI industry have become.

## Nvidia falls more than 11% as Wall Street faces pressure

The reports concerning the accounting focus of the SEC inquiry emerged during an already difficult trading session for US technology shares. Nvidia's stock had been down around 7% before the new details were published, and its decline widened to **more than 11%** as trading continued. That indicates regulatory uncertainty was not the sole driver of selling, but rather added to existing concerns about high valuations, substantial AI investment and weakening risk appetite.

Nvidia has an outsized influence on major US stock indexes, particularly the Nasdaq and S&P 500. A sharp decline in its shares can therefore weigh on the broader market, especially semiconductor manufacturers and other businesses connected with the development of artificial intelligence. The current correction also raises a broader question of whether market expectations for AI growth have become overly optimistic. The percentage declines cited refer to intraday trading and are not necessarily closing prices.

## Separate questions about AI chip exports to China

In addition to the accounting inquiry, Nvidia faces regulatory questions concerning US restrictions on exports of advanced AI chips to China. US authorities are examining possible attempts to circumvent those restrictions through intermediaries, including business relationships involving Singapore-based Megaspeed. Some members of Congress have also requested further explanations of the Commerce Department's actions and the company's potential influence on regulatory proceedings.

These matters are separate from the SEC's examination of accounting practices. Their simultaneous emergence adds to Nvidia's overall regulatory uncertainty, but does not demonstrate that the various inquiries are connected or that the company has violated any rules.

## What are investors watching next?

An important next development will be Nvidia's quarterly 10-Q filing, which could offer more detailed insight into financial obligations, accounting policies and business risks. It is not certain, however, that the document will contain additional information about the SEC investigation. Any official clarification from the regulator or the company will be equally important, since available details about the inquiry's focus remain limited.

Investors will also follow technology stocks and broader sentiment on Wall Street. If the sell-off continues, new regulatory information could add to volatility in Nvidia shares. Conversely, more transparent financial disclosures and reduced regulatory uncertainty could help restore confidence.
