**US regulators have closed United Fidelity Bank, a financial institution with $5.9 billion in assets and $5.4 billion in deposits. It is the largest US bank failure since April 2024, and the estimated cost to the US deposit insurance fund is $1.83 billion. First Citizens is taking over operations; the banking group also acquired much of Silicon Valley Bank's business in 2023.**

On November 27, 2026, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) closed United Fidelity Bank of Evansville, Indiana, and appointed the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) as receiver. The regulator determined that the bank could no longer operate safely and was unlikely to meet its obligations and depositors' demands in the ordinary course of business.

## First Citizens takes over another failed bank

First Citizens was selected through a competitive bidding process. According to the buyer, the agreement covers nearly all loans and other agreed assets, most deposits, branches and related companies, including real estate company Pedcor Homes. The bid includes an asset discount of approximately $2.6 billion, with no deposit premium.

| Financial indicator | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Assets as estimated by the buyer | $5.9 billion |
| Deposits covered by the acquisition | $4.5 billion |

Final amounts depend on the closing balance sheet and subsequent settlement with the FDIC. First Citizens acquired much of Silicon Valley Bank's business during the 2023 banking crisis, making this transaction another example of US banking consolidation following regulatory intervention.

## What happens to customers' money?

The FDIC says depositors automatically move to First Citizens, with continued access to funds and deposit insurance. Branches normally open on Saturdays reopen on November 28 under the new owner, while the others resume operations on Monday. Customers can use checks, ATMs and debit cards over the weekend, and loan repayments continue under existing terms.

The difference between total deposits of $5.4 billion and the estimated $4.5 billion acquired by the buyer requires further explanation. First Citizens excludes certain brokered deposits and other accounts from the agreement. The approximately $900 million difference does not automatically represent customer losses; final treatment depends on the resolution terms and individual accounts.

## Regulators warned of problems as early as 2023

The bank's problems did not arise overnight. On October 31, 2023, the OCC issued a regulatory order addressing capital management, liquidity, credit risks and internal controls. In June 2024, the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago entered into an additional regulatory agreement with companies in the bank's ownership structure.

Despite years of supervision, operations were not stabilized. Current announcements do not fully explain the immediate event that led to the closure, so identifying a single specific cause would be premature.

## US bank failures are rising again

Two US banks were closed in each of 2024 and 2025. According to the FDIC list, six banks failed in 2026 before United Fidelity Bank, bringing the total to seven with this closure.

## Who will pay the $1.83 billion cost?

The estimated cost will be borne by the Deposit Insurance Fund, administered by the FDIC. The fund is primarily financed by assessments on insured banks and investment income, so this amount is not automatically a direct cost to taxpayers. Standard deposit insurance is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank and per account ownership category.

## Are problems returning to the US banking sector?

The closure of United Fidelity Bank raises renewed questions about loan portfolio quality, funding costs, liquidity and commercial real estate exposure. The reaction of regional bank shares when markets reopen on Monday, November 30, could indicate whether investors view the event as an isolated case or a signal of broader risk. There is currently insufficient evidence linking the failure to any specific recent market event.
