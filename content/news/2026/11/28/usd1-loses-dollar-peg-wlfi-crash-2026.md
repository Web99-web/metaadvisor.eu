**On November 28, USD1, a stablecoin associated with the Trump family-backed World Liberty Financial project, temporarily lost its US dollar peg and fell to around 0.94 USDT on Binance. Meanwhile, the WLFI governance token lost almost 53% of its value, while a wallet linked to the project's strategic reserves sold approximately $20 million in Ethereum-related assets.**

The disruption in the USD1 market occurred during a sharp WLFI sell-off, and Tron founder Justin Sun called on exchanges to delist both tokens. USD1 subsequently approached its target peg, but the decline raised questions about liquidity, reserves and confidence in the project.

## USD1 fell almost 6% below its peg

Binance's USD1/USDT pair recorded a price of 0.94098 USDT, while the USD1/USDC pair reached approximately 0.9551 USDC. CoinGecko recorded a low of $0.9394 and a subsequent recovery towards $0.995. Different quotes reflect different markets and observation times; they should not be interpreted as a single price for all transactions.

For a stablecoin, a deviation of almost six percent represents a significant disruption because maintaining a stable value is its primary function. A recovery towards one dollar eases immediate pressure, but does not itself establish that all the causes of the sell-off have disappeared.

## WLFI lost more than half its value

According to CoinGlass data reported by Crypto.news, WLFI lost 52.9% over 24 hours and reached $0.01956. CoinDesk reported a low near $0.016. Futures liquidations reached $16.89 million, with approximately 90% involving long positions. Forced closure of leveraged positions may have accelerated selling.

WLFI and USD1 serve different functions. The former is a governance token with a freely fluctuating price, while the latter aims to maintain a value of one US dollar. A decline in WLFI therefore does not automatically imply a corresponding decrease in the value of USD1 reserves.

## Ethereum assets worth $20 million sold

Blockchain analysts identified asset sales from a wallet labelled as a strategic reserve of World Liberty Financial. Published analyses indicate that approximately 10,600 WETH and 1,378 mETH were sold, yielding approximately $20.06 million in stablecoins.

| Transaction | Proceeds |
|---|---:|
| Sale of approximately 10,600 WETH | 17.58 million USDC |
| Sale of approximately 1,378 mETH | approximately 2.48–2.49 million USD1 |
| Total value | approximately $20.06 million |
| Estimated realised loss | approximately $19.26 million |

The estimated loss is based on blockchain records of the earlier acquisition and subsequent sale, rather than audited financial statements. CoinDesk also reported a possible connection between the sales and an approximately $75 million loan on the Dolomite protocol, secured by WLFI tokens. TokenPost reported that most proceeds were transferred to Dolomite, but the reasons for these transactions and their direct connection to the USD1 deviation remain unconfirmed.

## Justin Sun calls for USD1 and WLFI delisting

Tron founder and early project investor Justin Sun called on exchanges on November 28 to restrict trading and new deposits of both tokens and requested transparency regarding movements of funds. He made allegations concerning circular lending, unauthorised sales and the freezing of investor assets.

Sun is already involved in a legal dispute with World Liberty Financial over the freezing of his tokens. The project filed a counterclaim challenging his conduct. Neither side's allegations have been established by a final court ruling, and Sun's call does not confirm that exchanges have already delisted the tokens.

## Does USD1 have sufficient dollar backing?

A temporary fall in a stablecoin's market price below one dollar is not proof that its issuer lacks sufficient reserves. Large sell orders, limited liquidity and differing conditions for direct redemption can lead to deviations across markets. According to the project's documentation, BitGo issues USD1 and redeems it at one dollar for eligible users. Dollar deposits, short-term US government securities and government money market funds are listed as backing assets.

**World Liberty Financial's strategic cryptocurrency reserves are not the same as the reserves backing USD1.** The sale of Ethereum assets is therefore not in itself proof that the dollar backing is at risk. Current reserve attestations, actual redemption availability and liquidity across individual markets are crucial to assessing stability.

According to TokenPost, USD1's market capitalisation declined by approximately 27% over the previous 30 days. This figure should be distinguished from the intraday price decline and from any directly confirmed amount of withdrawn funds.

## Could the loss of confidence spread?

Stablecoins serve as instruments for trading, transferring value and securing loans. A loss of confidence may prompt selling and a shift towards other stablecoins or traditional currencies, while related lending positions may increase liquidity pressure. There is currently insufficient evidence that USD1 is experiencing a solvency crisis or that the disruption will spread to other issuers.

## Our take

- **Dollar peg:** The drop towards $0.94 is a significant deviation from the target value.
- **WLFI sell-off:** The nearly 53% decline and liquidations illustrate the scale of market pressure.
- **Strategic reserves:** Asset sales raise questions about risk management and project indebtedness.
- **Separate reserves:** The project's cryptocurrency assets are distinct from the backing of USD1.
- **Investor dispute:** Justin Sun's allegations should be considered in the context of the existing legal dispute.
- **Price recovery:** A return towards the peg does not remove the need for transparency.

**A stablecoin is meant to provide stability in a volatile cryptocurrency market. What happens when that very instrument becomes a source of uncertainty?**
