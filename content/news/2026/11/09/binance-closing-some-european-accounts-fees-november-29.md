**Binance has confirmed that certain European user accounts will close on December 31, 2026. According to notices reported by affected customers, monthly charges on remaining crypto balances begin November 29. The measures concern specific accounts, not all Binance customers across the European Union.**

## Which European users are affected?

Customers in France, Spain, Italy and Poland have reported receiving account closure notices. A Binance spokesperson confirmed to CoinDesk that services were being restricted progressively and that affected accounts would close at the end of December. The exchange has not published an exhaustive country-by-country list or the total number of affected customers.

Some users in these countries were first contacted in June, when Binance began explaining changes following the end of national regulatory transition periods. Whether the same measures will extend to further EU countries remains unconfirmed.

## What happens on November 29?

Customer communications cited in media reports describe a monthly charge of 0.025% of remaining crypto holdings or a minimum of 10 USDC, whichever is higher. For an affected account holding around $100, the minimum fee would represent approximately 10% of its value. The notices reportedly provide for a first charge shortly after November 29 and another after December 24.

These figures come from reported customer emails; they should not be presented as a universal fee applying to every European Binance user. The status of each account must be checked through official Binance channels.

## Why is Binance taking this step?

The exchange is adapting to the EU Markets in Crypto-Assets framework, known as MiCA, and the expiration of national transitional arrangements. Binance withdrew its application for authorization in Greece in June and said it would pursue authorization in another EU member state. A new authorization had not been publicly confirmed when the closure notices emerged.

Binance says it remains committed to Europe. The account closures are therefore not equivalent to a confirmed exit from the entire European market.

## Can affected customers still withdraw?

Binance says affected customers retain access to crypto and fiat withdrawals, although deposits have been restricted. It describes the process as managed rather than an immediate freeze of user funds. Customers should consult notices inside the official app and remain alert to phishing messages exploiting news of the closures.

## Is this linked to the $6.34 billion hack?

There is no evidence that the European account closures were directly caused by the October 29 security incident. Binance had already begun its European regulatory communications months earlier. The hack and account restrictions are separate events, even if both could affect user confidence and the movement of assets.

A phased approach could reduce operational pressure, but there is no confirmation that Binance is selecting country groups to protect its liquidity. Differing national registrations and regulatory transitions provide a more directly documented explanation.
