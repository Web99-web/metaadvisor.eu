**U.S. technology markets showed signs of recovery on November 3 after several difficult trading sessions, but the gains in major indexes concealed a more complicated picture. Nvidia and semiconductor manufacturers led the advance, while Apple and several software companies continued to lose value. At the same time, Asian stock markets declined, Bitcoin stabilized above $77,000, and more than $1.3 billion flowed out of U.S. spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs during the two most recently completed trading sessions.**

## Wall Street rebounds, but not every stock is rising

After the Nasdaq ended Monday with a loss of approximately 1.1%, investors began buying selected technology stocks again on Tuesday. During the U.S. afternoon session, the Nasdaq Composite was up approximately 0.8% to 1%, the S&P 500 was also trading higher, while the Dow Jones remained in negative territory. This suggests that capital was not returning evenly across the market but was instead concentrating in particular sectors.

Nvidia gained approximately 3% after four consecutive negative trading sessions. AMD rose more than 5% ahead of its earnings announcement, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index advanced around 3%. Investors have clearly not abandoned artificial intelligence, but they are becoming considerably more selective when assessing which companies are likely to turn AI investment into profits.

## Apple and Palantir reveal the other side of the AI market

While semiconductor stocks advanced, Apple lost approximately 2% of its market value. The decline was not driven by weak financial results but by concerns about rising memory-chip costs and pressure on profit margins during the holiday quarter. Apple had previously reported strong revenue, yet investors focused on what could happen in the coming months.

A similar situation unfolded at Palantir. The company exceeded expectations and issued an optimistic revenue forecast, but its shares still declined. This is an important indication of changing market sentiment: strong financial performance is no longer always sufficient to justify elevated technology valuations. On Monday, the Nasdaq-100 closed more than 10% below its record closing high, entering correction territory. Tuesday brought a rebound, but a single positive trading session does not confirm that the correction has ended.

## Asian markets remain under pressure

Unlike U.S. semiconductor manufacturers, Asian technology markets suffered substantial losses on Tuesday. South Korea's Kospi fell approximately 4%, with pressure concentrated on companies connected to semiconductor production and technology exports. Weakness was also visible across other Asian markets.

This divergence between Asia and Wall Street demonstrates how quickly capital can move between markets and companies. The U.S. rebound should therefore not automatically be interpreted as a return of global optimism. Investors are still trying to determine whether investments in AI infrastructure are sustainable over the long term and when enormous expenditures will begin generating corresponding profits.

## Bitcoin stabilizes, but ETF outflows remain a warning sign

Bitcoin recovered from morning levels of approximately $76,500 on Tuesday and traded near $77,500 later in the session. This suggested that the market had stabilized at least temporarily following earlier pressure associated with the Binance security incident and a broader decline in risk appetite.

However, institutional fund-flow data present a more cautious picture. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded **$398.6 million in net outflows** on Monday, November 2. Most of the movement came from BlackRock's IBIT, which experienced $405.3 million in withdrawals. Combined with Friday's $643.2 million in net outflows, the two completed trading sessions produced a total net outflow of approximately **$1.04 billion**.

Ethereum ETFs simultaneously recorded their sixth consecutive trading day of net outflows. An additional $139.7 million left these funds on Monday. Together, Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs lost approximately **$1.34 billion in net capital** across Friday and Monday. Importantly, final ETF flow figures for Tuesday, November 3, were not yet available when this market review was prepared. ETF outflows also do not automatically predict further price declines.

## Oil prices fall again as geopolitical uncertainty persists

Oil markets continued experiencing substantial volatility. Brent crude traded around $103 per barrel on Tuesday, declining by more than 1% during parts of the session. Prices responded to political statements from the United States and the possibility of diplomatic progress involving Iran.

U.S. President Donald Trump stated that he had reached an agreement with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to end American military assistance to Israel after the end of the year. Meanwhile, tensions involving Iran continued. Markets were therefore attempting to assess whether diplomatic signals or the risk of further military operations would dominate. Lower oil prices could help ease some inflationary pressure, but the situation remains highly sensitive to political and security developments.

## U.S. elections and bond yields add further uncertainty

The United States is holding its congressional midterm elections on November 3. The outcome could affect tax policy, the federal budget, cryptocurrency and technology regulation, and the administration's ability to implement future economic measures.

At the same time, stronger U.S. job openings data pushed government bond yields higher again. The yield on the 10-year U.S. Treasury was trading near 4.95%. Higher yields can make bonds more attractive relative to riskier assets and make elevated technology valuations harder to justify. For that reason, Tuesday's Nasdaq gains should be considered alongside developments in bond yields, oil prices and election results rather than viewed as an isolated signal.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **Nvidia is attracting buyers again, but the technology sector is not moving in one direction.** Semiconductor gains alongside declines in Apple and selected software companies show that investors are increasingly distinguishing between potential winners and losers in the AI investment cycle.
* **Bitcoin's recovery is encouraging, but ETF flows have not yet confirmed a return of institutional optimism.** Two negative trading sessions do not necessarily imply further price declines. However, net outflows exceeding one billion dollars should not be ignored.
* **Asian market weakness remains a warning sign.** The sharp decline in South Korea's stock market demonstrates that risks facing the global technology sector have not disappeared simply because U.S. stocks are rebounding.
* **Falling oil prices may support markets, but the reason for the decline matters.** If prices fall because geopolitical risks are easing, the consequences may be positive. If they reflect weaker demand or concerns about an economic slowdown, the interpretation is different.
* **The most important signal today is not simply whether markets are rising or falling, but how divided they have become.** Capital is returning to selected semiconductor manufacturers while simultaneously leaving certain crypto funds, software companies and Asian equities. The next trading sessions will help determine whether this marks the beginning of a more sustainable recovery or merely a brief pause within a broader correction.
