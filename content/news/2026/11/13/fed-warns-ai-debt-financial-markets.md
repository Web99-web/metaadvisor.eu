**Artificial intelligence has become the most frequently cited concern among financial-market contacts surveyed by the Federal Reserve.** In its Financial Stability Report released on **November 13, 2026**, **87%** of respondents flagged AI-related risks, up from **50%** in the spring survey. The Fed also drew attention to rapidly growing data-center financing and the possibility that higher long-term interest rates could add pressure across the economy.

## From 50% to 87%: AI leads the concerns

The Fed surveys financial-market professionals about potential shocks to the US financial system over the following 12 to 18 months. The **87% figure does not mean that 87% of all investors expect an AI crash**. It is the share of surveyed market contacts who identified AI among significant risks, not a probability forecast made by the central bank.

Respondents pointed to elevated equity valuations, debt-funded capital expenditure, complex financing structures and cybersecurity threats. If corporate earnings disappoint, falling asset prices could affect credit availability and business investment beyond the technology sector.

## The $3 trillion figure is not a realized loss

According to Reuters' account of the Fed report, debt issuance linked to data centers and advanced computing reached about **$680 billion** by October 2026. That was approximately **five times** the total for all of 2023 and nearly one-third of nonfinancial investment-grade corporate debt issuance.

Taken together, debt, guarantees and future lease commitments associated with the AI infrastructure buildout could exceed **$3 trillion**. This is a potential scale of financial obligations and exposure, **not money already lost**, nor an estimate of debt immediately due. Interconnected corporate guarantees and financing arrangements can make it harder to identify who ultimately bears the risk.

## Why higher interest rates matter

Building large data centers requires enormous upfront spending on chips, electricity, land and construction. When long-term borrowing costs remain elevated or rise, these projects need stronger future cash flows to meet financing commitments.

The Fed highlighted **the risk of higher long-term market interest rates** alongside persistent inflation. That is **not an announcement of a new Fed policy-rate increase** or a promise that the central bank will raise its benchmark rate. In remarks on November 10, Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman also warned of a potential equity-market correction if returns on AI investments fall short of expectations.

## Risks could reach beyond AI companies

Banks, bond investors, private-credit funds, landlords and infrastructure providers all participate in funding the buildout. Financial strain at AI-focused companies could therefore spread through credit markets and other parts of the economy.

The Fed also identified elevated risk-asset valuations and leverage among some hedge funds as vulnerabilities. These are potential channels through which a shock could spread, **not evidence that a financial crisis has already begun**. Large, profitable companies may be able to absorb individual losses, but transparency around interconnected obligations remains important.

## The real test for the AI investment boom

As spending on advanced computing accelerates, investors are looking more closely at the relationship between revenue growth, capital expenditure, financing costs, depreciation and free cash flow. Strong demand for computing power does not automatically guarantee quick profitability.

The central question is not whether AI can create economic value. It is **who carries the financial exposure and how quickly the investment can generate sufficient returns**.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **87% versus 50%** captures a sharp rise in concern among surveyed market contacts, not a predicted crash probability.
* **$680 billion** refers to related debt issuance through October 2026, not realized losses.
* **More than $3 trillion** is a potential total of debt, guarantees and future leases, not one payable bill.
* **Higher long-term interest rates** could make projects harder to finance, but no new benchmark-rate increase was announced in this report.
* **Interconnected guarantees** may obscure concentrated exposure across financing structures.
* **The crucial test** is whether revenue and cash flow can support capital spending and future obligations.

**The Fed's warning is not a rejection of artificial intelligence. It is a reminder that technological progress can create financial vulnerabilities when funding commitments outpace the visibility of future returns.**
