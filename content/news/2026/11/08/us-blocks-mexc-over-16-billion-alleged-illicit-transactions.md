**US authorities are moving to cut cryptocurrency exchange MEXC Global off from the American financial system over at least $16 billion in transactions allegedly linked to sanctions evasion, cybercrime and other unlawful activity. MEXC says operations remain normal and customer funds are safe. The order raises serious questions about the exchange's financial connections, but it does not mean the entire platform has shut down.**

## What does the US Treasury allege?

On November 6, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, or FinCEN, issued an order targeting MEXC Global under Operation Economic Outcast. According to the Treasury, the exchange processed at least $16 billion in transactions associated with illicit activity from May 2021 through May 2026. The allegations involve Russia, Iran and North Korea, alongside ransomware payments, fraud and darknet activity.

Nearly $1.5 billion of the total is linked by US authorities to Russian sanctions evasion, including activity involving Garantex and Grinex. Crucially, the $16 billion figure is not a reported theft of customer assets. It represents the volume of transactions identified in the regulator's allegations.

## What exactly is being blocked?

FinCEN's order prohibits covered US financial institutions from transmitting funds to or from MEXC Global. It takes effect 30 days after publication in the Federal Register. That is not the same as a worldwide exchange shutdown or the automatic freezing of all customer accounts.

Nevertheless, losing access to major US financial channels can complicate banking relationships and international transfers. Further consequences will depend on how counterparties and regulators in other jurisdictions respond.

## MEXC says customer assets remain secure

In a November 7 statement, MEXC said its legal and compliance teams were reviewing the order. The exchange maintained that its services were operating normally, described users' assets as secure and promised to engage with regulators. It also announced an independent review of its anti-money-laundering and sanctions controls.

Those assurances are statements from the exchange itself, not independent proof of solvency. Smooth trading functionality today does not rule out operational changes later.

## What does this mean for European users?

The US order does not automatically prohibit every European customer from using MEXC. The company has separately announced service changes in certain countries, including the Netherlands. Those local wind-down arrangements should not be conflated with the new US measure.

No blanket closure of Croatian users' accounts has been confirmed as a consequence of this order. Customers should check notices applicable to their country and distinguish withdrawing available spot assets from managing open futures contracts.

## Why does this matter beyond MEXC?

The US Treasury says MEXC handled more than $1 billion in daily transactions in September and held approximately $5 billion in reserves. Its wide range of trading instruments and accessible interface help explain its popularity among active traders. Yet a polished user experience cannot guarantee regulatory compliance.

This case illustrates how an exchange may continue to operate technically while access to important financial infrastructure becomes restricted. Legitimate customers can face uncertainty even when they have no connection to the conduct being investigated.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **The $16 billion is not a hack loss.** It is the volume of transactions US authorities allege were linked to illicit activity.
* **MEXC has not been shut down worldwide.** The order directly restricts covered US financial institutions.
* **The customer-asset assurance comes from MEXC.** Users should monitor official notices and access to withdrawals in their own jurisdiction.
* **Great UX is not a substitute for compliance.** An exchange can be easy to use while facing serious legal and financial-infrastructure risks.
