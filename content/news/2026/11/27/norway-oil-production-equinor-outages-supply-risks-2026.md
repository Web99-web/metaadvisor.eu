**Just days after figures showed that Norwegian crude oil production rose by 8.6% in October, energy giant Equinor lowered its own production growth forecast for 2026. A fire on the Visund platform and oil loading problems at Johan Castberg could affect energy availability during the winter months.**

On November 25, Norwegian energy company Equinor announced that it expects production growth of approximately 3% in 2026, down from its previous forecast of 4%. The company had raised its forecast in late October following strong production results, but a series of unplanned technical problems changed its expectations.

The reduced forecast refers to Equinor’s total global production, including outages outside Norway, rather than Norway’s total production. The company did not provide a separate estimate of annual production losses for Norwegian fields alone in that announcement.

## Fire halts production at Visund

On November 4, a fire broke out in a room housing emergency power switchboards on the Visund platform in the northern North Sea.

Equinor expects production to be suspended for approximately two months, meaning operations could return to normal around the turn of 2027. Norway’s offshore energy safety authority is investigating the circumstances of the incident.

## Johan Castberg: Problems loading oil onto tankers

A further problem emerged at Johan Castberg in the Barents Sea, one of Norway’s newer oil projects, which began production in March 2025. Production was reduced to a minimum in the days preceding Equinor’s announcement due to a technical problem with the tanker loading system. The company is working to restore safe loading operations but did not specify a date for the return to full production.

The case illustrates the importance of transport infrastructure for energy supply. Even when a field is capable of producing oil, storage and shipment problems can force the operator to reduce production.

## Overview of Norwegian outages

| Field | Location | Cause | Expected duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visund | North Sea, Norway | Fire | Around two months |
| Johan Castberg | Barents Sea, Norway | Oil loading problems | Not announced |

Equinor emphasizes that the incidents do not affect recoverable reserves. They are temporary production and operational problems, rather than a permanent loss of fields.

## Norway produced 1.843 million barrels of crude oil per day in October

According to preliminary Norwegian Offshore Directorate figures published on November 20, Norwegian crude oil production in October rose by 146,000 barrels per day compared with September. However, production was lower than in the same month a year earlier.

| Period | Daily crude oil production |
|---|---:|
| October 2025 | 1,901,000 barrels |
| September 2026 | 1,697,000 barrels |
| October 2026 | 1,843,000 barrels |
| Month-on-month change | +8.6% |
| Year-on-year change | −3.1% |
| Difference from October forecast | +5.9% |

Total October liquids production, including crude oil, condensate and NGL, was approximately 2.03 million barrels per day, while natural gas sales reached 11 billion standard cubic metres, 2.1 billion more than in September. The October figures do not yet reflect the November outages. November data will be needed to assess their actual effect on total Norwegian production.

## Europe enters winter with additional energy risks

In previous MetaAdvisor analyses, we covered declining Middle Eastern crude oil exports and the announced sale of ConocoPhillips’ Norwegian operations to Vår Energi for $4.425 billion.

Equinor’s latest problems highlight another aspect of energy security: the reliability of existing production and transport infrastructure. While public attention tends to focus on geopolitical conflicts, sanctions and decisions by major producers, technical problems at individual fields can also affect physical oil supply.

It is particularly important to see whether temporary outages are resolved within the expected time frames or extend into the winter months. There is currently insufficient evidence to conclude that these incidents will cause a serious disruption to European energy supply.

## Will the outages affect oil prices?

Production cuts at individual energy companies can exert upward pressure on prices, but the ultimate effect depends on global supply, demand, available inventories and the ability of other producers to compensate for missing volumes.

It is important to distinguish the annual average reduction in Equinor’s production from the immediate daily loss of production capacity. A barrel of oil equivalent also includes natural gas, so these quantities cannot be directly equated with lost crude oil production. For now, the duration of outages, the restart of production and the actual volumes of oil reaching international markets are more important to monitor.
