**In September, Google announced a €13 billion investment in Finland’s digital infrastructure. But instead of a story about technological progress, new jobs and green energy, the project became the focus of an environmental controversy. Hundreds of hectares of forest were cleared before the mandatory environmental impact assessment was completed, work was suspended, and Finnish police launched a criminal investigation.**

The latest decision by the Finnish supervisory authority, dated November 24, 2026, permits essential work to protect waterways at the Muhos site, while other construction work remains suspended.

## From green forest to cleared land

New Google data centers are planned in the Finnish municipalities of Muhos and Kajaani. Approximately 330 hectares of forest were cleared in Muhos, while just under 200 hectares were felled in Kajaani. The independent organization Vertical52, in collaboration with Otter News, analyzed satellite images of both sites. According to its estimate, the work affected at least 647 hectares of land covered by trees and other vegetation.

| Location | Area affected |
|---|---:|
| Muhos | Around 330 hectares |
| Kajaani | Just under 200 hectares |
| Total according to published forest clearance figures | Around 530 hectares |
| Vegetation loss according to satellite analysis | At least 647 hectares |

The estimates are not directly comparable because the satellite analysis includes other forms of vegetation, not just forest. Nevertheless, both show the extensive scale of environmental change.

## The problem goes beyond felled trees

The work at the sites involved more than forest clearance. Preparatory operations included removing topsoil, building access roads and modifying drainage systems. The situation in Muhos is particularly concerning because the ground contains black shale. Disturbing such ground can increase water acidity and release metals.

According to data Google submitted to the authorities, changes indicating acidification have already been recorded in the water. The Finnish regulator also expressed concern about potentially increased amounts of sediment and nutrients entering waterways during heavy rainfall. It requested further explanations about the protection of moor frog habitats in Muhos.

## Finnish police launch a criminal investigation

On October 6, the Finnish supervisory authority LVV requested the suspension of work that significantly alters the environment at the Muhos and Kajaani sites. On October 29, police in Oulu confirmed the launch of a criminal investigation into a suspected environmental offense at the Muhos site. Five requests for investigation related to the project had been received.

Given the scale of the case, the investigation is expected to conclude during 2027, with further information from police expected in December 2026. The opening of an investigation does not mean criminal liability has been established.

## Google apologizes

In mid-October, Google’s Finland director Antti Järvinen publicly apologized for the situation. The company says it acted according to its understanding of its legal obligations and believed that the competent authorities were aware of the planned work. Google also announced nature restoration projects, including planting trees on approximately 130 hectares of land in Muhos.

However, planting new trees is not the same as restoring an existing forest ecosystem. The development of soil, natural habitats and biodiversity can take decades.

## A €13 billion investment and promises to protect nature

The developments on the ground are particularly interesting when compared with Google’s official investment announcement of September 9, 2026. The company presented a €13 billion investment over the following two years, covering digital infrastructure, energy and economic partnerships in Finland.

It also announced €31 million in investments in local communities over four years, workforce training, renewable energy, restoration of native forests and wetlands, and recreational facilities. Just weeks after that announcement, two projects came under regulatory scrutiny precisely because of their impact on nature.

## Latest decision: Work permitted only to protect the environment

On November 24, the Finnish supervisory authority issued a new decision allowing essential measures to prevent harmful effects on waterways in Muhos. This is not authorization to resume data center construction. It concerns limited water management and environmental protection work while environmental impact assessments are still underway.

The situation raises questions about how long it will take to restore the altered land and under what conditions Google will be able to proceed with its projects.

## How green is artificial intelligence really?

Discussions about AI’s environmental impact generally focus on electricity consumption, water use and carbon dioxide emissions. The Finnish case shows that environmental costs arise before the first server is switched on. Data centers require substantial land, transport infrastructure, electrical grids and cooling systems. Even when powered by low-carbon energy sources, their construction can have significant consequences for local ecosystems.

The case also raises questions about the responsibility of major technology companies. Can the speed of AI development justify starting work before all necessary assessments are completed? And how credible are sustainability promises when natural habitats are cleared before the consequences have been fully assessed?

## Our perspective

- **The hidden cost of AI:** The environmental impact of data centers begins long before they first consume electricity.
- **The order of procedures matters:** Environmental impact assessments should prevent damage, not merely document the consequences of work already carried out.
- **Green energy is not enough:** Using low-carbon energy does not automatically make an entire project environmentally sustainable.
- **Nature restoration is complex:** Newly planted trees cannot immediately replace a lost forest ecosystem.
- **Responsibility of technology giants:** Major investments and economic benefits should not diminish the importance of compliance with local laws and environmental protection.
- **A precedent for Europe:** The Finnish case could influence future regulatory requirements for data center construction across Europe.

**Artificial intelligence may live in the cloud, but the infrastructure that powers it occupies very real space on Earth.**
