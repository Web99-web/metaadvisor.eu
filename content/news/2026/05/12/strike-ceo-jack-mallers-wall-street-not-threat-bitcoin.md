# Jack Mallers Says Wall Street Is Not A Threat To Bitcoin

Strike CEO Jack Mallers believes Wall Street entering Bitcoin is not a danger to the cryptocurrency — but rather proof that Bitcoin is evolving into a truly global financial asset.

Speaking on the “What Bitcoin Did” podcast, Mallers dismissed fears that institutional involvement could somehow destroy Bitcoin’s original principles. According to him, if Bitcoin cannot survive Wall Street participation, then the system was never strong enough to begin with.

His comments come at a time when Spot Bitcoin ETFs continue attracting billions of dollars from institutional investors, while large firms like BlackRock, Fidelity and other financial giants expand their exposure to the crypto market.

## Bitcoin Was Always Meant For Everyone

Mallers argued that Bitcoin was designed as money for everyone — including institutions, governments and even critics of the technology itself.

He explained that many people inside the Bitcoin community still see Wall Street as an enemy because they fear centralization, large-scale custody and growing influence from traditional finance.

However, Mallers believes Bitcoin’s decentralized structure is strong enough to survive institutional participation. In his view, Bitcoin does not become weaker simply because banks and large investors decide to buy it.

## Wall Street And Bitcoin ETFs Continue Growing

The debate comes during a period of massive institutional growth around Bitcoin ETFs in the United States. Spot Bitcoin ETFs have already attracted tens of billions of dollars in net inflows since their launch.

Some Bitcoin supporters argue that ETFs concentrate too much Bitcoin inside the hands of a small number of financial companies. Others believe ETFs are helping Bitcoin become a mainstream global asset accessible to pension funds, traditional investors and institutions that previously avoided crypto entirely.

Mallers appears to firmly belong to the second group.

## Strike Became One Of The Most Recognized Bitcoin Apps

Jack Mallers is best known as the founder and CEO of Strike, a Bitcoin-focused payments company that gained global attention during El Salvador’s Bitcoin adoption phase.

Strike became particularly well known for using the Lightning Network to enable fast and low-cost Bitcoin payments across borders.

## Could Institutional Capital Actually Strengthen Bitcoin?

One of the most controversial parts of the debate is whether institutional money could eventually reshape Bitcoin itself.

Critics argue that Wall Street firms may gain too much influence over custody, ETFs and market direction. Supporters, however, say institutional participation is inevitable if Bitcoin wants to compete with traditional stores of value like gold, government bonds or real estate.

Mallers believes Bitcoin is ultimately competing for global capital, and that major financial firms joining the ecosystem is simply a natural consequence of Bitcoin becoming more valuable and widely accepted.

## Bitcoin’s Identity Crisis Is Far From Over

The argument over Wall Street and Bitcoin reflects a much larger ideological divide inside the crypto industry.

One side sees Bitcoin as a decentralized alternative to traditional finance. The other increasingly sees it as a new global financial asset that institutions will inevitably integrate into their systems.

For now, both worlds appear to be colliding — and people like Jack Mallers are becoming some of the loudest voices arguing that institutional adoption does not automatically mean Bitcoin has lost its original purpose.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

- The Wall Street vs Bitcoin debate is becoming one of the biggest ideological battles in crypto.
- Institutional money is helping Bitcoin gain legitimacy among traditional investors.
- ETFs may increase adoption, but they also raise concerns about concentration and custody.
- Jack Mallers represents a growing group that sees institutional involvement as a sign of Bitcoin’s success, not failure.
- The relationship between Bitcoin and traditional finance will likely remain one of the dominant crypto narratives throughout 2026.
