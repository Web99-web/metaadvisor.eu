# Saylor Changes Course? Strategy Considers Selling Bitcoin for the First Time

After years of repeating the same message — “Bitcoin should never be sold” — the narrative may finally be shifting. Strategy, the largest publicly traded corporate holder of Bitcoin, is now openly considering selling a portion of its BTC reserves for the first time.

This statement comes at a moment when the company has reported massive losses and is facing growing financial obligations. The question is no longer just what Strategy is doing — but what it means for the entire crypto market.

## Record losses change the tone

Strategy reported a net loss of approximately $12.5 billion, largely driven by unrealized losses on its Bitcoin holdings following a price decline in the first quarter.

Despite this, the company still holds over 800,000 BTC, making it by far the largest institutional Bitcoin holder.

But a large portfolio also comes with large obligations.

Strategy has around $1.5 billion in dividend and debt obligations, and estimates suggest it has roughly 18 months of coverage based on existing reserves.

This is the moment where theory meets reality.

## “Sell to prove we can”

During the Q1 earnings call, Michael Saylor introduced an idea that would have seemed unthinkable just months ago:

👉 Strategy may sell some Bitcoin to fund dividend payments

His explanation was striking:

“We will probably sell some Bitcoin to pay a dividend — just to inoculate the market and show that we did it.”

In other words, the goal is not purely financial — it is also psychological.

Saylor wants to signal to the market that:

* the company is stable
* the Bitcoin strategy remains intact
* selling does not mean abandoning the narrative

## The end of the “never sell” philosophy?

For years, Saylor has been one of the strongest advocates of the idea that Bitcoin should never be sold.

As recently as February, he stated that Strategy would “buy Bitcoin forever” and could withstand a price drop to $8,000 without needing to sell.

This latest statement represents a clear shift.

Even if it’s not a large-scale sale, the mere fact that selling is now being considered changes market perception.

{{< support2 >}}

## A model that works… until it doesn’t

The strategy Saylor describes is relatively straightforward:

* buy Bitcoin using debt
* allow it to appreciate
* sell a portion to cover obligations

This is effectively a form of “yield strategy” built on Bitcoin.

However, it depends on one key assumption:
👉 that Bitcoin will continue to rise over time

If the price stagnates or declines:

* pressure to sell increases
* debt becomes a burden
* investor confidence may weaken

## Market reaction

The market reacted almost immediately.

Strategy’s stock dropped more than 4% in after-hours trading, while Bitcoin fell below $81,000.

This highlights how sensitive the market is to shifts in narrative — especially when they come from major institutional players.

## The bigger picture: signal or strategy?

The key question is:

👉 Is this the beginning of a broader trend — or just a tactical move?

If Strategy begins selling Bitcoin:

* it could open the door for other institutions
* it could change how Bitcoin is viewed as a treasury asset
* it could impact long-term market sentiment

On the other hand, this may simply be a controlled move designed to stabilize the market and reinforce confidence.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

* This is the first real test of the “never sell” narrative — and cracks are already appearing
* Selling to fund dividends shows that even the largest BTC holder must act pragmatically
* The “buy with debt, sell later” model only works in a rising market
* Saylor is trying to manage perception, not just finances
* This is not the end of the strategy — but the beginning of its evolution
