# Samsung pays record bonuses to workers: AI boom pushes chip companies into the $1 trillion club

The AI boom is no longer just a story about large technology companies, stock market charts and new data centers. It is now becoming increasingly visible in the wages of the workers who produce key parts of that infrastructure. Samsung Electronics has reached an agreement with unions under which employees in its memory chip division are set to receive exceptionally large bonuses, after demand for memory chips surged because of artificial intelligence.

The agreement prevented a possible strike and showed how important the memory chip market has become in the new AI economy. While public attention mostly focuses on Nvidia, large AI models and data centers, the entire story also depends on the companies that produce the memory without which AI infrastructure cannot function. Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron are now among the biggest winners of this wave.

## AI is no longer lifting only stocks, but also bonuses

Under the agreement, Samsung will set aside 10.5% of the operating profit from its semiconductor division for special bonuses for workers in the chip sector. This is a major step, especially because it is not a symbolic reward, but amounts that for some workers could reach hundreds of thousands of dollars.

The most frequently mentioned example refers to a worker in the memory chip division with a base salary of around 80 million won. Such a worker could, according to calculations cited in media reports, receive a bonus of about 626 million won, or approximately $416,000, £310,000 or around €358,000, mostly in stock. The amounts will vary from employee to employee, but the scale alone shows how much the economics of chips has changed.

This is an important moment because it shows that the AI boom is no longer staying only at the level of managers, investors and stock markets. Profit is now, at least in this case, also flowing to some of the employees working in the most important segment of production.

## The agreement prevented a strike

Samsung’s agreement with the unions did not come in a calm atmosphere. The company was facing a possible strike at one of the most important moments for global chip production. According to reports, 74% of more than 62,000 workers who voted supported the agreement.

This matters because Samsung is not an ordinary technology company. Its production of memory chips is crucial for global supply chains, especially now that AI data centers require enormous amounts of memory and computing infrastructure. Any disruption in production could have much broader consequences than for one company alone.

That is why this agreement is not only a question of wages. It is also a question of production stability at a moment when the whole world is trying to build new AI infrastructure.

## Why are memory chips so important for AI?

When artificial intelligence is discussed, the focus is usually on GPUs, Nvidia, large models and data centers. But AI systems do not need only computing power. They also need enormous amounts of memory in order to process data quickly, train models and run increasingly complex applications.

This is where Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron come in. Their memory chips have become one of the key parts of the AI value chain. As demand for AI servers grows, the need for advanced memory grows as well. And when demand grows faster than supply, memory chip prices rise.

That is why these companies are in such a strong position. They are not selling just “another component.” They are selling infrastructure without which the AI boom cannot continue at the same pace.

## SK Hynix and Micron enter the $1 trillion club

The wider picture is even more impressive. SK Hynix and Micron have entered the club of companies whose market value has crossed the $1 trillion mark. This shows how dramatically investors have changed their view of memory chip companies.

Until recently, memory chip companies were often seen as cyclical businesses: in good times they earn a lot, while in bad times they suffer from falling prices, weaker demand and inventory problems. The AI boom has changed that story, at least for now. Demand for memory used in AI infrastructure is so strong that the market now values these companies as strategic winners of the new technology phase.

SK Hynix has benefited especially from its position in advanced memory for AI systems. Micron is also recording strong growth, while Samsung is trying to maintain and strengthen its position in the global competition.

## Bonuses can also create new tensions

Still, bonuses this large are not only a positive story. Inside Samsung, they could create new tensions because not all employees will receive the same amounts. Workers in the memory chip division are in the strongest position because that segment is currently generating enormous profits. Employees in other parts of the company, such as consumer electronics, smartphones or home appliances, could receive much smaller amounts.

This can create a sense of inequality inside the same company. On one hand, it is logical that workers in the most profitable part of the business demand a larger share of the profit. On the other hand, large differences in bonuses can create dissatisfaction among employees who also work for Samsung, but are not part of the sector currently at the center of the AI boom.

This is therefore both a labor story and a market story. AI is not changing only products and stock market valuations, but also the bargaining power of employees in sectors that have become strategically important.

## The AI boom becomes a question of labor, wages and power

This story shows something very interesting: artificial intelligence is not only a software revolution. It is changing industry, production, labor relations and the distribution of profits. If memory chips are becoming key infrastructure of the new economy, then the workers who produce them are gaining stronger bargaining power.

This may be one of the most important parts of the whole story. Usually, when people talk about the AI boom, they talk about investors, startups, executives and market capitalization. But here we see that part of the value must also be shared with workers, at least when they are sufficiently organized and when their work becomes critical for global production.

Samsung’s agreement could therefore have wider consequences. If it becomes clear that AI profits can finance enormous bonuses, workers in other technology and industrial sectors may demand similar profit-sharing models.

## What does this story say about the future of the AI market?

This is not just a story about one bonus. It is a signal that the AI boom is entering a new phase. The first phase was the excitement around software, models and applications. The second phase was the explosion in demand for GPUs and data centers. Now we are seeing a third phase: the entire industrial chain around AI infrastructure is beginning to change wages, bonuses, union negotiations and the market value of companies.

For investors, this confirms that memory chips are no longer a side topic. For workers, it shows that a strategically important sector can bring much greater bargaining power. For companies, it is a warning that profits from the AI boom will not remain only at the top, but will increasingly become the subject of negotiations.

Samsung avoided a strike with this agreement, but may also have opened a new era of expectations. If AI brings record profits, the question is who has the right to take part in those profits.
