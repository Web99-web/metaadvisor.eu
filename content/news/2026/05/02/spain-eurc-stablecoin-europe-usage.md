# Spain leads EURC usage in Europe: the first real test of a digital euro in practice

While Europe is still working on the official “Digital Euro,” real-world usage is already moving ahead. According to data from the Brighty platform, Spain has emerged as the leading market for the euro stablecoin EURC, offering an early glimpse of how a digital euro could function in everyday life.

This is not a story about speculation or trading — it’s about real usage.

## What the data shows

Available data indicates:

* Spain accounts for around **36% of all EURC transactions**
* about **25% of total volume** comes from Spain
* the average transaction is approximately **€49**

This means EURC in Spain is primarily used for:

* small payments
* peer-to-peer transfers
* everyday spending

In other words, it behaves like real money, not an investment asset.

## EURC as a “digital euro” in practice

One of the key reasons for EURC’s adoption in Spain is its simplicity.

According to Brighty, EURC functions like:

👉 “a euro on a card without exchange rate friction”

This means:

* no currency conversion losses
* no waiting for banks
* near-instant transactions

This may be one of the first real examples of how a digital euro could work in practice.

## Important distinction: EURC ≠ Digital Euro

Although they may appear similar, there is a critical difference:

* **EURC** → a private stablecoin (issued by Circle)
* **Digital Euro** → a future project by the European Central Bank

EURC is already in use, while the Digital Euro is still under development.

{{< support2 >}}

## The euro stablecoin market is still small

Despite recent growth, euro stablecoins remain far smaller than their dollar counterparts.

* total euro stablecoin market: ~**$887 million**
* EURC holds around **49% market share**

In comparison:

* USD stablecoins exceed **$300 billion**

This highlights how early Europe still is in this space.

## Why Spain?

There is no definitive answer yet, but several factors may explain this trend:

* strong adoption of digital payments
* a growing fintech ecosystem
* widespread use of cards and mobile payments
* potential regional focus from platforms like Brighty

What is clear:

👉 Spain is currently acting as a testing ground for euro stablecoin adoption

## The bigger picture: Europe is building its path

This development fits into a broader trend:

* **AllUnity (EURAU)** → infrastructure
* **Sentora** → analytics and DeFi tools
* **EURC / Brighty** → real-world usage

Together, they show that Europe is not just theorizing — it is actively building a digital financial ecosystem.

## What this means going forward

If this trend continues, we may soon see:

* more euro-denominated stablecoins
* deeper integration into everyday payments
* growing competition with USD stablecoins

And most importantly:

👉 a shift from experimentation to real-world adoption
