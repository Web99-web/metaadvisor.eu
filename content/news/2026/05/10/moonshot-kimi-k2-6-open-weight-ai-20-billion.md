# Moonshot and Kimi K2.6 open-weight AI model reach $20 billion valuation

Chinese AI startup Moonshot has become one of the most talked-about companies in the global AI industry after reports confirmed the company reached a valuation of roughly $20 billion.

At the same time, its AI model Kimi K2.6 is increasingly being discussed among developers, AI startups and the open-weight AI community as one of the most interesting new models emerging from China.

The story matters because it highlights how quickly the Chinese AI sector is approaching American giants such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.

And perhaps even more importantly:
➡️ how open-weight AI models are becoming serious competitors to closed AI systems.

## Who is Moonshot?

Moonshot AI was founded in Beijing in 2023 by Yang Zhilin, a former Meta AI and Google Brain researcher.

Although the company is still relatively young, its growth has been extremely rapid.

According to reports:
- Moonshot was valued at around $10 billion just a few months ago
- the valuation has now doubled to roughly $20 billion
- and the company reportedly raised nearly $4 billion over the past six months.

{{< support1 >}}

The latest investment round was led by Long-Z Investment, the venture capital arm of Chinese giant Meituan.

Other participants included:
- China Mobile
- Tsinghua Capital
- CPE Yuanfeng
- and other Chinese investors.

## Why has Kimi K2.6 become so popular?

Much of the attention revolves around:
👉 Kimi K2.6.

It is an open-weight large language model (LLM) that developers can more flexibly use, host and integrate into their own systems.

That is important because parts of the AI community are becoming increasingly frustrated with:
- closed AI models
- high API costs
- usage limitations
- and dependence on large cloud platforms.

That is why open-weight models have exploded in popularity in recent months.

According to OpenRouter data, Kimi K2.6 is now among the most widely used AI models on the platform.

It is particularly popular among developers working on:
- coding
- automation
- AI agents
- and API integrations.

{{< support1 >}}

## China is entering the AI race more aggressively

Moonshot is not the only Chinese AI company attracting attention lately.

Strong growth is also being seen from:
- DeepSeek
- Alibaba Qwen
- MiniMax
- Baichuan
- Zhipu AI
- and numerous other Chinese AI labs.

One especially interesting trend is that many Chinese companies are far more aggressively embracing:
➡️ the open-weight approach.

That gives developers:
- greater flexibility
- lower costs
- local model hosting
- and less dependence on American cloud platforms.

As a result, some analysts believe China could become a very serious challenger to American AI dominance.

{{< support2 >}}

## OpenAI, Anthropic and Google are facing new competition

Just one year ago, most AI discussions revolved almost entirely around:
- OpenAI
- ChatGPT
- Google Gemini
- and Anthropic.

Today the landscape looks very different.

Chinese models:
- are becoming increasingly capable
- offer lower inference costs
- are rapidly gaining developers
- and are growing aggressively within the open-weight community.

That does not necessarily mean they have already surpassed the best American models.

But:
➡️ the price-to-performance ratio is becoming an extremely important factor.

And that is exactly what is attracting investors right now.

## The AI race may not be won by the “smartest” model

More analysts now believe that the winner of the AI race may not necessarily be the model with the best benchmarks.

Instead, the winner could be:
- the cheapest model
- the most widely distributed model
- the easiest model for developers to integrate
- or the model with the largest ecosystem.

And that is precisely why open-weight models are becoming so important.

Developers increasingly want:
- flexibility
- lower costs
- control
- and the ability to self-host AI systems.

That is why companies like Moonshot are now attracting billions of dollars in investment.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

* Moonshot shows how quickly the Chinese AI sector is growing
* Open-weight AI models are becoming serious competitors to closed AI systems
* Kimi K2.6 is attracting significant attention among developers
* China is increasingly trying to close the AI gap with the United States
* The next major AI battle may revolve not only around model quality — but also around price, flexibility and ecosystems
