# Ethereum stagnates as the market searches for direction: Is its biggest buyer slowing accumulation?

Ethereum has entered a strange phase over the past few months. While Bitcoin and parts of the broader crypto market are attempting to regain momentum, ETH continues to struggle to sustainably hold above the $2,400 level. Just two days after fresh institutional headlines, Ethereum is again trading around $2,328, intensifying concerns that the second-largest cryptocurrency may be losing part of the momentum it once dominated the market with.

Beneath the surface, this is about far more than a simple price correction. Ethereum is no longer the only dominant smart contract ecosystem, and competition today looks much stronger than it did only a few years ago.

## Ethereum is no longer the only dominant story

For years Ethereum dominated decentralized finance, NFTs and much of the broader Web3 infrastructure. But the crypto market has changed significantly. Solana continues aggressively attracting retail users through faster and cheaper transactions, while Layer-2 networks such as Arbitrum, Base and Optimism are fragmenting activity and liquidity across the Ethereum ecosystem itself.

At the same time, DEX volumes and decentralized application revenues have been falling, while the memecoin mania that briefly boosted activity has started cooling down. Some analysts now believe Ethereum may be entering a kind of “identity crisis,” where the market is no longer entirely certain how ETH should be valued.

Is Ethereum still a high-growth asset capable of explosive upside? Or is it gradually evolving into critical infrastructure that remains important but no longer generates the same speculative excitement as before?

## Bitmine has become Ethereum’s key institutional buyer

A major focus for investors now is Bitmine, the largest Ethereum treasury company. The firm currently holds more than 5.1 million ETH, representing roughly 4.29% of the total Ethereum supply. During Consensus Miami, Bitmine chairman Tom Lee revealed that the company may slow the pace of ETH accumulation as it rapidly approaches its original target of acquiring 5% of Ethereum’s total supply.

That is a highly important signal for the market because Bitmine has been one of the few major institutional buyers still aggressively accumulating ETH even during weaker market periods. The company originally expected reaching the 5% target would take five years, yet it is now approaching that milestone in less than a year.

For investors, this immediately raises several concerns. If the market’s largest buyer slows accumulation, buying pressure weakens and sentiment could become more fragile. The market is now beginning to ask who the next major institutional buyer could be and whether Ethereum can regain stronger momentum without that constant institutional support.

## Ethereum is increasingly becoming a yield-generating asset

However, the story is not entirely bearish. One of the most important aspects of Bitmine’s strategy is that roughly 85% of its ETH holdings are staked. That means the company is not simply holding Ethereum as a reserve asset but is actively generating significant income from staking rewards.

According to available figures, annualized staking revenue now exceeds $300 million. That creates a completely different narrative compared to Bitcoin.

Bitcoin is primarily viewed as “digital gold” — a store of value. Ethereum, on the other hand, is increasingly being treated as a productive digital asset capable of generating yield through staking and decentralized financial infrastructure.

This distinction is attracting growing institutional interest. ETH is no longer viewed solely as a speculative asset but increasingly as part of the infrastructure behind a future digital financial system.

## Why ETH still lacks Bitcoin’s momentum

Despite these developments, the market still appears uncertain about Ethereum. Bitcoin has benefited heavily in recent months from ETFs, treasury strategies and its “digital gold” narrative. Ethereum has yet to generate the same level of institutional enthusiasm.

Some investors believe Ethereum now needs:
- a new major narrative
- another retail-driven cycle
- or a new generation of applications capable of reigniting market excitement.

Meanwhile, competition continues intensifying. Solana, Base and other chains are attempting to capture more users while Ethereum still struggles with higher costs, a fragmented Layer-2 ecosystem and liquidity fragmentation.

At the same time, large DeFi exploits and security incidents continue damaging confidence across parts of the ecosystem. During April alone, the crypto industry lost hundreds of millions of dollars through various hacks and exploits.

## Ethereum does not look dead — but the market is searching for a new direction

Most analysts still do not believe Ethereum is “dying.” Far from it. Ethereum continues dominating large parts of decentralized finance, maintains the largest developer ecosystem and remains the core smart contract infrastructure for much of Web3.

But increasingly, the market appears to be trying to redefine what Ethereum actually is today. Is it infrastructure for decentralized finance? A yield-generating digital bond through staking? Or still a high-growth crypto asset capable of another explosive cycle?

The answer to that question could become one of the most important themes for the entire crypto market over the coming months.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

* Ethereum appears to be entering a phase of market identity redefinition
* Bitmine has become one of the most important institutional ETH players
* Slower accumulation could impact short-term market sentiment
* Staking revenue gives Ethereum a very different institutional narrative than Bitcoin
* Competition from rival chains is far more serious today than it was a few years ago
