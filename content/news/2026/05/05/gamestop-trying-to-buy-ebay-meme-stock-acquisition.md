# GameStop 2.0: How GameStop is Trying to Buy eBay — a Company 4x Bigger

GameStop is once again in the spotlight — but this time not because of Reddit traders, but due to a bold move that could become one of the most controversial deals in modern financial history.

The company has submitted a proposal to acquire eBay in a deal worth around **$55 billion**, attempting to take over a company that is significantly larger than itself.

This is not just unusual — it challenges how modern capital markets operate.

## A smaller company trying to buy a bigger one

GameStop currently has a market value of around **$11–12 billion**, while eBay is valued at roughly **$45–50 billion**.

In simple terms:
👉 a smaller company is trying to acquire one nearly four times its size  

This is extremely rare in M&A (mergers and acquisitions — the process of companies combining or buying one another), where typically larger firms acquire smaller ones.

{{< support1 >}}

## How could this be financed?

The proposed structure includes:
- existing cash reserves  
- significant debt financing  
- issuing new shares  

GameStop reportedly holds around **$9 billion in cash**, but the rest would need to come from lenders and capital markets.

This leads to the key question:
👉 can a rising stock price help finance the deal?

## Can the “meme effect” return?

GameStop holds a unique position in financial markets.

Unlike most companies, it has strong backing from retail investors, meaning:
- news can quickly drive the stock higher  
- hype can boost liquidity  
- new shares can be sold more easily  

If the stock price rises sharply:
👉 GameStop could raise additional capital and move closer to completing the acquisition  

However, this path is far from guaranteed.

{{< support2 >}}

## Major risks

There are several critical risks involved:

- **share dilution** — issuing new stock reduces the value of existing shares  
- **dependence on market sentiment** — hype can disappear quickly  
- **financing uncertainty** — without institutional backing, the deal is unlikely to succeed  
- **regulatory hurdles** — large acquisitions require approvals  

In other words, this is not just a bold move — it is a high-risk financial experiment.

## The strategy behind the move

CEO Ryan Cohen aims to transform GameStop from a traditional retail company into a modern e-commerce platform.

The plan includes:
- turning physical stores into logistics hubs  
- reducing operational costs  
- competing more directly with Amazon  

If successful, the company would be fundamentally reshaped.

## A GameStop 2.0 moment

Many analysts are already calling this a **“GameStop 2.0” moment**.

Unlike 2021:
- back then, retail investors drove the price surge  
- now, the company itself is attempting a historic move  

This raises a key question:
👉 can market hype finance real-world corporate acquisitions?
