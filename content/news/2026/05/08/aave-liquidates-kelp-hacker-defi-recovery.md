# Aave liquidates Kelp hacker as DeFi tries to stabilize $293M chaos

One of the biggest DeFi exploits of the year is now entering a new phase.

After the Kelp DAO-linked hack caused chaos across the Ethereum and Arbitrum ecosystem, Aave has started liquidating the remaining rsETH positions connected to the attacker.

The goal is clear:

👉 stabilize the system and prevent further contagion across the DeFi market.

## How did the exploit turn into DeFi contagion?

The April attack did not remain isolated to a single protocol.

Hackers stole around 116,500 rsETH tokens and later used them as collateral on Aave v3 to borrow wrapped Ether.

That is why the incident did not impact only Kelp DAO.

The consequences spread across:

* lending markets
* cross-chain bridges
* restaking protocols
* and DAO governance systems.

That is why many analysts are now using the term:

👉 “DeFi contagion.”

A situation where one exploit creates a domino effect across multiple interconnected protocols.

## Aave is now trying to “clean up” the system

Aave has now started liquidating the hacker’s rsETH positions on Ethereum and Arbitrum.

According to available reports:

* collateral was transferred into a recovery multisig wallet
* the recovery process is coordinated by DeFi United
* and the goal is restoring rsETH backing.

Galaxy Digital says the system is now reportedly only around 10% away from full recovery.

This means the DeFi ecosystem is effectively trying to stabilize itself without a traditional central institution.

## Arbitrum DAO and the “legal limbo”

But the problem is no longer just technical.

Part of the funds are now trapped in legal uncertainty.

According to reports:

* more than 30,000 ETH remains frozen
* a US law firm filed a restraining notice
* and debate continues over whether Arbitrum DAO can redistribute the funds.

In other words:

👉 decentralized systems are now colliding directly with traditional legal systems.

And this is where DeFi becomes far more complex than most users realize.

## What exactly is rsETH?

rsETH is a restaking token tied to the Kelp DAO ecosystem.

The restaking concept became extremely popular following the rise of the EigenLayer ecosystem.

The simple idea:

👉 the same ETH capital can secure multiple protocols at once in order to generate additional yield.

But this exploit also revealed the darker side of the model:

* protocols are becoming increasingly interconnected
* risk spreads across the entire system
* and a single exploit can trigger much larger consequences than expected.

## Why does this matter for DeFi as a whole?

For years, supporters argued that decentralized finance could eventually replace parts of the traditional financial system.

But incidents like this show how fragile the infrastructure still is.

The most vulnerable areas remain:

* cross-chain bridges
* restaking models
* complex collateral systems
* and highly interconnected protocols.

## Kelp DAO is now moving to Chainlink

Following the incident, Kelp DAO announced it is migrating its rsETH infrastructure from LayerZero to Chainlink CCIP.

That is a very important signal for the market.

Because it effectively shows:
👉 a loss of confidence in the previous infrastructure.

Kelp DAO and LayerZero are now publicly blaming each other for the security failures.

LayerZero claims the issue was caused by Kelp’s configuration, while Kelp argues that LayerZero approved the setup later exploited by the attacker.

Behind the scenes, a much larger battle is now emerging:
👉 who will become the dominant infrastructure provider for future cross-chain DeFi.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

* Aave is now effectively participating in stabilizing part of the DeFi ecosystem
* This exploit showed how deeply interconnected DeFi protocols have become
* Restaking creates new yield opportunities — but also new systemic risks
* The conflict between Kelp DAO and LayerZero could have long-term consequences for the bridge industry
* The migration to Chainlink shows how trust has become one of the most important issues in modern DeFi
