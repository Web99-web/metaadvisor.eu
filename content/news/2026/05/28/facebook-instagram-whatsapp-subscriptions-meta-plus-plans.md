# Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp subscriptions: Meta introduces Plus plans, but small users first want account security

Meta is entering a new phase of monetizing its biggest apps. Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp are getting optional Plus subscriptions, while broader AI, creator and business plans are also being mentioned in parallel. The basic versions of the apps remain free for now, but users who want extra features will be able to pay a monthly subscription.

According to available information, Facebook Plus and Instagram Plus are expected to cost around $3.99 per month, while WhatsApp Plus is mentioned at around $2.99 per month. At first glance, these look like small add-ons, such as extra options for stories, stickers, pinned chats, themes and personalization. But the bigger picture matters much more: Meta is testing how willing users are to pay for social networks they have perceived as free for decades.

## What do Facebook Plus, Instagram Plus and WhatsApp Plus actually mean?

Facebook Plus is expected to offer additional options for posts and stories, Instagram Plus more control over stories and visibility, while WhatsApp Plus should bring extra options for organizing chats, themes, stickers and personalization. These are not functions without which the apps stop working, but a premium layer for users who want more control or a better user experience.

For large companies, such subscriptions are probably not a major cost. But for freelancers, small brands, local entrepreneurs and people who are just trying to start a side project, even a few dollars per month can be a decision worth thinking about. Many of them do not have a large budget, an agency or a marketing team, and they often try to build something alongside work, family and very limited resources.

## Small users might be willing to pay — but they do not want to be punished first

That is why this news is interesting from another angle. Small users might be willing to pay a small amount if it brought them better organization, more security, clearer tools or a more professional presence. For someone just building a small brand, $2.99 or $3.99 per month is not the same as expensive business software. It can be acceptable if it really helps.

The problem is that many users today do not feel protected by large platforms. On the contrary, in recent years there have been more and more cases in which long-term Facebook profiles, pages and groups are restricted, disabled or locked without a clear explanation. It happens suddenly: in one second, a user can lose access to Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and Meta Business Suite, without any prior warning. It is especially sensitive when this happens to users who have been on the network for 15 or 20 years and who spent years building contacts, audiences, pages and communities.

That question now becomes even more important. If Meta wants to charge users for extra features, users need to have at least a minimum sense of security. Nobody wants to pay for premium options on a platform where, at the same time, they fear that their account, page or group could disappear overnight because of an automated decision that nobody can explain.

## AI moderation, too many posts or the cleaning out of old users?

It is still not completely clear why some long-term users ended up under restrictions. It is possible that part of the problem is connected to AI moderation, automated detection of alleged spam, a high number of posts, links or behavior patterns that the system interprets as risky. But from the user’s perspective, the result is the same: the account disappears, access is lost and real human support is almost impossible to reach.

That is exactly why Plus subscriptions cannot be seen only as a nice extra for stories and chats. They are arriving at a moment when many users already have damaged trust in the platforms. If the pattern is truly that older, active and long-term profiles are also being affected, then Meta must explain how it will distinguish real spam accounts from people who have legitimately used the network for years.

## Subscriptions can help, but they do not solve the trust problem

For Meta as a business, subscriptions make sense. The advertising model is still huge, but privacy regulation, higher costs of artificial intelligence and increasingly expensive infrastructure are forcing large platforms to look for additional revenue sources. Monthly subscriptions bring more stable revenue and gradually get users used to premium digital services.

But for ordinary users, especially small creators and freelancers, the real question is not only how much something costs. The real question is what they get in return. If Plus plans bring only stickers, themes and slightly longer stories, they will be interesting only to part of the audience. If one day they bring better support, a clearer reputation system, safer accounts and a better appeal process, then they could become much more important.

## Meta is entering the subscription era

This is the beginning of a broader change. Social networks are increasingly moving away from a fully free model toward a combination of a free basic version and paid add-ons. This does not mean that all users will pay for Facebook, Instagram or WhatsApp tomorrow, but it does mean that the line between ordinary and premium use is shifting.

For Metaadvisor, the most important part of this story is not only the price of the subscription. The more important issue is trust. If platforms want small users to pay, they must stop treating long-term users as disposable accounts without history, without reputation and without the right to a clear explanation.

## Our view (Metaadvisor):

* Meta is not introducing mandatory payment for Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, but additional Plus subscriptions for users who want more features.
* Small brands, freelancers and creators might be willing to pay small amounts if they received useful tools and greater security in return.
* The biggest problem is not the price of a few dollars, but trust in the platform and the fear that an account can disappear without a clear reason.
* If Meta wants to seriously monetize small users, it must offer more than extra story options, stickers and themes.
* Reputation credit for long-term profiles could become one of the most important questions in the era of AI moderation and paid social networks.
