# Shock in crypto markets: fund investments fall from $2.6B to $659M

The crypto market is facing an unexpected shift. After a period of strong growth and high expectations, fund investments in startup projects have dropped to their lowest levels in nearly two years.

According to available data, total investments fell from around **$2.6 billion in March** to just **$659 million in April**, representing a decline of approximately **74% in a single month**.

Such a drop does not happen without reason — and it rarely comes without consequences.

## Sudden withdrawal of capital

The decline does not mean interest in crypto is disappearing, but it clearly shows a change in investor behavior. While funds aggressively invested in new projects in previous years, often driven by rapid growth expectations, the focus is now shifting toward caution and selectivity.

The number of funding rounds has also decreased, confirming that the market is cooling. Instead of spreading capital across many smaller deals, investors are now choosing fewer projects and evaluating them more carefully.

In other words, the era of “easy money” is coming to an end.

{{< support1 >}}

## Not all negative: a structural shift

Although the headline looks negative, this development may indicate something deeper — market maturation.

More capital is now being directed toward:

* infrastructure projects
* solutions with real-world use cases
* long-term sustainable business models

At the same time, projects built purely on hype or short-term growth without clear fundamentals are finding it increasingly difficult to secure funding.

This suggests that the crypto market is moving from an experimental phase into a construction phase.

## Opposite trend: rise of tokenization

While startup funding is declining, another segment is growing rapidly.

Tokenized real-world assets (RWA) have increased by more than **420% since the beginning of 2025**, showing that interest in blockchain technology is not disappearing — it is shifting.

This contrast is critical:

👉 less capital is flowing into ideas
👉 more capital is flowing into real applications

{{< support2 >}}

## What this means for the industry

The drop in funding has several implications:

* startups will face greater challenges in raising capital
* projects will need to demonstrate real value
* the market will become more selective

For investors, this means:

* lower risk, but fewer quick-return opportunities
* a shift toward quality over quantity

For the industry as a whole, this could represent a turning point.

## Bigger picture: the end of the hype phase?

Such data often appears when markets transition from one phase to another.

Crypto has already gone through several cycles:

* hype phase
* downturn
* consolidation

The current decline may signal the beginning of a new phase — one dominated by more stable and serious projects.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

* a 74% drop in one month is a strong signal
* investors are becoming more cautious and selective
* the market is moving away from hype-driven projects
* infrastructure and real-world applications are gaining importance
* this may mark a transition into a more mature phase of the crypto industry
