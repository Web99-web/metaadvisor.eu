# Bitcoin between weaker demand and a short squeeze: Can BTC return to $80,000?

Bitcoin has once again shown in recent days why it is difficult to look at the market through only one indicator. On one side of the market, there are signs of weaker demand, money flowing out of spot Bitcoin ETFs, lower accumulation by large holders and growing doubts about the narrative that BTC is “digital gold”. On the other side, the short-term technical picture shows that too many short positions above the current price could become fuel for a sharp move toward $80,000.

That is exactly why the current situation is not a simple story about a decline, but it is not a clean bullish story either. Bitcoin is moving in a zone where the market is trying to decide whether the latest decline was only a temporary correction or the beginning of a deeper problem. In recent days, the price has moved around $76,000 to $78,000, while traders are increasingly focused on the $80,000 level.

## The rally first stalled at the 200-day moving average

One of the important reasons why the market became more cautious is Bitcoin’s failed attempt to hold above the 200-day simple moving average. This indicator is often viewed as a dividing line between a short-term recovery and a more serious return to an uptrend.

Bitcoin began to recover after its February lows, and for a while that move looked like the beginning of new bullish momentum. However, the price stalled just above $82,000 and then returned toward the area around $77,000. That rejection from the 200-day moving average reminded some analysts of earlier market phases, when similar “relief rallies” ended with a continuation of the decline.

The problem was not only the technical indicator itself. Below the surface, it became visible that the rally no longer had the same foundations as before. Demand in the spot market weakened, leveraged futures buying was not enough to carry the price on its own, and U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs moved from inflows to outflows.

## ETFs, Coinbase premium and the question of U.S. demand

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs played an important role in Bitcoin’s previous move higher. When money flows into them, the market often reads that as a sign of institutional demand. But when inflows turn into outflows, the same logic starts working in the opposite direction. According to available market data from the analyzed sources, spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded large weekly outflows after a period of strong inflows. This is an important signal because it shows that some institutional and professional investors are no longer buying at the same pace as before. Bitcoin then loses one of the main sources of support that had previously helped the rally.

Another indicator that is often watched is the Coinbase premium. It shows whether Bitcoin is trading at a higher price on Coinbase than on offshore exchanges. When this premium is positive, it is interpreted as a sign of stronger U.S. demand. When it is negative, it means that U.S. buyers are not willing to pay more for BTC. This negative Coinbase premium additionally strengthened the impression that the rally has a buyer problem.

## Whales and large holders are no longer accumulating as before

On-chain data adds another layer to the story. In Bitcoin, large addresses, known as whales, are often watched because their behavior can show the mood among the biggest holders. If large players are aggressively accumulating, the market usually sees that as a sign of confidence. If they stop buying or begin distributing coins, the signal is much more cautious. According to data reported by crypto analytics sources, some large holders are still buying more than the new Bitcoin issuance, but the pace of accumulation has slowed significantly. Even more importantly, the largest whales, meaning entities holding more than 1,000 BTC, showed a very weak accumulation trend. That does not automatically mean that a major decline is coming, but it does mean that the market no longer has the same level of support from its largest players.

At the same time, realized losses rose to around $600 million. A realized loss happens when investors sell Bitcoin at a lower price than the one at which they bought it. When such losses increase, it often shows that part of the market no longer wants to wait for a recovery, but instead exits positions and accepts the loss.

## Mark Cuban and the crack in the “digital gold” narrative

A strong narrative blow was added to this technical and on-chain picture. Mark Cuban, the well-known American billionaire and investor, owner of the Dallas Mavericks, said back in 2021 that his crypto portfolio was roughly 60% Bitcoin, 30% Ethereum and 10% the rest. At the time, he argued that Bitcoin was a better “store of value” than gold because of its scarcity and said he had never sold it. But in a podcast, he said that he had sold most of his Bitcoin after losing confidence in the idea that BTC functions as protection against weakening fiat currencies and geopolitical instability.

Cuban had been known for years as someone who viewed Bitcoin as a better version of gold, precisely because of its limited supply and decentralized structure. But his disappointment comes from Bitcoin’s behavior during a crisis moment. When geopolitical tensions increased and gold strengthened, Bitcoin did not react the way it should have reacted according to the “digital gold” theory. He said Bitcoin was not the hedge he expected it to be.

That matters because Bitcoin has lived for years between two narratives. One says BTC is protection against inflation, weakening currencies and political instability. The other says BTC is a risk asset that behaves similarly to technology stocks, depending on liquidity, interest rates and risk appetite. In this case, Cuban’s message supports the second interpretation: Bitcoin did not behave like gold during the crisis, but more like a risk asset.

## But the short-term market is now looking toward $80,000

Still, this is where the story becomes more interesting. Although the longer-term signals are not ideal, today’s short-term technical data show the possibility of a sharp move toward $80,000. The reason is not necessarily strong organic demand, but the structure of the futures market and the distribution of liquidations. According to data cited by Cointelegraph, more than $4 billion in short positions sit above the current Bitcoin price and could be liquidated if BTC moves toward $80,000. Short positions are bets on a price decline. When the price starts rising against those positions, traders must close them or exchanges automatically liquidate them. That closing of shorts can push the price even higher, because short sellers effectively have to buy in order to close their positions.

On the one-hour chart, Bitcoin showed a bullish divergence between the price and the RSI indicator. RSI, or Relative Strength Index, measures the strength of market momentum. When the price is not rising strongly, but momentum is improving, traders often see that as a sign that selling pressure is weakening.

Bitcoin defended the zone around $76,100 several times and retested the area around $78,000. If there is a clear breakout above $78,000, the market could try to fill the zone between roughly $79,500 and $80,300, where the so-called fair-value gap is located. This is a lower-liquidity area created during a previous sharp selloff, and price sometimes returns to such zones before continuing its next larger move.

## A short squeeze is not the same as a healthy bull market

Here it is important to make a distinction. If Bitcoin rises toward $80,000 because of the liquidation of short positions, that does not necessarily mean the market is healthy again or that a new major bull run is beginning. A short squeeze can be very strong, but also very short. It happens because positions are forced to close, not necessarily because long-term investors want to buy BTC at higher prices.

That is why the current situation is ambiguous. In the short term, short sellers can become fuel for a rally. In the long term, the market still has to prove that real demand exists: ETF inflows, a positive Coinbase premium, greater accumulation by whales and a return of confidence in Bitcoin’s broader narrative.

In other words, Bitcoin can rise without all of its problems being solved. But if weakness appears again after that move, the market will very quickly ask the same question: who is buying Bitcoin above $80,000 and why?

## Bitcoin is currently between two truths

The most interesting thing about this phase is that both sides can have good arguments. The bearish side can say that ETFs are seeing outflows, the Coinbase premium is weak, whales are accumulating less, realized losses are rising and well-known investors such as Mark Cuban no longer believe in Bitcoin as a hedge. That is not a small thing.

The bullish side can say that Bitcoin defended support, that short sellers have overcrowded positions above the price, that technical momentum on lower time frames is improving and that a breakout above $78,000 could quickly open the path toward $80,000.

That is why it may be most accurate to say that Bitcoin is not in a simple trend, but in a decision zone. Short-term traders are watching liquidations and technical patterns. Longer-term investors are watching demand, ETF flows and the behavior of large holders. And the broader public is again asking the old question: is Bitcoin digital gold, a risky technology asset or something in between?

## Conclusion: $80,000 can come quickly, but the buyer question remains

If Bitcoin breaks above $78,000, a move toward $80,000 could be quick because a large amount of short positions is sitting above the price. However, such a move by itself would not solve the more important question: does the market have enough real buyers to continue rising after that?

The last few days have shown that Bitcoin is in a sensitive phase. Weaker institutional demand, ETF outflows, negative signals from the Coinbase premium and weaker accumulation by large holders are creating pressure. On the other hand, that same pessimism and the build-up of short positions can create room for a sharp counter-move.

Bitcoin is therefore now between weaker real demand and a possible technical squeeze. And that is often the most dangerous, but also the most interesting, phase of the market.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* Bitcoin is currently not sending one clear message: longer-term data show weaker demand, while the short-term technical picture allows for a move toward $80,000.
* Outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs and a negative Coinbase premium matter because they suggest that U.S. institutional demand is no longer as strong as before.
* Mark Cuban’s statement is especially interesting because it raises the question of whether Bitcoin truly behaves like digital gold or more like a risky technology asset.
* A possible short squeeze toward $80,000 should not automatically be mistaken for a healthy bull market; the key is whether real buyers appear afterward.
* For the broader market, the most important question remains simple: who is buying Bitcoin above $80,000 — short-term traders, ETFs, whales or a new wave of retail euphoria?
