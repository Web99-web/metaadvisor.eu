# HYPE above $60: Wall Street is now testing whether a DeFi exchange can be worth billions

Hyperliquid’s token HYPE has already moved above $61 according to MEXC data, further strengthening one of the most interesting crypto narratives of the day: can a decentralized exchange, built outside the traditional financial system, become large enough to be taken seriously by institutional investors?

At first glance, this looks like another crypto rally. A token breaks through a new psychological level, social media lights up, traders celebrate, and skeptics wait for the first larger pullback. But with HYPE, the story is more complex. This is no longer only about a token rising because it is popular among crypto users. Increasingly, Hyperliquid is being discussed as infrastructure: a decentralized exchange for perpetual futures, or open-ended futures contracts, trying to show that a large part of trading can move on-chain.

## Why is HYPE now in such strong focus?

For a broader audience, the term “perpetual futures” may sound technical and distant. In simple terms, these are a type of futures contract that does not have a traditional expiration date. Such products are especially popular among crypto traders because they allow users to trade on rising or falling prices, often with leverage. That is exactly why perpetual futures are among the most active and profitable segments of the crypto market.

Hyperliquid is trying to do what large crypto exchanges do, but with a different logic. Instead of the user having to fully trust a centralized company, the idea is that trading, liquidity and settlement increasingly rely on blockchain infrastructure. In other words, Hyperliquid does not want to present itself as just another app, but as an attempt to build new financial infrastructure.

That is an important difference. Many tokens rise on the basis of a story, a promise or short-term euphoria. HYPE has a platform behind it that users actually use for trading. That is why the question around it is increasingly becoming whether DeFi, or decentralized finance, can offer a serious alternative to centralized exchanges.

## Wall Street is not coming because it romantically loves decentralization

The rise of HYPE above $61 according to MEXC data is not happening in a vacuum. The market is increasingly watching institutional interest in Hyperliquid, as well as new ways for investors to gain exposure to HYPE without necessarily using a decentralized application directly. This is where the story around ETFs and similar investment products becomes especially relevant.

ETF stands for exchange-traded fund. For many investors, it is a simpler and more familiar way to gain access to a certain asset. Instead of opening a crypto wallet themselves, protecting a seed phrase or using decentralized protocols, investors can gain exposure through a financial product that feels more familiar to them. Bitcoin ETFs were exactly what changed the perception of Bitcoin in traditional finance, and now a similar logic is gradually spreading to other parts of the crypto market.

Still, it is important to stay realistic. Wall Street is not testing HYPE because it is in love with decentralization. Institutional capital usually goes where it sees volume, liquidity, fees, user growth and profit opportunities. If Hyperliquid shows enough activity and HYPE becomes liquid and recognizable enough, then it becomes interesting as a market product.

That is where the biggest paradox of this story lies. DeFi was created as an alternative to the traditional financial system, but now part of DeFi projects are growing precisely because traditional capital is starting to watch them. The more successful DeFi becomes, the more Wall Street tries to package it into familiar investment forms. And the more it is packaged into institutional products, the further it moves away from its original anti-institutional idea.

## Hyperliquid is not just a token, but an exchange

The biggest difference between HYPE and many other tokens is that there is a very concrete use case behind it. Hyperliquid is not only a project promising some distant future. It already functions as a decentralized exchange, especially focused on active trading and the perpetual futures market.

That does not mean the project is risk-free. Over the past few years, DeFi has shown both the best and the worst sides of the crypto industry. On one hand, it brought innovation, global market access and open financial protocols. On the other hand, it brought hacks, overheated yields, weak tokenomics, excessive leverage and many projects that did not survive their first serious market test.

Hyperliquid is therefore now in a sensitive phase. If the platform continues to grow, HYPE could become one of the most important tokens of the new generation of DeFi trading infrastructure. If the token price becomes too detached from the actual activity of the platform, then there is a risk that the market is once again building a story faster than the project can deliver results.

## Can a decentralized exchange really be worth billions?

The market is now essentially asking a very concrete question: how much is an exchange worth if it does not look like a traditional exchange? In traditional financial companies, value is often measured through revenue, user numbers, trading volume, fees and market share. With crypto projects, another layer is added: tokenomics.

If a token has a real connection to the growth of the platform, fees, staking, buyback mechanisms or other economic models, investors try to estimate whether the platform’s growth can justify the token’s rise. If that connection is not strong enough, the token price can become detached from the project’s real value.

With Hyperliquid, it is therefore important to watch not only the price of HYPE, but also trading volume, user activity, market share in the perpetual DEX segment and the sustainability of revenue. DEX means decentralized exchange. If these indicators continue to grow, HYPE gains a stronger foundation. If the price rises faster than real activity, the risk becomes greater.

In other words, HYPE above $61 is not, by itself, proof that the project is worth billions. It is a signal that the market wants to test that assumption. And when institutional investors also take part in such a test, the narrative quickly shifts from “another token is rising” to “can new financial infrastructure capture real market share?”

## Risk: when infrastructure becomes hype

The name of the token is almost ironic in this case. HYPE is rising, and the market is asking how much of it is real infrastructure and how much of it is actual hype. In the crypto world, that line is often not clear. The best projects can have speculative bubbles, while the loudest bubbles sometimes hide real innovation.

The biggest risk for HYPE is not only a possible price drop. The risk is that the entire narrative overheats before the long-term value of the platform is confirmed. If institutional products attract capital, the price can rise sharply. But if sentiment changes, liquidity can disappear just as quickly.

There is also the broader risk of decentralized exchanges: smart contract risk, market risk, leverage, technical problems, regulatory pressure and the question of what happens if a platform becomes so large that it attracts the attention of regulators. The more successful a project becomes, the more visible it becomes. And the more visible it becomes, the greater the chance that the traditional system will try to regulate it, limit it or fit it into its own rules.

That does not mean the story is negative. Quite the opposite: it means that HYPE has entered a phase in which it can no longer be viewed merely as a small crypto experiment. It now needs to be seen as a project standing at the intersection of DeFi, institutional capital and the future of trading infrastructure.

## Why does this matter even for those who do not trade HYPE?

Even if someone does not own HYPE and does not plan to buy it, this story matters because it shows the broader direction of the market. Crypto is becoming less clearly divided into the “old financial world” and the “new decentralized world.” Instead, we increasingly see a hybrid model: DeFi products receive institutional wrappers, while traditional investors search for ways to enter crypto without directly using crypto tools.

This has already happened with Bitcoin. It is partly happening with Ethereum. Now it is being tested on smaller, more specific and riskier projects. That is why HYPE is interesting as an indicator of how far institutional interest can go beyond the largest crypto names.

If Hyperliquid succeeds, it could become an example that decentralized trading infrastructure can have real market value. If it does not succeed, it could become another reminder that fast growth in crypto often comes before real maturity.

But that is exactly why this story matters. HYPE above $61 is not just a number on a chart. It is a market test of the idea that an exchange does not necessarily have to look like a traditional exchange, that financial infrastructure can be global and on-chain, and that Wall Street may no longer see DeFi only as a marginal experiment.

## Conclusion

Hyperliquid has entered a phase in which more is expected from it than a fast token rise. If HYPE wants to justify the new price level and market attention, it will have to show that there is sustainable activity, real demand and long-term usefulness of the platform behind it.

At the same time, the fact that institutional investors are increasingly looking at HYPE shows how much the crypto market has changed. In the past, projects like this lived almost exclusively inside the DeFi community. Today, investment products are being built around them, capital inflows are being analyzed and comparisons are being made with larger crypto names.

HYPE is therefore more than today’s market winner. It is a test. A test for Hyperliquid, a test for DeFi and a test for Wall Street, which is now trying to assess whether a decentralized exchange can truly be worth billions.
