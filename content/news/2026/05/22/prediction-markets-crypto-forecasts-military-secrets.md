# Prediction markets as a security risk: Can crypto forecasts (bets) reveal military secrets before an attack?

Prediction markets, or forecasting markets, have long been presented as an interesting combination of finance, statistics and the “wisdom of the crowd”. Users on these platforms do not buy classic stocks or tokens. Instead, they take a position on whether a real-world event will happen or not. That is why it is not completely accurate to call them ordinary bets, even though in practice they often strongly resemble betting.

The latest case connected to forecasts about American military actions against Iran shows why these markets can become much more than a crypto curiosity. If large and unusually precise positions on specific attacks, dates or ceasefires appear on a public blockchain before a military operation, then a prediction market is no longer just a market of opinions. It can become a signal read by analysts, regulators, journalists, but also by a state’s adversaries.

## What allegedly happened?

According to analyses by on-chain researchers, very precise positions connected to American military operations against Iran appeared on prediction markets. Dozens of forecasts were reportedly so accurate that they are difficult to explain by luck alone. What makes the situation especially problematic is that some positions allegedly appeared before important events.

Caution is important here. It has not been proven that military insiders or official persons directly traded using confidential information. But the very possibility that someone with access to non-public information could use a prediction market to make money is enough to open a serious debate.

## Why is this a security problem?

In classic insider trading, we usually talk about stocks, business results or regulatory decisions. Someone knows something before the market and uses that information to buy or sell. That is already a well-known problem in financial markets.

Prediction markets add a new layer because they do not trade only the market reaction to an event, but the event itself. For example, the question is not only whether a stock will rise after the news, but whether an attack will happen, whether there will be a ceasefire or whether a political decision will be made by a certain date. That is much more sensitive. If someone knows that a specific military action will happen and takes a large position beforehand, such a market can become a publicly visible trace of confidential information.

## Blockchain transparency has another side too

The crypto community often highlights blockchain transparency as a major advantage. Transactions are visible, data can be analyzed and suspicious patterns can be detected. In many cases, this really helps track manipulation and money flows. But in sensitive geopolitical events, transparency can become a weakness. If analysts can see unusual patterns before an attack, foreign intelligence services can see them too. If a large number of precise forecasts appears before a military action, an adversary may conclude that something is being prepared.

This is where prediction markets move from the zone of financial risk into the zone of national security. With financial risk, someone can lose money. With security risk, someone can change military plans, move equipment or endanger human lives.

## The problem is not only one platform

Although this story mostly mentions crypto prediction markets such as Polymarket, the problem is not tied to only one platform. The entire category of forecasting markets is growing quickly, and as markets grow, so does the motivation for abuse.

The more sensitive the questions are, the greater the risks become. Markets dealing with elections, regulatory decisions or economic data are already sensitive. But markets dealing with war, attacks, the death of political leaders or ceasefires belong to a completely different category. That is why regulators will probably increasingly examine where the line is between a useful forecasting market and a dangerous market for confidential information.

## Should forecasts about war be banned?

One of the key questions now is whether markets related to active military operations should be allowed at all. Prediction markets can have value when they measure expectations about the economy, technology or politics. But when they forecast the outcome of war, an attack or the death of a political leader, the situation becomes much more serious.

Such markets can create an uncomfortable incentive: someone’s tragedy becomes someone else’s trading opportunity. It becomes even more dangerous if a person with privileged information can profit before the event happens.

It is possible that platforms will have to restrict or remove sensitive market categories. Another possibility is stricter monitoring of suspicious patterns, connected accounts and unusually high success rates. But even then, the problem remains: if the signal is already visible on the blockchain, perhaps it has already been seen by everyone who knows how to read it.

## Why these forecasts still have market value

On the other hand, it cannot be ignored that geopolitics has a strong impact on financial markets. War, ceasefires, sanctions, attacks on energy routes or political instability can immediately change the price of oil, gold, stocks, bonds, Bitcoin and the broader crypto market. That is why prediction markets are not worthless. They can show how the market assesses risk before that risk is fully reflected in the prices of traditional assets. If investors already react to geopolitics through stocks, commodities and crypto, then forecasting markets also have their logic: they try to directly measure the probability of an event that can move markets.

The problem, therefore, is not in the idea of assessing geopolitical risks itself. The problem appears when forecasts move close to confidential information, active military operations or events where publicly visible patterns can endanger security.

## Conclusion: Forecasting market or security hole?

Prediction markets are interesting because they try to turn collective expectations into a market price. In an ideal case, they can show how a large group of people estimates the probability of a future event.

But when unusually precise forecasts appear before military actions, the story is no longer harmless. The biggest problem is not only the possibility that someone made money from confidential information. The bigger problem is that such patterns can become a signal that adversaries can also read.

That is why this topic is no longer only a crypto topic. It enters the areas of regulation, geopolitics, intelligence assessments and national security. Crypto markets are no longer an isolated experiment. They increasingly touch the real world — and sometimes its most sensitive parts.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* Prediction markets are not ordinary bets, but in sensitive events they can behave like very dangerous information markets.
* Blockchain transparency can help detect suspicious patterns, but at the same time adversaries can also see those patterns.
* The biggest risk is not only insider trading, but the possibility that the market reveals a signal about real military plans too early.
* Markets related to war outcomes, attacks or the death of political leaders will probably face increasing regulatory pressure.
* This is an example of crypto no longer being only a financial innovation, but entering the area of national security.
