# Bitcoin Pizza Day: the first known purchase of a physical product with BTC

Every year on **May 22**, the crypto community celebrates **Bitcoin Pizza Day**, one of the most famous and most retold moments in Bitcoin history. On that day in 2010, programmer **Laszlo Hanyecz** arranged the purchase of two **Papa John’s pizzas** for **10,000 BTC**. Today, that event is widely remembered as the **first known real-world purchase of a physical product with Bitcoin**.

At first glance, the story is often reduced to a joke about the “most expensive pizza in history.” But that is a very shallow way to look at the moment. Laszlo Hanyecz did not simply spend Bitcoin. He did something much more important: he showed that Bitcoin could move out of theory, forums, code and technical discussions — and become a medium of exchange in the real world.

It is important to be precise. Laszlo did not pay the pizzeria directly with Bitcoin. He posted on the **BitcoinTalk** forum offering 10,000 BTC to anyone who would order two pizzas for him. Someone accepted the offer, paid for the pizzas with regular money and had them delivered, while Laszlo sent Bitcoin in return. That is exactly why the moment became historic: BTC was publicly used for something real, ordinary and tangible.

## Without Laszlo, Bitcoin might have remained theory for much longer

In Bitcoin’s early days, there were no major exchanges, no ETFs, no institutional investors, no crypto cards, no hardware wallets and no millions of users. Bitcoin was an experiment run by a small group of developers, cypherpunk enthusiasts and people who believed that digital money without central control could work.

But every big idea needs a moment when it stops being just an idea. For Bitcoin, one of those moments was Bitcoin Pizza Day. Before that, there had already been the famous test transaction between **Satoshi Nakamoto** and **Hal Finney**, when Satoshi sent Hal **10 BTC** in January 2009. That was an important technical test, but it was not a purchase. There was no product, no service and no real everyday exchange.

Laszlo’s pizza was different. It was the moment when Bitcoin was first “spent” on a real product. Without early users like him, Bitcoin might have remained an interesting software project for much longer — but not a real economy.

## Laszlo is not a symbol of a mistake, but a symbol of the beginning

Today, it is easy to look back and say that **10,000 BTC** for two pizzas was an unbelievably expensive lunch. At the time, those 10,000 BTC were worth roughly **$25 to $41**. Today, the same amount of Bitcoin would be worth hundreds of millions of dollars, and in some market periods even more.

But that is exactly why we should be fair to Laszlo. If nobody had been willing to spend Bitcoin, how could Bitcoin have proven that it had value at all? If everyone had only held it as an experiment, without any real use, it might never have developed the market dynamics it has today.

**Laszlo Hanyecz** was not the man who “did not know what he had.” He was one of the people who helped show that Bitcoin could function as money. He did not just buy pizza. He helped build the first small bridge between digital money and the real world.

## From two pizzas to a global financial phenomenon

Today, Bitcoin is watched by banks, funds, regulators, governments, companies and millions of retail investors. There are **Bitcoin ETFs**, regulated exchanges, custody providers, mining companies and an entire industry built around digital assets. But the story began much more modestly: one forum, one offer, two pizzas and 10,000 BTC.

That is why Bitcoin Pizza Day is not just a nostalgic crypto anecdote. It is a reminder that major technologies often begin in strange, imperfect and almost funny ways. The internet once looked like a toy to many people. Online shopping looked risky. Mobile phones looked like luxury items. Bitcoin looked like an experiment without a real future.

And then comes a moment when someone takes the first practical step. That is exactly what Laszlo did for Bitcoin.

## The day that celebrates use, not just price

Bitcoin Pizza Day should not only be the day when people calculate how much 10,000 BTC would be worth today. That is too simple. The real point is not only the price, but the use. Laszlo’s purchase showed that Bitcoin could have market value because someone was willing to accept it in exchange for a real product.

That is the true strength of this story. Bitcoin did not become important only because its price later went up. It became important because people started to believe that digital money could have a real function, a real market and real-world use. Bitcoin Pizza Day is therefore not just a funny anecdote from the early crypto days, but a symbol of the moment when a technological idea first touched everyday life.

That is why **Laszlo Hanyecz** should not be remembered only as the man who bought the “most expensive pizza in history,” but as one of the people who helped give Bitcoin real life.
