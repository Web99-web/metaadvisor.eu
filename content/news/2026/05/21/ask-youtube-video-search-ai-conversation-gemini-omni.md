# Google Introduces “Ask YouTube”: Video Search Becomes an AI Conversation, Shorts Gets Gemini Omni

Google continues to change the way users search the internet, and that change is now moving more deeply into YouTube. The new **Ask YouTube** feature is meant to turn traditional video search into a conversation with an AI system. Instead of typing a short keyword and then going through dozens of videos manually, YouTube should be able to understand more complex questions, find relevant videos and build an answer from different video sources.

This means YouTube no longer wants to be only a platform where we search for titles, channels, recipes, reviews or tutorials. Google is increasingly turning it into an AI assistant for video content. Users will be able to ask something more natural and specific, such as how to teach a child to ride a bike or which cozy games to play before bedtime, while YouTube tries to connect Shorts, longer videos and relevant parts of content into a more useful answer.

## YouTube Search Will No Longer Be Just a List of Videos

Until now, YouTube search mostly worked as a combination of keywords, titles, thumbnails, recommendations and algorithmic ranking. Users had to decide for themselves which video was worth opening, which part of the video to watch and whether the answer was even inside. Ask YouTube changes that logic because search becomes more like a conversation with an AI assistant.

If the feature works the way Google is presenting it, users will be able to ask more complex questions and then ask follow-up questions. This is especially important for educational and practical topics. For years, YouTube has been a place where people learn how to repair devices, cook, edit videos, use software, choose products, learn languages or understand financial and technological concepts. Now Google is trying to turn that huge video archive into a conversational system that does not only show videos, but tries to extract an answer from them.

In the first phase, Ask YouTube will not be available to everyone. The feature is being introduced through YouTube’s experimental Premium tools, first for Premium users in the United States on desktop. This is Google’s typical way of testing larger changes: first a limited audience, then a broader rollout if it turns out that users actually use the feature and that the results are not problematic.

## The Big Question for Creators: Who Gets Visibility?

For users, this can be very practical. If AI immediately finds the most useful part of a video and puts together an answer, the viewer saves time. But for creators, a new and very sensitive question opens up: what happens if YouTube AI answers the user before they even click on the video?

Until now, the goal for creators was to get clicks, views, watch time and engagement. If AI search begins summarizing content, creators will also have to think about how their video is understood by an AI system. A title and thumbnail will no longer be enough. The structure of the video, chapters, description, transcript, clarity of explanation and the way the content is organized could become increasingly important.

In other words, YouTube SEO could shift toward something we might call AI optimization for video. If AI chooses which videos it will use to build an answer, creators will have to understand how to make their content clear, recognizable, reliable and useful not only for humans, but also for the system analyzing it.

## Gemini Omni Comes to Shorts and YouTube Create

The second major update is the arrival of **Gemini Omni** in YouTube Shorts Remix and the YouTube Create app. Gemini Omni is Google’s new AI video model that should allow users to remix, stylize and expand short videos more easily.

This means users will be able to take an existing Short and use AI to change it, rework it or turn it into a new visual style. Examples mentioned include styles such as pixel art, anime or horror aesthetics, as well as adding new elements, backgrounds, characters or effects. At first glance, that sounds like a fun creative toy, but in a broader context it is a much bigger strategic move.

YouTube does not want AI video creation to happen only in external apps. Google wants the AI creative process to happen inside the YouTube ecosystem: in Shorts, Remix and YouTube Create tools. That means users do not have to go to another service, generate a video there, download it and then publish it on YouTube. AI creation and publishing are increasingly merging into the same workflow.

## Creativity or Even More AI Slop?

This is where the other side of the story begins. Gemini Omni can help users who do not have technical knowledge, expensive equipment or editing experience. It can enable faster experimentation, visual effects, new ideas and creative remix formats. But the same technology can also lead to a large amount of generic AI content.

The term **AI slop** is already being used more and more often for content that feels quickly generated, without a clear idea, without human work and without real value for the viewer. If YouTube makes AI remixing too easy, the platform could receive even more videos that look technically interesting but do not actually bring anything new. This is a problem YouTube will have to balance carefully.

On one side, the platform wants to encourage creativity and give users new tools. On the other side, it does not want to flood viewers with content that looks like an endless series of meaningless AI effects. That is exactly why the quality of recommendations, labeling of AI content and control over remixing will become very important.

## Protecting Creators From Deepfakes Becomes a Necessary Topic

At the same time, YouTube is expanding its likeness detection tool, meaning detection of when someone’s image, face or appearance is used in AI content. This feature should help creators aged 18 and older more easily recognize when they appear in an AI video without consent.

This is extremely important because AI video tools do not only open creative possibilities, but also the risk of deepfakes, false representation and manipulation. If someone can make a video in which a well-known creator “says” something they never said, or appears in a context where they never were, YouTube must have a mechanism for reporting, reviewing and removing such content.

Google is trying to do two opposite things at the same time: open the door to AI video creation and reduce the risk of abuse. That will not be simple. The more accessible AI tools become, the greater the opportunity for creativity becomes, but also the greater the opportunity for manipulation.

## YouTube Is Becoming an AI Layer Over Video

The biggest change is not only that YouTube is getting a new search feature or a new AI tool for Shorts. The bigger change is that YouTube is increasingly becoming an AI layer over video. Users no longer have to only search for a video. They can ask. They do not have to only watch. They can receive a summary. They do not have to only record. They can remix with AI.

This is part of Google’s broader strategy. AI is being built more deeply into Search, Gmail, shopping, video, creative tools and everyday digital habits. YouTube is especially important because it is one of the largest collections of video knowledge and entertainment in the world. If Google succeeds in turning that archive into a conversational AI system, the way we search for information could change significantly.

For users, this can mean faster answers and easier navigation through an enormous amount of content. For creators, it can mean a new fight for visibility in a world where AI decides which parts of videos appear in an answer. For the platform, it means even greater responsibility: how to label AI content, how to protect original creators, how to prevent deepfakes and how to preserve content quality.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **Ask YouTube could make it easier for users to find answers in videos**, especially in educational, practical and tutorial content.
* **For creators, a new visibility question opens up**, because AI can summarize content before the user clicks on the original video.
* **Gemini Omni in Shorts can encourage creativity**, but it can also increase the amount of generic AI content.
* **Likeness protection and deepfake detection are becoming necessary**, because AI video tools can easily misuse someone’s appearance or identity.
* **YouTube is increasingly becoming an AI assistant for video**, and that could change the way we search, watch and create digital content.
