# Estonian Kriptomat Is Closing Its Platform: Customers Can Move to Kraken as the MiCA Licensing Question Remains Open

Estonian crypto platform Kriptomat has announced that it is gradually winding down its business and will cease regulated activities by 30 June 2026. Customers have two options: they can withdraw their EUR and crypto balances, or transfer their assets to Kraken through an agreed transition process.

It is important to make one thing clear from the start: according to Kriptomat’s official statements, the reason for the closure is not a hack, the disappearance of customer assets, insolvency or a classic crypto collapse. The platform says customer funds are safe, accessible and ready for withdrawal.

The main reason cited by Kriptomat is regulatory uncertainty around the MiCA license. The company had been operating under the Estonian regulatory framework, but concluded that the unclear timeline of the process was no longer compatible with a sustainable and transparent continuation of the business.

## What Did Kriptomat Announce?

Kriptomat said it is no longer accepting new customers and that existing users can settle their assets before the deadline in one of two ways: by withdrawing their funds or by transferring them to Kraken. In this way, the platform is trying to carry out an orderly exit without panic and without uncertainty around customer assets.

This news is therefore very different from earlier crypto collapses such as Celsius, BlockFi or Voyager. In those cases, the issue was a loss of trust, liquidity problems and customer funds. In Kriptomat’s case, based on the available information, the central issue is not a security incident but regulatory uncertainty.

## MiCA Was Supposed to Bring Clarity

MiCA, or the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, was introduced as a common European framework for the crypto market. Its goal was to bring order to the sector, protect users and provide clearer rules for exchanges, wallet services and other crypto-asset service providers.

However, the Kriptomat case shows that simply having regulation in place is not enough. If a company must meet strict requirements and operate under a new regime, but does not know when the regulator will process its application, legal certainty remains only partial. One side has deadlines and costs, while the other side does not appear to have an equally visible and predictable timeline.

For a crypto platform, a MiCA license is not just a formality, but a condition for continuing business. Banking relationships, customer support, employees, technology investments and user trust all depend on it. It is also interesting that Kraken received its MiCA license in Ireland from the Central Bank of Ireland, and that this authorization enables it to offer regulated services across all 30 countries of the European Economic Area. That is exactly why this case raises a broader question about the practical efficiency of the MiCA system: regulation can bring more order and security, but only if the licensing approval process is also clear, fast and predictable enough.

## Our View (Metaadvisor)

Kriptomat’s closure does not look like a classic crypto collapse, but rather like a controlled exit caused by regulatory uncertainty. That distinction matters, because according to the official statements, customers have access to their funds and the option to move to Kraken.

This news shows that MiCA is not only a question of user protection, but also a question of administrative efficiency. If the EU requires crypto platforms to have licenses, deadlines, documentation and accountability, then the bodies deciding on those licenses should also have clear deadlines and a comparable level of accountability.

Regulation can be useful, but only if it is predictable. If it is not, it hits smaller market participants the hardest, while large players have enough money, lawyers and time to survive long procedures. That does not automatically mean MiCA deliberately benefits large players, but cases like this show that the result in practice can move in exactly that direction.
