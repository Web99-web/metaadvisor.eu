# Are CME and ICE Afraid of Hyperliquid? Wall Street Is Watching the Rise of On-Chain Trading More Closely

Bloomberg reported on May 15 that CME Group and ICE — the owner of the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) — have allegedly expressed concerns to US regulators about the growth of Hyperliquid and similar decentralized perpetual trading platforms. According to the report, major Wall Street players fear that offshore and anonymous on-chain derivatives trading could create risks related to market manipulation, sanctions evasion and market surveillance.

Traditional financial institutions are reportedly becoming increasingly concerned that decentralized perpetual platforms are moving into areas historically dominated by classic financial markets.

## Why is oil suddenly part of the story?

One of the more interesting aspects of Bloomberg’s report involves energy derivatives and global price discovery. Traditional exchanges such as CME and ICE have played a central role in oil and commodity price formation for decades.

Meanwhile, decentralized perpetual platforms now offer:
- 24/7 leverage trading
- global market access
- trading without traditional brokers
- and extremely fast on-chain liquidity infrastructure.

If enough trading volume moves toward these systems, part of global price discovery could gradually shift outside traditional financial institutions. Many observers believe this is the real reason Wall Street is becoming nervous.

## Hyperliquid is no longer a small niche project

Over the past few months, Hyperliquid has become one of the most closely watched projects in the crypto industry thanks to large trading volumes, fast execution and a growing community of professional traders. Additional attention came from the recent connection between the Hyperliquid ecosystem, Coinbase and Circle’s USDC stablecoin infrastructure. Many see this as a sign that the project is slowly entering a more institutional phase.

Unlike traditional centralized exchanges, a large part of the activity happens fully on-chain. Wallets are visible, liquidation events are public and large positions can be tracked almost in real time.

Many people in the crypto community therefore argue that certain aspects of DeFi markets are actually more transparent than traditional financial systems.

## Hyperliquid responds to Wall Street

After reports about regulatory pressure emerged, Hyperliquid responded through its Policy Center and described concerns about transparency and manipulation as “unfounded concerns.” The project argues that blockchain technology actually provides greater transparency because transactions are publicly visible and permanently recorded on-chain.

In other words, Hyperliquid believes the issue may not be transparency itself, but rather the fact that financial markets are changing and decentralized systems no longer fully depend on traditional financial institutions.

Critics, however, continue to warn that full decentralization creates additional concerns related to:
- regulatory oversight
- anonymous leverage trading
- sanctions compliance
- and user protection.

## TradFi versus DeFi?

The entire situation increasingly resembles a broader conflict between traditional financial markets and decentralized blockchain systems. A few years ago, many major financial institutions did not take decentralized perpetual platforms seriously. Today, that attitude appears to be changing rapidly.

The fact that CME and ICE are now being mentioned in the context of Hyperliquid is, for many people, proof of how large the project has become. One well-known quote is also appearing more frequently in crypto discussions:

“First they ignore you. Then they laugh at you. Then they fight you.”

## Maybe this is not about transparency — but control

Many observers believe transparency itself is not the real issue. Blockchain markets are often more transparent than traditional systems because wallet activity, leverage and liquidation events can be publicly tracked.

However, decentralized trading systems operate globally, 24 hours a day and without traditional gatekeepers. If enough liquidity and trading volume move onto these platforms, part of the control over global price discovery could gradually shift away from Wall Street. Control over market infrastructure has historically been one of the most important forms of power in the financial world.
