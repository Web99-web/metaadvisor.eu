# Bitcoin Falls Below $78,000: Bear Trap or Start of a Bigger Drop?

Bitcoin dropped below the $78,000 level on Saturday, reaching its lowest point since the beginning of May. Although traders had been discussing the possibility of a pullback for several days, many expected stronger downside pressure to arrive later in the month.

Instead, the market reacted suddenly to a combination of geopolitical tensions, rising oil prices and fears of a new inflation wave. Bitcoin briefly fell toward $77,600, while major altcoins such as Solana and XRP lost around 5% of their value.

## More Than $500 Million in Liquidations

According to market data, more than $500 million worth of long positions were liquidated within a short period of time. This means many traders were overly optimistic and used leverage while expecting the market rally to continue.

When the market suddenly moves downward, exchanges automatically close such positions to cover losses. These forced liquidations often accelerate price declines even further and create a snowball effect. What makes the situation especially interesting is that some analysts do not believe this marks the beginning of a major bear market, but rather a possible “bear trap” scenario.

## What Is a Bear Trap?

A bear trap is a situation where the market falls sharply and convinces a large number of traders that an even bigger crash is coming. As a result, many open short positions or panic sell their assets. However, if the market suddenly reverses upward afterward, short traders are forced to close their positions, which can trigger a rapid price recovery.

Many crypto traders are now closely watching funding rates on the futures market, which recently turned negative. This suggests that a large portion of market participants are currently betting on further downside for Bitcoin.

## Iran, Oil and New Inflation Fears

The background behind the latest decline is not limited to crypto alone. Over the past few days, investors have once again been closely monitoring tensions surrounding Iran and the Strait of Hormuz — one of the world’s most important routes for global oil supply. Reports about possible new restrictions and rising tensions in the region pushed US WTI oil prices back above $100 per barrel.

Such a surge in energy prices immediately raises inflation concerns again. When energy becomes more expensive, transportation, production and food costs usually rise as well. Because of this, many analysts are already comparing the current environment to 2022, when markets experienced a major inflation shock.

## The FED Is Back in Focus

The FED (Federal Reserve — the central bank of the United States) is once again at the center of investor attention. Markets fear that a new inflation wave could delay potential interest rate cuts. Bitcoin and the broader crypto market benefited heavily in recent years from high liquidity and cheap money. That is why investors are now carefully watching every signal that could point toward a more aggressive monetary policy once again.

At the same time, some investors still believe in a long-term bullish scenario due to institutional interest, Bitcoin ETFs and continued accumulation by large wallets.

## A Market Between Fear and Hope

The crypto market currently finds itself caught between two completely different narratives. Some believe a new risk-off cycle and a deeper correction have already begun, while others see the current decline as nothing more than short-term panic before a possible continuation of the uptrend.

For ordinary users, moments like this are often among the hardest parts of the crypto market because sentiment can completely shift within just a few hours. Over the next several days, traders will closely watch whether Bitcoin can stabilize above the $77,000–$78,000 zone or whether selling pressure will continue to intensify.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

- This Bitcoin drop was not caused only by crypto market conditions, but also by broader geopolitical and macroeconomic fears.
- The Strait of Hormuz and rising oil prices once again show how connected crypto markets have become to the global economy.
- Massive long liquidations accelerated the sell-off and increased panic among traders.
- Negative funding rates suggest the market is becoming aggressively bearish, which can sometimes also create conditions for a sharp short squeeze.
- The next few days could become very important for the direction of the entire crypto market.
