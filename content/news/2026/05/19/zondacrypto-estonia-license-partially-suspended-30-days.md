# Zondacrypto in Estonia gets 30 days to comply: exchange operator’s license partially suspended

The story around the **Zondacrypto** exchange has entered a new regulatory phase. After earlier problems involving user withdrawals, an inaccessible cold wallet and an investigation in Poland, Estonia has now stepped in as well, through its **Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)**. The regulator has partially suspended the operating license of **BB Trade Estonia OÜ**, the company behind the Zondacrypto brand.

This does not mean the exchange has immediately lost its license completely, nor does it mean the regulator has ordered the platform to shut down immediately. But it does mean that Zondacrypto has entered a serious supervisory phase: it cannot onboard new users, cannot accept new funds and must prove within **30 days** that it meets the requirements to continue operating under its Estonian license.

## What exactly did Estonia decide?

According to the Estonian FIU’s decision, BB Trade Estonia OÜ is not allowed to accept additional assets from existing clients, including fiat money and crypto assets, and it is not allowed to enter into business relationships with new clients. Existing users, at least according to the regulatory decision, must still be allowed to withdraw their funds.

In other words, this is a regulatory stop signal for new money and new clients, but not a full blockage of withdrawals. That is why the wording **partial license suspension** matters: the exchange still has room to respond, but the regulator has clearly signaled that legal requirements must be met if the license is to be retained.

If that does not happen within 30 days, the Estonian FIU must, under the law, move toward a full license revocation. That is a very serious signal because this is no longer only a reputation problem or user anxiety, but an official regulatory measure inside the European Union.

## The shadow of the earlier affair: 4,500 BTC and the investigation in Poland

The new Estonian decision does not come out of nowhere. Zondacrypto has been under pressure for weeks because of the earlier story involving a cold wallet allegedly holding around **4,500 BTC**, with access to it unclear or disputed. At the same time, Polish authorities launched an investigation after users reported that they could not access their funds.

That story raised key questions that are now becoming even more important: who actually controls the keys, where user funds are held, how safe the custody model of centralized exchanges really is, and what happens when users can no longer normally access their own assets.

One especially problematic issue is that the public often mixes up **crypto networks** and **crypto exchanges**. Bitcoin as a network can continue operating normally, but if a user holds funds on a centralized platform, they do not control their own private keys. The exchange does. That is why cases like this bring back the well-known crypto phrase: **not your keys, not your coins**.

## MiCA, the TeamPL token and new rules for European exchanges

Another problem for Zondacrypto is the broader regulatory framework in Europe. The Estonian decision comes at a time when the entire European Union is preparing more seriously for the implementation of the **Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA)**, the EU regulation for crypto-asset markets.

Earlier concerns also appeared around the **TeamPL** token, for which Estonian supervision issued a warning because the required white paper was allegedly not available. In a MiCA environment, such issues are no longer just administrative details, but can become serious regulatory problems. For exchanges, this means it will no longer be enough to have a known brand, users, an app and marketing. Regulators will increasingly look at who issues tokens, what documentation exists, how funds are held, who has access to wallets, how risks are managed and whether it can be proven that user assets actually exist and can be accessed.

## For users, the key question is: can they withdraw their funds?

At this point, the most important question for users is not only whether Zondacrypto will keep its license, but whether users can normally withdraw their funds. According to the regulatory decision, the partial suspension should not prevent refunds or withdrawals for existing clients, but the fact that the regulator had to intervene shows how serious the situation has become.

If users cannot access their funds, the problem is no longer just compliance, but potentially a matter of financial damage, criminal responsibility, and cross-border legal action and cooperation. This is especially sensitive because the story stretches across several jurisdictions: Poland, Estonia, Israel and the broader European regulatory framework.

For crypto users, this is another reminder that centralized exchanges are not the same as self-custody. An exchange can look professional, have advertising, sponsorships, an app and many users, but if users do not hold their own keys, their assets depend on the business operations, internal controls and solvency of that platform.

## This is no longer just a local problem of one exchange

The Zondacrypto case is increasingly turning into a broader European story about regulation, supervision and accountability for centralized crypto platforms. Poland is conducting an investigation, Estonia is responding through the license, and the European MiCA framework is becoming more important for every exchange that wants to operate legally in the European Union market.

What makes this story especially important is the fact that this is not a small anonymous project, but a known regional exchange that has been present in Central and Eastern Europe for years. That is exactly why this case could have a much larger impact than an ordinary local incident.

If Zondacrypto can prove compliance within 30 days and stabilize the situation, the story could calm down. But if the problems continue, the partial license suspension may only be a transitional phase toward a much more serious regulatory outcome.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* A **partial license suspension** is not the same as a full operating ban, but it is a very serious regulatory signal.
* Zondacrypto now has **30 days** to comply; if it fails to do so, it may lose its license.
* The earlier story involving **4,500 BTC** and an inaccessible cold wallet remains the central shadow over the entire case.
* MiCA is changing the rules of the game in Europe, and exchanges will find it increasingly difficult to operate without clear documentation, risk controls and transparency.
* For users, the most important question remains simple: can they safely withdraw their funds?
* This case once again shows the difference between holding crypto on a centralized exchange and real self-custody.
