# EU investigates Meta algorithms and “dark patterns”: users speak about digital erasure

Ireland’s media regulator has opened two new investigations into Meta related to the recommendation systems that promote content on Facebook and Instagram.

The investigations will assess whether Meta has breached the EU Digital Services Act (DSA).

What is particularly interesting is that the investigations are now increasingly expanding toward recommendation algorithms, AI-driven feeds and so-called “dark patterns” systems that may influence user behaviour, attention and visibility online. At the same time, the removal of users from platforms, AI moderation mistakes and sudden digital “erasure” of users still remain in a kind of grey zone that regulators rarely mention directly or seriously open as a topic.

## Why is Ireland once again in focus?

It is particularly interesting that the key investigations are once again being led from Ireland — a country that for years served as the European headquarters for almost every major tech company including Meta, Google, TikTok and X.

Regulators are now investigating whether Meta uses manipulative UX design to make it harder for users to choose a feed that is not based on profiling and AI personalization.

In practice, this means that the EU is no longer looking only at illegal content, but at the very architecture of social platforms:

* recommendation algorithms
* AI-driven feeds
* autoplay systems
* infinite scroll
* engagement mechanics
* “dark patterns”
* the psychology of keeping users’ attention.

In other words, the question is no longer only:

> “Should a platform remove certain content?”

but rather:

> “How much do platforms and algorithms control visibility, behaviour and even the digital existence of users?”

## More and more users report AI moderation problems

This becomes especially interesting at a time when more and more users across Europe report:

* sudden AI moderation bans
* shadow restrictions
* account shutdowns
* support problems
* and nearly impossible appeal processes.

At MetaAdvisor, we have already written multiple times about AI moderation problems and the fact that many users feel completely lost inside a system where they can no longer reach real human support — neither from the platforms nor often from EU regulators. For them, part of the digital world has become locked away, which is especially difficult after Covid, when much of the world moved almost entirely online. What then happens to their rights to equality and access to real-time information that are now effectively blocked?

## DSA and Appeals Centre Europe under criticism

Many users believe that DSA mechanisms often fail to provide meaningful answers in practice, while Appeals Centre Europe decisions are non-binding — meaning platforms can ignore both users and the systems that were publicly presented as protection mechanisms. To many people, the protection appears effective only “on paper”.

What is the purpose of protection on paper? What can people who spent years building trust and presence online do with a document confirming they were removed without reason, while they still cannot reach any real human support, email support or meaningful communication from the platforms? The platforms continue to ignore them — just as they appear to ignore EU Appeals Centre Europe as well. What is the purpose of decisions that do not obligate anyone? Simply to place some people on EU salaries?

## The “digital disappearance” of users

Perhaps the most interesting part of the entire story is the feeling more and more people describe — that they simply “disappear” digitally without explanation. One moment everything is normal, and the next second the platform disconnects them and that is — the end.

Many critics believe the entire system is particularly disturbing precisely because it can practically silence users within seconds without any explanation. Once they lose access to their account, visibility or reach, they often no longer even have a way to inform their own network of friends, followers or business contacts about what happened.

When a platform limits an account, reduces visibility or suspends a user, friends and colleagues often do not even notice for months what happened. Their voice has been silenced and their access to today’s digital universe restricted. The user simply disappears from feeds, recommendations and communication.

## Are platforms still “neutral tools”?

This is exactly why some critics argue that today’s platforms are no longer neutral technological tools, but systems that actively control attention, visibility and people’s social presence online. Many people already refer to them as a kind of “utility”.

At the same time, the EU is attempting to establish global rules for AI and digital platforms before the United States, raising another question:

> Is this primarily about protecting users — or about a new regulatory and political battle against Big Tech?

## 2026 could become a turning point

Regardless of the answer, one thing is clear:

2026 could become the year when serious discussions finally begin not only about content moderation, but about who truly controls digital visibility and digital existence on the internet.

## Our view (Metaadvisor)

* AI moderation and recommendation algorithms now have enormous influence over user visibility online.
* More and more people report a feeling of digital “disappearance” without any clear explanation.
* Many users see DSA and Appeals Centre Europe as weak or ineffective protection mechanisms.
* The EU is increasingly investigating not only content itself, but also the architecture of algorithms and platforms.
* The question of digital visibility could become one of the biggest tech and social issues of the coming years.
