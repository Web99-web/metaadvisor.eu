# ShinyHunters hacked again: this time Canvas used by millions of students and schools was hit

The hacker group ShinyHunters has been extremely active in recent months and appears to be operating across a surprisingly large number of institutions and global cyber incidents — and those are likely only the cases that become public. Metaadvisor.eu has already covered several stories connected to this group, including:

- [EU hacked through a security tool: how a single download opened the door to an attack](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/eu-hack-trufflehog-supply-chain-attack/)
- [GTA (Grand Theft Auto) under hacker attack: Rockstar Games given until April 14 to pay](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/gta-rockstar-hack-shinyhunters-supply-chain-attack/)

This time the target is **Canvas** — one of the world’s largest education platforms used by schools, colleges and universities globally. The incident caused days of outages, login page defacements and growing concern over the possible theft of enormous amounts of sensitive student data.

According to reports from major media outlets including The Guardian, Reuters and AP News, the attack affected **Instructure**, the company behind the Canvas platform.

## Allegedly stolen data connected to hundreds of millions of users

According to information published by media outlets and cybersecurity analysts, ShinyHunters claims to have stolen as much as 3.6 TB of data connected to around 275 million students, staff members and users from approximately 9,000 schools and educational institutions worldwide.

The compromised data allegedly includes:
- email addresses
- student identification numbers
- usernames
- internal messages
- information connected to educational systems.

The attack also had visible real-world consequences. Some schools and universities were reportedly forced to extend assignment deadlines because students could not access the platform.

Particular attention was drawn to Instructure’s wording when the company stated that it had reached an “agreement with the unauthorized actor.” Security experts believe this could be an indirect indication that a ransom payment may have been made, although the company has not officially confirmed this.

## The “data returned” wording raised new questions

According to The Guardian, the hackers threatened to publish the stolen data if the company refused to negotiate. Instructure later stated that the data had been “returned” as part of the agreement with the attackers.

That specific wording triggered significant discussion inside the cybersecurity community. Many companies never publicly admit paying ransomware or extortion groups and instead use phrases such as:
- “situation resolved”
- “systems restored”
- “agreement reached”
- “data secured”.

## ShinyHunters are appearing again in major global incidents

ShinyHunters has been one of the most recognizable names in cybercrime for years. The group has previously been linked to attacks targeting technology companies, the gaming industry and cloud services.

In recent months, the name has resurfaced again in connection with multiple major security incidents, including:
- attacks connected to EU-related infrastructure
- the Rockstar Games and GTA incident
- cloud and SaaS attacks
- identity and phishing campaigns.

Interestingly, some security analysts have recently started comparing ShinyHunters tactics with methods used by other well-known cybercrime groups such as Scattered Spider and Lapsus$.

## Education systems are becoming a new major target

This incident shows how centralized and vulnerable modern education and cloud systems have become. When a single platform becomes critical infrastructure for millions of users, the consequences of a security breach are no longer just a technical problem.

More schools, universities and institutions now depend on a small number of major SaaS and cloud platforms. That transforms cyberattacks from local incidents into global disruptions capable of affecting education systems across multiple countries at the same time.

# Our view (Metaadvisor):

- Major cyberattacks are increasingly targeting not only banks and corporations, but also the education sector.
- The phrase “data returned” sounds highly unusual and many interpret it as a possible sign of a ransom payment.
- Cloud and SaaS platforms are becoming central points of risk for millions of users.
- Large platforms often communicate very cautiously and vaguely during incidents like these.
- Attacks of this type could further increase pressure for stricter cybersecurity and digital infrastructure regulation.
