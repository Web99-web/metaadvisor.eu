# Malta and OpenAI Launch Free ChatGPT Plus for Citizens: Beginning of a New AI Era?

OpenAI and Malta have launched one of the most interesting AI projects so far — Maltese citizens will be able to receive free ChatGPT Plus access, but only after completing a special government-backed AI education program. It is the first partnership of this kind between a country and OpenAI, and many already see it as the possible beginning of an entirely new model of national AI strategy.

According to information released by OpenAI and Maltese authorities, all citizens who complete the official AI literacy course will receive one year of free access to ChatGPT Plus. The education program was developed together with the University of Malta and focuses on the basics of artificial intelligence, practical AI usage, and understanding both its capabilities and limitations.

In other words, Malta does not simply want to “give away an AI tool” to citizens, but first wants to teach people how to use artificial intelligence responsibly and effectively in everyday life and at work.

## AI Education as the New Digital Literacy

Maltese Minister for Economy Silvio Schembri stated that Malta does not want its citizens to fall behind in the digital age and that the goal is to transform artificial intelligence from an “unfamiliar concept” into a practical tool for families, students and workers.

Although the story may initially sound like a simple ChatGPT promotion campaign, many believe this is actually a much larger experiment. Malta is a relatively small country with just over half a million residents, making it an ideal testing ground for a project like this. Because of that, some analysts believe OpenAI is not only testing the popularity of ChatGPT, but also how citizens use AI when it becomes freely accessible, how AI affects daily life and work, how many people are willing to complete AI education programs, and how governments can integrate AI into public services and education systems.

In other words, Malta could become the first real national-scale “AI sandbox.”

## OpenAI for Countries

What makes the project especially interesting is that it comes through the “OpenAI for Countries” initiative, where OpenAI aims to cooperate with governments on national AI strategies. Instead of offering one universal model for every country, OpenAI reportedly wants to tailor its approach to each nation’s priorities — from education and workforce development to public services.

This is not the company’s first move connected to education. Last year, OpenAI also partnered with Estonia on AI tools for schools and teachers. However, Malta is the first country to go a step further and directly connect AI education with access to premium AI services for citizens.

## A Massive PR Experiment or the Start of a New AI Policy?

Naturally, the project is already raising many questions. Some wonder whether governments should promote a single private AI company, what happens with user privacy, whether citizens could become dependent on one AI ecosystem, and what will happen once the free access period ends. Others are asking where European AI alternatives fit into the bigger picture.

At the same time, supporters argue that this is a logical step because artificial intelligence could soon become a basic tool for education, work and everyday communication — much like the internet or smartphones did over the past two decades.

Another interesting detail is that Malta represents a relatively “low-cost” experiment for OpenAI. For a company of OpenAI’s size, offering ChatGPT Plus access to a small country is not an enormous expense. In return, OpenAI gains massive global PR exposure, real-world data about AI usage on a national scale, political experience working directly with governments, and potentially a blueprint that could later be expanded to other countries. If the initiative proves successful, many believe similar AI programs could soon appear in other smaller European nations as well.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

- Malta is a relatively small country, which makes organizing a project like this far easier than in nations with tens of millions of citizens.
- OpenAI is likely testing far more than simple interest in ChatGPT Plus — including user behavior, AI education and integration of AI into everyday life.
- AI education could eventually become just as important as digital literacy became 10–15 years ago.
- The project raises serious questions about privacy, the role of private AI companies and possible dependence on a single technology ecosystem.
- If Malta succeeds, similar AI programs may soon begin appearing across other European countries.
