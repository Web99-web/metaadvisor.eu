# Bitcoin climbs back above $81,000 as markets watch inflation, the AI race and new money printing

Bitcoin has climbed back above the $81,000 level despite rising inflation, geopolitical tensions and growing nervousness across global markets. After the latest US CPI report showed annual inflation at 3.8%, higher than analysts had expected, bitcoin briefly dropped below $80,000 before recovering very quickly. The dip was aggressively bought and BTC is now trading back in the green above $81,000.

The latest data showed that rising energy prices are once again putting strong pressure on inflation in the United States. A large part of the market is linking the new inflation surge to higher oil prices, the war between the US and Iran and broader global energy tensions. Because of this, investors are once again closely watching the Federal Reserve, possible interest-rate changes and future central bank actions.

## Traditional markets reacted worse than crypto

Despite rising macroeconomic uncertainty, bitcoin is so far showing relative strength. While traditional markets reacted negatively to the latest CPI data, the crypto market stabilized very quickly. The S&P 500 fell 0.2%, while the Nasdaq 100 dropped 0.9%, with the biggest pressure once again hitting technology and semiconductor stocks after several weeks of strong gains.

At the same time, capital continues flowing into the crypto sector. CoinShares reported that global crypto funds recorded inflows of $858 million last week. The largest share of capital flowed into bitcoin products, which absorbed $706 million in new investments. Ether products saw $77 million in inflows, Solana $48 million and XRP around $40 million.

One of the most interesting data points was the $14 million outflow from bitcoin short positions, marking the biggest unwind of bearish bitcoin bets during 2026. This suggests that part of the market is gradually abandoning expectations of a larger bitcoin decline even as inflation and macroeconomic uncertainty continue rising.

## The AI race and money printing are back in focus

Additional attention recently came from comments made by Arthur Hayes, who believes that the AI race between the United States and China, rising military spending and massive investments into data centers and energy infrastructure could lead to even more fiat money printing. According to Hayes, governments will increasingly rely on new debt, bank loans and monetary easing to finance AI infrastructure and national security.

Hayes believes this environment could ultimately benefit crypto and bitcoin because a growing supply of fiat money often increases interest in scarce assets such as BTC. Some analysts believe the market is increasingly beginning to view bitcoin as protection against the long-term debasement of fiat currencies.

Additional optimism also came from the CryptoQuant Bull-Bear Indicator, which turned green again for the first time since 2023. Historically, similar signals have often preceded stronger bullish phases in the crypto market, although analysts warn that no indicator guarantees continued upside.

For now, bitcoin continues to show resilience despite rising inflation, higher energy prices and growing nervousness across global markets. Traders are now closely watching whether BTC can hold key levels above $80,000 while capital continues flowing into crypto and bearish positioning gradually weakens.
