# DeepSeek heads toward a $45 billion valuation as China’s AI war enters a new phase

Chinese AI startup DeepSeek could soon reach a valuation of as much as $45 billion — all during its very first major funding round.

But this is not just another startup story from the tech world.

👉 This is a story about the growing AI race between China and the United States.

DeepSeek became globally known in early 2025 after launching AI models that delivered surprisingly strong results using far less compute power and significantly lower costs than major US rivals like OpenAI and Anthropic.

At a time when the AI industry is spending billions on GPU infrastructure and data centers, DeepSeek demonstrated something that caught the West’s attention:

👉 competitive AI models may be possible without massive amounts of Nvidia hardware.

{{< support1 >}}

## Valuation exploded within weeks

According to multiple reports from Financial Times, Bloomberg and Reuters, DeepSeek’s valuation has risen at an extraordinary pace.

Estimates moved within just a few weeks:

* from around $10 billion
* to $20 billion
* and now potentially toward $45–50 billion

That shows how aggressively investors are searching for AI companies capable of becoming global powerhouses.

## China wants its own AI ecosystem

One of the most important parts of the story is who may be funding the company.

Reports suggest the investment round could be led by China’s state-backed “Big Fund,” officially known as the China Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund.

In other words:

👉 China does not just want an AI company — it wants an AI ecosystem independent from the United States.

That includes:

* domestic AI models
* domestic cloud platforms
* domestic AI chips
* domestic infrastructure

{{< support1 >}}

## Huawei instead of Nvidia

One of the key reasons DeepSeek became so important is that its models are reportedly optimized for Huawei chips.

That matters because US sanctions have made it much harder for China to access the most advanced Nvidia AI GPUs.

If China succeeds in building:

* competitive AI models
* on domestic hardware
* without relying on American technology

👉 it could significantly reshape the global AI balance of power.

That is why many analysts no longer see this as a simple startup story, but rather as part of a broader technological and geopolitical conflict.

## Tencent and Alibaba want in

According to Bloomberg and Reuters, Chinese tech giants:

* Tencent
* Alibaba

are also reportedly involved in investment discussions.

That alone shows how seriously China’s tech industry views DeepSeek.

If the funding round succeeds, DeepSeek could become one of the most valuable AI companies outside the US.

## The AI talent war is becoming brutal

But perhaps the most fascinating part of the story is not the technology — it is the people behind it.

Reports suggest DeepSeek is currently losing researchers as competitors aggressively attempt to poach talent.

{{< support2 >}}

## AI talent may become more valuable than GPUs

Major Chinese companies are reportedly offering:

* massive salaries
* stock ownership
* access to GPU infrastructure
* stronger research environments

The problem is simple: there are not enough real AI experts — not in China, not in the US.

People who understand:

* LLM architecture
* reasoning models
* AI agents
* training optimization
* inference scaling

👉 are now among the most valuable professionals in the world.

Interestingly, DeepSeek reportedly resisted taking venture capital for a long time. Founder Liang Wenfeng is still believed to control nearly 90% of the company.

However, the competition for talent now appears to be forcing the company to open up to outside investors in order to offer shares and ownership incentives to employees.

{{< support1 >}}

## AI is no longer just about chatbots

Over the past year, the AI industry has entered a major transition.

The focus is gradually shifting:

* away from traditional chatbots
* toward AI agents and autonomous systems

In other words:

👉 AI is no longer expected to simply answer questions — but to make decisions, manage workflows and perform tasks autonomously.

That is exactly why DeepSeek matters so much.

If China succeeds in building a powerful AI ecosystem independent of US technology, the consequences could be enormous for:

* Nvidia
* OpenAI
* American cloud companies
* the global AI economy

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

* DeepSeek is no longer just a startup — it is becoming a strategic AI project
* China is clearly trying to reduce dependence on American AI infrastructure
* AI talent may soon become even more important than GPU chips
* The Huawei + DeepSeek combination could become a serious challenge for the West
* The AI war is increasingly turning into a geopolitical and economic conflict
