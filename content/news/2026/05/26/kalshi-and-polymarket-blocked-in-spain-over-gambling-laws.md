# Kalshi and Polymarket blocked in Spain over gambling laws

Spanish authorities have ordered the blocking of Kalshi and Polymarket, two well-known platforms for so-called prediction markets. On these platforms, users can put money on the outcome of future events — from politics and economics to sports, elections, geopolitical crises or other public issues.

According to Spanish media such as El País and a notice from the Ministry of Social Rights, Consumer Affairs and Agenda 2030, the problem is not only that these platforms are connected to the crypto world. The key issue is that Spanish regulators see such services as a form of gambling when users place money on uncertain future outcomes.

{{< support1 >}}

## Why Spain is blocking the platforms

Spain’s gambling regulator, DGOJ, considers that Polymarket and Kalshi are operating without the necessary administrative authorization for gambling services. As a result, legal proceedings have been opened and a temporary block of their websites has been ordered for users in Spain.

In practice, this means the state is not only looking at the technology behind the platform, but also at user behavior. If a user puts money on whether a certain event will or will not happen, the regulator can treat that as betting. And if it is betting, the platform needs a license, user checks, protection for minors and rules that apply to gambling operators.

## Prediction markets or online betting?

In the crypto community, prediction markets are often described as “information markets”. The idea is that the price of a certain outcome shows what the market believes is likely to happen. If people put their own money at risk, the theory says their forecasts may be more serious than ordinary polls or social media comments.

For regulators, however, the matter is much simpler. If money can be won or lost on an uncertain event, it starts to look like gambling. This is exactly where the conflict begins between the crypto narrative and laws that were written long ago in many countries for games of chance and betting.

{{< support2 >}}

## Why this matters for the crypto market

This decision does not matter only for users in Spain. It shows that prediction markets are coming under increasing regulatory pressure, especially in Europe. Platforms like Polymarket became popular because they offer a simple way to trade the outcomes of real-world events, but that popularity is now attracting the attention of authorities.

Reuters reports that this is a temporary measure while the proceedings continue, and the process could last several months. But the block itself already sends a clear message: states will not necessarily wait for prediction markets to regulate themselves. They can quickly place them inside the framework of existing gambling laws.

## The line between innovation and regulation is getting thinner

Kalshi and Polymarket do not look like traditional betting companies. Their language, design and audience feel more modern, more technological and closer to financial markets than to old-style online gambling. But that may not be enough for regulators.

If a platform enables betting on future events, authorities will likely demand the same basic requirements as they do for other forms of gambling: licensing, oversight, user protection and clear rules. For the crypto industry, this means that a new interface and a blockchain narrative will not always be enough to avoid old regulation.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

- Spain’s decision shows that prediction markets are no longer a small crypto niche, but a sensitive regulatory issue.
- Polymarket and Kalshi try to look like information markets, but authorities increasingly treat them like gambling.
- Users should understand that “trading outcomes” can legally be viewed in a similar way to betting on an uncertain event.
- This case could slow the expansion of prediction market platforms in Europe.
- In the long run, the key question will not only be whether these platforms are useful, but whether they can obtain licenses and operate in line with local laws.
