# South Korean card giant Shinhan moves to blockchain: Solana partnership opens path for stablecoin payments

While Europe continues to debate regulation and the risks of digital finance, South Korea is already testing real-world implementations. One of the clearest examples comes from the banking sector, where a major card issuer is moving into blockchain-based payments.

Shinhan Card, the second-largest credit card provider in South Korea, has signed a partnership with the Solana Foundation to test stablecoin payments and explore new DeFi-based financial models.

## One of the biggest players in Korea

Shinhan Card is not a small experiment.

It represents:

* the second-largest credit card issuer in South Korea
* a market share of around 16–17%
* millions of users in everyday financial transactions

In other words, when a player of this scale tests new technology, it signals potential system-level change.

## What they are building

The partnership with Solana goes beyond a symbolic move.

The goals include:

* testing stablecoin payments in real conditions
* developing advanced digital transaction models
* exploring the use of non-custodial wallets

After an initial pilot phase, the project is now moving into a more advanced stage focused on:

* commercial viability
* scalability
* smart contract stability

## Traditional finance meets DeFi

A key objective of the project is to develop a hybrid financial model.

This combines:

* traditional finance (TradFi)
* decentralized finance (DeFi)

In practice, this could enable:

* faster and cheaper payments
* more transparent transactions
* new blockchain-based financial products

## Why Solana

The choice of Solana is not random.

The network offers:

* high-speed transactions
* low transaction costs
* scalability for mass adoption

This is essential if blockchain is to be used for everyday payments rather than just trading.

## Technologies being tested

The project also includes advanced components:

* oracles — systems that connect real-world data with blockchain networks
* smart contracts — automated digital agreements
* testing infrastructure stability and security

The goal is not just experimentation, but real-world usability.

## The bigger picture: Korea is accelerating

This move is not isolated.

South Korea is increasingly investing in:

* blockchain technology
* digital finance
* tokenization of assets

At the same time, other institutions are testing:

* blockchain-based bonds
* digital settlement systems
* new financial market structures

This signals a clear direction:

👉 Korea is not waiting — Korea is testing

## What this means globally

The difference in approach is becoming more visible.

While many countries:

* debate regulation
* analyze risks

Korea is:

* running pilot programs
* testing real-world applications
* building infrastructure

If successful, these models could become global standards.
