# Former Celsius CEO seeks to vacate sentence: FTX, lawyers and a new fight over 12 years in prison

Former Celsius CEO Alex Mashinsky is once again trying to reopen the legal battle around his sentence. After being sentenced to 12 years in prison for fraud and market manipulation, Mashinsky has filed a motion in a US court seeking to vacate his sentence. In the new filing, he claims that he had ineffective legal counsel, that his lawyers stopped communicating with him, and that part of the pressure on Celsius belongs in a wider context involving FTX and former executives from inside the company itself.

This is not just another court note from the crypto world. The Celsius case remains one of the most important examples of how, during the previous crypto cycle, promises of high yields, opaque management, internal tokens, market panic and user trust became dangerously mixed. Many users believed they were using a more modern version of a bank. Now Mashinsky is trying to change the narrative: instead of a story in which Celsius simply misled users, he is trying to present Celsius and himself as part of a larger conflict involving market manipulation and internal power struggles.

## What is Mashinsky asking for now?

Alex Mashinsky was sentenced to 12 years in prison in May 2025 after previously pleading guilty to commodities fraud and securities fraud. The US Department of Justice, or DOJ, said that Mashinsky misled users about the safety of the Celsius platform and manipulated the market for the CEL token. That is an important part of the story because his new request does not come from someone who never admitted wrongdoing, but from a convicted defendant who now argues that the legal process was flawed.

In the new filing, Mashinsky is asking for his 144-month sentence to be vacated. What makes the move especially interesting is that he filed the documents himself, without a lawyer, meaning he is representing himself in court. According to available reports, he claims that he did not formally dismiss his lawyers, but that they stopped communicating with him. For that reason, he says he had to file his reply directly with the court. This is legally sensitive because he is trying to build the argument that he did not have effective legal assistance during a key phase of the case.

## What does “fruit of the poisonous tree” mean?

Another part of his argument refers to the legal concept known as “fruit of the poisonous tree.” This is a doctrine under which evidence may be challenged if it was obtained as a result of unlawful or improper conduct by authorities. In practice, these arguments are not simple. The court must see not only that an irregularity is being alleged, but also whether that irregularity actually affected the evidence, the proceedings or the outcome.

For someone who has already pleaded guilty, the threshold for success is usually very high. That is why Mashinsky’s new move does not automatically mean that the sentence will change. Instead, he is trying to open the door to a new legal debate. The court will have to consider whether there is a real legal basis for vacating the sentence or whether this is an attempt to revisit a process that has already reached judgment.

## Why is FTX being brought back into the story?

The most interesting part of the story for the crypto public is Mashinsky’s attempt to bring FTX and Sam Bankman-Fried back into the case. In the documents, he claims that the former FTX CEO intended to “destroy Celsius” and that FTX played a role in market pressure on the CEL token. That is a serious claim, but at this stage it is important to be clear: this is Mashinsky’s claim in a court filing, not a fact established by a court.

After the collapse of FTX, many earlier crypto stories started to look different. FTX was not just one failed exchange, but a central part of a large crypto ecosystem that influenced liquidity, trust, token prices and the business of other platforms. Still, that does not automatically mean that every defense strategy mentioning FTX can change a sentence. The court will likely look at a very specific question: can the claims about FTX legally change the facts for which Mashinsky was convicted?

## The role of former Celsius insiders

Roni Cohen-Pavon, the former chief revenue officer of Celsius, also appears in the background. Mashinsky claims that Cohen-Pavon attempted a “hostile takeover” of the platform and has submitted messages that, according to his interpretation, support that version of events. But there is another side to the story as well: Cohen-Pavon cooperated with prosecutors, pleaded guilty and was an important witness in the case.

That is why Mashinsky’s attempt to portray his role differently may be interesting for the public, but it may not necessarily be enough for the court. In large business and criminal cases, questions often arise later about who knew what, who made which decisions, who cooperated with prosecutors and who received more favorable treatment. Legally, however, the key question is whether it can be proven that those relationships changed the core of the proceedings.

## Celsius as a lesson about centralized crypto

Celsius was one of the best-known crypto lending platforms before its collapse in 2022. It offered users high yields on crypto deposits and promoted the idea that people could move away from the traditional banking system. The problem began when it became clear that the business model was not as safe and transparent as it had been publicly presented. The platform froze withdrawals, entered bankruptcy and many users lost access to a large part of their assets.

That is why the Celsius case matters far beyond the courtroom. It shows how, in crypto, an illusion of safety can often be created through big words: community, decentralization, yield, financial freedom and a new banking system. But when a platform actually functions as a centralized intermediary managing other people’s funds, the risk has not disappeared — it has only changed form. Users may not have been dealing with a traditional bank, but they still had to trust one company, its executives, internal decisions and risky strategies.

## What does this story mean for the crypto industry?

Mashinsky’s new move raises an uncomfortable question: how many of the crypto collapses of 2022 were the result of individual fraud cases, and how many were the result of an entire ecosystem in which platforms, tokens, exchanges and lending models were far more deeply connected than users understood? FTX, Celsius, BlockFi, Three Arrows Capital and other cases showed that risk spread through the system like a chain. When one major player falls, trust, liquidity and token prices can collapse very quickly.

Even if FTX had an impact on the broader market, that does not automatically erase Celsius’s responsibility toward its own users. That is the key difference. It is one thing to say that the market was chaotic and that other actors made the situation worse. It is another thing to prove that Mashinsky was not responsible for the statements, business decisions and manipulation to which he has already pleaded guilty. The court will likely focus on exactly that boundary.

For the crypto industry, this story comes at an interesting moment. On one side, the market is trying to rebuild trust through regulated products, ETFs, institutional investors and clearer rules. On the other side, old cases from the previous cycle continue to return through court proceedings, appeals, bankruptcy distributions and attempts to rewrite responsibility. It is a reminder that a “new market” cannot simply forget old wounds.

## Why does this matter for ordinary users?

For ordinary users, the message is simple: high yields are never free. If a platform promises to pay users above-average returns, the first questions should always be: where does that money come from, who manages the risk, who controls the funds and what happens if the market suddenly falls?

Celsius showed that a “crypto bank” without clear rules, transparency and real oversight can end worse than the bank it was supposedly meant to replace. That is why this story is not only about Mashinsky, FTX or one court filing. It is a reminder that trust in crypto should not be built only on big names, marketing slogans and promises of yield.

## Our view (Metaadvisor):

* Mashinsky’s motion is legally interesting, but for now it does not change the basic fact that he previously pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 12 years in prison.
* The mention of FTX may matter for the broader context of the 2022 market chaos, but it does not automatically mean that Celsius had no responsibility of its own.
* The claims about ineffective counsel and “fruit of the poisonous tree” will likely be examined very strictly by the court, especially because this involves someone who has already entered a guilty plea.
* For crypto users, this is another reminder that centralized lending platforms are not the same as a safe bank, even when they use words such as community, yield and financial freedom.
* The most important lesson is not to accept any narrative without checking it: not the official one, not the defense narrative, and not the one that tries to move all blame onto FTX.
