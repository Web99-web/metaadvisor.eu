# Bitcoin Fell Toward $74,000 Yesterday Despite Pro-Crypto Fed Chair: Why Markets Are Watching Yields

Bitcoin slipped toward the $74,000 area yesterday, reaching levels around $74,289 on some spot markets. The decline came after BTC failed to hold above the $80,000 level, while U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded significant capital outflows.

At first glance, the situation looks contradictory. Kevin Warsh, known for more favorable views toward Bitcoin and private financial innovation, has taken over the Federal Reserve. Yet the market is not only watching whether the new Fed chair is pro-crypto. It is watching interest rates, bond yields and liquidity.

## What Happened to Bitcoin?

Bitcoin tried in May to hold the psychologically important $80,000 level, but failed to do so. After that, the price began sliding into lower ranges, and investors became more cautious.

The move toward $74,000 does not by itself mean the trend has collapsed. But it does show that the market currently lacks enough strength to continue the rally. When price fails to hold an important level, short-term traders often exit positions and wait for a new confirmation of direction.

## ETF Outflows Strengthened Negative Sentiment

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded significant outflows over the past two weeks. According to market data, more than $2 billion has been pulled from these funds, quickly strengthening the narrative that Bitcoin demand is weakening.

ETFs matter because they have become one of the main bridges between traditional finance and Bitcoin. When capital flows into ETFs, it is read as a positive signal. When capital flows out, the market quickly flips the story: investors are cooling off, and the price may remain under pressure.

## Why Was a Pro-Crypto Fed Chair Not Enough?

Warsh’s earlier pro-crypto views may be positive for the institutional framework in the long run, but they do not automatically mean lower interest rates. For Bitcoin, the more important question right now is whether monetary policy will become easier or tighter.

If the market believes the Fed will not cut rates quickly, or that another hike could even become possible, risk assets remain under pressure. Bitcoin is no exception, even if the new Fed chair may be more open to crypto innovation.

## Bond Yields Are Driving the Story Right Now

The U.S. 2-year Treasury yield is especially important. Markets often use this yield as a signal of expectations for short-term interest rates.

When short-term yields rise, investors have less reason to move into assets that do not produce income, such as Bitcoin. BTC has no coupon, no dividend and no regular cash flow. That is why it is sensitive to more expensive money and weaker liquidity.

## Santiment Sees a Possible Contrarian Signal

Santiment argues that ETF outflows do not necessarily have to be only a negative signal. In its interpretation, such outflows often show retail investor fatigue more than the exit of long-term capital.

This is known as a contrarian signal. When small investors lose patience and exit after a failed rally, more patient capital sometimes starts seeing lower prices as an accumulation opportunity. Of course, this does not mean Bitcoin must immediately rise. But it does mean panic should not automatically be read as the end of the trend.

## Capital May Be Rotating Into Other Big Stories

Another possibility is that capital is not fleeing risk altogether, but rotating into a different kind of risk. Markets constantly rotate between narratives: Bitcoin, AI, space companies, commodities, equities and pre-IPO stories.

If investors expect major opportunities around SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic or other technology themes, part of the speculative capital may temporarily move out of Bitcoin. That does not mean the crypto story is over. It means the market is searching for the next strongest magnet for capital.

## What Should Investors Watch Next?

The three most important things to watch are ETF flows, bond yields and Fed expectations. If ETF outflows slow down or turn back into inflows, the market could receive a new positive signal.

If yields calm down, Bitcoin may also find it easier to form a bottom. But if short-term yields continue rising and the market begins to price in a stricter Fed more seriously, BTC can remain under pressure regardless of the pro-crypto political tone.

## Conclusion: Bitcoin Is Not Falling Because of One Single Headline

Bitcoin is not currently falling for just one reason. The decline is a combination of ETF outflows, rising yields, weaker liquidity, shifting expectations around the Fed and possible capital rotation into other major stories.

Warsh taking over the Fed may be important for the crypto sector in the long run, but in the short term the market is watching monetary conditions more than personal views. Pro-crypto does not automatically mean bullish if interest rates and yields are working against risk assets.

Santiment’s thesis that ETF outflows may act as a contrarian signal is therefore interesting, but it should be read carefully. Outflows may indicate retail panic and possible accumulation, but they do not erase macro risks.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* A pro-crypto Fed chair is not enough if the market also expects more expensive money and higher yields.
* ETF outflows can look negative, but they may also signal retail panic rather than the end of the long-term trend.
* Bitcoin currently depends more on liquidity, interest rates and yields than on positive crypto statements.
* Capital may be rotating into other major stories such as AI, SpaceX, OpenAI or Anthropic.
* For small investors, the key lesson is not to look at only one number, but at the full picture: ETF flows, the Fed, yields and market sentiment.
