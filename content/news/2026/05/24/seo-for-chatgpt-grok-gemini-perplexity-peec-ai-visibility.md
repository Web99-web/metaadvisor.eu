# SEO for ChatGPT? Berlin’s Peec AI Shows Why Brands Track AI Visibility

Digital marketing is entering a new phase. For years, the main question was how to become more visible on Google. Now a new question is emerging: what do AI systems say about your brand when users ask them for a recommendation, comparison or explanation? That is exactly the shift behind Peec AI, a Berlin startup helping companies track and improve their visibility in AI answers.

According to reports from the tech scene, Peec AI has sharply accelerated its revenue growth and has become one of Europe’s more interesting young AI startups. But the revenue figure itself is not the most important part of the story. What matters more is that Peec AI’s growth shows a new industry is already forming around AI search, AI answers and a kind of “SEO for ChatGPT.”

## What Does Peec AI Actually Do?

Peec AI develops tools that help brands track how they appear in the answers of AI systems such as ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and other models. If a user asks which email marketing tools are worth using, which hotels are good in a city, or which software is best for teams, companies want to know whether they are mentioned at all and in what context.

That is a major change compared with traditional SEO. In classic search, brands mainly looked at Google rankings, clicks and keywords. With AI answers, the logic is different because users often do not click ten blue links. Instead, they receive a summary, recommendation or comparison generated by an AI model.

## Why Does This Matter for Brands?

When user behavior changes, marketing strategies have to change as well. More and more people are not just typing a few keywords into a search engine. They are asking full questions: which tool is best, where should I book, which solution is reliable, what is the better choice? AI then gives a more complete answer.

For brands, this means it is no longer enough to be visible only in traditional search engines. They want to know whether they appear in AI answers, in what context they are mentioned, how AI compares them with competitors and how often they appear in recommendations at all. This is a new layer of digital visibility, and Peec AI is building its value around exactly that.

## GEO: A New Version of SEO Logic

The term GEO, or Generative Engine Optimization, is increasingly used around this topic. In simple terms, it means optimizing visibility inside generative AI systems. This does not mean SEO disappears. It means SEO expands into a new field.

SEO has long focused on ranking in search results. GEO focuses more on how AI models understand, summarize and recommend content, products or brands. This is not the same logic, because AI answers often do not depend on one page or one link, but on a wider set of sources, reputation, structure and clear explanations.

## Peec AI Signals a Larger Market Shift

The Peec AI story is not interesting only because it is a Berlin startup. It is interesting because it shows a broader shift in the European and global tech scene. Investors and founders are paying more attention to real revenue growth, but also to new product categories that respond to changing user behavior.

If companies are starting to pay for tools that measure AI visibility, that means the need already exists. It also means AI search is no longer just an experiment or an add-on to classic search. It is becoming a space that can be monetized. In that sense, Peec AI is not just another startup, but an early sign that new marketing infrastructure is being built around AI answers.

{{< support1 >}}

## What Does This Mean for Google and Classic SEO?

The important point is that this does not automatically mean the end of Google. But it does mean Google is no longer the only place where the fight for digital visibility happens. If a user asks ChatGPT, Grok or Perplexity a question and immediately receives an answer, the traditional website click is no longer the only goal.

That also changes how brands measure success. Instead of looking only at organic traffic and search rankings, they will increasingly need to track whether and how they are mentioned in AI answers, how well AI summaries describe them and what reputation they have across different models. SEO is not disappearing, but on its own it is no longer enough.

## Can AI Visibility Be Controlled?

This is one of the most interesting questions. Nobody can fully control what an AI system says, especially because different models use different sources, summary logic and answer structures. Still, brands can influence how clearly they are described, how present they are in relevant sources and how well their content is structured for both machines and humans.

That is the gap tools like Peec AI want to fill. They do not promise a magic button that will make a brand the first answer in every AI system. But they offer something the market will increasingly need: measurement, monitoring and an attempt at optimization. In digital marketing, measurement is often the first step toward larger budgets and a more mature market.

## Why This Also Matters for Smaller Projects

At first glance, this may seem relevant only for large companies with big marketing budgets. But that is not entirely true. If AI systems increasingly become the first layer of search and recommendation, smaller projects also need to think about how these systems see them.

This is especially important for niche projects, startups, specialized services and media sites. If AI starts playing a major role in how users find information, smaller players will need to think not only about classic SEO, but also about whether they can appear in AI recommendations. That is why the Peec AI story matters beyond the startup world.

## Conclusion: AI Answers Are Becoming the New Visibility Battlefield

Peec AI shows that digital marketing is changing faster than many people think. What used to be strongly connected to Google SEO is now moving into the world of AI answers as well. Brands no longer want to know only whether they rank high in search. They want to know whether ChatGPT mentions them, whether Perplexity recommends them, whether Gemini understands them and how AI systems compare them with competitors.

That is why the Peec AI story matters. The key point is not only that a Berlin startup is growing quickly. The bigger signal is that companies are already investing seriously in a new kind of visibility. That is a clear sign that the rules of online search are changing again.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* Peec AI is interesting not only as a startup, but as a signal that AI search is creating a new category of marketing tools.
* SEO is not disappearing, but it is no longer enough on its own. Brands now want to know what AI systems say about them and whether they recommend them.
* GEO, or Generative Engine Optimization, could become an important term over the next few years.
* The biggest shift is not only technical, but behavioral: users increasingly ask AI systems directly instead of searching the web in the classic way.
* This topic also matters for smaller projects, because AI visibility will not remain relevant only to large brands.
