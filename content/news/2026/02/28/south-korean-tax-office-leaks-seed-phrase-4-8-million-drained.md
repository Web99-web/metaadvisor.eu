In one of the most striking institutional security blunders in recent months, South Korea’s National Tax Service (NTS) reportedly published a crypto wallet seed phrase in an official press release. The consequences were immediate: tokens worth approximately $4.8 million were swiftly drained from the associated address.

The incident occurred during a public announcement detailing enforcement actions against tax delinquents. As part of the disclosure, authorities showcased seized assets, including cryptocurrencies stored on a hardware wallet. However, a photograph released alongside the statement clearly displayed a sheet of paper containing the full mnemonic seed phrase next to a Ledger device — completely unblurred.

Within hours, the funds were gone.

## What is a seed phrase — and why is it critical?

A seed phrase, also known as a mnemonic phrase, is a sequence of 12 or 24 words that allows a crypto wallet to be fully restored. It functions as a backup to the private key. Whoever controls the seed phrase controls the assets.

Unlike traditional banking systems, blockchain transactions are irreversible. There is no central authority capable of freezing funds or reversing transfers once they are executed. That is why a seed phrase is considered the ultimate key to digital ownership.

As MetaAdvisor consistently emphasizes, the most fundamental rule in crypto security is simple: a seed phrase must never be shared, photographed, emailed, or exposed in any publicly accessible format.

## How were 4 million PRTG tokens drained?

Onchain data reveals that the Ethereum address linked to the leaked seed phrase had received three inbound transfers totaling 4 million PRTG (Pre-Retogeum) tokens. Shortly after the press release went public, a single outbound transaction transferred the entire 4 million PRTG to another wallet.

The estimated value at the time of transfer was approximately $4.8 million.

The speed of the transaction suggests that automated bots or monitoring systems detected the exposed seed phrase almost instantly. In today’s digital environment, sensitive information published online can be harvested within seconds by automated scanners searching for exploitable mistakes.

MetaAdvisor observes that this was not a sophisticated cyberattack, but rather a severe operational failure with financial consequences comparable to a major hack.

## Institutional custody and operational security challenges

Governments worldwide have increasingly seized cryptocurrencies in tax enforcement and criminal investigations. However, managing digital assets requires specialized knowledge and strict custody protocols.

Traditional administrative practices — such as photographing confiscated assets for documentation — do not translate safely into decentralized finance environments. In crypto, one unprotected image can result in the immediate loss of millions.

This incident raises important questions about whether public institutions are adequately trained and equipped to handle private keys and cold wallet storage systems.

As MetaAdvisor has previously highlighted, the gap between blockchain security at the protocol level and operational security at the human level remains significant.

## Can the funds be recovered?

Technically, blockchain transactions are transparent and traceable. The movement of the stolen tokens can be monitored in real time. However, recovery is extremely difficult, especially if assets are quickly transferred through multiple wallets or swapped via decentralized exchanges.

Reports indicate that other seed phrases visible in the same document did not contain substantial funds, potentially limiting the total financial damage. Nevertheless, the reputational impact on the institution is considerable.

MetaAdvisor expects that this case may lead to internal reviews and stricter handling procedures for digital asset disclosures in the public sector.

## A broader warning for the crypto ecosystem

The South Korean case is more than a local administrative error. It serves as a global reminder of the core principle of decentralized finance: responsibility is absolute.

Whether individual, corporation, or state authority — whoever holds the keys bears full responsibility for their protection.

The lessons are clear:

A seed phrase is equivalent to a vault master key.  
Public exposure means total loss of control.  
Digital assets require a fundamentally different security mindset.

MetaAdvisor believes this case underscores a recurring truth in the crypto industry: the technology itself may be cryptographically secure, but human error remains the weakest link.
