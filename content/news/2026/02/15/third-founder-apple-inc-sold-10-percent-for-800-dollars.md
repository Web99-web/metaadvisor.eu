# The Third Founder of Apple Inc.: The Man Who Sold 10% of the Company for Just $800

<small>Symbolic image.</small>

Two names are legendary.  
The third is almost forgotten.

When people think of Apple, they picture Steve Jobs. Some remember Steve Wozniak, the engineering genius behind the first Apple computer.

But there was a third founder.

His name was **Ronald Wayne**.

He owned 10% of Apple.

And he sold it.

For $800.

## The garage, two dreamers and one realist

In 1976, two young visionaries believed personal computers could change the world. They had ambition. Energy. Almost nothing to lose.

Ronald Wayne was 41 years old.  
He had a house. Savings. Responsibilities. And experience — including a failed business behind him.

He wasn’t the dreamer.

He was the stabilizer.

Wayne drafted the original partnership agreement. He handled the paperwork. He even designed Apple’s first logo.

Without him, Apple was just an idea.  
With him, it became a company.

## Why he left

Apple began as a partnership. That meant personal liability.

When Jobs and Wozniak ordered parts for their first machines, financial risk became very real. If customers didn’t pay, creditors could go after personal assets.

Jobs and Wozniak had little to lose.

Wayne had everything.

Twelve days after founding the company, he stepped away.

He sold his 10% stake for $800.  
Later, he received roughly $1,500–$1,800 more to fully relinquish any future claims.

Today, that number sounds surreal.

Back then, it meant safety.

## What would it be worth today?

Apple is now one of the most valuable companies on Earth.  
Wayne’s 10% stake would today be worth tens — possibly hundreds — of billions of dollars.

But the deeper question remains:

Would he have wanted that risk?

## A decision history judges differently

Steve Jobs risked everything because he had little to lose.  
Steve Wozniak risked everything because he believed in technology.

Ronald Wayne risked — and chose security.

Ronald Wayne did not make a crazy decision.  
He made a responsible decision — for a man in his situation, a man who had something to lose.
