# Bitcoin ETFs under pressure after yesterday’s market shock

Bitcoin’s sharp selloff yesterday, which briefly pushed the price toward **$60,000**, marked one of the most **stressful trading days for Bitcoin ETFs** since their launch. Rather than acting as a stabilizing force, institutional products showed clear signs of **forced selling and risk reduction**.

At the center of attention was **BlackRock’s spot Bitcoin ETF (IBIT)**, which recorded a **record trading volume of more than $10 billion in a single session**. Over **284 million shares** changed hands — a level of activity typically seen only during periods of extreme market stress.

## Record volume amid falling prices

At the same time as volume surged, IBIT’s price fell by roughly **13%**, hitting its lowest level since October 2024. This combination of **sharp price declines and explosive volume** is widely viewed in market analysis as a hallmark of **capitulation** — a phase in which investors sell out of necessity rather than conviction.

IBIT alone recorded **net outflows of approximately $175 million** on the day, accounting for roughly **40% of total outflows across all spot Bitcoin ETFs**.

## Broad-based ETF outflows

Across the broader market, **spot Bitcoin ETFs saw more than $430 million in net outflows in a single day**. Over a two-day period, total redemptions exceeded **$900 million**, effectively wiping out earlier inflows seen at the start of the week.

These developments have reignited debate around so-called **“paper Bitcoin”**, where the same underlying BTC is used to back multiple financial instruments simultaneously, potentially amplifying volatility during periods of stress.

## Options market reflects fear

Stress signals were also visible in the options market. Demand for **put options**, used to hedge against further downside, surged significantly. Historically, such a heavy put bias tends to emerge during periods of **peak uncertainty and fear**, when market participants focus on protection rather than upside.

While this does not guarantee that a market bottom is immediately at hand, similar patterns in the past have often appeared **late in major correction phases**.

## Capitulation or just a pause?

Despite the dramatic figures, some analysts caution against interpreting yesterday’s selloff as a definitive turning point. Instead, it may represent **institutional portfolio rebalancing** amid heightened macroeconomic uncertainty and a broader risk-off environment.

What is clear, however, is that this was **not a retail-driven selloff**. Activity in ETFs, options markets and trading volume strongly suggests that **institutional players were at the center of the move**.
