# XRP Outruns Bitcoin and Ether: Did Investors Buy the Dip After the Crash?

XRP, the payments-focused cryptocurrency closely associated with Ripple, is mounting a strong comeback after a sharp sell-off earlier this month. While Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH) have also rebounded, XRP has significantly outperformed both in percentage terms, raising an important question: did investors aggressively buy the dip?

Since hitting a local low on Feb. 6, XRP has climbed roughly 38%, trading around $1.55. In the past 24 hours alone, the token gained more than 5%. By comparison, Bitcoin and Ether have advanced approximately 15% over the same period, giving XRP a clear short-term momentum advantage.

## What Does “Buy the Dip” Mean?

“Buy the dip” refers to a strategy where investors purchase an asset after a steep decline, betting that the drop is temporary and that prices will recover.

In XRP’s case, market data suggests that this is exactly what may have happened. According to analytics platform CryptoQuant, Binance’s XRP reserves fell by 192.37 million tokens between Feb. 7 and Feb. 9, dropping to 2.553 billion XRP. That 7% decline marked the lowest exchange balance since January 2024.

Analysts often interpret falling exchange balances as a sign of accumulation. The reasoning is straightforward: investors who intend to hold long-term typically withdraw their tokens from exchanges into private wallets, reducing the supply available for immediate sale.

## Reduced Supply, Increased Price Pressure

When a substantial number of tokens are withdrawn from exchanges, the tradable supply decreases. If demand remains steady or increases, reduced supply can create upward pressure on prices.

A similar pattern emerged in late 2024, when XRP surged from around $0.60 to over $2.40 within two months. During that period, exchange balances declined rapidly, reinforcing the narrative that accumulation preceded the rally.

These historical precedents are fueling speculation that the current rebound could be more than just a short-lived bounce—especially if exchange outflows continue.

## XRP in the Broader Market Context

XRP’s rebound is unfolding amid heightened volatility across the broader crypto market. While Bitcoin and Ether continue to dominate overall market capitalization, altcoins like XRP often exhibit stronger percentage swings during recovery phases.

Ripple’s long-running legal battle with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC – Securities and Exchange Commission, the U.S. financial regulator overseeing securities markets) has also shaped investor perception for years. Any regulatory clarity or reduction in legal uncertainty could significantly impact sentiment around XRP.

Although the current rally appears largely driven by market dynamics and positioning, investors are also closely monitoring macroeconomic conditions, interest rate expectations, and overall risk appetite in global financial markets.

## Relief Rally or Start of a New Trend?

The key question now is whether XRP’s recent surge represents a short-term relief rally or the beginning of a sustained upward trend.

If exchange withdrawals persist, it may signal stronger long-term conviction among holders. However, the crypto market remains highly sensitive to shifts in sentiment, regulatory headlines, and macroeconomic shocks.

History shows that rapid price increases can reverse just as quickly under changing conditions. The coming weeks will likely determine whether XRP’s recovery has lasting momentum or fades amid broader market uncertainty.

## What Should Investors Watch?

To assess XRP’s next move, market participants may want to monitor:

- Changes in XRP balances on major exchanges  
- Trading volume and open interest in derivatives markets  
- Regulatory developments surrounding Ripple  
- Overall Bitcoin market sentiment  

XRP’s recent performance underscores how quickly sentiment can shift in the crypto space. What looked like a severe correction only days ago is now being framed as a potential accumulation opportunity.
