# Mirae Asset Acquires Controlling Stake in Korbit for $93 Million

South Korean financial powerhouse Mirae Asset is accelerating its expansion into digital assets. Its subsidiary, Mirae Asset Consulting, has agreed to acquire a 92.06% controlling stake in the cryptocurrency exchange Korbit for approximately $93 million in cash, according to a recent regulatory filing.

The move sends a strong message from one of Asia’s largest financial institutions: crypto is no longer a fringe experiment — it is becoming a strategic pillar of long-term financial growth.

## A Cash Deal Securing Near-Total Control

Mirae Asset plans to purchase 26.9 million shares of Korbit for 133.48 billion Korean won, giving it a 92.06% ownership interest in the exchange. The transaction will be fully funded in cash, underlining the group’s financial strength and commitment to the sector.

The company’s board approved the acquisition on Feb. 5, and the deal is expected to close within seven business days after contractual conditions are satisfied. Although reports about the potential acquisition surfaced last year, the agreement has now been formally confirmed through regulatory disclosure.

## Why Korbit Matters Strategically

Korbit is one of South Korea’s oldest and most established cryptocurrency exchanges. It holds a full operating license and maintains a robust compliance framework, making it an attractive entry point for major financial institutions seeking regulated exposure to digital assets.

In its most recent fiscal year, Korbit reported 8.7 billion won in revenue and 9.8 billion won in net profit, reversing losses from prior years. This return to profitability likely strengthened investor confidence and increased the exchange’s appeal as an acquisition target.

Before the deal, Korbit was primarily owned by NXC and its subsidiary Simple Capital Futures, which together held around 60.5% of the company, while SK Square owned an additional 31.5%.

## The Institutionalization of Crypto

The acquisition comes at a time when traditional financial institutions are stepping more decisively into the cryptocurrency space. Rather than building platforms from scratch, many financial giants are choosing to acquire existing, regulated exchanges with established infrastructure.

According to its regulatory filing, Mirae Asset aims “to secure future growth drivers through digital-asset (virtual-asset) businesses.” In practical terms, this means diversifying revenue streams and positioning the firm within what is increasingly viewed as the next phase of global financial infrastructure.

South Korea remains one of the most active crypto markets worldwide, characterized by strong retail participation and a strict regulatory framework. The entry of a major institutional player like Mirae Asset could further professionalize and stabilize the domestic crypto ecosystem.

## Regulation as a Competitive Advantage

Unlike unregulated offshore exchanges, Korbit operates within South Korea’s clearly defined regulatory environment. This includes strict compliance with Anti-Money Laundering (AML) requirements and Know Your Customer (KYC) standards.

As global regulators intensify oversight of the crypto sector, compliance is becoming a crucial differentiator. Large financial institutions seek transparency, legal clarity, and operational safeguards — attributes that regulated exchanges are better positioned to provide.

## Potential Market Implications

The acquisition could have several broader effects:

- Increased institutional confidence in the Korean crypto market  
- Heightened competition among domestic exchanges  
- Deeper integration between traditional finance and blockchain infrastructure  
- The potential rollout of new institutional-grade crypto products  

At the same time, continued consolidation may reduce the number of independent players, gradually reshaping the structure of the crypto industry.

## What Comes Next?

The transaction has not yet been finalized, but once completed, Mirae Asset will effectively assume operational control over one of South Korea’s leading cryptocurrency exchanges.

The deal reinforces a clear trend: crypto is steadily integrating into the mainstream financial system. What was once considered experimental technology is increasingly viewed as part of the broader global financial architecture.
