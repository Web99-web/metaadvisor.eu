# Stripe, Bridge and Tempo: Stablecoins Grow Despite “Crypto Winter”

While the broader crypto market continues to face cooling sentiment and volatility, Stripe is sending a different message: stablecoins are not only surviving the “crypto winter,” they are entering a phase of accelerated growth.

In its annual letter, the global payments giant emphasized that stablecoin usage has risen significantly and that real-world adoption is increasingly decoupled from Bitcoin and broader crypto price cycles.

## Bridge: Transaction Volume Quadruples

A key pillar of Stripe’s crypto strategy is Bridge, a stablecoin orchestration platform the company acquired in 2024. Bridge enables businesses to integrate stablecoins into payment flows, manage conversions, and optimize cross-border settlements.

According to Stripe’s report, transaction volume on Bridge more than quadrupled over the past year. This growth is particularly notable given the broader weakness across crypto asset prices during the same period.

Stripe argues that the practical utility of stablecoins explains their resilience. Unlike speculative tokens, stablecoins serve a clear function — enabling faster and cheaper settlement, particularly in international business transactions.

## Stablecoins as Infrastructure, Not Speculation

Stablecoins, currently valued at approximately $300 billion in total market capitalization, are typically pegged to fiat currencies such as the U.S. dollar. Their price stability makes them suitable for everyday payments and corporate transactions.

Stripe cited data suggesting that stablecoin payment volume reached around $400 billion, with roughly 60% stemming from business-to-business activity. This indicates that stablecoins are increasingly used for enterprise payments, supply chains, and global settlement flows.

The trend signals a shift from niche crypto usage toward foundational financial infrastructure.

## Tempo: A Payments-Focused Blockchain

Stripe also confirmed plans to launch the mainnet of its proprietary blockchain, Tempo, developed in partnership with crypto investment firm Paradigm. Testing began in December, and the network is designed specifically for payments.

Unlike general-purpose blockchains supporting a wide range of decentralized applications, Tempo is optimized for speed, reliability, and integration into fintech ecosystems.

If successful, Tempo could further strengthen Stripe’s position as a bridge between traditional finance and blockchain infrastructure.

## Decoupling from Market Cycles

One of the most important themes in Stripe’s letter is the apparent decoupling of stablecoins from traditional crypto market cycles. While Bitcoin fell roughly 50% from its previous highs and posted additional declines in 2025, stablecoin payment volume continued to expand.

This divergence suggests increasing market maturity. Instead of being tied exclusively to speculative price action, stablecoins are emerging as functional financial tools.

In other words, infrastructure continues to evolve even when speculative markets cool.

## Stripe’s Broader Strategy

Stripe processed $1.9 trillion through its network last year, marking a 34% increase from the previous year. The company also announced a tender offer valuing it at $159 billion.

Such valuation reflects continued investor confidence in fintech companies that successfully integrate blockchain technology into real-world financial flows.

For Stripe, stablecoins are not an ideological bet — they are a pragmatic solution for improving global payments.

## What This Means for Fintech and Crypto

If the trend persists, stablecoins could become standard instruments in international commerce. Their near-instant settlement and programmability open the door to automated financial processes.

In this context, “crypto winter” may primarily affect speculative assets, while the infrastructure layer continues to expand.

Stripe’s strategy demonstrates that major fintech players are building long-term systems independent of short-term market cycles.

## Our View

Stripe’s stablecoin narrative highlights a structural transformation within the crypto sector. Instead of viewing the market solely through Bitcoin price movements, it becomes clear that the real shift is happening at the infrastructure level.

If stablecoins continue gaining traction through B2B adoption and integration into mainstream payment networks, blockchain technology could become an invisible but essential layer of everyday financial infrastructure.

And that may prove more significant than any short-term price rally.
