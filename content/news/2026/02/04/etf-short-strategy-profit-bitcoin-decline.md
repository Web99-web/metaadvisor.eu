# ETFs Thriving as the Market Falls

While **bitcoin** and shares of **Strategy (MSTR)** are going through one of their most challenging periods in recent years, a parallel and less visible story is unfolding across financial markets: **certain ETFs are posting record gains precisely because prices are falling**.

These are *short* and *leveraged* exchange-traded funds — instruments specifically designed to benefit from downside moves. In the current environment, the focus has turned to Strategy, the world’s largest publicly listed corporate holder of bitcoin.

## How a “2x Short” ETF Works

At the center of attention is the **GraniteShares 2x Short MSTR Daily ETF (MSDD)**, a product designed to deliver **–200% of Strategy’s daily share price movement**. In simple terms:

- If MSTR falls 2% in a single trading day  
- the ETF aims to rise by roughly 4% (before fees and daily reset effects)

These products are **not intended for long-term holding**, but rather as short-term tactical tools during periods of heightened volatility. That very volatility has made MSDD exceptionally profitable.

The ETF recently reached an **all-time high**, posting gains of more than **270% year-over-year**. A similar pattern can be seen in the **Defiance Daily Target 2x Short MSTR ETF (SMST)**, which also climbed to its highest level in nearly a year.

## Strategy’s Decline and Its Bitcoin Exposure

Strategy’s share price has dropped to around **$126**, its lowest level since September 2024 and roughly **76% below its all-time high** from November last year.

The reason is straightforward: Strategy holds more than **713,000 bitcoin** on its balance sheet, effectively making the stock a **leveraged proxy for bitcoin’s price**. When BTC rises, the stock tends to outperform; when bitcoin falls, losses are amplified.

Bitcoin itself recently dipped toward **$73,000** before staging a modest rebound. Market positioning suggests, however, that traders expect volatility to remain elevated.

## “Feasting on Carnage” as a Market Metaphor

In English market jargon, the phrase **“feasting on carnage”** is often used to describe such situations. While dramatic when translated literally, it carries **no moral judgment** and does not imply wrongdoing by specific companies or individuals.

Instead, it refers to:
- sharp market sell-offs  
- forced liquidations  
- extreme volatility  
- strategies designed to profit under those conditions

These are market participants who have **positioned themselves commercially for falling prices** — a strategy that can be profitable in the current environment, but one that **can just as quickly generate losses if the market reverses**.

## A Broader Market Signal

The rise of these ETFs sends a clear signal: capital is not necessarily exiting crypto markets, but **repositioning within them**. Rather than pursuing simple “buy and hold” strategies, some investors are deploying more sophisticated tools to manage risk and capitalize on downturns.

That said, leveraged short products carry **substantial risk**, particularly due to daily rebalancing and compounding effects in volatile markets.
