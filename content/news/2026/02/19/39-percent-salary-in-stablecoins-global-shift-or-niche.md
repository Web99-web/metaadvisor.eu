# 39% receive part of their salary in stablecoins

*Image is symbolic.*

A recent survey suggests that 39% of respondents receive part of their income in stablecoins. The figure sounds striking — but it raises an important question: does this represent a global shift in employment practices or a statistic drawn primarily from crypto-native communities?

Stablecoins are cryptocurrencies pegged to stable assets, most commonly the US dollar. Examples include USDT (Tether) and USDC (USD Coin). Unlike Bitcoin or Ethereum, they are designed to minimize volatility, making them more suitable for payments.

## Who is actually getting paid in stablecoins?

Context matters. Many surveys of this type focus on crypto platform users, freelancers in Web3, developers and globally distributed teams already operating within the digital economy.

In regions facing high inflation or limited banking access, stablecoins can serve as a store of value and payment alternative.

In contrast, within the European Union, salary payments remain legally structured in fiat currencies, with taxation and accounting frameworks tied to traditional systems.

## Regulation and compliance

The EU’s MiCA regulation (Markets in Crypto-Assets) provides a framework for stablecoin issuance and use.

Paying salaries in crypto raises key questions:

- How are taxes calculated?  
- How is income officially reported?  
- How are balances recorded in accounting systems?  

In most developed economies, official payroll remains denominated in fiat currency.

## Advantages in digital industries

Nevertheless, stablecoins offer practical advantages:

- instant cross-border transfers  
- lower transaction fees  
- 24/7 availability  
- protection against local currency instability  

Within Web3 and global freelance sectors, this payment model is increasingly common.

## Is 39% realistic?

The critical issue is sample bias. Among crypto-native respondents, the percentage may indeed be high. Globally, however, the share of workers paid in stablecoins remains relatively small.

Stablecoin salaries are primarily concentrated in tech-driven and blockchain-based industries.

## Conclusion

Getting paid in stablecoins is no longer unusual — but it is far from mainstream across traditional labor markets.

The trend exists, yet it remains sector-specific and region-dependent.
