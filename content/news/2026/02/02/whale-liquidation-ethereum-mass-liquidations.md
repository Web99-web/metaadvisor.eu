# Whale liquidation: How a $220 million ETH trade vanished in hours

During the sharp weekend sell-off across crypto markets, Ethereum experienced one of the **largest liquidation waves of the current cycle**. A single whale position stood out, being **force-closed with losses exceeding $220 million**. The move unfolded amid **thin liquidity**, accelerating downside momentum and amplifying market stress.

## What happened?

Market data shows that the largest single liquidation occurred on **decentralized derivatives exchange Hyperliquid**, where an **ETH-USD position worth roughly $222.6 million** was automatically closed after prices breached margin thresholds.

Within just 24 hours:
- around **435,000 traders were liquidated**
- total losses reached approximately **$2.6 billion**
- more than **90% of losses came from long positions**

This imbalance suggests the market was **heavily leveraged and overly bullish** before the downturn.

## Ethereum takes the hardest hit

Ethereum absorbed most of the damage:
- over **$1.15 billion in ETH positions** liquidated  
- Bitcoin followed with roughly **$788 million**
- Solana saw close to **$200 million** wiped out  

By venue, **Hyperliquid** accounted for the largest share (about **$1.09 billion**), followed by **Bybit** and **Binance**.

## Why liquidations accelerate crashes

Liquidations occur in **leveraged trading** when price moves beyond a trader’s margin limits. In fast-moving markets, this creates a **cascade effect**:
1. price drops trigger forced closures  
2. forced selling increases downward pressure  
3. additional liquidations follow  

In low-liquidity conditions, this feedback loop can unfold **within minutes**.

## Is this a market bottom?

Large long liquidations are often interpreted as potential **panic lows**, flushing excess leverage from the system. However, such signals are **not guarantees of a reversal**. Sustainable stabilization requires renewed liquidity, reduced leverage and genuine spot demand.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):
- Even large whales are **not immune to market risk**.  
- Leverage dramatically magnifies losses in volatile conditions.  
- Events like this often reset market positioning.  
- Short-term volatility remains high, but long-term structure can improve.
