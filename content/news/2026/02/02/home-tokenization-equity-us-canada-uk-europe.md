# Home tokenization: Why it works in the U.S. — and why it feels foreign in Europe

U.S. retailer **Bed Bath & Beyond (BBBY)** drew market attention after announcing plans to **acquire Tokens.com** and launch a platform focused on **asset tokenization**, starting with **real estate**. Following the announcement, BBBY shares rose by roughly 7%, signaling strong investor interest.

Several established players from the U.S. fintech and blockchain ecosystem are involved. **Tokens.com** is expected to provide the tokenization infrastructure, **tZERO** will operate as the regulated trading and custody platform, while **Figure**, a blockchain-based fintech founded by former SoFi CEO Mike Cagney, will offer financial products such as mortgages, renovation loans and property-backed credit lines.

The platform is expected to launch in the summer of 2026.

## What this platform does — and what it does not

One common misconception needs to be addressed upfront: **this is not about turning homes into cryptocurrencies**.

Properties are not being split into freely tradable tokens like Bitcoin or Ethereum. Instead, the platform focuses on digitizing **financial rights linked to real estate**, primarily **home equity**.

In simple terms, it is the **value homeowners already own** that is being tokenized — not the physical home itself.

## What is equity and why it matters

**Equity** represents the difference between a property’s market value and the outstanding debt secured against it.

Example:
- Property value: €200,000
- Remaining mortgage: €80,000
- Equity: €120,000

In the **U.S., Canada, the UK and Australia**, this equity is an active financial tool. Homeowners can:
- take out a **home equity loan**  
  *(a one-time loan based on the already repaid portion of the home’s value)*

- open a **home equity line of credit (HELOC)**  
  *(a flexible credit line that can be drawn as needed, similar to an overdraft)*

- refinance existing loans
- use equity as collateral for other financing needs

> *In Anglo-Saxon markets, homeowners can either take a loan secured by their home equity or open a revolving credit line (HELOC) — a practice that is uncommon across much of Europe.*

Tokenization in this context means that this right to value:
- is recorded digitally
- divided into tokens
- used for financing or trading within a regulated framework

## Why this sounds abstract to European readers

The explanation lies in Europe’s legal and banking structures.

In many European countries, during the life of a mortgage:
- **formal ownership of the property is effectively held by the bank**
- the homeowner remains only the user/possessor
- **full ownership is restored only after the final payment**

Under such systems, equity cannot easily be activated as a financial instrument.

By contrast, in the **U.S., Canada, the UK and Australia**:
- the bank **does not own the property**
- it holds a **lien or mortgage as collateral**
- **ownership remains with the homeowner throughout**

This structural difference explains why real estate tokenization feels natural in Anglo-Saxon markets but remains largely theoretical in Europe.

## Is this a crypto revolution?

No. This is **not a mass crypto revolution**, but rather:
- the digitization of existing financial models
- the application of blockchain technology within **regulated finance**
- an attempt to make processes faster and more transparent

Without legal reform, technology alone cannot replicate this model across Europe.

## Our view (Metaadvisor):

- This project highlights how blockchain is increasingly used to **enhance traditional finance**, not replace it.
- Real estate tokenization only makes sense where home equity markets already exist.
- European readers should see this as a financing innovation, not “crypto homes.”
- Without regulatory change, such models will remain limited to Anglo-Saxon markets.
- Long-term, this is a key test of how far TradFi and blockchain can integrate.
