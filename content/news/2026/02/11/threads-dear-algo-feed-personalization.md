# Threads launches “Dear Algo”: talking directly to the algorithm

Threads, Meta’s microblogging-style social network built to compete with X (formerly Twitter), and particularly popular in the United States, the United Kingdom, India and Brazil, is rolling out a new AI-powered feature called “Dear Algo.” The tool allows users to publicly request changes to what appears in their feed.

Instead of simply clicking “Not interested,” users can create a public post beginning with “Dear Algo,” followed by a request such as wanting to see more tech news or less entertainment content. The platform then adjusts their feed for three days.

## Personalization made public

A distinctive aspect of the feature is its public nature. Other users can see these requests and even repost them to apply similar adjustments to their own feeds. Meta frames this as a way to turn personalization into a shared, community-driven experience.

However, the public visibility may deter users who prefer not to broadcast their content preferences.

## Temporary control over the algorithm

The adjustment is time-limited, meaning users gain short-term influence over the algorithm without permanently resetting their preferences. In an era of growing scrutiny over algorithmic power, this approach appears to offer users a sense of agency without fully decentralizing content curation.

## A competitive edge in AI-driven social media

While platforms like X and Bluesky already offer feed controls, Threads’ explicit AI command differentiates it within the social media landscape. By leaning into AI-based personalization, Meta positions Threads as a more adaptive, responsive platform in the competition for user attention.
