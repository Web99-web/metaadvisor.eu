# Stablecoin Bill Talks Stall as White House Meeting Yields No Breakthrough

The second White House-brokered meeting between crypto industry representatives and banking leaders failed to produce an agreement on key stablecoin provisions within the proposed digital asset market structure bill. While participants described the session as “productive,” no formal resolution was reached.

At the heart of the debate are stablecoins — digital tokens pegged to fiat currencies like the U.S. dollar — and whether issuers and platforms should be allowed to offer yield to holders.

## Yield Dispute and Financial Stability Concerns

Banking lobbyists argue that yield-bearing stablecoins could draw deposits away from traditional banks and potentially destabilize the financial system. The crypto sector counters that stablecoins are foundational to liquidity, DeFi applications and cross-border payments.

## Political Gridlock

U.S. lawmakers are attempting to clarify regulatory oversight between the SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) and the CFTC (Commodity Futures Trading Commission). Although the House previously passed a similar bill, the Senate has yet to secure bipartisan backing.

Without compromise, regulatory uncertainty may persist, weighing on market confidence.
