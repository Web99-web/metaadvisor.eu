# Zedcex and Zedxion, UK-Registered Crypto Exchanges, Hit by U.S. Sanctions

The United States has set a **major precedent in crypto regulation** by sanctioning **entire cryptocurrency exchanges** for the first time — not just individual wallet addresses, smart contracts, or infrastructure providers.

The exchanges targeted are **Zedcex** and **Zedxion**, both **registered in the United Kingdom**, which U.S. authorities say played a role in facilitating Iranian financial operations.

## What happened?

According to the U.S. Treasury’s **Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC)**, Zedcex and Zedxion:
- enabled transactions linked to Iran’s financial system,
- helped bypass international sanctions,
- and supported networks connected to the **Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC)**.

U.S. officials claim that at least one of the exchanges processed **over $94 billion in transactions** since being established in 2022.

## Why this matters: a regulatory precedent

Until now, U.S. crypto-related sanctions mainly targeted:
- individual wallet addresses,
- mixing services,
- money laundering infrastructure,
- or specific software providers.

This case marks the **first time entire crypto exchanges have been sanctioned**, despite being registered in a Western jurisdiction.  
It sends a clear message: UK or EU registration does not provide immunity if platforms are used for geopolitical or sanctions-evasion purposes.

## The stablecoin angle

U.S. authorities also stated that the **Central Bank of Iran used stablecoins such as Tether (USDT)** to manipulate foreign exchange markets during periods of severe pressure on the Iranian rial.

This adds to growing scrutiny of stablecoins as tools not only for payments and liquidity, but also for **geopolitical and monetary influence**.

## Impact on the crypto market

Following OFAC’s designation:
- all U.S.-linked assets of the exchanges are frozen,
- U.S. individuals and companies are prohibited from interacting with them,
- secondary effects may impact partners, liquidity providers, and international users.

The move highlights how crypto exchanges are increasingly viewed as **systemic financial actors**, not merely neutral technology platforms.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor):

- A clear turning point for global crypto regulation.
- Western registration no longer guarantees regulatory safety.
- Stablecoins are now firmly on the geopolitical radar.
- Similar actions against other platforms are likely.
- Crypto markets are being shaped as much by politics as by technology.
