# UK Plans to Limit Stablecoins: Regulation or Innovation Brake?

The United Kingdom is accelerating the development of a formal rulebook for stablecoins. However, alongside regulatory progress, resistance from parts of the crypto industry is growing. The Bank of England — the central bank of the United Kingdom — has proposed caps on how much stablecoin individuals and businesses can hold, while the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), the UK’s financial regulator, moves forward with real-world testing of stablecoin issuance.

At Metaadvisor.eu, we see this as a defining moment: can London position itself as a global digital asset hub while simultaneously limiting the scale of digital currency usage?

## Regulatory Sandbox: A Controlled Digital Currency Experiment

The FCA has selected Revolut, Monee Financial Technologies, ReStabilise and VVTX to participate in its Regulatory Sandbox — a supervised testing environment designed to allow financial innovation under structured oversight and consumer safeguards.

The sandbox will focus on stablecoin issuance and real-world use cases, including:

- Digital payments  
- Wholesale settlement  
- Crypto trading  

Testing begins in the first quarter of 2026, and the findings are expected to feed directly into the UK’s final stablecoin regulatory framework later in the year.

Matthew Long, Director of Payments and Digital Assets at the FCA, stated that the goal is to ensure UK-issued stablecoins can be trusted for payments, settlement and trading while maintaining financial system resilience.

## Proposed Caps: How Much Stablecoin Is Too Much?

In November 2025, the Bank of England published a consultation paper outlining proposed “caps” — maximum holding limits — for stablecoins.

The suggested thresholds are:

- Individuals: between £5,000 and £20,000  
- Businesses: between £1 million and £10 million  

According to the central bank, these limits are designed to prevent large-scale migration of bank deposits into stablecoins, which could undermine traditional banking liquidity in stress scenarios.

Stablecoins are digital tokens pegged to fiat currencies such as the U.S. dollar or the British pound. They promise faster, cheaper and borderless transactions. Yet from a regulatory perspective, they also represent potential systemic risk, particularly if a sudden withdrawal wave — similar to a traditional bank run — were to occur.

At Metaadvisor, we note that this debate is less about technology and more about financial stability versus structural change.

## Industry Pushback: Is the UK Undermining Its Own Ambitions?

The proposal has drawn criticism from leading industry voices. Brian Armstrong, CEO of Coinbase, publicly warned that strict caps could damage the UK’s competitiveness in the global digital economy.

On social platform X, Armstrong urged UK users to sign a parliamentary petition calling for reconsideration of the limits. The petition has already gathered more than 80,000 signatures, with 100,000 required to trigger a parliamentary debate.

Critics argue that overly restrictive caps could send the wrong message to innovators and institutional investors. Capital and talent might migrate toward jurisdictions offering clearer or more flexible regulatory structures, including parts of the United States, Asia or the Middle East.

From Metaadvisor.eu’s perspective, regulatory clarity is welcome — but regulatory rigidity can have unintended economic consequences.

## London’s Digital Asset Vision at a Crossroads

The UK government has repeatedly stated its ambition to transform London into a global center for digital assets and fintech innovation. Yet the stablecoin cap proposal highlights a strategic tension.

On one hand, regulation provides certainty, investor confidence and systemic safeguards. On the other, hard limits may constrain large-scale adoption in payments infrastructure, enterprise settlement systems and cross-border trade applications.

The outcome of this debate may ultimately determine whether the UK becomes a controlled testing ground for digital finance — or a competitive global leader in its development.

For Metaadvisor and our readers, this story is not simply about caps; it is about how nations balance innovation with institutional stability in the emerging digital economy.
