# USDT Records Biggest Monthly Decline Since the FTX Collapse

The stablecoin USDT, issued by Tether, has seen its circulating supply decline by approximately $1.5 billion so far in February, following a $1.2 billion decrease in January. According to Artemis Analytics data reported by Bloomberg, this puts USDT on track for its largest monthly supply contraction in three years — since the weeks following the collapse of cryptocurrency exchange FTX in November 2022.

While USDT continues to maintain its 1:1 peg to the US dollar, the total number of tokens in circulation is shrinking — raising questions about liquidity dynamics and broader market positioning.

## What Does a Decline in USDT Supply Actually Mean?

USDT is “minted” when large clients — typically exchanges or institutional investors — send US dollars to Tether. When they want to redeem their holdings, USDT is returned to Tether and the tokens are “burned,” effectively removed from circulation.

If total supply declines, it means more capital has been redeemed than newly issued during a given period.

Possible explanations include:

- Investors reducing crypto exposure  
- Capital rotating into Bitcoin or other assets  
- Institutional portfolio rebalancing  
- Funds moving back into the traditional financial system  

As Metaadvisor often highlights, stablecoin flows are one of the clearest indicators of liquidity dynamics in crypto markets.

## The FTX Parallel

After FTX collapsed in November 2022, USDT’s circulating supply fell by roughly $2 billion in December of that year. That contraction was driven by a sharp loss of confidence and widespread capital withdrawals following the failure of FTX and its numerous affiliated entities.

Today’s environment appears fundamentally different. There are no visible signs of a systemic exchange collapse or cascading insolvencies. However, the fact that the current decline is the largest since that period gives it structural relevance — particularly for long-term liquidity analysis within the crypto market.

## Liquidity Is the Market’s Lifeblood

Stablecoins function as the backbone of crypto trading. They allow rapid entry and exit without converting to fiat.

A decline in USDT may indicate:

### 1. Less “parked” capital  
Investors may be deploying capital elsewhere rather than holding stablecoins.

### 2. Market consolidation  
Supply contraction sometimes accompanies reduced speculative activity.

### 3. Institutional caution  
Mint and burn operations primarily occur at large-scale levels.

At the time of writing, USDT continues to hold its peg, which suggests no immediate stability concerns.

## The Trust Factor

Tether has long faced scrutiny regarding the transparency of its reserves. The company states that USDT is backed by liquid assets, including US Treasury bills.

While Tether now publishes regular reserve attestations, it still does not provide a full-scale Big Four audit.

Like fiat currencies, the system ultimately relies on:

- Confidence  
- Transparency  
- Reputation  

Metaadvisor.eu emphasizes that trust, once shaken, can have rapid market consequences — but at this stage, no systemic breakdown is evident.

## Impact on Bitcoin and Altcoins

Bitcoin has not shown signs of panic in response to the USDT supply decline. That could indicate markets interpret this as technical rebalancing rather than a crisis.

Altcoins — especially DeFi tokens — may be more sensitive if liquidity tightens further.

## Regulation and Structural Shifts

Regulators, including the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), are increasingly focused on the stablecoin sector.

At the same time, institutional investors are gaining Bitcoin exposure via ETFs, potentially reducing the need to hold capital in stablecoins.

This suggests that the USDT decline may reflect structural evolution rather than weakness.

## Cause for Alarm?

Metaadvisor does not see evidence of an FTX-style systemic event. However, stablecoin metrics remain critical indicators for anyone analyzing crypto markets.

Key questions ahead:

- Will supply contraction continue?  
- Will USDT maintain its peg under pressure?  
- Are other stablecoins showing similar trends?  

If stability persists, this episode may prove to be cyclical capital rotation rather than crisis.
