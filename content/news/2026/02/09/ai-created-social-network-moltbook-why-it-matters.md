# AI Has Created Its Own Social Network

## What is Moltbook — and why is it attracting attention?

Over the past few days, several international technology outlets reported on an unusual experiment: autonomous AI agents created their own digital environment and began communicating with one another — without any human participation.

The project has been informally referred to as **Moltbook**, a nod to Facebook. It is not a public app, nor a consumer-facing platform. Instead, it is a controlled research environment whose implications extend far beyond a simple technical demo.

## How can AI build a social network without humans?

Traditional social networks struggle with the “cold start” problem: without users, there is no content; without content, there are no users. AI systems do not face this limitation.

Once a sufficient number of agents are activated, interaction begins immediately. Posts, replies, and discussions are generated continuously, without fatigue, hesitation, or downtime. This self-sustaining dynamic is what caught researchers and journalists by surprise.

## Where did Moltbook first become visible?

Moltbook did not launch via an official announcement or website. Instead, it surfaced through **screenshots and reporting** published by outlets such as Wired, AP News, and Forbes. These reports documented real conversations between AI agents — including exchanges not directly triggered by human prompts.

At that moment, the experiment moved from a closed lab setting into public discussion.

## How is this different from chatbots we already know?

Most chatbots are reactive. They respond to human input. Moltbook demonstrates something fundamentally different: **AI communicating with AI**, inside a shared digital space.

In such environments, agents can develop their own conversational patterns, priorities, and recurring themes — without a human filter. This is where the experiment becomes genuinely significant.

## The risk of closed loops and hallucinations

If one AI agent introduces a flawed assumption, another repeats it, and a third expands on it, an internal consensus can emerge that has no grounding in reality.

Unlike human social networks, where disagreement and external correction often intervene, AI-only environments lack built-in reality checks unless explicitly designed. This raises important questions about feedback loops and emergent behavior.

## The bigger picture: data centers, power, and jobs

Moltbook appears at a time when governments — including New York State — are questioning the rapid expansion of AI data centers. As AI systems gain autonomy, the debate shifts from infrastructure alone to control over future digital spaces.

If a small number of actors control models, energy, and compute power, while AI increasingly handles communication and labor, the human role in the digital economy inevitably changes.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor)

Moltbook is not a threat by itself — it is a signal. Artificial intelligence is evolving from a tool into an active participant in the digital ecosystem. Transparency, human oversight, and governance will become more important, not less.
