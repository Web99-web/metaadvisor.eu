## An unusual incident at one of Asia’s largest crypto exchanges

This week, South Korean cryptocurrency exchange Bithumb found itself at the center of a serious operational incident. Due to an internal settlement or accounting error, certain user accounts were mistakenly credited with extremely large amounts of Bitcoin — reportedly on the order of **around 2,000 BTC per user**.

The timing immediately drew attention, as the incident occurred shortly before one of the sharpest market downturns in recent months, during which Bitcoin briefly fell toward the $60,000 level. This coincidence naturally raised questions about whether the erroneous payouts could have contributed to heightened volatility.

## What exactly happened?

Based on available information, the incident does not appear to have been the result of a hack or external breach. Instead, it seems to have stemmed from an internal processing error related to withdrawals, settlements, or balance reconciliation.

Bithumb reportedly acted quickly to halt the issue, freezing affected accounts and initiating recovery procedures. However, it remains unclear whether all mistakenly distributed Bitcoin was fully recovered or whether some assets may have temporarily entered circulation.

## Did this trigger the market selloff?

Despite the dramatic nature of the incident, **there is currently no evidence** that the Bithumb error was the primary driver behind the broader crypto market crash.

Prior to the incident, market conditions were already highly fragile, shaped by:

- Heavy outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs  
- Large-scale liquidations of leveraged positions  
- A broader risk-off environment across global financial markets  

In such an environment, even isolated operational mishaps can act as stress amplifiers — not as root causes, but as additional signals that undermine confidence in market infrastructure.

## Why incidents like this still matter

Even if the Bithumb incident did not directly cause the selloff, it highlights a persistent vulnerability in centralized crypto exchanges: operational risk during periods of extreme stress.

For institutional participants in particular, confidence in custody, settlement, and counterparty reliability is critical. Unexpected errors — especially involving unusually large balances — can prompt risk managers to reduce exposure preemptively, contributing to short-term selling pressure.

When combined with ETF redemptions, options hedging, and algorithmic trading strategies, such events can intensify already ongoing market moves.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor)

- There is no indication that Bithumb “caused” the global market crash  
- The incident occurred during an exceptionally fragile market phase  
- Operational errors can amplify volatility even if they are not root causes  
- The broader selloff remains primarily driven by liquidations, ETF outflows, and macro uncertainty
