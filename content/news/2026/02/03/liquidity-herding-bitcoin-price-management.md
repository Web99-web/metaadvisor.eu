# Liquidity Herding or Price Management: How Large Players Deliberately Keep Bitcoin Below Key Levels

As **gold and silver rallied strongly**, **Bitcoin appeared unusually sluggish**. Despite supportive market conditions, it repeatedly failed to break above the **$90,000** level — a behavior that later preceded a sharp drop toward $75,000.

At the time, many blamed weak demand or macro uncertainty. However, **order-book analysis** revealed a different story: Bitcoin’s stagnation was driven less by sentiment and more by **intentional liquidity management**.

## What is liquidity herding?

**Liquidity herding** refers to a strategy where large market participants — banks, funds and institutional investors — shape market behavior by placing visible liquidity at strategic levels.

This involves:
- large visible sell orders
- controlling liquidity around key technical zones
- influencing trader psychology

The goal is not to crash the market, but to **control the pace and path of price movement**.

## How it appears in practice

Data from **Material Indicators** showed repeated sell-side liquidity walls just below $90,000. These discouraged buyers and capped upward momentum.

Tools like **FireCharts** made it clear that price was being deliberately pinned within a narrow range.

## Why large players do this

Institutional investors operate on multi-year horizons. They prefer:
- consolidation phases
- controlled volatility
- time to build positions without pushing prices higher

Higher prices become attractive only after accumulation is complete.

## A maturing market

With ETFs, derivatives and institutional capital, Bitcoin has evolved into a **more structured financial asset**. Retail emotions no longer dominate price discovery as they once did.
