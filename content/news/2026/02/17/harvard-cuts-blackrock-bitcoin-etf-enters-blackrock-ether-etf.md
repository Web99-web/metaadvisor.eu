# Harvard Cuts BlackRock Bitcoin ETF (BTC), Enters BlackRock Ether ETF (ETH)

<small><em>Image is symbolic.</em></small>

One of the world’s most influential university endowments has made a move that caught the crypto market’s attention. Harvard University, whose endowment manages approximately $56.9 billion, reduced its exposure to the BlackRock spot Bitcoin ETF by about 21% while simultaneously opening a new, sizable position in BlackRock’s spot Ether ETF.

According to filings with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC — the main regulator of U.S. capital markets), Harvard Management Company (HMC) lowered its stake in the iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT) to roughly $265.8 million. At the same time, it purchased nearly 3.9 million shares of the iShares Ethereum Trust (ETHA), valued at approximately $87 million.

## Rebalancing, Not Exiting Crypto

At first glance, cutting Bitcoin exposure might appear bearish. However, the broader context suggests this is a portfolio rebalancing rather than a retreat from crypto.

Harvard’s total crypto allocation still represents less than 1% of its overall assets — around 0.62%. This points to a tactical adjustment rather than a fundamental shift in strategy.

Bitcoin surged above $120,000 in late 2025 before correcting below $90,000 in early 2026. Such volatility often prompts institutional investors to lock in gains and optimize allocations.

## Why ETFs Matter

Harvard does not directly purchase Bitcoin (BTC) or Ether (ETH). Instead, it uses regulated spot ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds).

ETF structures allow institutional investors to gain exposure without handling custody, private keys, or operational crypto risks. For large endowments and pension funds, regulated ETF vehicles are the preferred gateway into digital assets.

## Why Ether?

Ethereum has increasingly positioned itself as the infrastructure layer for tokenized real-world assets (RWAs), stablecoins, and decentralized finance (DeFi).

While Bitcoin is primarily viewed as digital gold, Ethereum plays a functional role in financial infrastructure. Tokenized U.S. Treasuries, money market funds, and other traditional financial products are increasingly issued on Ethereum.

If 2026 becomes the year of deeper crypto integration into traditional finance, Ether’s strategic role could extend beyond simple price appreciation.

## Market Dynamics and mNAV

Another factor may be the dynamics surrounding digital asset treasury (DAT) companies such as Strategy (MSTR).

During Bitcoin’s rally, these firms traded at significant premiums to the value of the BTC they held. This premium is measured by mNAV (multiple of Net Asset Value — the ratio of enterprise value to net asset value).

Some investors combined ETF exposure with short positions in such equities to capture premium compression. As markets cooled, these trades were partially unwound.

## Institutional Signal

Harvard’s move reflects increasing sophistication among institutional investors. Rather than treating crypto as a single asset class, capital is being allocated selectively within the ecosystem.

The key takeaway: capital is not leaving crypto — it is rotating within it.
