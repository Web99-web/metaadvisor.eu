# Could Bitcoin fall to $28,000? Bloomberg analyst softens shock forecast

*(The image is symbolic.)*

**One of the better-known macro analysts, Mike McGlone of Bloomberg Intelligence, has pulled back. After sparking strong reactions with a call that Bitcoin could fall as low as $10,000, he now points to $28,000 as a more realistic level. Despite the significant moderation, the debate around his analysis is only intensifying.**

This shift is not minor. The difference between $10,000 and $28,000 is not merely numerical — it represents the gap between a systemic breakdown scenario and a deep but historically familiar correction.

## From extreme downside to historical price distribution

McGlone initially argued that if U.S. equities peak and a recession follows, Bitcoin could face substantial downside pressure. In this framework, the cryptocurrency remains a **high-beta asset**, meaning it tends to move more aggressively than the broader market in both directions.

Following public criticism, he cited historical price distribution and identified $28,000 as a **more probable correction level** under a negative macro scenario.

This illustrates how sensitive market projections are to changing assumptions.

## Macroeconomics at the center

At the core lies the broader economic backdrop. If U.S. capital markets enter a serious correction, the effects could spill into crypto.

The **FED (Federal Reserve – the U.S. central bank)** has maintained restrictive monetary policy to combat inflation. Higher interest rates reduce liquidity and make risk assets less attractive.

Bitcoin has shown correlation with tech stocks in recent cycles. In a tightening environment, volatile assets often face pressure.

However, today’s Bitcoin market is more structurally developed than in past cycles:

- institutional ETFs  
- higher trading volumes  
- global participation  
- advanced derivatives strategies  

This increases resilience — but does not eliminate volatility.

## Is $28,000 realistic?

A move to $28,000 would represent a significant correction, but not an unprecedented collapse. Bitcoin has historically experienced drawdowns of 50% or more before entering new bull phases.

Such a scenario would likely require a strong negative catalyst — deep recession, geopolitical escalation, or a sharp liquidity contraction.

Markets respond not only to numbers, but to **narratives**. Extreme forecasts generate attention — and emotional reactions.

## Market psychology and perception

The gap between $10,000 and $28,000 highlights how communication influences sentiment. One figure can trigger selling pressure, while another may be interpreted as a buying opportunity.

Markets function on expectations. If investors believe a severe downturn is coming, behavior adjusts accordingly — and prices may reflect that belief.

If macro data stabilizes, extreme scenarios can quickly lose traction.

## Conclusion: Volatility remains constant

Whether Bitcoin ever tests $28,000 will depend on macroeconomic conditions, monetary policy, and investor confidence. A single forecast does not dictate long-term direction — but it can influence short-term sentiment.

One thing remains certain: **volatility is an inherent feature of the crypto market.**
