# Anchorage Enables Borrowing Against Staked SOL Without Leaving Regulated Custody

<small>Image is symbolic.</small>

Anchorage Digital, a federally chartered US crypto bank, has introduced a new framework allowing institutional investors to borrow against staked Solana (SOL) without moving their assets out of regulated custody. The move represents a significant step in bridging traditional finance and decentralized finance (DeFi – Decentralized Finance).

At a time when US lawmakers continue debating how to regulate DeFi markets, this structure demonstrates that institutional capital is not retreating — it is adapting, seeking compliant pathways into blockchain-based liquidity.

## How the structure works

Anchorage has integrated its Atlas collateral management platform with Kamino, a Solana-based DeFi lending protocol. In collaboration with Solana Company, the framework enables institutions to:

- Hold SOL at Anchorage Digital Bank (qualified regulated custody),
- Continue earning staking rewards,
- Simultaneously use the same SOL as collateral for onchain borrowing.

In practical terms, capital remains within a regulated banking environment while gaining access to decentralized liquidity markets.

## What does “staked SOL” mean?

Solana operates under a Proof-of-Stake consensus mechanism, meaning token holders can lock (stake) their SOL to help secure the network and earn rewards.

Previously, institutions wanting to use staked SOL as collateral had to transfer tokens into smart contracts — a step many regulated entities could not take due to compliance and custody restrictions.

Under the new model, collateral remains in segregated, regulated custody. Anchorage manages:

- Loan-to-Value ratios (LTV – Loan-to-Value),
- Margin requirements,
- Potential liquidations if thresholds are breached.

This removes the need for institutions to interact directly with smart contracts or move into self-custody environments.

## Why this matters for institutions

Institutional investors — including asset managers, corporate treasuries and publicly listed companies — operate under strict risk management and regulatory oversight. The ability to combine staking yield with liquidity access while maintaining custody compliance is a meaningful innovation.

The model effectively creates a bridge between TradFi (Traditional Finance) and DeFi ecosystems without abandoning regulatory safeguards.

## Implications for Solana

Solana continues positioning itself as a high-performance blockchain with a growing DeFi ecosystem. If institutional capital increasingly uses SOL not only as an investment asset but also as operational collateral, it could strengthen long-term demand and network stability.

In a broader context of tokenized funds, stablecoins and onchain financial infrastructure, this development signals how future financial markets may evolve: regulated custody paired with decentralized liquidity rails.

## Regulatory backdrop in the United States

This initiative unfolds amid ongoing regulatory discussions in Washington regarding DeFi oversight and market structure. Policymakers are still defining the boundaries of control, responsibility and compliance in decentralized environments.

A custody-based model centered on actual control over assets may offer a practical compromise — institutions retain custody while financial functionality operates on blockchain infrastructure.

If such hybrid structures gain traction, DeFi may gradually transition from experimental finance into institutional-grade infrastructure.
