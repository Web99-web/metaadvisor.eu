# Google Cloud Outage Disrupts Revolut, Banks and Courts Across Europe

**Card payments failed, customers struggled to access banking apps, delivery services reported technical problems, and British courts experienced disruptions to remote hearings. An outage affecting Google Cloud infrastructure in London on October 20, 2026, disrupted Revolut, Lloyds, Halifax, HSBC and other digital services. The incident highlighted how heavily European banking, businesses and public institutions depend on infrastructure operated by a small number of global technology companies.**

Millions of people use digital banks, card payments, online stores and government services every day without considering where the servers supporting those services are located. However, a major infrastructure failure in a Google Cloud region in London demonstrated how quickly a technical problem in one location can affect multiple industries and countries. Although affected services gradually recovered throughout Tuesday, the incident raised a question extending far beyond the temporary unavailability of individual applications: what happens when banks, retailers and public institutions depend on the same underlying technology systems?

## Revolut Was Not the Only Affected Bank

Among the most visible consequences of the outage were disruptions experienced by Revolut customers. The digital bank confirmed difficulties affecting card payments, bank transfers, access to its application and cash withdrawals at ATMs. Some customers were unable to complete everyday transactions, while others reported declined payments and problems checking their account balances.

During the incident, Revolut stated that customers' money and data remained safe and that its teams were working to restore services. Based on the information available, there is no confirmation that the disruption resulted from a cyberattack or that it enabled unauthorized access to customer accounts.

Customers of British banks Lloyds, Halifax and HSBC also reported problems. These included difficulties accessing online and mobile banking services and completing certain transactions. Emma, a personal finance management application, was also among the financial platforms reported to have experienced disruptions.

For customers, the distinction between a problem originating at their bank and one caused by an external technology provider may make little practical difference. When a card is declined at checkout or a banking application becomes unavailable, access to money can be temporarily restricted even when the funds themselves remain secure.

## From Court Hearings to Parcel Deliveries

The broader scale of the incident became clearer as disruption reports emerged outside the financial sector. Britain's courts and tribunals service experienced problems with its Cloud Video Platform, which supports remote court hearings. The platform's limited availability disrupted some proceedings and demonstrated how cloud infrastructure failures can directly affect the functioning of the justice system.

Delivery company DPD also experienced difficulties, with its website reportedly suffering connectivity problems. Disruptions were also reported at Royal Mail, Britain's postal service. This does not mean that all physical parcel deliveries stopped, but certain digital services associated with deliveries and customer support were affected or unavailable.

Other affected online platforms included Rightmove, one of Britain's best-known property websites. Problems were also reported by some retailers, hosting providers and other business services.

British media reports additionally mentioned difficulties involving certain digital services associated with the National Health Service (NHS). However, a direct technical connection to the Google Cloud incident has not been clearly confirmed for every reported disruption. It is therefore important to distinguish officially acknowledged outages from individual user reports.

The incident illustrates that cloud infrastructure is much more than a place to store data. It supports access to applications, database operations, communication between business systems and numerous everyday processes that many organizations now depend on.

## Failure in London, Consequences Across Europe

The disruption originated in Google Cloud's europe-west2 region, specifically its europe-west2-b zone in London. Google confirmed that the incident was associated with a power-related problem that caused infrastructure errors and disrupted the availability of certain services.

Although the technical failure occurred in the United Kingdom, its effects were not limited to British users. Revolut operates in numerous European countries, meaning customers outside the UK also experienced difficulties.

Problems involving Revolut payments, transfers and application access were reported in Ireland, Spain, Italy, France and the Netherlands. However, it has not been confirmed that every simultaneous disruption involving other banks and online services in those countries resulted from the same Google Cloud incident.

This distinction matters because two technical problems occurring at approximately the same time do not necessarily share the same cause. Nevertheless, the fact that an international financial platform can experience difficulties in multiple countries due to a failure in shared infrastructure demonstrates the cross-border nature of modern digital services.

## What Happened Inside Google Cloud?

Google Cloud is one of the world's largest cloud infrastructure providers. Businesses use Google's servers, databases, storage systems, networking services and other technologies instead of operating all the required infrastructure in their own data centers.

This model enables more flexible operations, easier capacity expansion and access to sophisticated infrastructure. However, it also creates shared dependencies. When several organizations rely on the same infrastructure components or interconnected cloud services, a major failure can simultaneously affect numerous companies that appear unrelated.

According to Google's incident records, the disruption affected 38 Google Cloud products, including different categories of infrastructure and data services. This does not mean that 38 companies stopped operating. Rather, multiple Google technology products were affected, potentially disrupting the external organizations that depend on them.

Google's records indicate that the underlying infrastructure incident lasted approximately seven hours and 52 minutes, although individual customer services recovered at different rates. Revolut, for example, continued experiencing difficulties during part of the broader recovery period.

Google announced that a more detailed technical analysis would follow. Such a report could clarify why existing protection and recovery mechanisms did not prevent the wider consequences and whether additional technical factors extended the disruption.

## Why Didn't Backup Systems Prevent the Outage?

Major cloud platforms use several protection mechanisms, including backup power supplies, redundant network infrastructure and the ability to distribute services across different availability zones or regions. However, the existence of these safeguards does not automatically guarantee that every application will continue operating during a major incident.

Organizations using cloud services must design their own systems appropriately, distribute critical components and establish recovery procedures. If an application depends on a database, networking component or authentication service without sufficient alternative arrangements, the failure of a single infrastructure component can affect the entire service.

It is also important to distinguish data security from application availability. Information can remain safely stored while users are temporarily unable to access it because the systems required to retrieve it are unavailable.

Without a final technical report, it is not possible to reliably determine whether the consequences of Google's incident resulted primarily from the scale of the infrastructure failure, the architecture of individual customer applications or a combination of several factors.

## Banks Are Increasingly Dependent on Google, Microsoft and Amazon

The incident has renewed concerns about the concentration of technological infrastructure. A significant portion of the digital economy relies on a small number of major cloud providers, including Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud.

Their infrastructure supports financial institutions, healthcare organizations, online retailers, government agencies and numerous technology businesses. Such concentration does not mean that cloud systems are inherently insecure. However, it does mean that a serious incident affecting one provider can simultaneously disrupt many of its customers.

European regulators have already recognized this risk. In November 2025, the European Supervisory Authorities designated 19 information and communication technology providers as critical to the financial sector under the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA). Google Cloud was among those designated.

DORA establishes requirements related to ICT risk management, reporting major incidents, testing operational resilience and supervising critical external technology providers.

Its objective is not to prohibit banks from using cloud technology, but to ensure that dependence on external companies does not threaten the continuity of essential financial services.

Following the outage, British financial regulators began gathering information about its impact. The incident provides a concrete example of the operational risks that have led European and British authorities to strengthen their oversight of major technology suppliers in recent years.

## Outages Can Also Create Opportunities for Banking Scams

Although there is no confirmation that Google's infrastructure failure resulted from a cyberattack, cybersecurity specialists warn that incidents of this kind can create opportunities for fraud.

Criminals may exploit the fact that customers are aware of a genuine banking disruption by sending fraudulent messages impersonating official support teams. These messages may claim that the previous day's incident requires customers to verify their identities again, update security settings or take urgent action to protect their accounts.

Requests for passwords, one-time security codes, payment card details or transfers to supposedly safe accounts are particularly dangerous. A genuine technical problem can become a convincing pretext for attempted theft.

Customers should verify information exclusively through official banking applications and trusted communication channels. Unexpected links received through text messages, emails or messaging applications should not be opened simply because they refer to a real incident.

Importantly, warnings about possible scams do not constitute evidence that attackers obtained Revolut customer information during the outage.

## What Should Banks and Technology Companies Learn?

The outage demonstrated that digital resilience is not solely about protection against cyberattacks. Technical availability, backup capacity, recovery procedures and transparent communication with customers are equally important.

For banks, ensuring that critical functions such as account access and payment processing are supported by appropriate business continuity plans is particularly important. This includes testing failure scenarios, reviewing dependencies on external providers and establishing clear procedures for situations in which individual infrastructure components become unavailable.

For cloud companies, incidents of this magnitude are also a matter of trust. Customers do not necessarily expect an absolute guarantee that failures will never occur, but they do expect existing safeguards to limit their impact and support recovery as quickly as possible.

For regulators, the central question is how well organizations are actually prepared to continue operating if a major technology provider becomes unavailable for an extended period.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **A single infrastructure failure exposed connections between seemingly unrelated industries.** Revolut, British banks, court video platforms and delivery services may have little in common at first glance, but they can depend on digital infrastructure with the same critical points of failure.
* **Secure money does not necessarily mean accessible money.** Customer funds may remain protected while payments, cash withdrawals or account access temporarily stop working. Service availability is an equally important part of digital trust.
* **Cloud concentration creates significant operational risks.** When numerous financial and public institutions depend on a handful of global providers, the consequences of one incident can extend far beyond the location where it originated.
* **Backup and recovery systems must be tested in practice.** Having a data backup or an additional hosting location is not enough. Organizations need to demonstrate that their most important functions can continue during major infrastructure failures.
* **Europe's DORA regulation is becoming increasingly relevant.** Oversight of critical technology suppliers and digital resilience testing can help reduce risks, but their effectiveness will ultimately depend on implementation.
* **Technical disruptions can create opportunities for fraud.** Customers should be particularly cautious about messages referring to banking outages while requesting security codes, passwords or urgent money transfers.
* **Local processing and offline functionality deserve greater attention.** For certain digital tools, the ability to operate without a constant connection to remote servers reduces dependence on cloud availability, although this approach is not suitable for every type of service.

The Google Cloud outage on October 20 did not cause a complete collapse of Europe's financial system, nor did it mean that money disappeared from customers' bank accounts. However, it demonstrated how quickly a technical problem in a single infrastructure region can become a problem for banks, businesses, public institutions and customers across several countries.

Revolut, Lloyds, Halifax and other affected services gradually restored normal operations, but the most important question remains unresolved: how resilient are Europe's digital systems when their everyday operation depends on a small number of shared technology providers?

**The next major outage may not begin at a bank, hospital or government institution. It could originate in a data center on which all of them depend.**
