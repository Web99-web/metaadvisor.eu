# MiniMax Unveils New AI Model as Stock Slides Toward IPO Price

**Chinese artificial intelligence company MiniMax unveiled its new M3.1 Flash model on October 19, 2026, claiming nearly three times faster performance and a 70% reduction in usage costs. However, the announcement failed to deliver the market recovery investors might have hoped for. Shares of MiniMax Group closed at HK$171.50 on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on October 20, falling another 5.82% during the session. In just over a month, the stock has lost approximately 43% of its value, raising questions about the relationship between technological progress and the financial sustainability of AI companies.**

MiniMax is among the Chinese technology companies seeking to compete with American AI developers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google. The company develops large language models, multimodal AI systems, AI agents, coding tools and technologies for generating video, speech and music. Its products are available to international users, but the sharp decline in its share price during October suggests that technological innovation alone may no longer be enough to sustain investor confidence in its long-term valuation.

## New M3.1 Flash promises faster performance and lower costs

MiniMax introduced M3.1 Flash on October 19, positioning the new AI model for coding, automation and complex tasks performed by AI agents. According to the company, the model can complete certain tasks nearly three times faster than its predecessor while reducing usage costs for agent-based workflows by approximately 70%.

The model supports a context window of up to one million tokens, enabling it to process extensive documents and large software projects. It is available through an API and the MiniMax Code development environment, with the company highlighting improvements in programming, computer interaction and multi-step task execution. However, its performance claims are still largely based on the developer's own testing. Independent comparisons and real-world adoption will be needed to establish how much of an advantage the model offers over competing systems.

## MiniMax shares lose more than 43% of their value

While MiniMax continues to introduce more advanced AI models, its stock has experienced one of its most difficult periods since going public. Shares closed at HK$303 on September 18 before entering a steep decline. On October 8, the stock fell 13.74%, followed by another 12.10% drop on October 15, when it closed at HK$179.50.

Selling pressure intensified again on October 20, when the stock declined 5.82% to HK$171.50, with trading volume reaching approximately 10.95 million shares. During the session, the price fell as low as HK$168.30, approaching its January 2026 IPO price of HK$165. Comparing closing prices between September 18 and October 20, MiniMax lost approximately 43.4% of its market value per share.

What makes the latest decline particularly notable is its timing, coming immediately after the unveiling of M3.1 Flash. This does not mean the new model is a technological failure, but it does suggest that the announcement has not yet been sufficient to reverse negative market sentiment.

## BNP Paribas cuts price target by 30%

Another sign of changing expectations emerged on October 15, when BNP Paribas lowered its price target for MiniMax from HK$500 to HK$350, a reduction of 30%, while maintaining its Outperform rating. The bank also reduced its target for rival Chinese AI developer Zhipu AI from HK$1,150 to HK$850, suggesting a broader reassessment of valuations among Chinese companies developing large AI models.

The revised MiniMax price target remains substantially above its current trading level, but analyst forecasts are not guarantees of future stock performance. Such estimates depend on assumptions about revenue growth, development costs, profitability and competitive positioning.

## Are major investors selling their shares?

One of the key questions surrounding the October decline concerns early shareholders and the restrictions governing their ability to sell stock. Following MiniMax's initial public offering, certain holdings were subject to lock-up periods during which investors could not freely dispose of their shares. Some restrictions expired in July, while additional arrangements affecting certain shareholder groups were linked to MiniMax's inclusion in the Stock Connect program.

MiniMax joined Stock Connect on August 6, 2026, allowing eligible mainland Chinese investors to trade its shares. According to the IPO prospectus, some additional selling restrictions could have expired in early September. This means previously locked-up shares may have become available for sale before the sharp October decline.

**However, the ability to sell shares does not prove that major investors actually sold them.** Available checks have not established that the founder or large institutional shareholders disposed of significant holdings on the exchange during the sell-off. Previously reported ownership changes also included transfers between related entities, which should not be confused with ordinary open-market selling. The latest individual disclosures of substantial shareholding changes have not been fully verified.

## New share issuance and rising AI development costs

MiniMax also carried out several capital-related activities during 2026. In July, the company issued new shares and convertible bonds, while additional shares were issued under compensation programs in September. Shareholders also approved authority for potential future share issuance, raising concerns about possible dilution of existing ownership stakes. It is important to distinguish completed share issuances from authorizations that the company may or may not use later.

Developing artificial intelligence requires substantial and continuing investment in computing infrastructure, specialized talent and model training. At the same time, competitors are launching increasingly capable models at lower prices, potentially putting pressure on revenue per unit of usage. MiniMax's claimed 70% cost reduction could provide a competitive advantage, but only if growth in customers and usage is sufficient to offset pricing pressure across the industry.

## Why technological progress is no longer enough

MiniMax illustrates the growing distinction between technological innovation and building a financially sustainable business. A company can introduce a faster, more capable AI model while investors simultaneously question how much that progress costs, how many paying customers it attracts and whether it can generate lasting revenue.

Competition in the global AI market is no longer defined by model capabilities alone. Pricing, reliability, execution speed, API availability and integration into business workflows are becoming equally important. MiniMax is competing against companies with significant financial resources and, in some cases, their own computing infrastructure.

The company's next financial results, growth in commercial model usage and greater transparency around development costs may therefore prove more important for investor confidence than another AI model announcement. Any new HKEX disclosures concerning major shareholder sales or additional capital measures will also be relevant when assessing potential selling pressure on the stock.
