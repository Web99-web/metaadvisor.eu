# Global Market Focus: Fed Warns of Rising Inflation Pressures as Nasdaq Falls More Than 2%

**The Federal Reserve's latest Beige Book report has intensified concerns about the combination of persistent inflation and weakening economic activity. Wall Street responded with another wave of selling, pushing the Nasdaq down more than 2% during the trading session. Technology and semiconductor stocks were among the hardest hit as investors reassessed the outlook for interest rates, corporate costs and economic growth.**

The Federal Reserve released its latest Beige Book on October 14, 2026, at 2:00 p.m. Eastern Time, corresponding to 8:00 p.m. in Zagreb. The report provides an overview of economic conditions across the Fed's twelve districts, based on information collected through October 5.

Although the Beige Book does not represent an interest rate decision, it offers important insight into how the central bank assesses developments in prices, employment, production costs and overall business activity.

The most significant finding was the persistence and broadening of inflation pressures. Three of the Fed's twelve districts reported strong price increases, while another eight recorded moderate increases. Three districts also specifically highlighted intensifying input costs.

## Beige Book Raises Concerns About Weak Growth and Persistent Inflation

The report indicates that higher diesel, transportation and logistics costs continue to spread through supply chains. This development has reinforced concerns that inflation pressures are no longer confined to the energy sector but are increasingly affecting a wider range of goods and services.

At the same time, the labor market offered little reassurance.

According to the Beige Book, employment remained broadly stagnant, with relatively few new hires and limited layoffs. This combination suggests that companies are becoming more cautious about expanding their workforces while remaining reluctant to make substantial staffing reductions.

For investors, the combination of weak hiring momentum and persistent cost pressures creates a difficult economic environment.

It raises concerns that the economy may struggle to generate stronger growth while inflation remains too elevated for the Federal Reserve to consider a rapid easing of monetary policy.

Market participants therefore interpreted the report as another indication that restrictive monetary conditions could remain in place for longer than previously expected.

## Nasdaq Falls More Than 2% as Technology Stocks Weaken

During the US trading session, the Nasdaq declined 2.02%, while the S&P 500 fell 1.35%.

The technology sector lost 2.64%, and semiconductor stocks dropped nearly 4%, placing them among the weakest areas of the market.

Smaller US companies also faced considerable selling pressure. According to reports from American media, the Russell 2000 declined approximately 2.1%, demonstrating that market weakness extended beyond the largest technology companies.

One particularly concerning indicator was the reported 466 new lows on the Nasdaq.

A high number of stocks reaching new lows can signal deteriorating market breadth, meaning that losses are spreading across a wider range of companies rather than being concentrated in a handful of major names.

This distinction matters because headline index movements can sometimes conceal weakness beneath the surface.

When a growing number of individual stocks reach new lows, it may indicate that investor confidence is deteriorating more broadly.

Wall Street is therefore responding not only to individual corporate developments but also to a more challenging macroeconomic environment characterized by persistent inflation, elevated financing costs and uncertainty about future growth.

## Selling Pressure Intensifies Across AI, Technology and Semiconductors

Technology, artificial intelligence infrastructure and semiconductor companies were particularly affected by the latest selloff.

According to the reported market data, CoreWeave shares fell approximately 15%, while Dell declined around 12%.

Semiconductor companies were also among the weakest performers, reflecting growing concerns about investment expectations and the sustainability of valuations across technology-related sectors.

These developments are particularly important because AI infrastructure and semiconductor stocks have been major contributors to optimism in US equity markets.

Companies involved in advanced computing, data center expansion and semiconductor production have attracted substantial investor attention because of expectations surrounding artificial intelligence.

However, these sectors can also be sensitive to shifts in interest rate expectations and investor risk appetite.

When market leadership begins to weaken, investors often interpret the development as a potential sign of a broader change in sentiment.

Semiconductors are especially relevant because their performance is frequently viewed as an indicator of expected demand for computing power, digital infrastructure and technology investment.

Significant declines in this sector may therefore influence market confidence beyond the companies directly affected.

## Capital Rotation: Defensive Sectors Show Relative Strength

While technology companies and smaller-cap stocks recorded substantial losses, defensive sectors demonstrated greater relative resilience.

Healthcare and consumer staples performed comparatively better than the technology sector during the session.

This development suggests that investors may be continuing to rotate capital away from higher-risk market segments toward businesses considered more resilient during periods of economic uncertainty.

Defensive companies often benefit from relatively stable demand for essential goods and services, including healthcare products and everyday consumer necessities.

However, relative strength does not necessarily mean that defensive stocks are rising.

It may simply indicate that they are experiencing smaller losses than the broader market.

The distinction is important when evaluating whether market movements represent a genuine shift toward defensive investments or a broad-based reduction in risk exposure.

## Energy Prices and Transportation Costs Remain Inflation Risks

Energy prices represent another important factor influencing the current market environment.

Brent crude traded near $106.50 per barrel during the session, reinforcing concerns that elevated energy costs could contribute to persistent inflation.

The Beige Book specifically highlighted rising diesel and transportation costs.

Such increases can have consequences far beyond the energy industry because transportation, logistics and distribution expenses affect businesses throughout the economy.

When companies face higher operating costs, they may attempt to pass part of those increases on to customers.

This creates a potential channel through which energy-related inflation can spread into a broader range of consumer prices.

For the Federal Reserve, the situation presents a challenging policy environment.

Economic activity appears to be weakening in some areas, yet important cost pressures remain elevated.

This combination complicates efforts to balance inflation control with the need to avoid an excessive slowdown in economic growth.

## Interest Rate Expectations Rise Again

Following the Beige Book release, market expectations increasingly reflected the possibility that the Federal Reserve could maintain restrictive monetary policy for longer or consider further tightening.

According to market figures cited by Reuters using CME FedWatch data, the estimated probability of an October interest rate increase rose to approximately 37%, compared with 22% the previous day.

However, other estimates have also circulated, reflecting differences in market models, data sources and measurement methods.

These figures should not automatically be considered directly comparable.

They also do not represent an official Federal Reserve forecast or an announcement of future policy decisions.

Instead, they reflect market-implied expectations at a particular moment and can change rapidly as new economic information becomes available.

The broader implication is that investors appear increasingly cautious about expecting near-term monetary easing.

As long as inflation remains persistent, hopes for lower interest rates may remain limited, placing additional pressure on risk-sensitive assets.

## Market Bias Remains Bearish

Taken together, the day's developments support a continued negative short-term market bias.

The losses do not appear to be limited to one isolated sector.

Instead, selling pressure has spread across technology companies, semiconductor stocks, smaller US businesses and other risk-sensitive segments.

The Nasdaq's decline, weakness in semiconductors, losses in the Russell 2000 and the elevated number of new lows collectively suggest deteriorating market breadth and investor sentiment.

For the remainder of the trading session, one of the most important signals will be whether the Nasdaq maintains a decline of more than 2% through the closing bell.

Investors will also be watching whether semiconductor stocks continue to underperform into the final stages of trading.

Because the reported figures reflect intraday market conditions rather than final closing prices, the overall picture may still change before the end of the US session.

* **Market breadth matters more than the Nasdaq decline alone.** When technology stocks, semiconductors and smaller companies weaken simultaneously, accompanied by a large number of new lows, the selling pressure may reflect broader risk aversion rather than ordinary daily volatility.
* **Semiconductors and AI infrastructure remain critical sectors to watch.** These industries have been major drivers of market optimism. Their performance may provide important clues about changing investor expectations and the sustainability of technology-related valuations.
* **Energy and logistics costs are returning to the center of the inflation debate.** Persistently elevated fuel and transportation costs could spread inflation pressures across multiple industries and complicate the Federal Reserve's policy decisions.
* **Markets need stronger evidence that inflation is sustainably declining.** Until that happens, expectations for lower interest rates may remain constrained, leaving risk-sensitive investments vulnerable to further volatility.
* **The US market close will be particularly important for the near-term outlook.** If the Nasdaq remains sharply lower and semiconductor stocks continue to lead the declines, negative sentiment could extend into the next trading session.

The day's market developments highlight how sensitive Wall Street remains to macroeconomic signals.

In an environment of persistent inflation and weakening economic momentum, even modest changes in expectations for interest rates and corporate costs can trigger substantial adjustments in investor risk exposure.

The combination of broad selling pressure, weakness in former market leaders and renewed inflation concerns suggests that investors will continue to closely monitor economic data and Federal Reserve communications.
