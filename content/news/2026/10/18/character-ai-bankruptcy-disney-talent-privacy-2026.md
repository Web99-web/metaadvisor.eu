# Character.AI Had Millions of Users but Still Went Bankrupt: Disney Wants Its Talent, but What Happens to Private Conversations?

**One of the world's best-known artificial intelligence chatbot platforms has entered US bankruptcy proceedings despite attracting approximately 12 million users. Character.AI was losing between $8 million and $9 million per month while facing mounting legal challenges, regulatory scrutiny and high operating costs. Now Disney is pursuing some of its technology and specialists, while American authorities are raising a question that could become crucial for the entire AI industry: what happens to millions of private conversations when the company behind them fails?**

Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on October 12, 2026, allowing the company to reorganize its operations and sell assets under court supervision. Although the platform continues operating for now, its long-term future remains uncertain. Particular attention has focused on the Walt Disney Company's interest in its technology and employees, alongside concerns raised by US state attorneys general about the protection of sensitive user information.

## Millions of Users Were Not Enough to Survive

Founded in 2021, Character.AI quickly became one of the most recognizable platforms for conversations with virtual personalities. Users could create their own AI characters and interact with chatbots portraying fictional figures, historical personalities, virtual friends or entirely original characters. Unlike conventional AI assistants designed primarily for professional tasks, Character.AI gained popularity through entertainment, creative writing, interactive storytelling and extended personal conversations.

This business model attracted millions of users but also created substantial infrastructure expenses. Every conversation requires computing resources, and users who spend hours interacting with virtual characters can generate considerable processing costs. When subscription revenue and other income sources fail to grow quickly enough, greater popularity does not necessarily translate into greater profitability.

According to information disclosed in connection with the bankruptcy proceedings, Character.AI had approximately 12 million users but was recording monthly losses of between $8 million and $9 million. The figures illustrate how difficult it can be to sustain an AI platform where users expect extensive conversations, rapid responses and relatively inexpensive access to advanced models.

Chapter 11 bankruptcy does not automatically mean an immediate shutdown. A company can continue providing services during the proceedings, negotiate with creditors and seek buyers for some or all of its assets. Nevertheless, Character.AI's need for court protection demonstrates that a substantial user base alone was insufficient to support a sustainable business model.

## Lawsuits and Child Safety Concerns Deepened the Crisis

Alongside its financial difficulties, Character.AI has faced serious lawsuits involving the safety of underage users. Some families allege that interactions with chatbots contributed to harmful behavior among children and teenagers, including cases involving self-harm and suicide. These allegations have intensified debate about the responsibilities of technology companies developing AI systems capable of emotionally intense and prolonged conversations.

It is essential to distinguish allegations made in legal proceedings from facts established through final court judgments. Filing a lawsuit does not mean every allegation has been proven. Nevertheless, the seriousness of the claims, potential litigation costs and regulatory scrutiny create significant business risks for a platform whose appeal depends heavily on personal interactions with artificial intelligence.

Regulators are increasingly examining how chatbots communicate with children, whether they can encourage unhealthy emotional attachments and what happens when users perceive an AI system as a friend, adviser or trusted companion. In this environment, safety features are no longer merely optional product enhancements. They are becoming central to legal accountability and long-term business sustainability.

## Disney Pursues Character.AI Technology and 66 Specialists

As Character.AI entered bankruptcy proceedings, one of the world's largest entertainment companies expressed interest in parts of its business. According to published reports, the Walt Disney Company agreed to an initial $20 million bid for a perpetual, nonexclusive license covering certain Character.AI intellectual property and related technology rights.

This offer does not mean Disney has acquired the entire company or that the bankruptcy proceedings have concluded. It represents an initial bid in a court-supervised process where other interested parties may submit competing offers. The final outcome will depend on subsequent decisions and sale conditions.

The employment component is particularly notable. According to media reports, Disney offered positions to 66 Character.AI specialists, with combined annual compensation of approximately $60 million and additional incentives designed to retain employees. These figures demonstrate the substantial value that major technology and entertainment companies place on experienced professionals capable of developing advanced AI systems.

The relationship between the companies is further highlighted by former Character.AI chief executive Karandeep Anand's move into a senior technology position at Disney. Disney's interest therefore extends beyond individual algorithms or licenses to include experienced teams capable of developing future generations of digital products.

## Why Does Disney Want Technology From a Failing AI Company?

Disney owns one of the world's most valuable collections of characters, stories and entertainment franchises. Technology developed by Character.AI could eventually support interactive conversations with virtual characters, personalized digital experiences or new forms of entertainment in which users actively participate in shaping a story. These are potential applications rather than confirmed announcements of specific Disney products.

Interestingly, Disney had previously challenged Character.AI over the unauthorized use of its copyrighted characters. Now the same entertainment giant is interested in technology that could help it create its own controlled and commercially viable AI experiences.

The situation illustrates an important distinction between the value of technology and the commercial success of the company developing it. A startup can build an advanced product and attract a substantial audience yet still run out of money. Meanwhile, its technology, patents, models and specialists may remain extremely valuable to a larger corporation with greater financial resources, established distribution channels and an existing business ecosystem.

For Disney, this technology could eventually become part of a broader digital entertainment strategy. For Character.AI, bankruptcy demonstrates that technological achievements and user popularity were not sufficient to sustain the company's independent operations under its previous business model.

## What Happens to Millions of Private Conversations?

Perhaps the most sensitive aspect of this case has less to do with Disney's plans than with the information users have entrusted to Character.AI over the years. Unlike an ordinary internet search engine, the platform encouraged lengthy and often highly personal conversations with virtual characters. Users could discuss their feelings, family problems, relationships, health concerns and other private experiences.

Such conversations may contain sensitive personal information, including details about mental health, emotional difficulties and the private lives of minors. When a company responsible for storing large volumes of such information enters bankruptcy, questions arise about who may access those records, whether they can be transferred to a new owner and what rights users retain.

A group of 40 US state attorneys general has requested additional protections for user data during the bankruptcy proceedings, particularly information involving children. Their concerns relate to the potential consequences of selling or transferring assets belonging to a company that stores extensive collections of personal conversations.

**There is no confirmation that Disney is purchasing Character.AI users' private conversations.** According to available information, Disney's offer concerns certain technology rights and intellectual property. However, the broader bankruptcy process raises questions about the future handling of user information, making it important to distinguish confirmed elements of the proposed deal from hypothetical scenarios involving future data transfers.

Privacy protection does not end with publishing a privacy policy on a website. What matters is how access rights, retention periods, deletion procedures and potential transfers are actually governed, along with the safeguards required by the court and relevant authorities throughout the proceedings.

## Could Users Lose Access to Their Conversations?

Character.AI continues to provide its service during the bankruptcy proceedings, and existing subscriptions reportedly remain active. The company expects the sale process could conclude in late November or early December 2026, although the actual timeline depends on the progress of the proceedings and court decisions.

If a buyer is found who wishes to continue operating the platform, some or all of its services could remain available. If no suitable buyer emerges or a different distribution of assets is approved, a shutdown is also possible. Neither outcome should currently be presented as a final decision.

For users who have spent years creating their own AI characters, stories and conversations, this presents a practical concern. If the platform shuts down, some content could become inaccessible. It would therefore be sensible for users to check whether official options exist for downloading or exporting their information before any final decision is announced.

It is also important to distinguish exporting data from deleting it. Downloading a copy of conversations does not automatically mean the same information has been removed from all company systems, backups or records that must be retained under applicable law.

## Google Previously Recruited Key Character.AI Talent

Character.AI's present difficulties also have an important history. In 2024, Google entered into a licensing agreement involving the company's technology, while co-founders Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas returned to Google. The arrangement became an example of a growing practice in which major technology companies gain access to AI startup expertise and technology without completing a conventional acquisition of the entire business.

Such arrangements can provide financial benefits and open new development opportunities. At the same time, they can change a smaller company's ability to continue developing independently, particularly when founders and leading researchers depart.

Disney's current interest illustrates how different the market for AI talent can be from the market for AI applications themselves. While one company attempts to resolve a financial crisis, its most experienced employees may become highly sought after by some of the world's largest corporations.

This does not mean the departure of individual employees caused Character.AI's bankruptcy. However, the combination of high operating expenses, competitive pressure, leadership changes and legal challenges created an exceptionally difficult business environment.

## What Does Character.AI's Bankruptcy Reveal About the Future of AI?

Character.AI demonstrates that success in the artificial intelligence industry cannot be measured solely by user numbers, conversation volumes or social media popularity. An AI platform can become a global phenomenon while remaining financially unsustainable if computing infrastructure, development costs and legal obligations grow faster than revenue.

A second major issue concerns the responsibilities of companies developing systems intended for personal and emotional communication. When a chatbot becomes someone's everyday virtual companion, questions about child protection, safety and accountability are no longer secondary matters. They can directly affect business operations, regulatory obligations and a company's future.

A third challenge involves the fate of user data. Traditional bankruptcy proceedings often involve the sale of buildings, equipment, patents or trademarks. In the digital economy, however, a company's assets may also include vast amounts of information entrusted to it by users who expected a certain level of privacy.

For the AI industry, it is therefore becoming increasingly important to plan not only how information is collected and processed while a company grows, but also how users will be protected if its business model fails.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **Millions of users do not guarantee profitability.** Character.AI shows that the popularity of an AI product can grow alongside substantial financial losses, particularly when lengthy conversations and infrastructure generate exceptionally high costs.
* **Disney is seeking access to expertise, not necessarily the entire company.** Interest in technology and 66 specialists demonstrates that employees and intellectual property can retain considerable value even when a startup enters bankruptcy.
* **Private conversations must not become an afterthought in bankruptcy proceedings.** Users, especially minors, deserve clear information about who manages their data and what could happen to it.
* **There is no confirmation that Disney is acquiring user conversations.** Its reported offer concerns specific technology rights, while the fate of other assets and information depends on the ongoing legal process.
* **The safety of AI companions is becoming a business issue.** Lawsuits and regulatory investigations show that companies must seriously address the consequences of emotional interactions between users and chatbots, particularly when children are involved.
* **The departure of specialists can reshape an AI startup's future.** Agreements involving Google and Disney demonstrate the intense competition for experienced AI researchers and development teams.
* **AI companies must prepare for the possibility of shutting down.** User rights, data exports, deletion procedures and service continuity should not be addressed only after a financial crisis emerges.

Character.AI has become an example of one of the greatest contradictions in today's AI industry: a company can attract millions of users, develop recognizable technology and build highly valuable expert teams while failing to establish a sustainable business model. Disney's interest shows that technology may continue to evolve beyond the company that originally developed it, but responsibility toward the people who used that technology must not disappear in the process.

**Perhaps the most important question in this story is not who will buy Character.AI's technology, but who will take responsibility for the millions of private conversations users entrusted to the platform.**
