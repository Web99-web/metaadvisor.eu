# DeepSeek Nears $15 Billion in New Funding as Chinese AI Company Prepares for IPO

**Chinese artificial intelligence company DeepSeek is nearing the completion of a new funding round worth approximately 100 billion yuan, or nearly $15 billion. According to information reported by Bloomberg on October 22 and subsequently covered by Reuters, technology giant Tencent and battery manufacturer CATL are among the major investors. The financing could strengthen DeepSeek's position in its global competition with leading US AI labs, but one distinction is essential: the company has not gone public. This is a private fundraising round ahead of a possible initial public offering.**

DeepSeek attracted worldwide attention in early 2025 with models that challenged assumptions about how expensive it must be to develop competitive artificial intelligence. Today, the same company is seeking billions of dollars to expand its computing infrastructure, develop new models and compete for top engineering talent. Its evolution shows that more efficient model development does not eliminate the need for substantial investment when AI technology scales to a global market.

## Funding Round Expands to Nearly $15 Billion

According to Reuters' October 22 report, DeepSeek is close to completing a funding round of approximately 100 billion yuan. That is roughly twice its original target of 50 billion yuan, with the transaction expected to close in the coming days. The final amount and terms may still change. DeepSeek, Tencent and CATL had not immediately confirmed all the details when the reports were published.

Strong investor interest follows an earlier financing round. According to previous Reuters reporting, DeepSeek raised approximately $7.4 billion in June 2026 in its first major external funding round. In July, reports emerged of a further capital raise targeting a pre-money company valuation of around 500 billion yuan, or approximately $74 billion.

That valuation should not be confused with a public market capitalization. DeepSeek does not yet have publicly traded shares, and the price at which its stock might eventually trade is unknown. If the 500 billion yuan pre-money valuation remains unchanged and the entire additional 100 billion yuan is invested as equity, the implied post-money valuation would be approximately 600 billion yuan. This is only an illustrative calculation, not a confirmed valuation for a completed transaction.

## Why Are Tencent and CATL Investing in DeepSeek?

Tencent is one of China's largest technology groups, operating across digital services, communication platforms and computing infrastructure. Its interest in DeepSeek fits into the broader competition among Chinese technology companies to gain an advantage in artificial intelligence.

CATL's involvement is particularly notable because the company is best known for electric vehicle batteries and energy systems. This does not mean CATL is becoming an AI research lab. It does, however, show that artificial intelligence is increasingly attracting investment from major industrial businesses as well as software companies. For these investors, AI may represent a long-term technological and strategic opportunity rather than simply a short-term financial trade.

Public reports have not yet disclosed the full structure of the transaction, the individual amounts contributed by every investor or the rights they will receive. Those details will matter when assessing DeepSeek's actual valuation and who will retain control over the company.

## DeepSeek Is Not Publicly Traded Yet: Shanghai IPO Plans

In September, Reuters reported that DeepSeek had hired CITIC Securities to prepare for a potential listing on China's STAR Market in Shanghai. This exchange segment focuses on technology and innovation-driven businesses. A debut in 2027 has been discussed as a possibility, but no official IPO prospectus, trading date, offer price or share count has been confirmed.

An initial public offering, or IPO, is the process through which a company offers shares to public investors for the first time and enters regular stock exchange trading. DeepSeek's current private funding round is not an IPO. The nearly $15 billion headline therefore does not mean that retail investors can already buy DeepSeek shares on a stock exchange.

If the listing goes ahead, it could become an important test of investor demand for Chinese AI companies, particularly as the costs of developing models and operating data centers continue to rise. It also remains unclear to what extent international investors would be able to participate in a potential offering.

## From Cost-Efficient AI Models to Billions in Infrastructure Spending

DeepSeek built its global reputation by demonstrating that certain advanced AI models could deliver competitive performance through more efficient use of computing resources. Its breakthrough in early 2025 challenged widespread assumptions about AI development costs and contributed to significant volatility in technology stocks.

However, the cost of training an individual model is not the same as the total expense of operating a major AI business. Continuous development requires skilled researchers, data centers, energy infrastructure and enough computing capacity to serve a growing user base. Fresh capital could allow DeepSeek to expand more rapidly, but it also raises investor expectations about future revenue.

China's AI industry must also contend with US export restrictions on advanced semiconductors. Those limitations have increased the importance of domestic technology suppliers and more efficient use of available hardware. Against this backdrop, DeepSeek's financing has broader strategic significance for China's technology sector.

## A High Valuation Does Not Guarantee High Profits

Although the new round signals strong investor demand, important questions remain unanswered. Investors will need to assess how much revenue DeepSeek can generate from its models and services, how quickly operating costs may increase and whether it can remain competitive against leading US labs and other Chinese AI developers over the long term.

One particular challenge is monetizing a business built partly around open or widely accessible AI models. That approach can accelerate adoption and ecosystem development, but it does not automatically translate into substantial revenue. Fundraising supports continued growth, while sustainable profitability still needs to be demonstrated.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **DeepSeek has not raised this money through a public stock market offering.** The news concerns a private funding round nearing completion, while a possible IPO remains in preparation.
* **Nearly $15 billion signals exceptional investor interest, but the amount is not final.** The terms may still change before the transaction officially closes.
* **Tencent and CATL's involvement shows how strategically important AI has become beyond the software industry.** Major technology and industrial companies want exposure to its long-term development.
* **DeepSeek challenges the simplistic idea that more efficient AI models automatically mean a low-cost AI business.** Developing a single model and operating a global service for millions of users involve very different cost structures.
* **A reported pre-money valuation of about $74 billion does not guarantee a future stock price.** The final funding terms and any eventual IPO will offer a clearer picture of what investors are willing to pay.
* **Monetization will be the key test.** Technological breakthroughs and enormous funding rounds create lasting value for investors only if the company can build sustainable revenue and a viable business.

DeepSeek has evolved from a symbol of more efficient and relatively low-cost artificial intelligence into one of the most prominent candidates for major new investment in China's technology sector. Its next challenge is no longer simply proving that it can develop capable models, but demonstrating that it can build a sustainable business in an industry where infrastructure spending continues to grow.

**A potential IPO in 2027 could reveal whether today's enthusiasm among private investors translates into equally strong demand from the broader market. Until then, reported funding targets, completed investment rounds and actual public trading in shares must be clearly distinguished.**
