# GLOBAL MARKET WATCH: Bitcoin Attracts Millions as AI Stocks Fall Again

**US spot Bitcoin ETFs attracted $524.8 million in net inflows on October 21, while Ethereum ETFs ended a streak of outflows and the Nasdaq closed higher. However, optimism did not extend across all markets. Asian semiconductor manufacturers suffered renewed losses on October 22, while Brent crude traded above $110 a barrel amid escalating tensions in the Persian Gulf. Global financial markets are sending conflicting signals: institutional capital is returning to selected risk assets, but concerns over AI investment, inflation and geopolitical risks continue to limit the recovery.**

Global financial markets are approaching the final days of October with increasingly divergent performances across sectors. Following Wall Street's positive close on October 21, investors had hoped for a continued recovery in technology stocks, but Thursday's Asian trading session demonstrated that confidence remains fragile. At the same time, Bitcoin ETFs are attracting renewed institutional inflows, while most altcoins have yet to show comparable strength. Rising energy prices and new security concerns in the Persian Gulf are adding further pressure.

## Bitcoin ETFs attract more than half a billion dollars

According to Farside Investors, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $524.8 million in net inflows on October 21, compared with just $23.2 million during the previous trading session. The largest contributions came from BlackRock's IBIT fund, which attracted $235 million, and Fidelity's FBTC, which added another $212.7 million.

Ethereum ETFs also recorded positive net inflows of $14.9 million, their first positive daily result following five consecutive trading sessions of outflows. However, the recovery remains uneven. BlackRock's Ethereum fund ETHA reported $29.3 million in outflows on the same day, illustrating differences in investor demand across individual funds.

Combined, Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs attracted approximately $539.7 million during the latest completed US trading session. This represents a significant shift in institutional fund flows, but it does not automatically mean cryptocurrency prices will continue rising. ETF inflows may reflect long-term accumulation or shorter-term changes in investor positioning.

## Altcoins lag as PEPE loses momentum after strong gains

Despite improving institutional flows, the broader cryptocurrency market has yet to confirm a strong upward trend. During the morning snapshot on October 22, Bitcoin was trading around $82,777, while Ethereum stood near $2,439, both showing relatively small daily changes. Meanwhile, TAO, AAVE and HYPE remained under pressure, while Zcash maintained relative strength following a stronger seven-day rally.

PEPE, a meme token built on the Ethereum blockchain, presents an interesting example. After significant gains over the preceding days, its momentum has slowed. PEPE often reacts to changes in sentiment surrounding Ethereum, but with considerably larger price swings. This makes it one of several potential indicators of speculative investors' willingness to take on greater risk.

However, Ethereum currently lacks sufficient upward momentum to interpret PEPE's recent strength as confirmation of a broader altcoin recovery. The movement of a single meme token cannot serve as a reliable indicator of the direction of the entire cryptocurrency market.

## Asian semiconductor manufacturers face renewed selling pressure

The most significant negative development during the morning trading session on October 22 came from Asian stock markets. South Korea's KOSPI fell approximately 2.6%, Japan's Nikkei declined around 1.6%, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost roughly 2.7%. Among the companies facing notable declines were Samsung Electronics, down about 3.9%, SK Hynix, down 4.7%, Tokyo Electron, down 5.9%, and SoftBank, down 5.6%.

These declines are particularly important because they followed a positive close in the US technology sector. The Nasdaq gained approximately 1.05% on October 21, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) rose around 2.1%. The divergence between US and Asian trading suggests that investors remain divided over the future valuations of AI infrastructure providers and semiconductor companies.

One of the main concerns involves the enormous costs of building data centers and financing artificial intelligence development. As technology companies continue expanding their investments, shareholders are increasingly questioning when those expenditures will generate sufficiently strong and sustainable financial returns.

## Wall Street rises, but AI investment concerns remain

US stock markets ended October 21 with gains. The Nasdaq rose approximately 1.05%, the S&P 500 advanced 0.58%, and the Dow Jones gained 0.69%. Technology was among the strongest sectors, supported by earnings reports from major companies and renewed interest in stocks associated with artificial intelligence.

However, positive quarterly results are no longer enough to eliminate investor concerns. Markets are paying closer attention to the costs of expanding data centers, financing computing infrastructure and the possibility that future earnings may not justify current spending levels.

US monetary policy is creating additional uncertainty. The Federal Reserve's next interest rate decision is expected on October 28, while the yield on the US 10-year Treasury note was around 5.1% during the morning. Elevated capital costs can be particularly challenging for companies whose valuations depend heavily on expectations of future growth.

## Brent above $110 as tensions rise in the Persian Gulf

Alongside concerns about artificial intelligence, global markets are facing growing energy-related risks. Brent crude was trading above $110 per barrel on the morning of October 22, supported by tensions in the Middle East and concerns about the safety of international shipping routes.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed that four vessels had been struck in the Persian Gulf, while three others allegedly encountered naval mines west of the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters reported the Iranian claims, but not all circumstances surrounding the incidents have been independently verified.

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most important routes for oil transportation. Any significant disruption to maritime traffic could increase shipping and energy costs, further complicating efforts to control inflation. If oil prices remain elevated, central banks may face even more difficult monetary policy decisions.

## Capital returns selectively, but a global recovery remains unconfirmed

The most important characteristic of the current market environment is not a complete withdrawal from risk assets, but growing selectivity among investors. Bitcoin ETFs are attracting substantial institutional capital, US technology stocks are experiencing intermittent recoveries, while Asian semiconductor manufacturers and parts of the altcoin market continue to show weakness.

These movements do not necessarily represent a lasting change in market direction. One day of ETF inflows does not guarantee higher Bitcoin prices, just as one day of semiconductor losses does not confirm a long-term downtrend. However, the combination of sharp technology stock fluctuations, elevated energy prices and monetary policy uncertainty indicates that investors remain cautious.

A sustained global recovery will depend on upcoming earnings reports from major US technology companies, Wall Street's reaction to the latest Asian sell-off, the Federal Reserve's next decision and developments surrounding security in the Persian Gulf.

* **The decline in Asian semiconductor stocks is particularly significant following the Nasdaq's positive close.** Markets continue to question the financial sustainability of massive investments in AI infrastructure, and the US technology rebound has not received equally strong confirmation in Asia.
* **Elevated oil prices represent an additional inflation risk.** Prolonged pressure on energy prices could limit central banks' ability to ease monetary policy.
* **Geopolitical claims must be distinguished from independently verified events.** Reports of incidents in the Persian Gulf require further confirmation, particularly when they have the potential to significantly influence financial and energy markets.
* **Markets currently reflect selective risk-taking rather than broad global optimism.** Capital is returning to certain sectors, but differences between US, Asian and cryptocurrency markets highlight the fragility of the recovery.

Global markets are sending two very different messages on October 22. On one hand, strong Bitcoin ETF inflows and Wall Street's positive performance suggest that investors have not abandoned risk assets. On the other, renewed declines in Asian semiconductor stocks, weakness across parts of the cryptocurrency market and rising energy prices demonstrate that significant reasons for caution remain.

**The next major test will be how US markets respond to the Asian sell-off. If Wall Street also experiences renewed selling pressure in AI and semiconductor stocks, the divergence between institutional optimism toward Bitcoin and concerns surrounding the technology sector could become even more pronounced.**
