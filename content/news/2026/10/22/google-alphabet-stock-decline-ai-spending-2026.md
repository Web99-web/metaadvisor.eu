# Google Loses Roughly One-Fifth of Its Value: Why Alphabet Stock Is Falling Despite Strong Revenue and AI Growth

**Google parent Alphabet is facing renewed selling pressure in the stock market even as its financial results show strong revenue growth, particularly in cloud computing. Alphabet shares (GOOGL) continued to decline on October 22, 2026, after already moving significantly below their May peak of $408.61 in previous trading sessions. The pressure on Google is part of a broader technology sector correction, but it also reflects a growing concern on Wall Street: technology companies are investing hundreds of billions of dollars in artificial intelligence, while investors increasingly demand evidence that these investments will generate sufficient returns.**

Alphabet finds itself in an unusual position. Revenue is growing, Google Cloud is experiencing exceptional demand, and artificial intelligence is becoming an increasingly important part of the company's business. Nevertheless, its stock continues to lose value because investors are no longer focused solely on growth. They are also examining how much that growth costs. The central question is how much money Alphabet must invest in infrastructure before its AI investments begin generating the expected returns.

## Why Is Google Stock Falling on October 22?

US stock markets experienced another wave of selling in technology and AI-related shares on October 22. Concerns about the enormous cost of developing artificial intelligence, reactions to major technology companies' earnings reports and geopolitical uncertainty intensified pressure on the sector.

Meta attracted particular attention after its latest financial results showed strong revenue growth alongside increasing financial pressure from investments in AI infrastructure. Its shares fell sharply following the announcement, adding to nervousness across the technology sector. Investors are increasingly questioning whether revenue generated by artificial intelligence can grow quickly enough to justify massive investments in data centers, advanced chips and computing capacity.

Alphabet had already been under pressure on October 21, when its shares declined even as the Nasdaq index advanced. That divergence suggests investor concerns are not solely related to broader market conditions but also to expectations surrounding Google's business. Additional uncertainty comes from Alphabet's approaching third-quarter earnings announcement, scheduled for October 28.

## Alphabet Plans to Spend Up to $205 Billion in 2026

Google has significantly increased its investments in recent years, expanding data centers, servers, Gemini AI models, proprietary Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) and the infrastructure required to operate advanced artificial intelligence systems. According to its published financial figures, Alphabet expects capital expenditures of between $195 billion and $205 billion in 2026, illustrating the extraordinary scale of the global AI infrastructure race.

These investments have a clear long-term business rationale. Google wants to secure sufficient computing capacity for its own products and for companies using Google Cloud. Demand for infrastructure used to train and run AI models continues to grow rapidly, while access to advanced chips and modern data centers has become a major competitive advantage.

However, building this infrastructure requires enormous upfront investments, electricity, advanced processors and continuous maintenance. Companies must spend substantial amounts today, while much of the expected revenue and financial benefit may not arrive until future years. This imbalance between immediate investment costs and future profits has become one of the main reasons investors are reassessing the valuations of major technology companies.

## Google Cloud Grows 82%, but Investors Remain Concerned

Alphabet's second-quarter 2026 financial results demonstrate the paradox behind its declining share price. The company reported total revenue of $119.8 billion, representing year-over-year growth of 24%. Meanwhile, Google Cloud generated $24.8 billion in revenue, an increase of 82% compared with the same period a year earlier.

Such growth rates are exceptionally strong for a company of Alphabet's size. They demonstrate that demand for Google's services and AI infrastructure is substantial. The problem is not a lack of revenue but uncertainty about how much additional capital the company will need to maintain that growth and convert it into sustainable cash flow.

Investors are increasingly distinguishing between companies already generating revenue from artificial intelligence and those primarily spending money to develop it. Alphabet belongs to both groups. Its AI-related businesses are growing rapidly, but the company is simultaneously financing one of the largest infrastructure investment programs in its history.

## Tens of Billions Raised Through Share Issuances

Alphabet's massive capital-raising initiative in June 2026 added another layer of concern. The company announced financing through the issuance of new shares and other equity instruments, with the total announced offering exceeding $80 billion. According to financial documents accompanying its second-quarter results, Alphabet generated approximately $49.6 billion in net proceeds from equity instruments issued during June.

Part of this capital is intended to finance AI infrastructure and expand computing capacity. Raising funds through equity can reduce the need for additional borrowing, but it also raises concerns among existing shareholders about potential dilution of their ownership stakes.

Dilution occurs when a company issues additional shares, potentially reducing the percentage ownership held by existing investors unless they participate in the new offering. In other words, investors are not only evaluating how much profit Google can generate but also how much of that future profit will be attributable to each individual share. If earnings growth fails to keep pace with an increasing share count, the stock may remain under pressure even as the company's total revenue continues to rise.

## Could Artificial Intelligence Threaten Google's Advertising Business?

Beyond rising costs, investors face another important long-term question: how will generative artificial intelligence change the way people search for information online? Google has generated a substantial portion of its revenue from advertising associated with traditional internet searches for decades. AI assistants, however, increasingly allow users to receive direct answers without visiting numerous websites.

Google is responding by integrating Gemini and additional AI capabilities into its search engine. At the same time, it must adapt a business model that has delivered exceptional profitability for many years. The key question is not whether Google will lose all its search users, but whether it can preserve its advertising revenue as search behavior evolves.

So far, there is no evidence of a sudden collapse in Google's core business. On the contrary, Alphabet reported 19% revenue growth in its search business during the first quarter of 2026. Nevertheless, investors value stocks not only on current financial performance but also on expectations for future earnings.

## Meta, Chipmakers and AI Infrastructure Companies Are Also Under Pressure

Alphabet's decline comes during a period of increasing nervousness across global artificial intelligence markets. Investors are reassessing the business models of major technology companies, semiconductor manufacturers and AI infrastructure providers. Concerns about a potential slowdown in the AI investment cycle have spread throughout several parts of the technology sector.

The risks are not identical across industries. Semiconductor manufacturers depend on continued large orders from data center operators, while companies such as Google, Meta and Microsoft must demonstrate that their investments will eventually generate sufficient profit growth. If major customers slow their infrastructure expansion, suppliers could also experience significant consequences.

Higher energy prices, financing costs and geopolitical tensions are creating additional pressure. Rising electricity costs are particularly important for data centers, which require large amounts of power and reliable energy supplies. As a result, fears surrounding the sustainability of the AI investment cycle are affecting not only smaller speculative businesses but also some of the world's largest technology corporations.

## What Should Investors Expect From Google's October 28 Earnings?

Alphabet's third-quarter earnings announcement could become one of the most important short-term events for its share price. Investors will closely examine whether Google Cloud can maintain exceptionally high growth rates, how AI infrastructure costs are developing, how capital expenditures affect free cash flow and whether search and advertising revenue continue to expand.

Positive earnings do not necessarily guarantee a higher stock price. If market expectations exceed the company's actual performance or Alphabet announces another increase in capital expenditures, shares could continue declining despite revenue growth. Conversely, convincing evidence of improving AI profitability and better cost control could help restore investor confidence.

It is important to distinguish between a temporary market correction and a long-term deterioration in business performance. Alphabet continues to generate substantial revenue, maintains a strong market position and operates an extensive technology infrastructure. However, these advantages do not guarantee future share price appreciation.

## Our Take (Metaadvisor)

* **Google's loss of roughly one-fifth of its market value from its May peak does not mean the company has lost its competitive position.** Its latest published results continue to show strong revenue growth, particularly in Google Cloud.
* **Alphabet's biggest challenge is currently not a lack of demand but the extraordinary cost of building the infrastructure required for artificial intelligence.** Investors want evidence that these expenditures can generate sustainable profits within a reasonable timeframe.
* **Planned capital expenditures of up to $205 billion in a single year represent an enormous financial commitment even for a company of Google's size.** The risk is not simply the amount being spent but the possibility that future revenue may fail to meet expectations.
* **Google Cloud's 82% revenue growth is particularly striking while Alphabet shares continue to decline.** This demonstrates how investor expectations have changed: strong growth is no longer enough when maintaining it requires continuously increasing investments.
* **The issuance of new shares has raised additional concerns about ownership dilution and future earnings per share.** Investors are therefore paying closer attention not only to business growth but also to how that growth is financed.
* **Google has important advantages over many smaller AI companies.** Its profitable existing businesses, proprietary chips, established infrastructure and global user base provide greater financial resilience, although they do not eliminate the risks associated with poor investment decisions.
* **The October 28 earnings report could become a crucial test of market confidence.** The central question will not simply be how much revenue has increased, but how much Google has had to spend to achieve that growth.

Alphabet shares are not falling because artificial intelligence has become irrelevant or because Google has stopped growing. They are under pressure because investors are increasingly questioning the economic sustainability of AI investments measured in hundreds of billions of dollars. Alphabet has become one of the clearest examples of a changing mood on Wall Street, where nearly unconditional optimism about artificial intelligence is giving way to demands for measurable evidence of profitability.

**The next major test arrives on October 28. Google will need to demonstrate not only how quickly its AI business is growing, but also whether that growth can ultimately justify the extraordinary cost of competing in the global artificial intelligence race.**
