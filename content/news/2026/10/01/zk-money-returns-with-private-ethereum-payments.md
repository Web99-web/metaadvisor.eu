# zk.money Returns After Three Years and Brings Private Payments Back to Ethereum

**Aztec Labs has relaunched zk.money, a wallet for private crypto payments that was shut down three years ago. The new version runs on the Aztec Network, a privacy-focused network connected to Ethereum, and allows users to send stablecoin payments without publicly exposing balances, payment amounts or recipients. Privacy has a clear boundary, however: deposits from Ethereum and withdrawals back to Ethereum remain visible on the public blockchain.**

The return of zk.money comes at a time when privacy is once again becoming a major topic on Ethereum. A standard Ethereum wallet is public by design, meaning anyone who knows a particular address can view its balance and transaction history. That can allow a business partner to see how much a company pays other suppliers, or let someone who received a payment once continue tracking future activity from the same wallet.

## What does zk.money actually hide?

The new version of zk.money moves funds onto the Aztec Network, where payments between users are handled privately. According to Aztec documentation, balances, payment amounts and the people involved in a transaction are hidden inside the system, so those details do not appear on the public Ethereum blockchain.

Users can deposit DAI, USDC or USDT from Ethereum. USDC and USDT are converted into DAI on entry, meaning DAI is currently the only currency used inside the zk.money system. Payments can also be simpler than standard crypto transfers because funds can be sent to a readable name such as `bob.zk.money` or through a payment link, without manually copying a long wallet address.

## Privacy begins only after entering Aztec

The most important limitation is that zk.money does not make Ethereum itself private. If a user deposits funds into zk.money from a public Ethereum address, the Ethereum blockchain still shows the address, amount and time of that transaction. The same applies when funds are later withdrawn back to Ethereum.

Only activity inside the Aztec Network becomes private. That means zk.money is not a tool that makes a user’s entire financial activity invisible, but rather one that primarily hides what happens after funds enter the private part of the system.

## The wallet is self-custodial

Aztec Labs specifically emphasizes that zk.money is self-custodial. Users control their own keys, while the wallet operator cannot spend, move or freeze their funds. There is no privileged administrator key that would allow Aztec Labs to take control of a user wallet.

That model also increases user responsibility. If a passkey is lost, Aztec cannot restore access because it does not keep a backup. Self-custody therefore increases control, but also the risk of permanent loss of access if login credentials are not stored safely.

## The previous zk.money had more than 75,000 wallets

This is not Aztec Labs’ first attempt to make private payments more accessible to Ethereum users. The first version of zk.money launched in 2021 and attracted more than 75,000 unique wallet addresses before it was shut down, while processing more than $100 million in transactions.

The original product ran on Aztec Connect infrastructure, which the company later discontinued because it was difficult to scale and expand with new features. The new version runs on a different architecture, the Aztec Network, so the return of zk.money should not be viewed simply as switching the old product back on.

## The Alpha version has a limit below $2,500

The new version is currently in Alpha and is deliberately limited. Every individual deposit, payment or withdrawal must be below $2,500, clearly showing that the product is not yet intended as a finished payment system for larger amounts.

CoinDesk also reports that certain addresses are screened under a sanctions policy before deposit and withdrawal transactions. Privacy inside the Aztec Network therefore does not mean that compliance mechanisms are completely absent at the edges of the system.

## The relaunch comes while Aztec continues to improve security

Another important point is that zk.money is returning while Aztec is still working on improvements to its security infrastructure. Unchained reported on a previously discovered issue in the network’s proving system, which is why limits on individual transactions are an important part of reducing potential risk during the product’s early phase.

That does not mean the new version of zk.money is currently compromised, but it does mean it should be viewed as an early-stage product that is still being developed. Aztec also experienced incidents during the year involving old smart contracts from discontinued products, but the company says those contracts are not part of the current Aztec Network.

## Mobile app and DeFi integrations are expected later

zk.money is currently available on the web, and Aztec Labs is also planning a mobile app. The company is additionally working on integrations with DeFi protocols on Ethereum, which could extend privacy beyond simple stablecoin payments.

If those integrations are implemented, the private wallet could become only the first layer of a broader financial system in which users access DeFi services without publicly exposing every detail of their activity.

## Ethereum is taking privacy more seriously again

The return of zk.money comes as Ethereum developers are also considering changes that could give privacy applications a larger role in future network upgrades. Standard Ethereum transactions would still remain public, meaning privacy would continue to come mainly through additional applications and specialized network layers.

That raises a broader question again: should a digital payment automatically mean that a person’s financial history becomes publicly visible? With a bank account, someone who receives payment for dinner does not automatically gain access to all previous transactions. On a public blockchain, that can happen once an identity is linked to a particular wallet address.

## Privacy is not the same as anonymity

zk.money is not a fully anonymous system. Deposits and withdrawals involving Ethereum remain public, while a readable name such as `name.zk.money` can be linked to a specific user if that person uses it publicly. In some situations, timing and amount analysis may also help connect incoming and outgoing transactions.

It is therefore more accurate to describe zk.money as providing private payments rather than complete anonymity. The system reduces the amount of financial information visible to the public, but it does not erase every trace of user activity.

## Our view

* **The most important thing about zk.money is not only hiding the amount.** A standard Ethereum wallet publicly exposes financial history, while zk.money tries to separate an individual payment from a complete view of a user’s finances.
* **Privacy has a clear boundary.** Ethereum deposits and withdrawals remain public, while privacy begins inside the Aztec Network.
* **The new version is not a return to the old technology.** The original zk.money was shut down after scaling limitations, while the new product runs on the new Aztec Network infrastructure.
* **More than 75,000 wallets and over $100 million in volume in the old version show that interest in this type of product is not merely theoretical.**
* **The sub-$2,500 limit is an important reminder that this is an Alpha product.** Privacy does not remove technical risk or the fact that the infrastructure is still being developed.
* **Self-custody increases user control, but also user responsibility.** Aztec cannot freeze funds, but it also cannot restore a lost passkey.
* **The most interesting development may come later with DeFi integrations.** If Aztec can connect privacy with a broader part of the Ethereum financial ecosystem, zk.money could become more than a private payment wallet.
* **The broader story is the return of privacy as a serious Ethereum theme.** The question is no longer only whether blockchain can be transparent, but how much financial data actually needs to be public.
