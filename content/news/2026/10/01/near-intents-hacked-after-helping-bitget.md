# NEAR Intents Hacked for $3.8 Million After Helping Block Funds From the Bitget Attack

**Cross-chain platform NEAR Intents suffered a security incident worth around $3.8 million after a bug in the interaction between its Omni deposit and withdrawal infrastructure and a NEAR Intents smart contract. The platform temporarily paused parts of its services, patched the vulnerability and promised to fully reimburse affected users. The incident comes only days after the same protocol helped block transfers linked to the major Bitget hack.**

NEAR Intents is designed to simplify trading across different blockchains. Instead of asking users to choose a bridge, exchange and transfer route themselves, users specify the result they want, while independent market makers known as solvers compete to execute the transaction. According to the platform, it has processed more than $30 billion in volume across roughly 35 blockchains.

## Bug affected deposits and withdrawals

NEAR Intents said the security incident was caused by a bug in the way its Omni deposit and withdrawal infrastructure interacted with a NEAR Intents smart contract. The preliminary estimated loss is around $3.8 million, and the team said the contract-side vulnerability has already been patched to prevent similar attacks.

After discovering the incident, the platform temporarily paused parts of its cross-chain services and disabled deposits and withdrawals on several networks while additional checks were completed. The exact scope of the affected components is expected to be explained in more detail in a technical post-mortem report that NEAR Intents said it would publish in the coming days.

## Users promised full reimbursement

NEAR Intents said affected users will be fully compensated for lost funds. The incident has been reported to law enforcement, while the team says it is working with security firms and blockchain analytics partners to trace the stolen assets and pursue recovery.

That is important because the initial announcement does not yet provide a complete technical sequence of the attack. We know where the vulnerability was located and the approximate size of the loss, but not yet exactly how the attacker exploited the bug or how long the vulnerability existed before it was discovered.

## ZachXBT: funds moved through KuCoin into Bitcoin

Blockchain investigator ZachXBT said the irregular withdrawals began from a hot wallet linked to NEAR Intents on BNB Chain. According to his analysis, the stolen funds were then sent to KuCoin and later converted into Bitcoin.

That type of movement is not unusual in crypto attacks, as attackers often try to quickly change the form of stolen assets and move them across different networks to make tracking more difficult.

The attacker’s identity had not been confirmed at the time of writing. Some secondary sources attempted to link the incident to known hacking groups, but there is not yet enough solid public evidence to present such attribution as fact.

## The NEAR Protocol blockchain itself was not hacked

It is important to distinguish NEAR Intents from the underlying NEAR Protocol blockchain. Information released so far points to a vulnerability in NEAR Intents’ cross-chain infrastructure and smart contract interaction, rather than a compromise of the consensus mechanism or the core security of the NEAR blockchain.

That distinction matters because an application can have its own security weaknesses even when the underlying blockchain is functioning normally. In this case, current information suggests the problem was in the way different components of the NEAR Intents system communicated during deposits and withdrawals.

The NEAR token still fell during the day, but the price move cannot be attributed solely to the exploit because broader crypto market conditions were also changing at the same time.

## NEAR Intents had just helped against Bitget hackers

The timing of the incident is particularly striking. Only a few days earlier, NEAR Intents said its security system had identified more than $50 million in attempted transfers linked to attackers behind the Bitget hack.

That figure needs to be interpreted correctly. NEAR Intents did not freeze the entire $50 million. Most of the amount represented attempted transfers that were identified and rejected, while just over $500,000 was actually frozen during the process.

That makes the latest incident especially ironic: a protocol that had just helped stop the movement of stolen funds has now become the victim of an exploit itself.

## Bitget hack was almost 100 times larger

Bitget suffered one of the largest crypto attacks of the year in late September, with losses of more than $350 million. Later estimates pushed the potential total even higher, while the exchange paused withdrawals and said it would cover losses using its own reserves and protection fund.

NEAR Intents’ $3.8 million loss is much smaller in absolute terms, but it remains technically significant because it again shows how complex cross-chain infrastructure can be and how a single bug in the interaction between systems can create a serious security issue.

There is currently no public evidence that the Bitget and NEAR Intents incidents are connected or that the same attacker was responsible for both.

## Cross-chain infrastructure remains a sensitive target

Cross-chain protocols need to coordinate smart contracts, hot wallets, deposits and withdrawals across multiple networks, liquidity providers and mechanisms for moving information between blockchains. Every additional component increases the number of places where an implementation mistake can become a security risk.

In this case, current information points specifically to a bug in the interaction between two components, rather than a classic breach of the underlying blockchain itself.

The incident again shows that blockchain security and the security of applications built on top of it are not the same thing. The base network can operate normally while the application layer above it still contains a critical vulnerability.

## Another exploit in an expensive year for crypto

The $3.8 million incident is relatively small compared with the biggest attacks of the year, but it comes during a period of very large losses across crypto security. Bitget lost more than $350 million only days earlier, while other protocols in 2026 have also suffered incidents worth hundreds of millions of dollars.

Large pools of digital assets, the ability to move funds quickly across blockchains and the technical complexity of cross-chain systems continue to make crypto platforms attractive targets.

That is why NEAR Intents’ full technical report will matter more than the headline loss figure alone. The key questions will be how the bug was introduced, how long it existed and whether similar components are present elsewhere in the infrastructure.
