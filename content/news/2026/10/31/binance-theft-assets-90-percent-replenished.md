**Binance is trying to restore customer confidence after a theft of approximately USD 6.3–6.36 billion, the largest in cryptocurrency history according to estimates so far. The exchange said on October 31 that it had replenished 90% of affected assets. The attack occurred on October 29. The immediate questions are when customers can access their funds again and what the investigation reveals about the security failures.**

For customers waiting to regain access to their assets, progress on replenishment matters. It does not close the case. An exchange can restore financial backing while efforts to recover stolen tokens and verify the security of its systems remain unfinished.

## Which ten tokens does Binance say are fully backed?

According to Binance, **BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD and ZEC** are fully backed by reserves. The remaining approximately 10% concerns other, less liquid tokens that the exchange continues buying through market and over-the-counter channels.

The reported 90% therefore describes progress in replacing missing assets. It does not mean investigators have recovered 90% of the funds taken by the attacker.

## SAFU, the Futures Insurance Fund and Binance fund replenishment

The exchange identifies three sources: part of the **SAFU fund**, part of the **Futures Insurance Fund**, and its own funds. SAFU is an emergency security reserve, while the Futures Insurance Fund supports protective mechanisms in derivatives trading.

That funding explains how the shortfall is being covered. It does not, by itself, establish that the cause of the attack has been fixed. Customer asset backing and the system's ability to execute the next withdrawal securely need to be verifiable separately.

## Withdrawals expected November 1, full reserve proof November 3

In its October 31 announcement, Binance scheduled withdrawals for **November 1** and the complete cryptographic proof of reserves for **November 3**, following the October 31 snapshot at 00:00 UTC.

Both dates are announcements by the exchange. They do not confirm that services have already resumed or that the complete proof is already available. Customers therefore need the actual withdrawal status and the ability to verify their own balance in the published proof.

## How did the largest known crypto theft happen?

TRM Labs estimates the theft at USD 6.36 billion and assesses that the evidence points to compromised withdrawal-signing infrastructure. The firm attributes the attack with high confidence to the North Korea-linked TraderTraitor group; that remains its investigative assessment.

The finding raises a broader question: who can request a signature, and what checks come before a transaction is approved? Protecting a key is insufficient if an authorized system can be made to approve an unauthorized withdrawal.

## Frozen assets are different from recovered money

For its snapshot at **16:51 UTC on October 31**, Bitquery reports roughly USD 2.7 billion frozen or rescued and approximately USD 3.1 billion under the attacker's control. This is a point-in-time assessment, not a final loss calculation.

Freezing can stop tokens from moving further. Recovery means assets become available to their rightful owner again; replenishment means replacing a shortfall using other funds. These measures can happen in parallel, but their amounts should not be added together as independent successes.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

* **Covering the loss is the first requirement for rebuilding trust, but not sufficient proof of security.** Customers need to know their balance is backed and their next transaction will not face the same weakness.
* **The central security issue is signing authority.** The investigation should explain how withdrawal requests were approved and which additional controls will prevent a repeat.
* **Using multiple funds raises questions about resilience after remediation.** Covering the current shortfall matters, but confidence also depends on the protective reserves left for a future incident.
* **Resuming withdrawals will be the first practical test of the promises.** An announced date gives customers a timeframe; reliably executed transactions will show whether it can be delivered.
* **Proof of reserves and a technical report answer different questions.** Asset backing does not explain how the attack succeeded, and a description of the fix does not prove that every customer balance is backed.
* **An exchange's size does not eliminate the risk of centralized control.** Where a few systems manage large amounts, access checks and limits on authority become as important as the capital available to cover a loss.

Binance now needs to show that financial remediation is accompanied by changes that reduce the possibility of another attack. Rapid replenishment can ease some concerns. Lasting confidence, however, depends on reliable withdrawals, verifiable information and a clear explanation of what went wrong.
