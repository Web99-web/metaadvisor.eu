By 2025, Ethereum’s ownership landscape reveals a striking concentration. 

The single largest holder is the **Beacon Deposit Contract**, securing over **50% of the entire ETH supply**. This highlights Proof-of-Stake’s dominance but also raises liquidity concerns, as much of ETH remains locked while staked.

Next come **centralized exchanges** like **Binance and Coinbase**, which custody millions of ETH on behalf of their users. This has prompted concerns about centralization and regulatory vulnerability.

**Institutional funds and ETFs** are also emerging as major players, adding a layer of professionalism but concentrating governance power.

Among individuals, **Vitalik Buterin** is estimated to hold around **240,000 ETH**, while **Rain Lohmus**, an Estonian banker, is believed to own **250,000 ETH**—yet famously lost access to his private keys, leaving the coins dormant.

The ETH rich list demonstrates that Ethereum is increasingly in the hands of **protocols and institutions**, not just individuals—providing both stability and raising debates over decentralization.
