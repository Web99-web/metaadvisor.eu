A Victorian lawyer has become the first in Australia to face professional sanctions for using artificial intelligence in a court case, being stripped of his ability to practise as a principal lawyer after AI generated false citations that he had failed to verify.  

Guardian Australia reported in October last year that in a July 2024 hearing, the solicitor provided the court with a list of prior cases at the request of Justice Amanda Humphreys. When the judge reviewed it, she and her staff found that none of the cases actually existed.  

The lawyer later admitted the list was produced using AI-powered legal software and that he had not checked its accuracy.  

He offered an “unconditional apology” to the court, saying he would “take the lessons learned to heart.” Still, Justice Humphreys stressed that referring the matter for investigation was important in the public interest.  

The lawyer also agreed to pay the opposing party’s legal costs for the wasted hearing.
