**NVIDIA reported record revenue of $115.8 billion for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, a 103% increase from a year earlier. AI data centers accounted for the vast majority of sales, and the company expects approximately $130 billion in revenue next quarter. But these remarkable figures come as expectations of higher U.S. interest rates weigh on technology stocks and other risk assets.**

The U.S. chipmaker released its results on November 17 for the quarter ended October 25, 2026. Revenue rose 20% from the previous quarter, while GAAP net income reached $65.3 billion, up 105% year over year. GAAP earnings per share were $2.70, with a gross margin of 74.8%. The figures underline how profoundly the buildout of artificial intelligence infrastructure has reshaped a business once best known for graphics processors.

## Data centers account for almost all revenue

The data center segment generated $107.6 billion, or approximately 93% of total quarterly revenue. Sales in the segment rose 110% from a year earlier and 21% quarter over quarter. NVIDIA has become increasingly dependent on the capital-spending cycles of major cloud providers and companies developing advanced AI models.

Among the developments highlighted by the company were initial shipments of Vera Rubin NVL72 systems and the rapid scaling of production for the new computing platform. These systems are designed for intensive AI model training and inference, including growing use of AI agents. NVIDIA also pointed to expanding collaboration with major technology and infrastructure partners, including Microsoft, Google, Oracle and Meta.

This demand suggests that AI is no longer merely a promise of future sales. Major companies are already spending extraordinary sums on chips, networking equipment and servers. However, growing orders for an infrastructure supplier do not automatically mean that all of its customers are already generating returns sufficient to justify their own AI investments.

## NVIDIA forecasts $130 billion in quarterly revenue

For the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027, NVIDIA expects revenue of approximately $130 billion, plus or minus 3%. The outlook excludes data center computing revenue from China. The next quarter also contains an extra week, complicating direct comparisons with the preceding period. Forecast gross margins of 71% to 72% are below the latest reported level, suggesting that rising sales may not translate into equally strong profit-margin growth.

NVIDIA returned $28.1 billion to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends during the third quarter. At the end of the period, approximately $227.3 billion remained under its authorized repurchase program. Those figures demonstrate substantial financial strength, while reflecting the lofty expectations surrounding the continuation of the AI infrastructure investment cycle.

## Record earnings meet a nervous market

The earnings announcement coincided with deteriorating sentiment across financial markets. On November 18, the Federal Reserve published the minutes of its October meeting, indicating that nearly all participants considered further increases in interest rates necessary to return inflation toward the 2% target. Higher expected interest rates raise the cost of capital and can pressure technology-stock valuations even when individual businesses are delivering record profits.

That distinction matters: NVIDIA's underlying financial performance is not the same thing as the short-term direction of its share price. Results confirm strong demand for AI infrastructure but do not eliminate the risks associated with more expensive financing, competing chips, restrictions on sales in certain markets, or the possibility that large customers eventually slow investment growth.

## Our Perspective (Metaadvisor)

* **AI demand remains powerful.** Record quarterly sales suggest investment in data center capacity has not yet slowed.
* **Revenue concentration is substantial.** Roughly 93% of sales come from data centers, making the business sensitive to the plans of a limited number of major customers.
* **Vera Rubin begins another product cycle.** The new platform could support growth, but sustained demand still needs to be demonstrated.
* **Margins deserve scrutiny.** The next-quarter gross margin forecast is below the latest level despite projected revenue growth.
* **The Fed remains a market risk.** Strong earnings do not guarantee higher share prices when investors are also anticipating higher interest rates.

**NVIDIA has shown how quickly the AI industry can expand. For the wider market, the unresolved question is whether record corporate earnings can outweigh tighter financing conditions and increasingly demanding investor expectations.**
