The **Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)**, as part of its ongoing “crypto sprint,” issued an advisory on Thursday stating that foreign crypto firms can directly serve U.S. customers if they register as **Foreign Boards of Trade (FBOTs).**

“American companies that were forced to relocate abroad to facilitate crypto trading now have a path back to U.S. markets,” said **Acting Chair Caroline Pham**. The advisory did not alter existing policy but served as a reminder of available avenues.

Pham noted:  
> “Since the 1990s, Americans have been able to trade on non-U.S. exchanges registered with the CFTC as FBOTs. Starting now, the CFTC welcomes back Americans who want to trade efficiently and safely under CFTC rules, opening U.S. markets to the world.”

The notice, issued by the **Division of Market Oversight**, was described by Pham as “another example of how the CFTC will continue to deliver wins for President Trump as part of our crypto sprint.”

The agency has received growing interest in FBOT registration. Firms that qualify **do not need to register as U.S. Designated Contract Markets (DCMs)** to give American clients direct access, though they must be strictly regulated in their home jurisdictions.

Trump has nominated **Brian Quintenz**, a former CFTC commissioner, as permanent chair, pending Senate confirmation.
