Jean Innes says it is time for a “new chapter” at the AI institute, after staff revolt and government demands for change.  

The chief executive of the UK’s leading artificial intelligence institute is stepping down after staff protests and government calls for a strategic overhaul.  

Jean Innes has led the Alan Turing Institute since 2023, but her position came under pressure amid widespread dissatisfaction within the organisation and a demand from its biggest funder – the UK government – for a change in direction.  

ATI said the search for a successor is already under way. Innes had held senior roles in the civil service and technology industry before her appointment.  

Government sources pointed to a letter sent by technology secretary Peter Kyle to ATI’s chair in July, demanding strategic change and highlighting the need for new leadership.  

In the letter, Kyle said the institute should refocus on defence and national security and urged “careful consideration” of the executive team for such a shift.  

Innes said on Thursday: “It has been a great honour to lead the UK’s national institute for data science and artificial intelligence, implementing a new strategy and overseeing significant organisational transformation. With that work concluding, and a new chapter starting for the institute, now is the right time for new leadership.”  

A source familiar with the internal debate said Innes’s departure shows Kyle’s letter was taken seriously. However, changes at board level would also be needed if ATI is to fully pivot to defence and security.
