Two MIT-educated brothers accused of allegedly stealing **$25 million** in crypto via a blockchain exploit are seeking to keep their **Google search history** out of court, saying prosecutors want to unfairly use queries like “**top crypto lawyers**” and “**wire fraud statute of limitations**” to show criminal intent.

**Anton and James Peraire-Bueno** filed a motion in **Manhattan** federal court on Friday, arguing the searches are **unfairly prejudicial** and occurred during **privileged attorney consultations** following the alleged April 2023 heist.

U.S. District Judge **Jessica G.L. Clarke** must now decide whether searches conducted after the alleged offense demonstrate **consciousness of guilt** or simply **prudent legal consultation** amid the investigation.

The brothers were arrested in May 2024 on **conspiracy, wire fraud, and money laundering** charges, with prosecutors calling it a “**first-of-its-kind manipulation of the Ethereum blockchain**.”

Authorities allege they used their **specialized skills and education** to exploit **MEV-boost** in April 2023, intercepting private transactions and diverting **$25 million** in just **12 seconds**.

Court filings say they retained counsel immediately after being **threatened by “anonymous sandwich attackers,”** who demanded the return of the allegedly stolen funds.

Defense attorneys submitted detailed **privilege logs** showing Google searches lined up precisely with attorney communications. A search for “**top crypto lawyers**” occurred the **same day** as “**communications with potential counsel**” seeking representation, according to filings.
