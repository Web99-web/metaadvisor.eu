The U.S.-based Bitcoin services firm **River** published ownership distribution research on August 25. The study groups the circulating bitcoin supply into several categories and maps the market share of each, using public filings, custodial address tagging, and prior blockchain research.  

River estimates that **individuals control about 65.9% of circulating BTC** (13.83 million coins). This bucket includes self-custodied wallets and exchange accounts that River classifies as individual.  

On the institutional side, River divides holdings into **businesses, ETFs, and funds**.  

Governments are estimated to hold about **1.5% (306,000 BTC)** based on tracked sovereign addresses.  

Two special categories complete the distribution:  

- **Lost or inactive coins** (wallets assumed permanently inaccessible).  
- About **5.2% of supply (1.09 million BTC)** has yet to be mined before the 21 million cap.  

River emphasizes that this research is meant to map current ownership, not predict price action. The estimates are not definitive since custodians pool many clients, wallet classification can be inaccurate, and ownership is often opaque.  

The conclusion: **individuals still dominate Bitcoin ownership**, but institutions are expanding their share, supported by ETFs and corporations now adopting BTC as a balance-sheet asset.
