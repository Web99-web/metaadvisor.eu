Nike Inc. and StockX, a Detroit-based online marketplace, settled a three-year lawsuit in New York federal court over sneaker-linked NFTs and trademark misuse. The deal, reached last Friday, canceled a jury trial that had been scheduled for October.  

The case began in February 2022 when Nike accused StockX of trademark infringement, arguing its “Vault” NFTs misused Nike sneaker images to sell tokens tied to physical shoes. StockX countered that the NFTs were intended to track ownership of frequently traded goods, not mislead consumers.  

By May 2022, Nike amended its complaint to allege that StockX was also selling counterfeit sneakers. In March this year, Judge Valerie Caproni found StockX liable for distributing counterfeit goods tied to several pairs sold to Nike investigators and customers.  

The settlement dismisses all remaining claims with prejudice, allowing both companies to avoid legal uncertainty and reputational risks.  

**Our comment:** The Nike–StockX saga highlights how legacy brands and emerging NFT marketplaces collide, raising questions about ownership, authenticity, and the boundaries of trademark law.
