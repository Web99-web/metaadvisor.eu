**TL;DR — What’s an L2?**  
A layer-2 sits **on top of** Ethereum (L1). It processes/batches transactions and publishes **proofs/data** back to L1, inheriting Ethereum’s security while cutting fees and boosting throughput. Typical designs are *optimistic* or *zk rollups*.

Why “our own L2” is usually a bad first move: liquidity/user **fragmentation**, ongoing **ops/TCO**, **bridge/upgrade risks** — and the fact that **mature alternatives exist** (established L2s, rollup-as-a-service). It only makes sense with massive distribution and specific, measurable needs (privacy/compliance, performance, protocol-level monetization).

**Pre-flight checklist:** user value vs. building on an existing L2; proof choice (optimistic/zk) & data-availability layer; sequencer governance/centralization risk; bridge design; incident/rollback playbooks; monitoring & upgrade cadence.

## Our take
**Traction first.** Build where users and liquidity live today; earn the right to own more of the stack later.
