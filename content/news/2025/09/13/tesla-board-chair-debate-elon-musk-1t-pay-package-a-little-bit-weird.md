With Tesla shareholders set to vote in **November** on a **10-year, $1 trillion** compensation plan for **Elon Musk**, board chair **Robyn Denholm** defended what would be the **largest package in corporate history** in comments to *The New York Times*.

Denholm, who served on the special committee that crafted the proposal, said Musk should be motivated by **extraordinary goals** tied to **extraordinary pay**. She also suggested he’s more focused on **voting influence** tied to stock than on additional personal wealth.

Skeptics note the apparent **disconnect** between a huge award and **falling profits/vehicle sales**. Denholm maintains the plan is about **future performance**: if targets aren’t met, **he gets nothing**. *TechCrunch* has also pointed out the package targets are **less ambitious** than some of Musk’s past promises for Tesla.

The vote will test how shareholders weigh **retaining Musk** against **governance concerns** around dilution, power concentration and the rigor of the goals.

## Our take
- The headline “**$1T**” grabs attention, but what matters is **goal design**, timelines, dilution math and **pay-for-performance** alignment.  
- Emphasis on **voting power** implies greater **control concentration** — faster execution, but higher **governance risk**.  
- Given softer fundamentals, shareholders should demand **ambitious, transparent milestones**, not lowered bars.  
- Watch **proxy advisor** recommendations (ISS/Glass Lewis) and the official vote materials (targets, dilution scenarios, sensitivities).  
- For investors: expect near-term **headline volatility**; over the long run, **execution** will matter more than narrative.
