*Image is symbolic.*

**Summary:** According to the *Guardian*, Meta pushed **Threads promos** into a **37-year-old man’s** Instagram feed featuring **photos of schoolgirls** — some **as young as 13**, with visible faces and, in many cases, names. The images originated from parents’ **back-to-school posts**. Some parents were unaware that **settings** allowed this re-use: one said her account was **private** but posts were **auto-cross-posted to Threads**; another had a **public** Instagram. Meta said it recommends Threads by showing **publicly shared content** that complies with community standards.

### How it happened
- The user saw **“Get Threads”** prompts with **embedded posts** of uniformed schoolgirls (often with names/faces).  
- These appeared as **“suggested threads.”**  
- Parents say they didn’t realize Meta could **recontextualize** their images for **promotional** purposes, especially in a way that felt **provocative**.

### Reactions
- A father of a 13-year-old called it **“absolutely outrageous,”** saying the use felt **sexualized**.  
- Campaigners warn that **algorithmic recommendations** can create **inappropriate contexts** involving minors.

### Meta’s response
- The company said the images **did not violate** its rules because they were **publicly shared** and met **community standards**.  
- The intent, Meta said, was to **recommend Threads**, not to create sexualized content.

---

**Our take (Metaadvisor):** Public content can still be problematic when **algorithms shift the context** — especially involving minors. Platforms need clearer **opt-outs** and stricter defaults for children’s images, while parents should review **cross-posting and visibility** settings. Even absent a policy breach, the **perception** alone can trigger reputational damage and regulatory pressure.
