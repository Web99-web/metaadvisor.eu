A report by two research teams concludes there is **less than a 0.001% chance** MYX trading was organic. Comparing thousands of minute-level datapoints, they found **synchronized illiquidity spikes**, atypical **bid–ask spread** behavior and **shrinking average order sizes** precisely when spreads would usually widen — patterns more consistent with **coordinated activity** than with natural price discovery.

On-chain forensics add context: dozens of newly created wallets captured a large share of the airdrop and quickly **monetized** it, resembling a **Sybil-style** strategy. That alone doesn’t prove price manipulation, but it **fits** the picture painted by the trading anomalies.

For retail, the takeaway is straightforward: **order books can be deceptive**. Apparent liquidity may vanish when you need it most, and price can **slide** once incentives fade. The **risk** is higher than a clean chart suggests.
