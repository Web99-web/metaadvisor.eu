As robots step out of fenced cells into **warehouses, hospitals and retail**, safety in **human spaces** becomes a first-order requirement. Norway-based **Sonair** argues the missing piece is **sound**. Rather than sweep a scene with a laser beam, its **ADAR** (acoustic detection and ranging) unit **fills the environment with ultrasound** and returns a three-dimensional picture.

The approach is straightforward: emit **ultrasonic waves**, measure the **echo**, and reconstruct a **3D map** — especially effective for **depth** and obstacle detection. These data **don’t replace** cameras and other sensors; they **complement** them, adding a “second sense” that’s less sensitive to **dust, glare and lighting changes** that can trip up optics.

“Humans rely on vision, but we also use **hearing**,” co-founder and CEO **Knut Sandven** told *TechCrunch*. “Robots are similar: **cameras are great**, but **not reliable in all circumstances**. **Sound** fills the gaps.” His analogy: “**LIDAR is like swiping a laser pointer.** **Shout in a room and you fill it with sound — that’s what we do.**”

For manufacturers, that promises **more robust collision safety** where people and machines share the aisle — and potentially **lower cost** than optical systems in the same class, aiding broader deployment in mobile robotics. Sandven adds the **sensor output uses a standard industry format**, making **integration into existing stacks** and safety certification more straightforward.
