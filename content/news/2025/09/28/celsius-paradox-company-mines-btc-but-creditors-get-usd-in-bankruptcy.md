## Introduction: when “saving in BTC” turns into a fiat illusion
Celsius was marketed as a crypto savings platform. People deliberately held **BTC/ETH**, expecting that in a worst-case scenario they’d receive **coins** back. Instead, bankruptcy converted everything into **USD** at the **petition date**.  
**Clarification:** Once distribution notices started, it became clear: **“70%” in USD is the petition-date (2022) value of your BTC/ETH — not the price on your payout day — which is why 70% USD never meant 70% BTC.**

---

## Timeline (key milestones)
- **May 2022** – **Celsius Mining LLC** confidentially files an S-1 for a mining IPO (pre-crisis announcement).  
- **June 12, 2022** – Celsius **pauses withdrawals/swaps/transfers** (“The Pause”).  
- **July 13, 2022** – **Chapter 11 filing** (official **petition date**).  
- **2023** – plan vote; **Convenience Class** offers faster payout (~70% USD) **without** equity/IRR.  
- **Early 2024** – **first distribution waves** begin.  
- **Throughout 2024** – many **delays** due to **Coinbase onboarding** (KYC/AML, jurisdiction, compliance flags); some users receive **multiple tranches**.

---

## The paradox: mining in BTC, accounting in USD
Celsius operated **mining** (Celsius Mining LLC) before bankruptcy; in restructuring, **Ionic Digital** took over the mining assets and continued producing BTC. Yet creditors did **not** receive a **coin-first** treatment.  
Bankruptcy law is **fiat-first**: all claims are valued in **USD** at the **petition date**; the **recovery %** applies to that USD claim; only at **distribution** is this fixed USD amount converted into BTC/ETH at the **then-current** price.

---

## How the mechanics work (plain English)
1) **USD lock-in (petition date: 2022-07-13)** – your BTC/ETH becomes a **USD claim** (*Allowed Claim*).  
2) **Apply recovery (e.g., 70%)** – you are entitled to **70% of that USD claim**.  
3) **Convert at payout (waves in 2024)** – that **fixed USD amount** is converted to BTC/ETH at the **price on your actual payout date**.  
→ If BTC rose by then, you receive **fewer BTC**; if it fell, **more BTC**. The USD value still targets ~70% of the claim.

> This is **not shorting** creditors; it’s the **accounting design**.

---

## Illustrative example (1 BTC) — petition fixed, payout variable
*(Illustrative numbers; petition date is fixed: 2022-07-13)*

- **Start (2022-07-13):** 1.000000 BTC × **$19,393/BTC** ≈ **$19,393** (USD claim)  
- **Plan (70% recovery):** **$13,575.10 (USD)**  
  **BTC equivalent “then”:** $13,575.10 ÷ $19,393 ≈ **0.7000 BTC**  
- **Payout (one wave in 2024, e.g., BTC ≈ $62,000):**  
  **delivered BTC:** $13,575.10 ÷ $62,000 ≈ **0.2190 BTC**

**Gap:** expected (70% coins) **0.7000 BTC** vs. received **0.2190 BTC** → **−0.4810 BTC** (only **21.9%** of the original 1 BTC).

---

## Time effect: early vs. late wave (extra short-change)
The same USD amount from the plan **does not** buy the same BTC/ETH if someone tries to re-enter coins on their **payout day**.

**Comparison (1 BTC start; USD claim = $19,393; 70% recovery = $13,575.10):**
- **Early wave (e.g., BTC = $45,000):** $13,575.10 ÷ $45,000 = **0.3017 BTC**  
- **Late wave (e.g., BTC = $62,000):** $13,575.10 ÷ $62,000 = **0.2190 BTC**

**Time-driven difference:** 0.3017 − 0.2190 = **0.0827 BTC** less for late recipients (≈ **−27%** vs. early wave, measured in BTC quantity).

> Bottom line: those **paid earlier** could receive **more BTC** for the same USD and/or accumulate immediately at a lower price. **Later payouts** (at higher BTC prices) mean **fewer coins** — an additional short-change in coin terms.

---

## Glossary
- **Petition date** – 2022-07-13, the day all claims are valued in USD.  
- **Allowed Claim** – recognized USD value of your claim.  
- **Recovery %** – the percentage paid (e.g., 70%).  
- **Convenience Class** – faster USD-based payout, no equity/IRR.  
- **Ionic Digital** – entity that took over mining assets post-restructuring.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **Systemic issue**: **fiat-first** rules despite **coin-first** assets.  
- **Smaller creditors** were hit hardest: **coin upside** and **equity exposure** (Ionic/IRR) lost.  
- **Operational reality**: differing payout dates + Coinbase onboarding amplified the **unfairness** perception.  
- **Temporal unfairness:** payout waves created extra **time risk** — later waves (at higher BTC/ETH prices) receive **fewer coins** for the same USD amount, while early waves could **buy/hold** at lower prices.  
- **What now?** Fair coin treatment requires **in-kind (coin-first) accounting** or a hybrid; without it, every centralized crypto bankruptcy becomes a **fiat déjà vu**.

**Emphasized takeaways:**
- Celsius **cemented the gap** between crypto and fiat logic: people saved in BTC but were treated as **USD creditors**.  
- **Small creditors suffered most**: they gave up equity and received roughly **a fifth of the coins** they expected.  
- **A painful paradox**: a company that **mined BTC** paid out via a **USD formula**, not in coins.  
- **Until laws change**, centralized bankruptcies will keep using **fiat accounting** regardless of what the firm actually holds or produces.  
- **Lesson:** *“Not your keys, not your coins.”* Not a slogan — **real life in bankruptcies**.
