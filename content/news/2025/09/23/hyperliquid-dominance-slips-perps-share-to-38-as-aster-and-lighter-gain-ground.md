Once the **uncontested leader** in on-chain **perpetual futures**, Hyperliquid is **rapidly ceding share** to upstarts **Lighter** and **Aster**.

Back in **May**, Hyperliquid commanded **71%** of the on-chain perps segment; that figure now stands at **38%**, per pseudonymous Dune analyst **@uwusanauwu**. Meanwhile, **Lighter** (backed by a16z) and **Aster** (backed by Binance Labs) have **risen** to **16.8%** and **14.9%**, respectively, up from **low single digits** in May. Percentages are based on **weekly trading volumes** and **exclude wash trading**.

**On-chain perps** are **decentralized** perpetual futures traded **directly on-chain**, aiming for greater **transparency** and **immutability**. Via the **funding mechanism**, perps track **spot** more closely, enabling **leverage**—but also **liquidation risk** when trends reverse.

The market has **exploded in volume**: a combined **~$700B** over the **last four weeks**, including **$42B** in the **past 24 hours**. The number of protocols has climbed from **two in 2022** to **80+ today**—classic dynamics: **fat margins attract competitors**, who then **erode pioneers’ share**.

With **few entry/exit barriers** in crypto, anyone with the know-how can **launch a protocol** and **compete**. Recent weeks feature an **incentives-and-terms war**: **maker rebates**, **token distributions**, **liquidity programs**, **new listings**, **lower latency**, **robust APIs**, and **CEX-like UX**. As **incentives rotate or fade**, **users and liquidity** **migrate** to venues with **lower fees**, **less slippage**, **deeper books**, and **better tooling** (portfolio/cross-margin, trigger orders, copy trading).

In this context, **Hyperliquid’s share decline** doesn’t necessarily mean **product deterioration**, but rather **heightened competition**: new venues offer **aggressive terms**, and large players (MMs and whales) **rebalance capital**. In the short run, that **dampens momentum** and pressures **linked tokens/narratives**—until the market finds a **new equilibrium**.

### What this means for traders and protocols
- **Traders**: venue choice increasingly hinges on **fees**, **funding**, **depth** and **execution stability**. Differences in **10–50 bps spread** often decide where volume lands.  
- **Market makers**: move where **rebates** and **latency** are best, and where **risk** can be managed efficiently across pairs.  
- **Protocols**: durable growth needs **clear fee capture**, **predictable tokenomics** and a **roadmap** that keeps **reducing friction** (on-ramps, mobile UX, broker integrations).

### Conclusion – who sees opportunity here?
Some view the **dominance drop** as an **opportunity**: when a **leader** loses share, **discounted multiples** and **hype fatigue** can seed a **buy-the-dip** narrative. That thesis holds **only if** (1) **share stabilizes** and **volume returns**, (2) **tokenomics** clearly deliver **value to holders**, and (3) the **product** retains and expands **core users** amid tougher competition. Otherwise, the decline may be **structural**, not **cyclical**.
