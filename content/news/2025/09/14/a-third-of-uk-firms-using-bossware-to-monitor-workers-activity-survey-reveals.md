**What the survey found**  
The **Chartered Management Institute (CMI)** reports that roughly **one-third** of UK employers now deploy **employee-monitoring tools**. The most common methods are **email and web monitoring**, while **one in seven** say they **record or review screen activity**.

Compared with earlier research, workplace surveillance appears to be **on the rise**. In 2023, the **ICO** found **fewer than a fifth** of people believed they were being monitored. CMI adds today’s figure is likely **understated** because **about a third** of managers **don’t know** exactly what tracking their organisations use.

**Why firms say they monitor — and why many object**  
Common justifications include **insider-threat prevention**, **data protection** and **productivity**. Yet a **large minority** of managers oppose the trend, arguing it **undermines trust** and intrudes on **personal privacy**. One insurance manager, whose company is developing **AI screen monitoring**, called it **“unsettling.”**

## Our take
Monitoring should be **proportionate, transparent and purpose-limited**. Good hygiene includes:  
- **Clear employee notices** (what, why, how long),  
- a formal **DPIA**,  
- tight **access controls** and **retention limits**.  
Overbroad tracking tends to be **counterproductive** for culture and engagement; emphasising **outcomes** over click-by-click surveillance usually works better.
