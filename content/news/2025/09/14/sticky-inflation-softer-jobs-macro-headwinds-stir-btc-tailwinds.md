Bitcoin (BTC) at **$115,426** is about **4%** higher than a week ago — good for the digital asset, less so for the economy. The downbeat tone of last week’s macro data **lifted expectations** that the **Federal Reserve** will **cut rates** on Wednesday, making risk assets like equities and bitcoin more attractive.

**Quick recap:**
- **CPI (Thu):** headline came in **slightly above expectations**, hinting inflation remains **stickier** than anticipated.
- **Jobs revisions (Tue):** the U.S. created **nearly 1M fewer jobs** in the year through March — the **largest downward revision** on record.
- **Monthly jobs (Fri):** just **22,000** jobs in August; **unemployment 4.3%**. **Initial claims** rose to **263,000**, highest since Oct 2021.

That mix — **higher inflation, fewer jobs** — isn’t great for growth, so **“stagflation”** chatter is creeping back. Against that backdrop, **bitcoin kept grinding higher**, topping **$116K** on Friday and **nearly closing** the **CME futures gap** near **$117,300** from August. Meanwhile, the **S&P 500** closed at a **record** for a second straight day on rate-cut hopes.

**Chart view**  
Price action looks **constructive**: since early September BTC has been printing **higher lows**. As long as that structure holds — and the Fed narrative stays supportive — the **bullish bias** remains intact.

## Our take
Macro headwinds can morph into **BTC tailwinds** if they hasten **rate cuts**. But there’s room for disappointment: an unexpectedly **hot CPI** or a hawkish Fed could **cool risk-on** quickly. Near term, watch the **FOMC decision**, guidance on **further cuts**, and how the **S&P** reacts; technically, buyers maintain the edge while **higher lows** persist.
