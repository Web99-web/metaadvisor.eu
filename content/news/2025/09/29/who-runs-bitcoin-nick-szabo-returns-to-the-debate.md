### Who actually runs Bitcoin?

Unlike banks or big tech, **Bitcoin has no CEO and no headquarters**. The system is kept alive by:

- **Nodes** that validate all transactions/blocks,  
- **Miners** who secure the chain via proof-of-work,  
- **Developers** who maintain **Bitcoin Core** openly on GitHub.

Code changes go through reviews, tests and public discussion. **No one can force** an upgrade — it only “wins” if the community of nodes and miners actually adopts it.

---

### Who pays the developers?

Many contribute **voluntarily**; others receive **grants and donations** from:

- **Brink** and **Chaincode Labs**,  
- **Spiral (Block)**,  
- **MIT Digital Currency Initiative**,  
- exchanges and companies (e.g., Coinbase, BitMEX, OKX).

This keeps development **plural and decentralized**, without a single employer steering the roadmap.

---

### Nick Szabo is back

**Nick Szabo** — cryptographer behind **smart contracts** and **Bit Gold** — resurfaced after **five years of silence** to comment on the controversial **Bitcoin Core v30** changes (notably **OP_RETURN**, embedding non-monetary data).

His key concerns:
- larger data fields could **burden full nodes** and raise operating costs,  
- potential **legal exposure** for node operators hosting harmful content,  
- risk of diluting Bitcoin’s **“digital gold”** base-layer role.

While Szabo has never claimed to be **Satoshi Nakamoto**, many consider him a plausible early influence — which makes his views carry weight.

### Our take (Metaadvisor)

- **Decentralization in action:** there’s no off-switch or boardroom.  
- **Open-source transparency:** trust the code, not a title.  
- **Szabo effect:** a reminder to protect base-layer principles.  
- **Trade-offs:** more data freedom vs. higher node costs/compliance.  
- **Bottom line:** governance emerges from users and markets — not from a central authority.
