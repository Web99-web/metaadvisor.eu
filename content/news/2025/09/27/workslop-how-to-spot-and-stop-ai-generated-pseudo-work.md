> **Definition (HBR / BetterUp Labs)**  
> *Workslop* = **AI-generated work content that masquerades as good work but lacks the substance to meaningfully advance a task.**  
> The result: **the burden shifts downstream** — receivers must interpret, fix, or redo the work.

---

## Why workslop happens
1. **No clear goal** – “Make a deck on X” → 20 pretty slides, no point.  
2. **Weak prompts / missing context** – audience, decision-maker, deadline, success criteria unknown.  
3. **Metrics reward output, not outcomes** – “task delivered,” “doc submitted.”  
4. **Aesthetics > substance** – layout and banners overshadow numbers and decisions.  
5. **No guardrails** – nobody asks 3–5 tough questions upfront (What decision? Constraints? What is ‘good enough’?)

---

## Red flags: how to spot it
- **Slideware without a thesis**: 18–30 good-looking slides, no “so what?” nor recommendation.  
- **Meeting notes that change nothing**: long transcripts, zero decisions, owners, or deadlines.  
- **Endless pixel-pushing**: hours resizing banners/images that were fine.  
- **Generic best practices**: smart-sounding but no local data, trade-offs, or numbers.  
- **Fluff loops**: rephrases and synonyms without new information.

---

## Pre-flight checklist (before generating with AI)
**1. Goal (1 sentence)** – *What decision/action will this enable?*  
**2. Audience / decision-maker** – *Who decides and what do they care about?*  
**3. “Good enough” bar** – *What is the minimum viable outcome for a decision?*  
**4. Data & constraints** – *Which numbers/limits must the model respect?*  
**5. Decision format** – *1-page memo / 5-slide deck / KPI table — not a default 20-slide dump.*

No answers? → **Don’t generate yet.** Fix the context first.

---

## From “pretty” to “useful” (minimum standards)
- **Every doc includes**: *goal, insight, recommendation, owner, deadline*.  
- **Every slide answers**: *Point? Evidence? Next step?*  
- **CFO/PM test**: plain language, no empty buzzwords.  
- **Pixel budget**: 10–20% on visuals; 80–90% on numbers, decision, trade-offs.

---

## Converting workslop into real work
- **Instead of**: “Generate a deck about the mNAV issue.”  
  **Do**: “Write a 1-page memo: *Why mNAV misleads?* Include 3 numeric examples, 2 alternative metrics, and an IR recommendation.”

- **Instead of**: “Take meeting notes.”  
  **Do**: “Summarize 3 decisions, 3 action owners with deadlines, and 2 risks to escalate.”

- **Instead of**: “Polish banners to look premium.”  
  **Do**: “Run A/B on 2 sizes, 3 aspect ratios; report CTR and winner by 4 pm.”

---

## Team guardrails that kill workslop
1. **Brief before generation** (the 5 points above).  
2. **Default formats**: 1-pager / 5-slide deck / KPI table — *not* “whatever comes out.”  
3. **Gatekeeper questions**: *What decision? What numbers? What do you recommend?*  
4. **Time-box**: e.g., *30–60 min generation + 30 min data check;* no hours of polishing.  
5. **DOR/DOD** (Definition of Ready/Done): without owner, deadline, recommendation, it’s **not done**.

---

## Glossary
- **Workslop** – AI pseudo-work: looks finished, doesn’t advance the task.  
- **Guardrails** – agreed rules for AI use (brief, format, verification).  
- **Prompt engineering** – crafting inputs/context so models deliver useful output.

---

## Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **AI without a goal just accelerates nonsense.**  
- **Small teams suffer most**: polished fluff forces others to redo the job.  
- **Boring beats broken**: short brief, fixed format, five tough questions *before* design.  
- **Outcomes first**: decisions > aesthetics. Numbers > slogans.
