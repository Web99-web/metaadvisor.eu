Privacy advocates should be cheering on Wall Street’s adoption of cryptocurrencies, according to Etherealize co-founder and President Danny Ryan.

As markets move on-chain, financial institutions are expressing a need for infrastructure that mirrors elements of traditional markets — and privacy is **table stakes**, he told *Decrypt*.

“The market does not, and cannot, function fully in the clear,” he said. “If we’re going to onboard the world to blockchains, ‘everyone sees everything all the time’ just isn’t going to work.”

On Wednesday, Etherealize announced the close of a **$40 million** funding round. The startup said it will promote Ethereum’s use by developing infrastructure for trading and settling tokenized assets, built around **zero-knowledge (ZK) proofs** among other tools.

When transacting on a public blockchain, users leave an evidence trail for anyone to analyze, and elite entities may cringe at the thought of treasury operations and trading strategies playing out in the open — even if blockchains prove more efficient than legacy systems.

With U.S. prosecutions against developers behind coin-mixing services like Tornado Cash and Samourai Wallet, it can feel like privacy has taken a back seat. But Ryan described Wall Street’s needs as a potential **Trojan horse** for bringing privacy on-chain, with benefits and normalization trickling down to average users.

“As we begin to upgrade these markets, institutions will demand privacy, and we’ll move the needle toward **practical, applied and compliant** privacy,” he said.

A ZK proof (zero-knowledge proof) is a cryptographic method for proving a statement is true **without revealing any additional information**. In practice: prove you have sufficient funds without disclosing your balance, or prove KYC compliance without exposing personal data. Common families include **zk-SNARKs** and **zk-STARKs**; core properties are completeness, soundness, and zero-knowledge.

  
**Our view:**  
Institutions are pushing privacy from “nice-to-have” to **market requirement**. Expect **selective disclosure ZK patterns** (private to the public, auditable to regulators) to become the norm for tokenized assets — and then spill over into DeFi and consumer payments once tooling and UX mature.
