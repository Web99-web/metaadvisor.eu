### What the Fed decided — in plain English
The Fed **cut rates by 0.25%**. Think of it as **easing the brake** a notch: not full throttle, but a bit **less pressure**. Loans get **gradually** cheaper, giving households and companies **more breathing room**. The direction matters more than instant effects.

Why cut? The **job market is cooling**. The Fed wants to **avoid over-tightening**. Inflation isn’t gone, but the **bigger risk** right now is an **over-slowdown**.

### Why prices fell first and then bounced
Right after the announcement we got the usual **algo whipsaw**: quick pop, **drop**, **retrace**.

- **Equities:** mixed close.  
- **Bonds & USD:** classic **round-trip** — softer at first, then firmer.  
- **Bitcoin/crypto:** **pop-and-drop**, then **stabilised** above **$116k**.

Translation: markets heard “**we’re easing**,” but also “**slow and careful**.” No party, no panic.

### What Powell said at the presser — minus the jargon
- **Why not 50 bps?** **No need to rush.** Big moves are for **emergencies**.  
- **A longer cutting cycle?** **Maybe.** It **depends on data** (inflation, jobs). **Meeting by meeting**.  
- **Inflation vs. jobs?** The **risk balance** has tilted toward **employment**. Hence a **small, cautious** cut.  
- **Any dissent?** **One** member wanted **50 bps**; the majority went for **25 bps**.

Picture a **thermostat**: the Fed turns it **one click at a time**, checks the room, then decides the next click.

### What to watch next (and why crypto cares)
If the **dollar softens** and **yields** drift lower, **risk assets** (stocks, crypto) usually **find it easier to rise**. To sustain that, we’ll need **softer data** ahead (cooler inflation, no break in jobs). Until then, **choppiness** is normal.

For **Bitcoin**, the key is **spot demand** (beyond derivatives) and the **USD trend**. A sharp dollar rebound tends to **cap** crypto; a weaker dollar **helps**.
