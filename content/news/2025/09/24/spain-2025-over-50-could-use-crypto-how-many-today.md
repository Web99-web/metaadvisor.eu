In recent years, Spain has quietly moved crypto from “exotic” to everyday. Banking apps are faster, fees are lower, and the EU’s **MiCAR** finally provides clarity. So the idea that **over half the population** could be using crypto by **2025** no longer sounds far-fetched. The natural question: **how many actually use it today?**

It depends on what we count as a “user.” By **registrations** on exchanges/wallets, Spain looks crypto-savvy. By **active users** — those who monthly send small remittances, swap into stablecoins, or pay online — the base is smaller but **steadily growing**. In major cities, crypto already feels like a companion to cards and bank transfers; beyond metros, adoption is slower but no longer exotic.

What can push adoption toward 50%? **Banks** first: when buy/send sits **inside existing banking apps**, without new logins or seed phrases, barriers fall. Second, **payments & remittances**: steady P2P flows need a cheap, fast rail — stablecoins fit. Third, **forgiving UX**: seedless wallets, social login, and sensible fees turn a one-off trial into habit.

It won’t be linear. **Macro and sentiment** can slow things; **regulatory timing** (secondary acts, supervisory guidance) governs how fast banks can move. And users’ attention is finite: **AI apps** and new save/invest products compete for the same wallet. Still, with improving **on-ramps** and clearer consumer protection, crypto is leaving the niche and **becoming another everyday tool**.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **Active > registered:** track **monthly actives** and real P2P/bill-pay flows, not just accounts.  
- **Banking UX is the catalyst:** crypto **as a feature** inside the bank app matters.  
- **Payments first, DeFi later:** steady P2P/remittance flows build habit; complex products follow.  
- **Watchlist:** number of **MiCAR-enabled bank integrations**, growth in steady P2P volumes, and friction (KYC/fees/latency) in on/off-ramps.
