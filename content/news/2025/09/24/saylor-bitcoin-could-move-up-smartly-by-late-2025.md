**TL;DR:** **Michael Saylor** (MicroStrategy) outlines a case for a **late-2025** **renewed advance** in BTC — driven by **spot-ETF inflows**, **corporate balance-sheet allocations**, and **thinner new supply** after the halving. The premise: **structural buying** eclipses **cyclical drawdowns**.

### Where would demand come from?
- **Spot ETFs:** regulated rails convert lumpy buys into **steady absorption**.  
- **Corporate treasuries:** even **small BTC slices** at scale create **meaningful demand**.  
- **Long-term holders:** more **lock-up**, less **free float** on exchanges.

### Why is supply thinner?
- **Halving:** **cuts issuance**, making constant inflows **easier to absorb**.  
- **Institutional plumbing:** custody/accounting lower friction, accelerating **demand lock-ins**.

### Pushbacks / delays
- **Macro:** tight policy, strong USD, equity volatility.  
- **ETF cyclicality:** flows are **non-linear**, sentiment-driven.  
- **Policy risk:** tax/regulatory turns can raise risk premia.

### What a “smart” move would look like
- **Phases:** **stabilize → reclaim MAs → trend**, with **shallower pullbacks**.  
- **Breadth:** on-chain scarcity (lower exchange balances) + derivatives (**funding/skew**) confirming **organic** demand.

---

## Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **Thesis holds if** (1) **ETF net inflows** persist, (2) **corporate adoption** becomes policy, (3) **on-chain** keeps signaling **scarcity**.  
- **Timing is fuzzy:** late-2025 is plausible yet **macro-sensitive**.  
- **Dashboards:** **ETF flows**, **exchange balances**, **MVRV**, **derivatives conditions**.  
- **Risk/opportunity:** if **demand > supply** proves out, **pullbacks** are opportunities; if ETF flows fade, the story weakens.
