Spot ether exchange-traded funds (ETFs) logged their fifth straight day of outflows this week, shedding $952 million in total, including over $787 million during the four-day week alone.

The withdrawals followed a record-setting August when spot ether ETFs pulled in $3.87 billion even as bitcoin ETFs saw $751 million in net outflows, according to SoSoValue data.

Friday saw the sharpest decline, with $446.71 million leaving ETH-linked funds. By contrast, spot bitcoin ETFs posted $246.4 million in net inflows over the past week — notable after BTC funds recorded $751.1 million in net outflows last month.

Ether has climbed more than 16% over the past month, though it slipped 1.8% in the last week and now trades just below $4,300. The cryptocurrency has benefited partly from the GENIUS Act becoming law, restricting stablecoin issuers from paying interest and providing clarity that could spur greater institutional investment.

The latest drawdown is likely tied to a broader risk-off move. Weak U.S. jobs data reinforced expectations that the Federal Reserve will cut rates later this month, alongside growing recession fears.

Traders are now pricing an 89% chance of a 25 bps cut and an 11% chance of a 50 bps cut, according to the CME’s FedWatch tool. On Polymarket, odds of a 50 bps cut are about 12%.

Cooling macro data — plus rising economic uncertainty and geopolitical risks — also saw gold push above the $3,600 mark for the first time.

**Our view:**  
Short-term ETF outflows can overpower spot demand, but the **structural ETH drivers** (clearer rules, on-chain usage) remain intact. Expect choppy price action until macro stabilizes and ETF flows turn neutral to positive again.
