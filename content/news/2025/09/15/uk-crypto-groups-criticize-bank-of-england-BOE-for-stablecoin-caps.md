**What’s reported**  
Per the **Financial Times**, the **Bank of England (BOE)** is considering **quantitative holding limits** on “systemic” stablecoins: **£10–20k** per individual and roughly **£10m** per business. Rationale: curb **bank-deposit outflows** and safeguard **financial stability**, framed as potentially **transitional**.

**Named reactions**  
- **Sasha Mills** (BOE, Executive Director FMI): caps would **mitigate risks** from sudden deposit withdrawals and the scaling of new systemic payment systems.  
- **Tom Duff Gordon** (**Coinbase**, VP International Policy): capping stablecoins is “**bad for U.K. savers, bad for the City and bad for sterling**”; **no major jurisdiction** uses such per-user caps.  
- **Simon Jennings** (UK Cryptoasset Business Council): **enforcement is near-impossible** without **digital IDs**.  
- **Riccardo Tordera-Ricchi** (**The Payments Association**): caps **“make no sense”** given there are **no limits** on **cash** or **bank accounts**.

**How others handle it**  
The U.S. **GENIUS Act** and the EU’s **MiCA** focus on **issuer licensing, reserves, redemption and supervision** — **not** per-user quantity limits.

## Our take
The underlying concern is clear: banks fear **deposit flight** to **stablecoin rails**, which raises **funding costs** and can suppress **credit**. But **blanket quantity caps** are a blunt tool:

- **Not tech-neutral:** “Digital cash” would face **tighter limits** than **cash/deposits** — a **regulatory outlier** that erodes confidence.  
- **Hard to police, easy to route around:** without **digital IDs**, holdings can be split across **multiple wallets/issuers**; activity drifts **offshore**, weakening oversight.  
- **Stability paradox:** in stress, hard caps can **trap funds**, nudging users into **riskier rails** — **raising** systemic risk.  
- **UK competitiveness:** A world-class financial center shouldn’t confine users to **“pocket-money”** ceilings; capital and talent will gravitate to **clear, cap-free** regimes.

**A better path:** a **risk-based issuer regime** (robust **reserves/liquidity/redemption**, **stress-testing**, **near-real-time supervision**) instead of user caps; **time-boxed transitions** only if necessary; **carve-outs** for **payroll/suppliers/treasury** at regulated firms; **same risk, same rules** across e-money/stablecoins and traditional non-cash instruments.
