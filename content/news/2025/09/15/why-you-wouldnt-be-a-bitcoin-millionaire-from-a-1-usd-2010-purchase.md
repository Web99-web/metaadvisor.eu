**The myth in one line:** If you’d put **$1** into Bitcoin in 2010 and left it alone, you’d be a millionaire today. It’s a neat story that **skips** almost everything you had to survive to get anywhere near that outcome.

### 1) “Just buy $1” — the logistics didn’t exist
In 2010 there was no tap-to-buy app. Trading lived on clunky venues with **minimum order sizes** and **fixed fees** that ate tiny tickets. P2P deals had **spreads**; bank wires and on-chain frictions added cost and time. Practically, buying **exactly $1** of BTC was **hard** — you either bought **more** or **nothing**.

### 2) Price history: cheap, not “free”
In 2010 BTC traded **below $1**; late-year highs were **tenths of a dollar**. Breaking **$1** in 2011 was news. Yes, a dollar could have bought **dozens of BTC** — but that’s the start, not the end of the story.

### 3) Exchanges blew up; keys went missing
The bigger hurdle was **infrastructure & security**. Early exchanges failed a lot — **Mt. Gox** being the poster child (hacks, **collapse**). In parallel, **millions of BTC** are **lost forever** (misplaced seeds, dead drives). Odds that your “$1” escaped all that? Smaller than the myth suggests.

### 4) Psychology: would anyone truly hold 15 years?
Even with perfect custody you’d have to sit through **80–90% drawdowns** (2011, 2013–15, 2018, 2022). Most people **sell too early** (after the first 10x) or **panic** at the lows. Long-term, stone-cold holders are the **exception**, not the norm.

### 5) Taxes, rules, real life
Back then almost nobody kept **books**. Today, proving basis from 2010, moves between countries, inheritance, divorce — **paperwork** becomes the boss fight. “Bought in 2010 and forgot” plays well on podcasts; in real life it often ends in **unclaimable balances**.

## Mini what-if (everyone’s favorite — handle with care)
Say you somehow bought **$1** at **$0.05/BTC** in August 2010 ⇒ **20 BTC**. At **~$115k/BTC** that’s **~$2.3M gross**. Now subtract: **fees/spreads**, **exchange risk**, **taxes**, and the high probability you **sold much earlier**. Mathematically possible — **statistically & humanly**: rare.

---

## What this story is actually good for
It’s a reminder of three uncomfortable truths:

- **Infrastructure & custody matter most** — tiny technicalities can vaporize returns.  
- **Behavior beats spreadsheets** — your **nervous system** is the main risk.  
- **Wealth isn’t a formula** but a chain of choices about **security, patience and discipline**.

## Our take
You don’t need a **time machine**. Ditch “if only I’d bought in 2010” and build a **plan for today**:
1) **Protect keys** (hardware wallet, seed backup, recovery test).  
2) Remove **single points of failure** (don’t park everything on one exchange; use self-custody where it makes sense).  
3) **Pre-commit behavior** (DCA, position caps, pre-set take-profit/no-panic-sell rules).  
4) **Expect volatility** (70–90% drawdowns are historically normal; your strategy should assume them).  
5) **Paperwork before profit** (tax, basis, proof of ownership — squared away before it’s big).

Bottom line: the legend is pretty; real millions come from **discipline and custody**, not from a perfectly timed $1.
