The **Pixel 10 Pro** is the rare flagship that still feels **compact**. It takes the excellent Pixel 10 blueprint and dials up the **cameras**, while keeping the **6.3-inch OLED** at the same pocket-friendly size — now **brighter**, **smoother** and **crisper**. Polished aluminium sides and a matte back sell the premium vibe, even if Google’s **colour options** play it safe.

Power comes from **Tensor G5**, which the review pegs at roughly **35% faster** than last year’s chip. Day-to-day it feels **instant**, and Google’s **on-device AI** is the headline act: scene and subject detection, AI clean-up in photos, live transcription and translation, and a grab-bag of small touches that add up. For gaming the 10 Pro is **more than capable**, though phones with **Qualcomm’s top silicon** still top the pure fps charts.

The spec sheet has the right modern extras. **Qi2** magnetic wireless charging opens the door to a wave of accessories (including MagSafe-friendly ones). The OLED is one of the **best on any phone**, with silky refresh and serious peak brightness. **Battery life** is described as **solid** — a comfortable day unless you’re pushing hard with games or continuous video.

Starting at **£999 (€1,099/$999/A$1,699)**, the 10 Pro sits **below** the larger **10 Pro XL** and the **10 Pro Fold**, and lines up against **Apple’s iPhone 17 Pro** for the “small pro” crown. Drawbacks? The design is **very close** to last year’s and the palette is **understated**; if you need the **absolute ceiling** of graphics performance, a few Android rivals still edge it.

## Our take
This is a **daily-driver flagship**: easy to hold, quick to use, and packed with **AI that matters**. If you want a **smaller high-end phone**, the shortlist is short — and the Pixel 10 Pro looks like the **best-balanced** pick. Buy it for the **cameras, display and smart features**; skip it only if you demand a **bigger canvas** or chase **max-fps gaming** above all else.
