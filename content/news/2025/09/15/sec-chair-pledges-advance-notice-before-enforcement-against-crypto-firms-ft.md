**What’s changing — and why it matters**  
In an interview with the *Financial Times*, **SEC chair Paul Atkins** said the Commission will send **preliminary notices** to firms over **technical breaches** before launching enforcement actions. The aim is to reduce ambush-style actions, allow time to **remediate**, and rebuild trust after years in which the crypto industry decried U.S. policy as **“regulation by enforcement.”** It’s a meaningful **shift in posture**: the emphasis moves from “gotcha” to **“here’s what’s offside and how to fix it”** before penalties land.

**What this implies for crypto**  
Atkins is pairing the shift with a broader modernization agenda (often dubbed **Project Crypto**): clearer lines on **which tokens are securities**, fit-for-purpose rules on **custody** and **trading of tokenized securities**, and room for **24/7 on-chain market structure**. The thrust is **rulemaking and supervision** (licensing, reserves, redemption, transparency) rather than primarily retroactive lawsuits — while keeping full bite for **fraud** and investor-harm cases.

**The bigger backdrop**  
After years of high-profile clashes with major crypto firms, Washington wants to temper **legal uncertainty** and the **chilling effect** on domestic innovation. Unlike the prior leadership, Atkins’ approach prioritizes **standardized expectations** and **documented compliance paths** over expansive readings of legacy rules. That message also resonates with TradFi institutions exploring **tokenization**: **predictability** is the point.

**Where it could go wrong**  
Skeptics warn that a **too-soft** stance may undercut **investor protection** or invite **moral hazard** if “technical” breaches are widely forgiven. The answer will rest in the **design of the notice** (content, timelines, consequences for inaction) and **consistent application** across sectors and firm sizes.

## Our take
If the goal is **legal certainty** and sustained **U.S. competitiveness** in digital finance, this is a constructive move. A **pre-notice** model restores **due process** and nudges **proactive compliance** — provided the framework is **tight** and **serious violations** still draw **swift, decisive** action. For teams, now is the time to **document token classifications**, align **custody/exchange** processes, run **gap analyses** against the coming rules, and centralize **supervisory communications**. If the SEC can balance **predictability** with **investor protection**, it opens the door to **on-chain markets** compatible with a **24/7** financial system—without pushing innovation offshore.
