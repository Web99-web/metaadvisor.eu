**TL;DR:** By **2032**, the **illiquid** share of BTC supply could rise to **~8.3M BTC**, according to Fidelity. “Illiquid” refers to coins in wallets that rarely spend (long-term holders, institutional custody, lost keys), which reduces tradable supply and amplifies price sensitivity to demand shifts.

### What does “illiquid supply” mean?
- **Operational definition:** BTC that rarely moves or sits in cold custody — effectively **unavailable** for quick sale.  
- **Sources of illiquidity:** LTHs, ETF/custodial vaults, corporate treasuries, lost coins.  
- **Not permanent:** wallets can **reactivate**; today’s illiquid coins can become liquid again.

### Why the projection matters
- **Smaller float:** a higher illiquid share means **less BTC** on exchanges and in short-term circulation.  
- **Price elasticity:** with the same demand, a **thinner available supply** increases the chance of **sharper moves**.  
- **Institutional factor:** rising institutional ownership drives **long-term coin lock-up**.

### Bigger picture through 2032
- **Halving cycles:** continued **issuance reduction** supports the scarcity narrative.  
- **Adoption vs. risks:** macro, regulation and tech risks can still **swing sentiment**.  
- **Methodological limits:** on-chain providers **classify liquidity differently** — figures are estimates, not absolutes.
