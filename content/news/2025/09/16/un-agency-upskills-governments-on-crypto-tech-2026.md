According to Cointelegraph, the UN Development Programme (UNDP) will launch a **training initiative for governments and regulators** next year to provide practical understanding of blockchain and crypto. The aim is to close the gap between technology and policy so that public services can be deployed responsibly and measurably.

The curriculum will cover distributed-systems basics, how networks and tokens work, and **where blockchain truly adds value**: transparent registries, tracking public funds, smart procurement, digital identity and cross-border payments. A key emphasis is **AML/CFT compliance**, consumer protection and cybersecurity.

Planned components include **hands-on labs on testnets**, case studies from other countries and risk-assessment workshops spanning volatility, stablecoins, DeFi and tokenized assets. The program is modular so **civil-service academies and supervisory bodies** can adopt it.

**Our take (Metaadvisor):**
- Standardized skills in the public sector are a prerequisite for **sound regulation** and real-world projects.  
- Success should be judged by **measurable pilots** (savings, faster processes, fewer errors), not just headcount.  
- Keeping the focus on registries, identity and procurement can make **public services** more transparent and faster, without unnecessary intermediaries.
