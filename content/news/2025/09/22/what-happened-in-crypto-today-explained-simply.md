**Summary (per Cointelegraph’s daily wrap):**  
Three items today: **Wormhole** unveils updated **tokenomics** for **W** (a revenue-backed reserve + clearer staking), the **UK’s FCA** publishes a **consultation draft** for crypto rules, and **Bitwise** notes that **generic SEC listing standards** could make **crypto ETP/ETF** listings easier — though inflows still depend on **real demand**.

### 1) Wormhole: W tokenomics refresh
- **Protocol revenue reserve** as a buffer.  
- **Staking** with a baseline yield + extra incentives.  
- **Smoother unlock schedule** to reduce sell shocks.  
*Why it matters:* steadier incentives can support adoption.

### 2) United Kingdom: FCA draft for crypto firms
- Applying **existing financial rules** to crypto businesses.  
- Focus on **operational resilience**, **KYC/AML**, **consumer protection**.  
- **Consumer Duty**: requirement to deliver **fair outcomes** for customers.  
*Why it matters:* adds **legal clarity** but raises **compliance costs**.

### 3) United States: Bitwise on SEC ETP/ETF standards
- **Generic listing standards** → **faster/easier** listings.  
- **But**: without **demand**, products won’t see inflows.  
*Why it matters:* broadens the shelf; the market still decides with capital.

## Glossary (plain)
- **ETP/ETF**: exchange-traded products that track an asset (e.g., Bitcoin).  
- **Tokenomics**: a token’s rules (supply, unlocks, rewards, reserves).  
- **Staking**: locking tokens to earn rewards and/or secure a network.  
- **Consumer Duty**: rule that firms must deliver **fair outcomes** to consumers.

- **Wormhole**: stability focus (reserve + smoother unlocks) is long-term positive.  
- **FCA**: pro-institution clarity; tougher for small teams.  
- **SEC/ETP**: more products ≠ automatic inflows; **narrative & liquidity** remain key.
