China is once again reshaping the way people pay. After QR codes and mobile wallets, the next phase has arrived: **biometric payments using your palm — no card, no smartphone, no PIN.**

A viral video shows shoppers paying by simply holding their hand over a scanner.  
No PIN, no device, no confirmation — identity is verified through the unique vein pattern in the palm.

## How does it work?

The system combines:

- analysis of **subcutaneous palm vein patterns**  
- palm lines & contour mapping  
- real-time AI identity verification  

The data links to a user account — usually **WeChat Pay** or **Alipay**.

## Where is it already being used?

- supermarkets and retail stores  
- fast-food chains  
- public transport gates  
- power-bank rental kiosks  
- campus & access systems

China is skipping the “card era” and moving to **the body as the payment credential.**

## Why the palm?

Manufacturers claim it is **more secure than fingerprints or face recognition**:

| Biometric type | Can be copied? | Visible externally? |
|----------------|---------------|---------------------|
| Fingerprint | ✅ Leaves traces | ✅ Visible |
| Face ID | ✅ Can be filmed | ✅ Visible |
| Palm vein pattern | ❌ Under the skin | ❌ Not visible |

## Advantages

✅ No card, phone, wallet or battery  
✅ Faster than QR or POS (0.5–1 sec)  
✅ Touch-free & hygienic  

## Risks & issues

⚠️ Biometric data cannot be “reset” once leaked  
⚠️ Centralised databases = surveillance potential  
⚠️ China has weak privacy laws → risk of abuse  
⚠️ Western regulators may block or slow adoption

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

- China is clearly moving toward a **fully cashless, cardless society** — biometrics are the next step.  
- The tech is impressive, but the **risk of data misuse is huge**.  
- What happens if your hand is **wet, dirty, sweaty or injured**? Will the system recognise you — or will you **go hungry even if you have money**?  
- The real question isn’t just *“Can we do it?”* but *“Should we?”*
