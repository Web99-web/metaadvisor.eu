The **U.S. Internal Revenue Service (IRS)** and **Department of the Treasury** have introduced a new **“safe harbor” framework** that allows **crypto funds and ETFs** to participate in **staking** — *locking and delegating crypto assets to earn rewards* — under full tax and regulatory compliance.

Under the new rules, funds traded on regulated exchanges can now stake their tokens and **distribute staking rewards to investors**, without facing tax or legal uncertainty.

According to the IRS document, funds may take part in staking if they:
- are traded on a national securities exchange,  
- hold **only cash and one type of digital asset**,  
- use a **regulated custodian**,  
- and implement measures to **protect investors from risk**.

> “This framework provides long-awaited clarity for institutional funds, ETFs, and trusts, enabling them to stake digital assets while remaining fully compliant,” said **Bill Hughes**, senior counsel at **Consensys**.

---

### A milestone for the crypto market

The IRS decision marks a **historic shift in U.S. regulation**.  
Until now, ETFs were allowed to **hold crypto assets** (like Bitcoin or Ether) but not to participate directly in blockchain validation.  
Now, institutional funds will be able to **actively engage in blockchain networks** and earn **passive income through staking**.

For the **Ethereum ecosystem**, this is especially significant — demand for ETH staking is expected to rise, though yields per validator may decrease as more institutional participants join.  
Analysts predict that by late 2025, **over 15 % of all staked ETH** could come from institutional sources.
