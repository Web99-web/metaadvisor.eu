Tinder is turning to artificial intelligence to breathe new life into its dating app after **nine consecutive quarters of declining subscriber numbers**.  
The company behind Tinder, **Match Group**, has begun testing a feature called **“Chemistry”**, which uses interactive questions and, with user permission, **accesses private Camera Roll photos** to learn more about each user’s personality and interests through AI.

According to **Match Group CEO Spencer Rascoff**, the feature is already being piloted in New Zealand and Australia and will become **a key part of Tinder’s product experience in 2026.**

---

### Privacy concerns rise

Match isn’t the only company seeking access to users’ personal photos. **Meta** recently introduced a similar AI-powered feature that asks for permission to analyze photos not yet shared publicly, supposedly to suggest “smart edits.”  
In both cases, **users gain little real benefit**, while giving companies broad access to personal data.

---

### What Tinder really aims for

Match says the AI system will use questions and photo analysis to better understand each user and **recommend more compatible matches**.  
If your gallery shows hiking or cycling photos, for example, Tinder’s AI might pair you with someone who enjoys the same hobbies.  
But critics argue that **the line between personalization and privacy is growing dangerously thin.**

---

### Financial impact

While Tinder experiments with AI, the **Match Group’s revenue continues to slide**.  
The company said the testing would cut **Tinder’s direct revenue by around $14 million in Q4**, reducing total guidance to **$865–875 million**, below analyst expectations of $884 million.

Meanwhile, Match is expanding AI across its portfolio to improve engagement, but the bigger question remains: **how much privacy are users willing to trade for a “better match”?**
