Researchers from **Peking University** have developed an **analog AI chip** that, according to early testing, could reach **up to 1,000× higher efficiency** than advanced digital processors such as the **Nvidia H100 GPU**.  
The chip uses **RRAM (Resistive RAM)** technology and an analog data-processing method, enabling parallel computation with far lower energy consumption.

As published in *Nature Electronics*, the prototype showed remarkable performance in mathematical operations essential for artificial intelligence — including neural networks and matrix calculations.  
This could open the door to a new generation of **energy-efficient AI systems**.

While Western media reacted with skepticism, Chinese researchers emphasize that this is a **verified research success**, not mere media hype.  
China rarely publishes findings that have not been extensively verified, so further testing is expected to confirm the results.

The development also fits into a broader strategy: **China is building a self-sufficient technology ecosystem** to reduce dependence on U.S. chips and accelerate domestic high-performance production.  
If these lab results hold true in practice, it could represent a **turning point for the global chip and computing industry**.
