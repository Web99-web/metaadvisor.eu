A viral claim has been circulating online in recent months:  
**Telegram is worth $30 billion and operates with only 30 employees working fully remote.**

How much of that is true?  
How much is myth?  
And what does this mean for the future of tech companies?

---

## 🔍 What is confirmed — and what isn’t?

### ✔️ 1. A valuation of around $30 billion  
Several tech outlets and analysts mention a **valuation of roughly $30 billion** for Telegram.  
This refers to *investor estimates*, not an official market valuation, since Telegram **is not publicly listed** and does not publish financial reports.

➡️ The number is plausible — but not formally verified.

---

### ✔️ 2. The claim that Telegram has “only 30 employees”  
This is the part that went viral — but:

- Telegram’s official LinkedIn page lists **11–50 employees**
- various tech articles mention **around 30 core team members**
- there is no official, confirmed number
- “30 people” likely refers to the **engineering core**, excluding freelancers, contractors and remote contributors

➡️ Possible, but not proven.

---

## 🧩 How can a massive global platform operate with so few people?

Telegram is a very special case for several reasons:

### **1. Extremely efficient engineering**
The infrastructure is built to scale with a small group of highly specialised developers.

### **2. Minimal organisational structure**
No giant HR departments, no bloated marketing teams, no layers of management.

### **3. A wide network of external contributors**
Much of the work comes from remote specialists who are not listed as full-time employees.

### **4. Independent private financing**
Telegram has long been funded privately by Pavel Durov, meaning:
- no investor pressure  
- no quarterly reporting  
- no bureaucracy  

---

## 🌍 Why does Telegram have no HR department?

Telegram follows a simple philosophy:

> “If we need HR, we hired the wrong people.”

The company hires rarely and only the highest-calibre engineers.  
It’s the opposite of Silicon Valley’s “grow big at all costs” approach — and it seems to work.

---

## 📱 What does this mean for the future of the tech industry?

Telegram’s structure highlights a major trend:

- tech platforms may need **far fewer people** than we assumed  
- remote-first teams can outperform traditional office setups  
- lean models are becoming a competitive advantage  
- global platforms can be built by “micro-teams”  

➡️ **We are entering an era of extremely efficient, minimal tech organisations.**

---

## 🔎 Conclusion

Whether Telegram has 30 people or 50 — one thing is clear:  
➡️ The company operates with an unusually small core team.  
➡️ Its $30 billion valuation is plausible, though unofficial.

Telegram demonstrates that:

- productivity doesn’t come from team size  
- remote work can deliver world-class performance  
- modern platforms don’t need traditional corporate structures  

A reminder that in tech,  
**small teams can build big things.**
