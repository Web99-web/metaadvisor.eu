Artificial intelligence is accelerating faster than labour markets can keep up — and a new report from the UK’s **National Foundation for Educational Research (NFER)** warns that **up to 3 million low-skilled jobs could disappear by 2035**.

The most vulnerable roles include:

- administrative and clerical work  
- routine operational and machine-handling jobs  
- repetitive tasks that are easily automated  

At the same time, **high-skilled professionals will see rising demand**, as AI tools increase productivity and expand workloads across advanced industries.

## 🔵 Winners and losers in the AI transformation
Despite the projected loss of 3 million low-skilled jobs, the UK economy is expected to **add 2.3 million new jobs by 2035**, but in very different fields.

Growing demand will be concentrated in:

- AI and data specialists  
- managers, analysts and project leaders  
- digital creative roles  
- psychologists, educators and consultants  
- tech-savvy workers across all industries  

AI isn’t “removing all jobs” —  
**it is reshaping the labour market in favour of those who can work with new technologies.**

## 🔵 Why do studies disagree?
The NFER findings contrast with recent research suggesting that highly skilled roles may be just as exposed:

- A King’s College study found that **high-paying companies shed 9.4% of staff between 2021 and 2025**, largely after ChatGPT’s release.  
- The UK government warns that **management consultants, psychologists and legal professionals** face significant AI exposure.  
- Meanwhile, roles like roofers, bricklayers and professional athletes remain relatively safe.

The truth is simple:  
**AI is impacting every skill level — just differently.**

## 🔵 The shift has already begun
Several large employers are already citing AI as a factor in restructuring:

- **Clifford Chance** cut 10% of business-services staff due to automation.  
- **PwC** reversed its plan to hire 100,000 workers by 2026, saying “the world is different now” and AI has changed the company’s staffing needs.

In other words —  
**AI is not coming; it has already arrived.**

## 🔵 What does this mean for Europe and global markets?
Although these findings focus on the UK, the implications are global:

- repetitive office tasks are rapidly being automated  
- digital literacy is becoming non-negotiable  
- AI is becoming a standard tool in every profession  
- those who upskill early gain a major advantage  

One key message stands out:

**AI doesn’t replace people.  
It replaces people who cannot work with AI.**
