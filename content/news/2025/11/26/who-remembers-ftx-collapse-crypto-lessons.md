For years, FTX was marketed as a “safe, regulated exchange,” while its founder Sam Bankman-Fried was hailed as a prodigy who would bridge traditional finance and crypto.  
But within **72 hours**, everything collapsed — billions of customer funds vanished, and the industry suffered one of its biggest reputational hits.

### What actually happened?
Investigations revealed that FTX had **misused customer deposits**, secretly funneling them to Alameda Research to cover risky bets and mounting losses.  
Alameda enjoyed privileged access to FTX, no proper liquidation rules, and almost zero internal oversight — a structural failure waiting to happen.

When the FTT token crashed, the entire collateral system evaporated.  
A massive, unprecedented “bank run” followed — and FTX was unable to process withdrawals because the funds were already gone.

### Why did everything fall apart so quickly?
The domino effect started when Binance publicly announced it would sell its FTT holdings:

- users rushed to withdraw funds  
- Alameda couldn’t plug the holes  
- FTX ran out of liquidity  
- reserves evaporated  
- bankruptcy followed  

All within **three days**.

### Why does the FTX collapse still matter today?
Because it revealed what happens when:

- customer funds are not segregated  
- reserves are not transparent  
- internal controls don’t exist  
- a company uses its own token as collateral  
- too much power is concentrated in one place  

FTX became a symbol of **how not to operate** — in crypto or in traditional finance.

Since the collapse:

- exchanges are required to show *proof of reserves*  
- users increasingly choose self-custody  
- regulators worldwide tightened oversight  
- institutions demand greater transparency  
- DeFi gained credibility thanks to its inherent openness  

FTX was not just a collapse — it was a global wake-up call.
