In recent days, multiple users have reported receiving almost identical FUD messages on Instagram and Messenger — alerts claiming that Ethereum is “about to crash,” that ETFs are “bleeding out,” or that on-chain whales are “dumping everything.” These messages come from unknown profiles, often with generic names or crypto logos.

The most commonly reported senders include:

- @heyitsme_bjgarcia  
- @akshayyee  
- profiles such as “SOLANA | MEME | COIN”

The message content is nearly identical in every case:

Run! Ethereum is about to crash! It’s going to break below $1500 any moment! ETFs seeing continuous outflows, on-chain whales are dumping! A major US…

This is classic FUD (Fear, Uncertainty, Doubt) — a tactic used to create panic, emotional pressure, and irrational reactions. Bot networks send hundreds of these messages daily, hoping users will click profiles, open links, or act impulsively out of fear.

The messages themselves are not dangerous. They become dangerous **only** if the user clicks a link, opens the sender's profile, or interacts with the account. The recommended response is simple: ignore the message, block the sender, and report it as spam.

Another clear pattern is that bots claim Ethereum is falling below 1500 USD, even when exchanges are reporting price increases. This shows that bots do not follow real market conditions — their goal is panic, not information.

## 🛡️ How to Protect Yourself From FUD Bots

- ignore all messages from unknown profiles  
- never click links in private messages  
- block and report suspicious accounts  
- rely on verified exchanges and tools for price data  
- avoid emotional decisions — exactly what FUD bots want  

Crypto markets are volatile, but the biggest losses come from emotional reactions. FUD messages are engineered to cause panic — stay focused on data, not fear.
