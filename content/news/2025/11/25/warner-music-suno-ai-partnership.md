Warner Music Group (WMG) has officially announced a major partnership with the fast-growing AI music startup Suno — a deal that ends a high-profile copyright lawsuit and signals a fundamental shift in how the music industry approaches artificial intelligence.

According to WMG, the agreement will “open new frontiers in music creation, interaction, and discovery, while compensating and protecting artists, songwriters, and the broader creative community.”  
For the first time, a major music label is embracing a **fully licensed framework** for AI-generated music rather than fighting it in court.

## Suno acquires Songkick — expanding into the music ecosystem

Alongside the settlement, Suno has acquired **Songkick**, the well-known live-event and concert-discovery platform. Financial details were not disclosed, but the move marks Suno’s clear intention to build a deeper presence in the global music ecosystem.

- WMG acquired Songkick’s app and brand in 2017  
- Live Nation absorbed the ticketing portion of the business  
- Under Suno, Songkick will continue operating as a **fan-facing platform** within a broader AI-driven ecosystem  

This acquisition signals that Suno is transitioning from a pure AI music generator into a full-stack music technology company.

## What changes for users? Licensed AI models and new rules

The partnership introduces several major updates:

- Suno will launch **advanced licensed AI models** in 2026  
- Existing unlicensed models will be retired  
- Audio downloads will require a **paid subscription**  
- Free users will be able to play and share songs, but not download them  

Crucially, WMG artists will gain **full control** over how their names, images, voices, likenesses, and compositions are used in AI-generated music.

This addresses one of the biggest industry concerns: unauthorized cloning of artists’ voices and creative output.

## WMG’s major artists

Warner’s roster includes:

- Lady Gaga  
- Coldplay  
- The Weeknd  
- Sabrina Carpenter  
- and many more global acts

The agreement sets a precedent for artists to earn revenue from AI-generated music in a structured, transparent way.

## A turning point for the music industry?

Almost certainly.

After years of lawsuits, takedowns and disputes, the industry is shifting to a new model:

### 👉 lawsuits → licensing → partnerships → monetization

Suno and Udio — another AI startup that settled with WMG last week — are the first major examples.  
Other AI music platforms will now face pressure to adopt similar licensing frameworks or risk legal consequences.
