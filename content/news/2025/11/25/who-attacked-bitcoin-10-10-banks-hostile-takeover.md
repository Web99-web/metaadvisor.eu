## Did the banks attack Bitcoin on 10.10?  
### Was the crash accidental — or a coordinated hostile takeover?

Over the past weeks, social media has been flooded with analyses and speculation suggesting that the Bitcoin crash on October 10th **was not a “natural” move**, but rather **a calculated operation by major banking institutions** — most notably JP Morgan and elements of the traditional financial establishment — with a clear goal:

👉 **destabilize MicroStrategy (MSTR)**  
👉 **shake the broader market**  
👉 **buy Bitcoin cheaper.**

What started as a “Twitter theory” is now being openly discussed by respected figures in the industry: **Ran Neuner, Simon Dixon and Arthur Hayes** — all pointing in the same direction:

**The crash was not an accident.**

---

## 🔥 1. The spark — a viral post that set the market on fire

One of the most viral posts stated:

**“Users are allegedly rushing to close accounts at JP Morgan after a premeditated attack on MSTR shareholders.”**

The message struck a nerve.  
October 10th was a day of **very thin liquidity** — the exact moment when a coordinated attack would have maximum effect.

The visual shared alongside it framed the conflict clearly:  
**J.P. Morgan vs MicroStrategy — Wall Street vs Bitcoin.**

The community instantly sensed: **something here doesn’t add up.**

---

## 🔥 2. Simon Dixon: “They are on the same team.”

Dixon delivered the most direct accusation:

> **“JP Morgan and the broader financial-industrial complex are using old vassalisation tactics to take control of MSTR.  
> They will win — because Saylor was already owned by Wall Street.”**

In other words:  
**the attack was strategic, not random.**

Why target MicroStrategy?

👉 **it holds more than 226,000 BTC**,  
👉 **it is the largest corporate Bitcoin treasury in the world**,  
👉 effectively a **“Bitcoin vault”** capable of influencing the entire market.

Controlling MSTR → **means controlling a massive amount of Bitcoin.**

---

## 🔥 3. Ran Neuner: “MSTR has become a systemic risk.”

According to Neuner, MicroStrategy has:

- **grown too fast**,  
- **leveraged too aggressively**,  
- **concentrated too much Bitcoin**,  
- and **become too influential** for Wall Street’s comfort.

His conclusion:

> **“They’re too big for Wall Street to let them control that much Bitcoin on their own.”**

The options, as he sees them:  
**acquire them, break them, or discipline them.**

---

## 🔥 4. Arthur Hayes: “When the system feels threatened, it attacks.”

Hayes’ famous line explains the entire event:

**“When the system feels threatened, it attacks.”**

To him, the 10.10 crash looked like:

👉 **synchronised liquidity wipeouts**  
👉 **futures-driven liquidation cascades**  
👉 **a drop without any fundamental catalyst**  
👉 **too clean and too efficient to be organic**

Simply put: **a defensive strike by the legacy system.**

---

## 🤯 So was the crash orchestrated?

A growing number of analysts now believe: **yes — or at the very least, not entirely organic.**

**A plausible scenario:**

- Wall Street wanted deeper Bitcoin exposure  
- but not near all-time-high levels  
- so the easiest play was: **crash the price, trigger panic, accumulate cheap BTC**

Why specifically 10.10?

- lowest liquidity of the week  
- known vulnerabilities in MSTR  
- algorithms map out liquidation zones precisely  
- ideal timing for a futures-driven attack

**The outcome:**

⚡ **sharp crash**  
⚡ **panic selling by retail**  
⚡ **a V-shaped rebound indicating massive stealth buying**

Too many similarities with historical Wall Street tactics to ignore.

Whatever truly happened on 10.10, one thing is undeniable:

### **This was not a normal dip. It looked like a test of power — the opening move in a struggle for control over Bitcoin. A classic ‘divide and rule’ strategy: confuse, frighten, shake the market — then dominate.**

The 10.10 crash looked like:

2025 is shaping up to be the year where the global **battle for monetary dominance** truly begins.
