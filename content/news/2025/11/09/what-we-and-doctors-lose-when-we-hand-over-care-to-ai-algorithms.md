Eric Reinhart, a physician and anthropologist studying the culture of medicine, accompanied his seventy-year-old friend Pamela to a doctor’s appointment in a US clinic.  
That clinic uses an **AI assistant** – a system that automatically records, summarizes, and analyzes doctor-patient conversations.

The way the system worked left a strong impression on Reinhart.  
The computer began transcribing while Pamela was still speaking, effectively taking over the flow of the conversation.  
As she described her condition, the doctor clicked the mouse, and a block of text began appearing on the screen. The AI scribe captured their words in real time, highlighted key terms, suggested diagnoses, and added billing codes.

The doctor, apparently satisfied that the computer had captured enough, turned away to review the generated text while Pamela kept speaking.

After the appointment, Reinhart – as both physician and observer – asked to review the AI-generated note.  
The summary was fluent and accurate, yet it failed to capture the tremor in Pamela’s voice when she mentioned the stairs or the brief flash of fear when she admitted she now avoids them.  

Such moments are becoming increasingly common. For generations, doctors resisted technologies that threatened to alter their human connection with patients — but surrendering that connection to algorithms is now happening faster than ever.

### Our addition — Metaadvisor comment

When doctors write by hand, they rely on instinct and experience — sensing tone, expression, and context that no algorithm can reproduce.  

But once that process is handed to a system that compresses the conversation into a “neat” text, those nuances are lost — the ones that define empathy and understanding.  

Doctors typically ask, listen, write, and continue that natural rhythm of dialogue.  
From the text, we understood that in this case, the AI **summarized the entire conversation into a single text** at the end.  
That method risks losing small but important details — something mentioned early on that never makes it into the final note.  

---

### Our insight (Metaadvisor):

- AI can increase speed but not empathy.  
- Medical communication depends on focus, not automation.  
- Systems that summarize dialogues risk losing essential meaning.  
- The human ability to truly listen remains irreplaceable.
