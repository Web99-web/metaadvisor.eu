French **hardware wallet maker Ledger** is considering a **New York Stock Exchange (NYSE)** listing after its 2025 revenues surged into the **hundreds of millions of dollars**, fueled by a record wave of **crypto hacks** and growing demand for **cold storage devices**.

CEO **Pascal Gauthier** told the *Financial Times* that the Paris-based company, founded in 2014, is experiencing its **best year ever**, as both individuals and businesses rush to secure their assets from increasingly sophisticated cyberattacks.

> “We’re being hacked more and more every day — bank accounts, crypto — and it’s not going to get better next year or the year after that,” Gauthier said.

---

### Hacks are booming — and Ledger is rising

In the first half of 2025 alone, **over $2.2 billion in crypto assets** were stolen — surpassing the total for all of 2024.  
According to *Chainalysis*, **around 23% of the attacks targeted individual wallets**, directly boosting Ledger’s sales.

Ledger currently safeguards **around $100 billion worth of Bitcoin and other digital assets**. The company expects a further sales boost during **Black Friday** and the **Christmas season**, when many users seek safer storage for their crypto.

---

### Preparing for a U.S. listing

Gauthier revealed that **Ledger plans to raise capital next year** — either through a **private round** or a **U.S. public listing**.  
Ledger is already expanding its **New York office**, noting that:  
> “The money is in New York today — nowhere else in the world, certainly not in Europe.”

The firm was last valued at **$1.5 billion** in 2023, backed by investors including **Cathay Innovation, Draper Esprit, Draper Dragon, and Samsung Ventures**.  
Competitors such as **Trezor** and **Tangem** also offer cold-storage wallets, but Ledger remains the **dominant brand** in the sector.
