The U.S. **Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)** is planning a historic move — to launch **spot crypto trading under federal oversight**, without waiting for Congress.

According to CoinDesk, **acting chair Caroline Pham** has been in direct talks with several **regulated exchanges** about introducing the first spot products as early as December. The initiative seeks to bring Bitcoin and Ethereum — classified as commodities in the U.S. — under **federal regulatory supervision**, similar to oil or gold markets.

---

### What’s really happening

Congress has long debated expanding CFTC authority to spot markets, but legislation has stalled.  
Pham argues the agency already has enough legal authority, citing **existing mandates and recommendations from the Presidential Working Group on Digital Asset Markets**.  

In practice, this means **CFTC-licensed venues** such as **CME Group, LedgerX, or Cboe Digital (formerly ErisX)** could soon offer **spot trading for BTC and ETH**, but under stricter reporting and transparency rules.

---

### Why this is a precedent

Until now, U.S. spot crypto trading has been limited to **private exchanges** such as **Coinbase, Binance.US, and Kraken**, operating under **state-level money transmission licenses**.  
They are not federally regulated and exist in a “gray zone” between finance and technology.

If the CFTC proceeds, **federal spot markets** would effectively **end this gray zone**.  
Exchanges like Coinbase and Kraken would need to register as **federally licensed trading venues (DCM/SEF)**, adhere to audits, reporting, and compliance requirements comparable to derivative markets.

This could reduce **flexibility for new token listings**, increase compliance costs, and slow down approvals — while offering users more security but less choice.

---

### Coinbase, Binance and Kraken under pressure

Major exchanges — **Coinbase, Binance and Kraken** — now face a critical regulatory crossroads.  
The CFTC wants to bring parts of the spot market under its direct oversight, forcing global players to adapt to a stricter framework.  

**Will Coinbase, Binance and Kraken comply immediately, or seek delays and alternative paths?**  
The answer could define how the U.S. government and global crypto industry interact in the coming years.

Behind the scenes, a **power struggle between “Old Finance” (Wall Street) and “New Crypto”** is unfolding.  
While the CFTC aims to act decisively, exchanges are calling for a clear, stable legislative framework.

---

### The bigger picture — America’s race for crypto dominance

The move also carries a **geopolitical dimension**.  
The U.S. seeks to reclaim leadership from markets like Singapore, Dubai, and Hong Kong, which already have clear crypto frameworks.  
If successful, the CFTC could position the U.S. as the **global leader in institutional crypto trading**.

However, tighter oversight could **slow innovation** and **limit the speed of token listings**, distancing the market from its decentralized roots.  

In theory, **Bitcoin and Ethereum function as both commodities and securities** — a duality that continues to complicate regulatory clarity.  
Currently, the CFTC treats them as commodities, while the SEC maintains that many tokens should be classified as securities.
