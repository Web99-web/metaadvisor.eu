## Bitcoin ATMs: Common in North America, Unknown to Many Elsewhere

If you’re reading this from Europe, Asia, or another region where crypto infrastructure is limited, you may be surprised to learn that the United States, Canada, and several Latin American countries have **Bitcoin ATMs** — physical machines where users can buy or sell BTC using cash.

These devices look like traditional bank ATMs, but instead of withdrawing fiat, customers deposit banknotes and receive **Bitcoin directly to their wallet**.

The first Bitcoin ATMs appeared around **2013–2014**, initially in Vancouver and later across the US.  
Today, there are over **30,000 machines** globally, with the majority located in:

- United States  
- Canada  
- Mexico  
- Spain  
- UK  

They’re placed in convenience stores, gas stations, vape shops, electronic stores, and sometimes in shopping malls.

Despite higher fees (typically 6–12%), they remain popular among users who prefer:

- **instant cash-to-crypto conversion**  
- **no bank involvement**  
- **simple onboarding without complex apps**  

Bitcoin ATMs have become an important entry point for the unbanked population in North America.

## The US Operator Crypto Dispensers Now Facing a Potential $100M Sale

One of the more surprising developments this week:  
**Crypto Dispensers**, a Chicago-based Bitcoin ATM operator, is exploring a strategic sale worth **$100 million**, following federal charges against its CEO.

In a newly released statement, the company said it hired advisors to assess buyer interest. The firm had already shifted from physical machines toward **software-based solutions** back in 2020 to reduce fraud and regulatory pressure.

### What triggered the sale process?

Earlier this week, the US Department of Justice unsealed an indictment accusing CEO Firas Isa of facilitating a **$10 million money-laundering scheme** between 2018 and 2025.

According to prosecutors, some users allegedly deposited proceeds from wire fraud and narcotics trafficking into the Bitcoin ATM network. Isa and the company have pleaded **not guilty**, and no assets have yet been seized.

Whether the sale will proceed remains uncertain, but the case has reignited debate over the future of physical crypto infrastructure in the US.

## MetaAdvisor Commentary (EN)

Bitcoin ATMs highlight a fundamental reality:  
there is still a massive demand for **cash-to-crypto** access, especially in countries with fragmented banking systems or large unbanked populations.

However, the global environment is changing.  
Regulators are tightening compliance requirements, margins are shrinking, and the market is slowly consolidating — especially in the US.

While the future of physical BTC ATMs may not be as bright as a decade ago, the industry still plays an important transitional role in bridging **traditional cash economies** with **digital assets**.
