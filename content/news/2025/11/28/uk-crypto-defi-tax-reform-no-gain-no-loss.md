The United Kingdom has proposed one of the most significant crypto tax changes in years, introducing a **“no gain, no loss”** framework that could dramatically reshape how DeFi activities are taxed.

Under this proposed rule, DeFi users **would no longer trigger capital gains tax when depositing tokens into lending platforms or liquidity pools**.  
Instead, taxable events would only occur **when the tokens are actually sold** — aligning tax liability with real economic outcomes rather than technical blockchain movements.

This marks a major shift away from the current system, where even simple protocol deposits can be considered taxable transactions.

## What exactly is changing?

HMRC (His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs) proposes that the following DeFi actions would *not* trigger immediate taxation:

- lending a token and receiving the same token type back  
- borrowing against crypto collateral  
- moving tokens into liquidity pools  
- shifting assets between DeFi strategies without true economic gain  

Capital gains would be calculated **only at the moment of redemption**, based on:

- how many tokens a user receives back  
- relative to how many they originally contributed  

## Why this matters

In the UK, capital gains tax rates range from **18% to 32%**.  
Under the current regime, even a basic liquidity provision or lending deposit could create a taxable event — even if the user made **no real profit**.

The new approach aims to:

- reduce unnecessary tax burdens  
- bring tax rules closer to real-world financial logic  
- ensure DeFi users aren’t penalized for normal protocol interactions  
- make the UK more competitive as a global crypto hub  

## Positive reactions from the industry

**Sian Morton**, marketing lead at Relay Protocol, praised the reform as:

> “A meaningful step forward for DeFi users who borrow stablecoins against their crypto collateral.”

She argues that the changes better reflect “the true economic reality” of DeFi activity.

**Maria Riivari**, attorney at Aave, said the reform:

> “would bring clarity that DeFi transactions do not trigger tax until you truly sell your tokens.”

She added that other countries *should pay close attention* to the depth and practicality behind HMRC’s approach.

## Bigger picture: the UK wants to lead in crypto policy

Since Brexit, the UK has made clear it wants to position itself as a **European crypto powerhouse**.

If adopted, this reform could make the UK:

- one of the most DeFi-friendly tax jurisdictions in the Western world  
- a benchmark for tax clarity  
- a competitive hub for institutional and retail crypto markets
