The crypto market is once again under heavy pressure today. Bitcoin dropped intraday to **$93,935**, before recovering slightly to **$95,940**, remaining well below the key psychological $100,000 level.

The Fear & Greed Index fell to **10**, the lowest reading in nearly nine months, signaling **extreme fear** among investors.

For weeks, Bitcoin has been stuck in a frustrating pattern:
- when the **Nasdaq 100** falls, BTC drops even harder,
- but when the Nasdaq rises, BTC barely reacts.

This phenomenon — known as **negative performance skew** — has now reached levels last seen at the **end of 2022**, when Bitcoin was forming its previous bear-market bottom.

According to Wintermute analyst Jasper De Maere, this is **not** a loss of correlation, but **risk asymmetry**. BTC reacts aggressively to negative market events and weakly to positive ones.

## What’s driving the decline?

A strong combination of factors:

- profit-taking by long-term holders  
- institutional capital outflows  
- liquidation of overleveraged long positions  
- fading expectations of a Federal Reserve rate cut  
- delayed macroeconomic data due to the US government shutdown  
- low liquidity amplifying even moderate sell orders  

Together, these forces create a period of elevated volatility and uncertainty.

## Why this could still signal an early bottoming zone

Historically, extreme negative skew tends to appear **right before** a new market bottom forms:

- in 2018,  
- in 2020,  
- and in late 2022.

In each case, market structure signals looked very similar to today.

Bitcoin is currently trading in the **94k–96k USD** range, which resembles the beginning of **bottoming conditions**, though not yet a confirmed bottom.

Some analysts interpret the current mix of fear, liquidations, and skew behavior as evidence that the market is entering the **final capitulation and cleanup phase** before stabilizing.

## Our take (Metaadvisor)

- The drop is mostly **liquidity-driven and sentiment-driven**, not fundamentally driven.  
- Negative skew at 2022-like levels often appears close to bottoming zones.  
- Macro remains the biggest risk factor — especially without updated inflation data.  
- The 94k–96k range could mark the early development of a bottoming structure.  
- Short-term volatility between 94k and 98k is likely to continue.
