Robert Kiyosaki, author of the global bestseller *Rich Dad Poor Dad*, argues that the current market sell-off is driven primarily by a **global cash shortage**. According to him, investors are not selling because assets have lost their long-term value, but because they urgently need liquidity. Despite the decline, he says he is **not selling anything** and continues to hold Bitcoin and gold.

## A global liquidity squeeze

Kiyosaki claims that many investors are being forced to liquidate positions simply to meet obligations. Companies and households facing higher financing costs and tightening credit conditions are pushing markets lower through necessity, not sentiment.

This, he says, creates an exaggerated downward movement even when fundamentals remain intact.

## The “Big Print” scenario

Kiyosaki reiterates his long-standing view that governments will eventually turn to aggressive money printing once rising debt levels become unmanageable.  
In such an environment, he expects:

- stronger performance in gold and silver  
- renewed demand for Bitcoin due to its fixed supply  
- further weakening of fiat currencies  
- potential waves of inflation  

He points out that Bitcoin’s hard cap of **21 million coins** continues to set it apart from traditional monetary systems.

## Extreme Fear and shaken sentiment

The Fear & Greed Index has fallen into **Extreme Fear**, a zone historically associated with attractive long-term entry points. While not a guarantee, Kiyosaki believes the current fear is largely psychological and not a reflection of actual deterioration in key assets.

## Our commentary (Metaadvisor)

Kiyosaki’s framing of the downturn as a liquidity event is credible and consistent with broader macro signals. Forced selling often accelerates declines across markets. Bitcoin and gold have historically benefitted after major monetary expansions, and similar dynamics may appear again.  
Volatility is likely to persist until liquidity conditions improve. Market psychology remains a decisive factor: fear drives premature selling, while disciplined investors tend to benefit over longer horizons.
