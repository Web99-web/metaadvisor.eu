Germany, long seen as losing momentum in global tech, is suddenly entering a period of renewed industrial acceleration. Following the discovery of **[43 million tons of lithium](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/germany-43-million-tons-lithium-altmark-electric-vehicles/)** — the largest and most strategically important deposit in Europe — another major development has arrived. Tether, the world’s largest stablecoin issuer, is in talks to lead a **€1 billion** investment into the German robotics company **Neura Robotics**.

The combination of lithium, robotics and global crypto capital positions Germany as a potential new driver of Europe’s technological and industrial future.

## Tether moves into German robotics

According to the Financial Times, Tether is negotiating to lead a funding round of around **€1 billion** for Neura Robotics. This would raise the company’s valuation to between €8–10 billion — a dramatic jump from its €120 million round in January.

Neura develops AI-powered humanoid robots designed first for industrial applications and later for home use. The company aims to manufacture **5 million robots by 2030** and claims the robotics sector is entering its “iPhone moment.” It already has **€1 billion in pre-orders**.

Tether has been expanding into “frontier tech,” including agritech, brain-tech, sports tech and video platforms like Rumble. Investment into Germany reflects a broader strategy to enter advanced physical and AI-driven industries.

## Lithium: the foundation of Europe’s new industrial era

The discovery of **[43 million tons of lithium](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/germany-43-million-tons-lithium-altmark-electric-vehicles/)** is one of Europe’s most consequential geological findings. It could allow Germany to become a central supplier for:

- electric vehicle battery manufacturing  
- long-term energy storage  
- next-generation clean energy technologies  

The combination of robotics and lithium unlocks a new industrial model:

- vertically integrated European AI + robotics + battery supply chains  
- reduced dependency on U.S. and Asian tech ecosystems  
- accelerated industrial modernisation  
- stronger geopolitical and technological autonomy for the EU  

For the first time in many years, Germany is not merely reacting — it is setting the pace.

## What this means for the EU

The European Union has fallen behind in AI, robotics and deep tech innovation. But within weeks, two pivotal developments emerged:

- Europe’s largest lithium discovery  
- Massive global interest in German robotics from a top-tier crypto giant  
- Tech titans (Tesla, Nvidia, SoftBank) pushing into humanoid robotics  
- A realistic opportunity for Europe to build complete innovation cycles  

Germany is now emerging as the anchor of this transformation.
