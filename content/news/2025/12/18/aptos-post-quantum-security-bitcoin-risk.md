# What if quantum computers break Bitcoin? Aptos introduces technology designed to prevent exactly that

Aptos is launching one of the most advanced security innovations in crypto: **post-quantum digital signatures**, a mechanism that could protect blockchain networks even if future quantum computers become strong enough to break today’s cryptography.

The threat may not be immediate, but progress by IBM, Google and new NIST standards shows the security landscape is shifting — and the industry must prepare.

Aptos argues:  
**the best defense begins before the attack becomes possible.**

---

## Why could quantum computers threaten blockchain?

Current cryptographic signature schemes (including Bitcoin):

- cannot be broken by classical computers  
- but could be broken by sufficiently powerful quantum computers

This would theoretically allow:

- forging digital signatures  
- stealing assets from existing accounts  
- retroactively attacking old transactions  
- compromising entire blockchain networks  

This is why the Web3 industry is searching for **quantum-resistant solutions**.

---

## What is Aptos introducing?

Through proposal **AIP-137**, Aptos aims to add:

### **SLH-DSA post-quantum signatures (FIPS 205)**
A hash-based scheme selected by NIST as one of the new standards for the quantum era.

Benefits:

- users can optionally enable post-quantum protection  
- works with existing accounts  
- makes Aptos one of the first major blockchains with native PQ-security  

This strengthens long-term digital identity and asset protection.

---

## How does this impact everyday users?

If quantum computers become a real threat in 5–10 years, blockchains without protection could face severe security failures.

This upgrade matters because:

- Aptos preemptively protects user accounts  
- the network gains long-term defensive architecture  
- investors gain future-proof infrastructure  

In short:  
**this is defense before the enemy arrives.**
