Over the past decade, fintech innovation has reshaped how startups and corporations manage money. One major sector, however, has largely been left behind: nonprofit organizations.

Givefront is a fintech startup focused on modernizing financial operations for the US nonprofit sector — a segment representing roughly 6% of the US economy but still reliant on outdated financial tools.

## Built specifically for the US nonprofit market

Givefront is intentionally not a global product. The platform is designed around US nonprofit law, particularly organizations operating under **501(c)(3)** tax-exempt status.

While this status provides significant tax advantages, it also imposes strict requirements on how funds are tracked, spent and reported to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). Mistakes can result in the loss of tax-exempt status, donor confidence and operational viability.

## How Givefront modernizes nonprofit finance

Rather than introducing new fundraising models, Givefront focuses on improving core financial infrastructure.

First, it centralizes financial activity. Multiple bank accounts, spreadsheets and disconnected tools are replaced with a single platform that clearly tracks where funds come from, how they are allocated and how they are spent.

Second, Givefront automates compliance and reporting. Financial statements, usage tracking and audit preparation are structured to reduce human error and regulatory risk.

Third, the platform increases transparency for donors. Donations can be linked to specific programs and expenses, making reporting clearer and reinforcing trust.

## Why this matters now

Regulatory scrutiny of US nonprofits is increasing, while donors are demanding higher levels of accountability. At the same time, many organizations lack the internal resources to manage complex financial workflows.

Givefront addresses this gap by modernizing a critical but long-neglected layer of nonprofit infrastructure.
