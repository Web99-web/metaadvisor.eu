Bitcoin has been trading in a relatively narrow range between **$85,000 and $89,000** over the past several days, marked by sharp upward moves followed by equally rapid pullbacks. At first glance, the market appears indecisive and unstable. However, on-chain data suggests a different underlying dynamic: **new capital continues to flow into Bitcoin**, even as price fluctuates.

The key metric supporting this view is **realized capitalization**, which values each bitcoin at the price it last moved rather than its current market price. This measure provides insight into actual capital inflows rather than speculative price action. Bitcoin’s realized cap currently stands at a **record high of approximately $1.125 trillion**, indicating that investors are still accumulating BTC at elevated price levels.

At the same time, short-term price movements remain highly sensitive to macroeconomic developments. The latest U.S. Consumer Price Index (CPI) data showed **the lowest inflation reading since 2021**, triggering a rapid rally that pushed Bitcoin toward $89,000. That move, however, quickly reversed, with prices falling back into the $85,000–$86,000 range as markets questioned the sustainability of the inflation data and whether the Federal Reserve would actually move toward near-term interest rate cuts.

The result is a market characterized by **long-term capital accumulation alongside short-term volatility and frequent liquidations**. Unlike previous bear markets, where investors capitulated and sold coins below their cost basis, current on-chain data does not show widespread distribution at a loss.

The **$85,000–$89,000 zone** has therefore emerged as a consolidation range where the market is absorbing macroeconomic uncertainty while continuing to attract structural capital inflows. If realized capitalization remains elevated, this base could play a decisive role in Bitcoin’s next major move — whether that ultimately takes the form of a breakout or an extended period of sideways consolidation.
