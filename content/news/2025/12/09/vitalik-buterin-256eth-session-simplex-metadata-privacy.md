# Vitalik Buterin funds metadata protection: 256 ETH to Session and SimpleX to secure free and private communication

Vitalik Buterin has issued a powerful global signal about the future of digital freedom.  
He donated a total of **256 ETH** to two leading projects building *metadata-secure* communication systems:

- **128 ETH to Session**  
- **128 ETH to SimpleX**

His message is clear:  
**The next major battleground in technology is not message content — but metadata.**

Even with end-to-end encryption, metadata remains exposed: who talks to whom, when, how often, from which IP address, and on what device.  
For surveillance systems, these patterns are often more informative than the message itself.

---

## Why metadata is today’s biggest privacy risk

Modern encrypted messengers — WhatsApp, Signal, iMessage — protect content, but still collect:

- your phone number  
- your contacts  
- timestamps and frequency  
- IP address and location metadata  
- device fingerprints  
- social graph patterns

This creates a highly detailed picture of your digital life.

Buterin argues:  
**“True privacy begins only when metadata disappears.”**

---

## Session and SimpleX — projects pushing messaging into a new era

### 🔹 Session  
- uses onion routing (similar to the Tor network)  
- has no central server to hack, block or subpoena  
- identities are based on cryptographic keys, not phone numbers  
- the network is decentralized and metadata-resistant  
- ensures that no one can link sender, receiver and timing

### 🔹 SimpleX  
- messages never pass through a single shared server  
- relies on distributed relay nodes  
- stores no user profiles or contact databases  
- designed so that *no part of the system* can track communication patterns  
- aims at complete anonymity

For Buterin, these projects are the first viable paths toward global, mainstream metadata-private communication.

---

## Why now?

Worldwide pressure on digital privacy is growing fast:

- governments push for message scanning “for safety”  
- centralized servers are major attack points  
- corporate data harvesting expands yearly  
- **43% of public WiFi users report at least one cyberattack**

Buterin’s donation is a counter-move:  
**technology that protects users, not systems of control.**

---

## How messaging could change because of this

If this vision succeeds, we could see:

- decentralized social networks without phone numbers  
- censorship-resistant communication protocols  
- safe channels for journalists, activists and citizens  
- Web3 identities that reveal no metadata  
- messaging as an open protocol rather than a proprietary app

This is a new technological frontier:  
**the battle for control over the invisible data that shapes our digital lives.**
