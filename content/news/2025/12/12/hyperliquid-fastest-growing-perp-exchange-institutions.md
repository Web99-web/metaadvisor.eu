## Why is Hyperliquid exploding in growth?

Unlike dYdX, GMX or Aevo, Hyperliquid is not built on an existing chain.  
Its L1 is optimized for:

- ultra-low latency  
- deterministic finality  
- high-frequency execution  
- real-time orderbook updates  

This results in near-instant position openings and closes with minimal slippage.

### **2) Deep liquidity and rapidly increasing volume**
In recent months, Hyperliquid has occasionally surpassed dYdX, Aevo and GMX in daily volume — a sign that professional traders are migrating to the platform.

### **3) A CEX-style orderbook — but decentralized**
Hyperliquid offers a full on-chain orderbook and matching engine.  
This ensures:

- predictable spreads  
- stable liquidity  
- reliable execution  
- transparency for every order  

It behaves like a centralized exchange — without custody risk.

### **4) Fast expansion of markets**
Hyperliquid lists perpetuals for hundreds of assets, including highly volatile tokens unavailable elsewhere — attracting both retail and pro traders.

## Why are institutions suddenly paying attention?

Institutional desks value:

- consistency  
- ultra-low latency  
- predictable rule sets  
- reliable matching engines  

Hyperliquid provides all of this — and avoids the regulatory and geopolitical pressure facing centralized exchanges.

Following FTX, Binance lawsuits and SEC pressure on Coinbase, institutions seek infrastructure without **counterparty risk**.  
A decentralized orderbook fits that requirement.

## Can institutions legally trade on DEXs? Yes — with strict compliance

Institutions *can* use DEXs as long as they maintain:

- internal compliance frameworks  
- full transaction audit trails  
- proof-of-funds (KYC/AML)  
- institutional custody solutions such as Fireblocks, Copper, Anchorage, Ceffu  

This means institutions do NOT “retail-trade” directly from self-custody wallets — everything is permissioned and compliant.
