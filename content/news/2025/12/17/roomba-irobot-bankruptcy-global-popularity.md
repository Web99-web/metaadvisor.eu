## Roomba was ahead of its time

When **Roomba** launched in 2002, the idea of a small autonomous robot cleaning homes felt almost futuristic. Smartphones did not yet exist, Wi-Fi was not ubiquitous, and the concept of a “smart home” was largely niche.

Despite this, Roomba quickly became a **global phenomenon**. For millions of households, it was the first robot they ever owned — simple, reliable, and quietly revolutionary.

## The paradox: booming market, falling pioneer

Today, **robot vacuum cleaners are more popular than ever**. Remote work, larger living spaces, and a growing appetite for automation have pushed these devices firmly into the mainstream.

Ironically, this mass adoption arrived just as **iRobot**, the company behind Roomba, began to struggle. Market share eroded, costs rose, and profitability slipped away.

## Cheaper rivals and a lost technological lead

While iRobot focused on refining its own ecosystem, competitors — particularly from China — flooded the market with:
- more advanced sensors and mapping  
- combined vacuuming and mopping  
- faster product cycles  
- significantly lower prices  

Roomba remained a strong brand, but its **innovation advantage gradually disappeared**.

## The Amazon deal that never happened

In 2022, Amazon announced plans to acquire iRobot for approximately **$1.7 billion**. The deal was expected to stabilize iRobot financially and integrate Roomba deeply into Amazon’s smart-home ecosystem.

However, the **European Commission** raised serious competition concerns, fearing Amazon could favor Roomba on its platform and marginalize rivals. In parallel, U.S. regulators at the **Federal Trade Commission (FTC)** were increasingly skeptical of Big Tech acquisitions.

In early 2024, the deal was officially **abandoned**.

## Bankruptcy at the moment of mass adoption

By December 2025, iRobot filed for **Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection**. The company did not disappear overnight, but it lost its independence and entered restructuring under new ownership.

The timing is striking:  
➡️ robot vacuums are now everyday household devices  
➡️ yet the company that made them popular could not capitalize on the boom

## Our take (MetaAdvisor):

- Roomba was a **true pioneer**, but pioneers rarely dominate forever.  
- Innovation must be matched with **scalable economics and pricing power**.  
- EU regulators targeted market dominance, not robotics itself.  
- iRobot’s fall highlights how brutally fast consumer tech markets evolve.  
- Roomba remains a symbol of the first wave of home robotics.
