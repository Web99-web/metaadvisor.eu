On **Christmas Day, December 25, 2025**, a number of **Trust Wallet** users were impacted by a serious security incident that led to the theft of approximately **$7 million worth of cryptocurrencies**. While early headlines caused alarm across social media, one critical point must be made clear from the start: **neither the blockchain itself nor the Trust Wallet system as a whole was hacked**. The issue was isolated to a **Chrome browser extension**.

## What Exactly Happened?

The attack specifically targeted the **Trust Wallet Chrome extension (version 2.68)**. According to available information, attackers managed to inject malicious code into the extension’s update process, giving them access to sensitive user data.

This allowed attackers to obtain:
- private keys  
- seed phrases  
- and full control over affected wallets  

As a result, funds could be drained without any additional confirmation from users.

## Who first discovered the attack?

The attack was first identified by well-known on-chain investigator **ZachXBT**, who alerted the crypto community on December 25 to suspicious fund outflows linked to the Trust Wallet Chrome extension. His warning triggered a rapid community response and was later confirmed by Trust Wallet.

## Who Was Affected — and Who Was Not?

It is important to clarify:
- **The Trust Wallet mobile app was NOT affected**
- The incident was limited strictly to the **Chrome extension**
- Users on other browsers were not impacted  

Only users who had the compromised version installed and held funds in the wallet at the time of the attack were affected.

## Why This Matters — and Who Owns Trust Wallet?

Many users are unaware that **Trust Wallet has been owned by Binance since 2018**. While Trust Wallet operates as a **non-custodial wallet** — meaning no KYC and no access to user funds — this ownership helps explain the swift response.

Trust Wallet:
- removed the compromised version  
- released a secure update  
- urged users to update immediately  
- confirmed it will **fully reimburse all verified user losses**  

## The Bigger Issue: Browser Extension Security

This incident once again highlights the risks associated with **browser-based crypto wallet extensions**. While convenient, they introduce additional attack surfaces and depend on:
- third-party distribution platforms (such as the Chrome Web Store)  
- secure update mechanisms  
- the user’s local environment  

In other words, the issue lies **not with crypto technology itself**, but with the software layer used to access it.

## Conclusion

The Trust Wallet hack on Christmas is a sobering reminder that **non-custodial does not automatically mean maximum security**. For long-term storage, many experienced users prefer hardware wallets or mobile apps, while browser extensions are often reserved for smaller, operational balances.

This incident may not be the last of its kind, but it clearly reinforces a key lesson in crypto: **security is always a combination of technology and user decisions**.
