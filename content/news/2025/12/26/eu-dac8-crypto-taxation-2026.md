# EU Introduces DAC8: How Crypto Will Be Taxed From January 1, 2026

Starting **January 1, 2026**, the European Union will implement **DAC8**, a directive that formally brings crypto assets into the **EU’s cross-border tax reporting framework**.

It is important to clarify upfront:  
**DAC8 does not introduce a new crypto tax.**  
Instead, it defines how tax authorities obtain information about crypto transactions.

In short, the tax rules already exist — DAC8 removes the information gap.

## What Is DAC8 and Why Was It Introduced?

DAC8 is an extension of the EU’s existing administrative cooperation system used for banks, securities, and financial assets.

Its objectives include:
- closing gaps in crypto tax oversight  
- enabling automatic data exchange between EU member states  
- treating crypto assets similarly to other forms of financial wealth  

Crypto is no longer considered an “off-system” asset class.

## Who Reports Data to Tax Authorities?

Under DAC8, **users do not report data directly**.  
The obligation lies with **crypto service providers**.

This includes:
- crypto exchanges  
- brokers  
- custodial wallet providers  

These platforms must:
- collect customer identification data  
- report transaction activity to EU tax authorities  
- enable cross-border data sharing within the EU  

If you live in one EU country but use a platform registered in another, **your local tax authority will still receive the data**.

## When Does a Tax Obligation Arise?

Taxes do **not** apply to every transaction — only to **realized gains**.

### Taxable events include:
- selling crypto for fiat (EUR, USD)  
- exchanging crypto for another asset if a profit is realized  
- using crypto to purchase goods or services  

### Not taxable:
- purchasing crypto  
- holding crypto  
- transferring assets between personal wallets  
- selling at a loss  

Example:  
Buying ETH for €1,000 and selling it for €800 → **no tax due**.

## ETH → USDT → BTC: How It’s Treated

Each step is assessed independently:
- ETH → USDT is considered a sale of ETH  
- profit → taxable  
- loss → zero tax  

BTC then receives a new cost basis equal to the USDT value used.

There is **no double taxation**.

## Small Amounts and Frequent Trades

DAC8 sets **no minimum reporting threshold**.

This means:
- even small transactions may be reported  
- tax liability is still calculated according to national laws  

In practice:
- small transactions without gains → no tax  
- minor profits → minimal tax exposure  

Visibility does not equal automatic taxation.

## What DAC8 Actually Changes

DAC8:
- does not ban crypto  
- does not automatically collect taxes  
- does not alter blockchain technology  

However, it does:
- reduce tax anonymity  
- make undisclosed gains harder to hide  
- integrate crypto into a regulated financial framework  

Crypto in the EU is entering a more mature phase — with greater transparency and clearer rules.
