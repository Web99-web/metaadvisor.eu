# IMF and El Salvador: Bitcoin Remains Part of the Strategy as Tensions Ease

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has softened its stance toward El Salvador, while praising the country’s **stronger-than-expected economic growth**. The IMF now projects **real GDP growth of around 4% in 2025**, marking a notable shift from its previously critical tone regarding El Salvador’s Bitcoin policy.

## Bitcoin no longer the main point of conflict

In its latest statements, the IMF **no longer explicitly calls on El Salvador to halt Bitcoin accumulation**. While fiscal and operational risks are still acknowledged, the focus has shifted toward **governance, transparency and risk management**, rather than outright opposition to cryptocurrency exposure.

In November, El Salvador took advantage of a sharp market correction and purchased **more than 1,000 Bitcoin in a single move**. The country’s total holdings now stand at nearly **7,500 BTC**, worth approximately **$660 million** at current prices.

## The Chivo wallet remains the key issue

The main unresolved issue continues to be the **state-run Chivo crypto wallet**. The IMF confirmed that discussions around a potential **sale or restructuring** of the Chivo system are “well advanced.”

Key concerns include:
- protection of public funds  
- reduction of operational risks  
- clearer limits on direct state involvement  

This suggests that the IMF is not seeking to remove Bitcoin from El Salvador’s strategy, but rather to **scale back direct government control over crypto infrastructure**.

## From confrontation to pragmatic compromise

In March, El Salvador reached a **$3.5 billion financing agreement** with the IMF under an extended fund facility. Although the official documents referenced limits on state Bitcoin activity, the government’s actions indicate a **flexible interpretation** of those conditions — so far without serious consequences.

For global crypto markets, this represents an important signal: **Bitcoin is increasingly being treated as a fiscal reality**, even by institutions that were once firmly opposed to it.

## Conclusion

- The IMF acknowledges El Salvador’s economic progress  
- Bitcoin remains part of the country’s financial strategy  
- The relationship has shifted from confrontation to pragmatic compromise  
- Institutional attitudes toward Bitcoin are visibly evolving  

El Salvador remains a global outlier — not as a failed experiment, but as a **sovereign state pursuing an independent monetary path**.
