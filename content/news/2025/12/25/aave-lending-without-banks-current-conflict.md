# Aave: How lending money without banks works — and what’s behind the current conflict

Aave is one of the largest and longest-running decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols in the world. Its core purpose is simple: allow people and institutions to **lend and borrow money without banks**, without traditional contracts, and without intermediaries.

Everything operates through smart contracts on the blockchain, and the system runs continuously — 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

## How lending on Aave actually works

First, one important clarification: **Aave is not a bank and not an exchange**. It does not work like a traditional finance app.

Lending and borrowing happen through the Aave web application, using a self-custodied crypto wallet.

## What this looks like in practice (simple version)

- The user has a crypto wallet (e.g., MetaMask)  
- Opens **app.aave.com** in a browser  
- Clicks **“Connect wallet”**  
- Selects their wallet (MetaMask, WalletConnect, etc.)  

After connecting, the user can:
- deposit crypto assets as collateral  
- borrow stablecoins or other tokens  
- monitor interest rates and liquidation risk  

Everything happens **directly from the wallet**, without registration and without a user account.

If the value of the collateral falls below a predefined safety threshold, the system automatically triggers liquidation to protect the borrowed funds. This is precisely why Aave operates **without human intervention and without manual control**.

## Important warning

- Aave **does not have a traditional login**  
- There is no email, password, or user account  
- If a website asks for registration → **it is not Aave**  

Always verify that the URL is **app.aave.com** — phishing attempts are common.

## How Aave makes money

Aave generates revenue by retaining **a portion of the interest paid by borrowers**. Most of the interest goes to liquidity providers, while a smaller share remains within the protocol.

Another revenue stream comes from **flash loans** — specialized loans used for technical and financial operations in DeFi that are borrowed and repaid within a single blockchain transaction.

## <a href="https://metaadvisor.eu/hr/news/aave-dao-labs-sukob-cowswap-naknade/" style="color:#1e40af; text-decoration:none;">Internal conflict at Aave: DAO accuses Aave Labs over CoW Swap fee routing</a>

The current dispute within Aave is **not a technical failure or a security incident**, but a disagreement over revenue control and governance boundaries.

The trigger was Aave’s integration with CoW Swap, a trade execution tool. After the integration, certain fees were routed to entities associated with Aave Labs — the private company responsible for developing and maintaining much of Aave’s infrastructure.

Some DAO members argue that revenues generated under the Aave brand should be subject to greater community oversight. Aave Labs counters that interfaces, integrations, and operational layers fall under its responsibility.

Crucially, **this dispute does not affect the protocol’s operation or users’ ability to borrow funds**.

## Is lending on Aave at risk?

No.  
Lending and borrowing on Aave **continue to function normally**.

The protocol holds very large liquidity reserves, smart contracts operate as designed, and users can still:

- deposit assets  
- borrow stablecoins  
- repay loans  
- earn interest  

The conflict exists at the governance and revenue level — **Aave’s core functionality remains unaffected**. That is why Aave continues to be one of the most widely used DeFi protocols in the market.
