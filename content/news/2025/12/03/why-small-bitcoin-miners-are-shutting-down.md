# Why Small Bitcoin Miners Are Shutting Down
## Why large miners survive while small ones fail — and where American Bitcoin fits in

Bitcoin mining in 2025 is facing one of the toughest environments in years. According to industry data, hashpower revenue has dropped from **$55 per petahash (PH) per day** in Q3 to around **$35/PH per day**, while the median all-in cost for large public miners sits near **$44/PH per day**.

This means:  
**a significant share of the industry is now mining at a loss.**

Meanwhile, the network hashrate remains close to **1.0–1.1 ZH/s**, near record highs, creating intense competition for each block. Even the newest ASIC rigs no longer offer quick payback — ROI now exceeds **1,000 days**, while the next halving is roughly **850 days** away.

If conditions don’t improve, many miners who purchased hardware in 2024–2025 may never recoup their investment before the halving.  
This is why small and mid-size miners are shutting down their operations.

---

# Why large miners survive

Despite the harsh environment, the largest mining companies are stronger than ever.

## 1) Lowest energy costs in the industry
Large miners secure:
- long-term power contracts  
- access to stranded or excess energy  
- electricity at a fraction of what small miners pay  

Smaller miners cannot compete with such cost structure.

## 2) Access to capital
Big miners can:
- issue equity,  
- take loans,  
- lease ASIC hardware,  
- use extensive reserves.  

Small miners fund everything personally — leaving no buffer during downturns.

## 3) Expanding during crises
When small miners capitulate, large companies acquire:
- ASICs at steep discounts,  
- energy agreements,  
- land and data-center space.  

Crises become expansion opportunities.

## 4) Diversification beyond Bitcoin
Modern mining companies operate:
- AI/GPU compute centers,  
- grid-balancing services,  
- infrastructure hosting,  
- heat-recovery systems.

Small miners rely solely on Bitcoin block rewards.

## 5) Strategic mining even at temporary losses
Large miners can operate “in the red” to:
- maintain market share,  
- eliminate competition,  
- accumulate BTC during weak cycles,  
- position for long-term dominance.  

Small miners can’t absorb prolonged negative margins.

# How Does American Bitcoin (ABTC) Fit Into All This?

One of the most interesting developments of 2025 is the rapid rise of  
**[American Bitcoin (ABTC)](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/american-bitcoin-abtc-eric-trump-donald-trump-jr/)**.

The company is in the public spotlight because it is associated with **Eric Trump and Donald Trump Jr.**, and its business model perfectly illustrates why large miners dominate the industry.

ABTC benefits from all the advantages of large-scale systems:
- access to the cheapest energy in the U.S. (especially in Texas and Wyoming),
- strong political backing,
- aggressive acquisition of ASIC equipment from smaller competitors,
- extensive infrastructure and access to capital.

Its goal is to build the **largest “American” mining network**, as the U.S. increasingly develops a pro-Bitcoin regulatory framework.  
ABTC isn’t playing for 2025 — it’s playing for 2030.
