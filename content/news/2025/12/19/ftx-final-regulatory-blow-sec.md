# FTX: The Final Regulatory Blow and a Warning to the Crypto Industry

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is closing one of the most damaging chapters in the history of the crypto industry. Following the collapse of the FTX exchange and the criminal conviction of its founder, Sam Bankman-Fried, regulators have now imposed final sanctions on his closest associates.

This move formally brings the regulatory phase of a scandal that erupted in late 2022 to an end — but its consequences continue to shape how crypto markets are governed worldwide.

## Long-term bans for former executives

Several former senior executives of FTX and its affiliated entities have agreed to final settlements with the SEC. Among them is Caroline Ellison, the former CEO of Alameda Research, who accepted a 10-year ban from serving as an officer or director of any company. Other former executives and engineers received eight-year bans.

According to the SEC, the core issue was not market volatility but the systematic misuse of customer funds. Custom-built software allowed Alameda Research to access FTX customer deposits with virtually no limits, bypassing risk controls that applied to other users of the exchange.

## Why FTX became a turning point for crypto regulation

Unlike many previous crypto failures that could be attributed to poor risk management or market downturns, the FTX collapse exposed classic financial fraud wrapped in digital infrastructure. As a result, the case has had an outsized regulatory impact.

FTX provided regulators and lawmakers with a powerful argument that crypto exchanges cannot operate outside established financial oversight frameworks. Since the collapse, discussions around exchange supervision, custody of customer assets, and balance-sheet transparency have accelerated significantly, particularly in the U.S. and Europe.

## A message to the market: accountability arrives, even if delayed

While some individuals avoided prison sentences due to cooperation with authorities, the long-term professional bans send a clear signal: returning to positions of responsibility in the financial sector is effectively off the table for key FTX figures.

For the crypto market, this marks a transition into a new era. The period in which exchanges could present themselves as lightly regulated tech startups is drawing to a close. FTX remains a lasting reminder that the loss of user trust does not only destroy a single company — it can reshape an entire industry.
