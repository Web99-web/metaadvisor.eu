# Aave Governance Dispute Erupts Over CoW Swap Fees

One of DeFi’s flagship protocols, **Aave**, is facing a serious internal dispute that cuts to the core of decentralized governance:  
**Who ultimately controls revenue in a DAO-driven ecosystem — the community or the development company?**

The controversy erupted following Aave’s integration with decentralized exchange aggregator **CoW Swap**, after DAO members noticed that a portion of swap fees was no longer flowing into the DAO treasury.

---

## What is the dispute about?

According to several Aave DAO delegates, **fees generated via CoW Swap trades** are being routed to an **onchain address controlled by Aave Labs**, rather than the official Aave DAO treasury.

Estimated figures cited by DAO members include:
- roughly **$200,000 per week**
- up to **$10 million per year**

This has triggered backlash within the community, with critics arguing that fees generated under the Aave brand should belong to the DAO and its token holders.

---

## Aave Labs’ response

Aave Labs rejected the accusations, stating that:
- **frontend interfaces and integrations** fall under Aave Labs’ authority  
- the DAO governs **protocol-level decisions**, such as interest rates and smart contract upgrades  
- previous transfers of fees to the DAO were **voluntary contributions**, not contractual obligations  

From Aave Labs’ perspective, the disagreement reflects **different interpretations of governance boundaries**, not misconduct.

---

## Why this matters beyond Aave

This dispute highlights a broader structural issue in DeFi:
- Where does **decentralization end** and corporate control begin?  
- Who owns **revenue streams** in DAO-governed protocols?  
- How much real power does a DAO have without direct control over income?

These questions apply to many DeFi projects that rely on foundations or development companies to operate key infrastructure.

---

## Potential impact on the AAVE token

So far, markets have reacted calmly. However, longer-term risks include:
- reduced DAO revenue affecting **token economics**
- erosion of community trust
- increased pressure for clearer legal and governance frameworks

Aave has long been viewed as a model for DeFi governance. That is precisely why this dispute matters. If revenue control remains ambiguous, it challenges one of DeFi’s foundational promises: **community ownership and accountability**.
