Pi Network is one of the most unusual projects in the history of cryptocurrencies. For six years, it has continued to grow and attract millions of users worldwide, while never having an open, free market where its currency could be normally traded. This combination of mass popularity and complete illiquidity makes Pi Network unique — and deeply controversial.

---

## How Pi Network Started

Pi Network was launched in 2019 in the United States. The project was founded by a team connected to Stanford University, most notably Nicolas Kokkalis, a PhD in computer science focused on distributed systems, and Chengdiao Fan, a PhD in social sciences specializing in user behavior in digital networks.

From the very beginning, the goal was to make cryptocurrencies accessible to “everyday people,” without expensive hardware, technical knowledge, or upfront financial investment.

---

## How Pi “Mining” Works

Unlike Bitcoin, Pi is not mined using computational power. Users open the mobile app once per day and confirm their activity, which allows them to participate in token distribution.

It is important to note that this is not mining in the traditional sense, but rather an **internal token distribution within a closed system**, without a market mechanism to determine price.

---

## Where Pi Network Is Most Popular

Pi Network has gained particular traction in:
- Southeast Asia  
- Africa  
- Latin America  
- Eastern Europe and the Balkans  

In these regions, Pi is often perceived as a free opportunity — something that requires no money, only time and patience.

---

## Why Pi Is Popular Even Though It Can’t Be Sold

At first glance, Pi’s popularity seems irrational, but it is largely driven by psychology:
- users feel no financial risk because they didn’t buy Pi
- strong belief in a future exchange listing
- a massive community creates a sense of legitimacy

However, a large user base alone does not guarantee real market value.

---

## How People Try to Sell Pi

In practice, some users attempt to sell Pi through private peer-to-peer (P2P) agreements, typically arranged via social networks or messaging apps. These transactions happen directly between individuals, without any official intermediary.

### What Does “No Escrow System” Mean?

An **escrow system** is a protection mechanism where a neutral third party (such as an exchange or a smart contract) temporarily holds funds or cryptocurrency until both sides fulfill their obligations.

In standard crypto trading:
- the buyer deposits funds into escrow
- the seller sends the cryptocurrency
- escrow automatically completes the exchange once conditions are met

With Pi Network, **no such system exists**. One party must always send first — either Pi or money. If the other party disappears or acts dishonestly, there is **no refund, no protection, and no dispute resolution**.

This is why scams related to selling Pi are common.

---

## What Would Happen If Pi Reached the Market

If Pi Network were listed on exchanges, a massive sell-off would be highly likely. Millions of users who accumulated Pi at no cost would try to sell as soon as possible, creating enormous downward pressure on price.

Such a collapse would not necessarily kill the project, but it would immediately reveal whether there is real demand beyond the community itself.

---

## Does Pi Network Still Have a Chance?

From a technical standpoint, Pi Network could still be listed on exchanges, and its founders remain active. However, the longer the project operates without an open market, the higher expectations grow — and the greater the risk of disappointment.

Without real use cases and genuine buyers, sheer scale alone cannot sustain long-term value.

---

## Conclusion

Pi Network has spent six years defying the basic rules of the crypto market. It has grown without a price, without exchanges, and without liquidity — driven by hope, community, and the psychology of “free.”

Until a real, open market exists, Pi’s value remains purely theoretical.
