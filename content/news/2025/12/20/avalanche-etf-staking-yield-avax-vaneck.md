# Avalanche ETF with yield: what does this actually mean?

VanEck, one of the world’s well-known asset managers, has taken an important step in the evolution of crypto ETFs by filing an amended proposal for an **Avalanche ETF (VAVX)**. Unlike most existing crypto ETFs, this fund would not only track the price of AVAX, but also **actively generate yield through staking**.

In simple terms, the ETF would not just sit idle waiting for price appreciation — it would put part of its holdings to work.

## What is staking — explained simply

Staking can be compared to **earning interest or renting out an asset**. AVAX tokens held by the fund would be temporarily “locked” to help secure and operate the Avalanche network. In return, the network pays staking rewards.

Key points:
- the tokens are **not sold**
- there is no trading or speculation involved
- staking produces **ongoing rewards**, similar to interest

According to the filing, the ETF could **stake up to 70% of its AVAX holdings**.

## How staking works inside the ETF

Investors **do not need a wallet** and do not have to understand the technical side of staking. All operational aspects are handled by the fund itself.

VanEck plans to use **Coinbase Crypto Services** as its initial staking provider. Staking rewards would be reduced by a **4% service fee**, with the remaining yield flowing directly into the fund and reflected in its net asset value (NAV).

The AVAX tokens would be held with regulated custodians such as **Anchorage Digital** and **Coinbase Custody**, stored in secure cold wallets.

## Why this matters for investors

Until now, most crypto ETFs have offered **pure price exposure**. By adding staking, the Avalanche ETF moves into a new category — a fund that combines **price exposure with income generation**.

This is especially relevant for:
- institutional investors  
- funds seeking more predictable returns  
- investors who prefer something beyond pure price speculation  

Such structures bring crypto investment products closer to traditional financial instruments like bond or income funds.

## A broader trend: yield-generating crypto ETFs

VanEck is not alone in exploring this model. Other issuers have also updated filings for Avalanche and similar proof-of-stake ETFs to enable yield generation, suggesting that regulators are slowly opening the door to **income-producing crypto ETFs**.

If approved, this structure could become a template for future crypto investment products.

## Conclusion

An Avalanche ETF with staking rewards represents a meaningful step in the maturation of crypto investment vehicles. Rather than relying solely on price appreciation, the fund would offer a **blend of growth and yield**, bringing crypto closer to familiar financial models — while keeping blockchain mechanics behind the scenes.
