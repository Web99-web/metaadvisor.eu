In recent months, a growing number of commentators have claimed that **Bitcoin’s four-year market cycle is dead**. Spot ETFs, institutional capital, and regulatory acceptance are said to have smoothed out the boom-and-bust dynamics that historically defined the crypto market.

**Fidelity strongly disagrees.**

According to Jurrien Timmer, Director of Global Macro at Fidelity and one of the longest-standing institutional observers of Bitcoin, **the four-year cycle is still playing out in a remarkably consistent way**, despite changes in market structure.

## What is the four-year Bitcoin cycle?

Bitcoin’s cycle is closely tied to its **halving events**, which occur roughly every four years and cut the block reward for miners in half. This reduction in new supply has historically led to a familiar pattern:

- strong price appreciation following the halving  
- an euphoric late-stage bull market  
- a deep correction (often 70–80%)  
- a prolonged consolidation phase until the next halving  

This pattern was clearly visible after the 2012, 2016, and 2020 halvings.

## Why some believe the cycle is over

Proponents of the “new Bitcoin” thesis point to:
- spot Bitcoin ETFs  
- participation from pension funds and large asset managers  
- increasing regulatory clarity  

Under this view, Bitcoin no longer behaves like a fringe asset but more like a mature macro asset with lower volatility.

## Fidelity: the data tells a different story

Timmer argues that **market data does not support this conclusion**. When previous Bitcoin bull markets are aligned on a timeline, **the October 2025 peak around $125,000** fits neatly into the historical cycle — both in duration and structure.

In other words, ETFs and institutional flows have **not eliminated Bitcoin’s cyclicality**.

## What comes after the peak?

If the cycle remains intact, the next phase is not renewed euphoria but a **cooling period**.

Fidelity warns that **2026 could be a “lame year”** — characterized by sideways markets, muted returns, and reduced attention. This does not imply a collapse, but rather an extended consolidation phase before the next cycle begins.

Historically, such periods have been:
- frustrating for short-term traders  
- attractive for patient, long-term investors  

## ETFs don’t change Bitcoin’s core economics

While ETFs may bring more stable capital inflows, Fidelity emphasizes that they **do not alter Bitcoin’s fundamental economics**: fixed supply, cyclical demand, and strong market psychology.

Institutional capital may dampen extremes, but it **does not eliminate cycles**, especially for an asset still closely linked to global liquidity conditions.

## Conclusion

From Fidelity’s perspective, Bitcoin is currently behaving **not unusually, but consistently with its own history**. Halving, rally, peak, and correction remain the core structure of the market.

If this assessment proves correct, the market is entering a phase where **patience, discipline, and an understanding of cycles matter more than short-term hype**.

## Our take (MetaAdvisor)

- ETFs have not ended Bitcoin’s market cycles; they have made them more transparent.  
- Quiet market phases often precede the next growth cycle.  
- 2026 may be dull, but strategically important.  
- Long-term investors have historically built positions during such periods.
