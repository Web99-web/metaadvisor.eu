# Ethereum prepares Glamsterdam: why this upgrade matters

Fresh off the successful **Fusaka upgrade**, Ethereum developers are already moving toward the next major milestone: **Glamsterdam**. The goal is clear — to make Ethereum **fairer, more resilient to manipulation, and structurally more mature**.

The name Glamsterdam combines two parallel upgrades:
- **Amsterdam** on the execution layer (smart contracts and transaction rules)
- **Gloas** on the consensus layer (validators and block finalization)

Behind the branding lies a fundamental protocol-level shift.

## 🔵 [Fusaka upgrade: the foundation for Glamsterdam](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/ethereum-fusaka-upgrade-scaling-2025/)

The Fusaka upgrade reduced node operating costs and improved overall network efficiency. Glamsterdam builds on that foundation, focusing not just on performance, but on **fairness and market structure** within Ethereum.

## What problem is Ethereum trying to solve?

At the core of Glamsterdam is **enshrined Proposer-Builder Separation (ePBS)**, formally tracked as **EIP-7732**. Unlike today’s solutions, ePBS is designed to be **embedded directly into the Ethereum protocol**, rather than relying on external infrastructure.

Currently, the same actors can often:
- build blocks  
- decide which transactions are included  
- reorder transactions to extract additional profit  

This behavior is what gives rise to MEV.

## 🔵 [What is MEV and why it is a problem for Ethereum](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/what-is-mev-trading-defi/)

## How ePBS changes the rules

Under ePBS:
- **Builders** construct blocks and cryptographically seal their contents  
- **Proposers (validators)** select blocks **without seeing the transactions inside**  
- Transaction details are revealed **only after block finalization**

This design prevents advance inspection, reordering, or censorship of transactions — dramatically reducing the ability to exploit MEV.

Until now, this separation has depended on **off-chain relay services**, which introduced trust assumptions and centralization risks. Glamsterdam moves fairness **into the core protocol itself**.

## Block-level Access Lists: a quiet but critical improvement

Glamsterdam also includes **EIP-7928 (Block-level Access Lists)**. This change allows blocks to declare in advance:
- which accounts  
- which smart contracts  
- which data will be accessed  

As a result, Ethereum clients can preload data, reduce latency, and improve scalability — a key step for long-term network performance.

## The bigger picture: Ethereum is growing up

Glamsterdam signals a clear direction:
- fewer structural advantages for large players  
- less hidden manipulation  
- more transparent and predictable block production  

This is not an upgrade designed to “pump” prices. It is an upgrade designed to **strengthen Ethereum as financial infrastructure**.

## Conclusion

If implemented as planned, Glamsterdam could mark one of Ethereum’s most important transitions:
- MEV will not disappear, but it will become **far harder to abuse**
- validators lose the ability to quietly manipulate transaction ordering
- Ethereum further establishes itself as a serious, long-term network

It is a slow, technical path — but that is exactly what separates **mature infrastructure** from experimentation.
