Ten years ago, in December 2015, Bitcoin was a marginal curiosity — a digital experiment followed only by technologists, cryptographers and a handful of early adopters. It traded at around **$390**, and most observers dismissed it as too risky, too niche, too strange. Back then, spending $390 on a dinner, a weekend trip or a new gadget felt more sensible than buying an obscure internet coin.

A decade later, millions of people are asking themselves a quietly painful question:

**“If I had skipped that one dinner — could I travel around the world today?”**

Because Bitcoin now trades near **$90,000**, representing an extraordinary **23,000%** increase. No stock index, no blue-chip company, no commodity and no tech superstar has matched such growth over the same period.  
What once appeared to be a speculative toy has transformed into a *global monetary phenomenon*.

## **Bitcoin has risen by roughly 23,000% in just ten years**

The journey was anything but smooth. Bitcoin survived three major bear markets, lost over 80% of its value several times, and was declared dead in the media more than **700 times**. And yet, every cycle brought a new all-time high, more adoption, stronger infrastructure and deeper integration into global finance.

Today, the debate is no longer *whether* Bitcoin will stay — but who, if anyone, will manage to control it.

## **The contradiction no one wants to say out loud**

As regulators debate supervision, taxation, custody rules and reporting standards, one uncomfortable truth lingers in the background:

**If today’s regulatory framework had existed ten years ago — Bitcoin would never have been born.**

Bitcoin was not launched by a bank.  
Not by a startup.  
Not by a government or a VC-backed tech company.  

It emerged *outside* the system — anonymously, openly, without permission, without capital and without institutional approval.

Its creator, known as **Satoshi Nakamoto**, released the whitepaper not to obtain permission but to propose an alternative: a monetary system designed to function independently of politics, institutional control and central intermediaries.

The anonymity was not a side effect — it was a shield that made Bitcoin’s birth possible.

## **Why Satoshi matters now more than ever**

Today, as banks, governments and regulators attempt to “capture” Bitcoin, it is crucial to revisit the origins.  
Understanding Satoshi’s motives helps explain why Bitcoin remains resistant to censorship, centralization and political pressure — even as global institutions try to shape it.
