South Korea is preparing one of the strictest regulatory frameworks in the global crypto industry, aiming to hold exchanges to **the same liability standards as banks**.  
The move follows the significant security breach at **Upbit**, where millions were stolen:  
👉 https://metaadvisor.eu/news/upbit-hacked-36-million-sol-south-korea-tightens-crypto-rules/

## “No-fault liability” becomes the new standard
The Financial Services Commission (FSC) is reviewing legislation that would require exchanges to compensate users for losses caused by hacks, system failures or technical issues — **regardless of whether the exchange is at fault**.

Until now, such rules applied only to:
- banks  
- electronic payment providers  

If passed, crypto exchanges will be legally treated at the same level as traditional financial institutions.

## Background: The Upbit hack
On November 27, attackers withdrew over **104 billion KRW** (around 30 million USD) in Solana-based tokens from **Upbit** within minutes.  
Full details:  
👉 https://metaadvisor.eu/news/upbit-hacked-36-million-sol-south-korea-tightens-crypto-rules/

The incident exposed major weaknesses in operational security and sparked renewed regulatory pressure.

## 20 major system failures since 2023
According to data from the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS), South Korea’s five largest exchanges have reported:

- **20 significant outages**  
- over **900 affected users**  
- more than **5 billion KRW** in combined losses  

Upbit alone reported **six** failures affecting more than 600 customers.

## What will the new law include?
Lawmakers are considering:

- stricter IT security requirements  
- higher operational standards  
- mandatory user compensation — regardless of fault  
- fines of up to **3% of annual revenue** for hacking incidents  
- required emergency and compensation funds  

This would shift risk away from retail users and place it squarely on the exchanges.

## MetaAdvisor's take
- South Korea is setting one of the strongest regulatory precedents in the crypto world.  
- Exchanges will need to invest heavily in security or face substantial penalties.  
- Regulators aim to restore public trust shaken by the Upbit incident.  
- Nearby markets like Japan, Hong Kong and Singapore may adopt similar frameworks if this model proves effective.
