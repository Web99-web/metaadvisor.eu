Ether balances on centralized cryptocurrency exchanges have dropped to a historic low, raising concerns — and expectations — of a potential supply squeeze that could strengthen upward price momentum.  
According to Glassnode, only **8.8%** of all ETH remains on exchanges, after briefly falling to **8.7%**, the lowest level since Ethereum launched in 2015.

## Why this matters
When ETH leaves exchanges, it typically moves into places where it is **not sold**, such as:

- staking  
- restaking platforms  
- Layer-2 networks  
- DAT (digital asset treasury) accumulation  
- long-term storage in custody wallets  

Since July — when DAT purchasing accelerated — ETH balances on exchanges have fallen by over **43%**.

Milk Road describes the market conditions bluntly:  
**“ETH is quietly entering its tightest supply environment ever.”**  
For comparison, Bitcoin’s exchange balances remain significantly higher at around **14.7%**.

## Technical picture: bullish divergence
Analyst Sykodelic highlighted a recent **OBV breakout** (On-Balance Volume), a strong indicator of rising momentum.  
Although ETH’s price saw a short-term rejection, such divergences often point to **hidden buying strength**, which historically precedes upward moves.

ETH has traded above **$3,000** for five consecutive days, but has not yet broken through key resistance.

## MetaAdvisor’s take
- Ethereum is entering the tightest supply conditions in its history — fewer coins are available for sale.  
- Long-term locking in staking and DAT structures continues to reduce market liquidity.  
- Structural demand now removes ETH faster than new supply enters circulation.  
- Both fundamental and technical factors support the potential for a renewed bullish trend.
