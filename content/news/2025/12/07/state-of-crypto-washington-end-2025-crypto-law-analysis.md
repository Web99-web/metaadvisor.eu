As 2025 draws to a close, Washington presents a strange contrast: regulators are moving, but Congress and the Senate are stalled. The crypto industry — now a major player in global finance — enters the final weeks of the year without a foundational market structure bill, and without clarity on when one will arrive.

In November there was still hope for a December markup hearing. Now, in early December, it is clear: **2025 will end without legislative progress.**  
This shifts the spotlight to 2026 — a year that may become decisive for the future of U.S. crypto regulation.

## What *is* moving? Regulators — but not Congress

The Trump administration advanced two major appointments in December:  
- **Mike Selig** (CFTC)  
- **Travis Hill** (FDIC)

Both have passed their respective Senate committee evaluations and are close to official confirmation.  
This means 2026 will begin with leadership that views crypto more pragmatically than previous administrations.

But without a market structure law, even supportive regulators have little authority. The U.S. still lacks clear rules for:

- classifying tokens (commodity or security)  
- defining SEC vs. CFTC jurisdiction  
- regulating stablecoins  
- compliance requirements for U.S. exchanges  
- oversight of DeFi platforms  

Regulators are working — but they are working **inside a legal vacuum**.

## The Senate: deadlines passed, no agreement

The Senate Banking and Agriculture Committees considered December 18 as a possible markup date.  
But negotiations remained slow throughout November, with no public signs of progress.

Now, in early December, the reality is unavoidable:  
**there will be no markup in 2025.**  
The House has also made no move to advance its version of the bill.

The American legislative process remains frozen.

## What does this mean for the industry — now, at the end of 2025?

### 1) Regulatory uncertainty is increasing  
The SEC and CFTC continue interpreting decades-old laws in different ways.  
Retroactive enforcement remains a persistent risk.

### 2) Institutional capital is waiting  
Large U.S. funds do not want deep exposure without a predictable regulatory framework.

### 3) Innovation continues to move offshore  
Europe (MiCA), Hong Kong, Singapore and the UAE are now more appealing jurisdictions.

### 4) U.S. crypto startups stagnate  
Long-term planning is nearly impossible without legal clarity.

## Yet there are small signs of progress

Despite congressional gridlock, some developments offer cautious optimism:

- There is growing bipartisan recognition that a market structure bill *must* exist.  
- The CFTC is signaling readiness for a larger regulatory role.  
- The FDIC and CFTC will likely be led by crypto-informed officials in 2026.

This is not a breakthrough — but it is movement.

## What to expect in 2026?

As the year ends, one thing is becoming increasingly clear:

- **2026 may be the decisive year for U.S. crypto regulation.**  
- The industry expects a proper market structure bill at last.  
- Congress will be under pressure to define jurisdiction and regulatory boundaries.  
- The U.S. must move quickly to avoid losing long-term competitiveness.

If 2026 also ends without regulatory clarity, the U.S. risks falling permanently behind Europe and Asia.

## Our take (Metaadvisor):

- 2025 ends without a market structure bill — a major setback for the industry.  
- Regulators are active, but cannot fix the fundamental issue: *there is no legal framework.*  
- 2026 will likely be the pivotal year for America's crypto future.  
- Europe and Asia continue advancing while the U.S. stagnates.  
- Without action, the U.S. risks a structural disadvantage in the global crypto economy.
