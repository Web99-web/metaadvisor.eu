For years, critics have compared Bitcoin to the famous 17th-century Dutch tulip mania. But according to ETF specialist Eric Balchunas, this comparison is not only outdated — it is fundamentally incorrect.  
The reason is simple: **Bitcoin has survived 17 years of shocks, deep market crashes and recoveries, while tulip mania collapsed once and disappeared forever.**

Yet the comparison keeps resurfacing. *The Big Short* investor **Michael Burry** once again called Bitcoin “the tulip bulb of our time,” and parts of the market speculated that he even scaled down or temporarily closed fund positions due to his negative outlook on BTC.  
Whether true or not, the comparison itself simply doesn’t hold up under scrutiny.

## What tulip mania actually was
In the 1600s, rare tulip bulbs became luxury status symbols in the Netherlands. Prices skyrocketed — some tulip varieties were worth more than homes in Amsterdam. But the boom was short-lived.  
In 1637, the entire market collapsed, with prices falling over 90% in a matter of weeks.  
Most importantly: **the market never recovered.**

## Bitcoin tells the opposite story
Unlike tulips, Bitcoin has:
- survived more than six major market crashes  
- reached new all-time highs after every collapse  
- built a global user and developer base  
- gained institutional recognition  
- received official spot ETFs  

Balchunas puts it simply:  
**“Tulips were knocked out with one punch. Bitcoin keeps coming back.”**

## Why Burry’s comparison remains popular
Michael Burry is historically skeptical of speculative assets. His warnings often focus on retail investors underestimating risk or chasing hype.  
But even if some of his concerns are valid, they overlook a critical point:

**Bitcoin is not a regional trend — it is a global monetary and technological network.**

## Why the tulip comparison no longer works at all
- Bitcoin is **global**, not local  
- institutional interest is at an all-time high  
- demand remains strong and continues to grow  
- Bitcoin is not a consumer good — it is **a protocol and decentralized infrastructure**  
- tulips burst once; Bitcoin has survived every cycle  

## MetaAdvisor’s take
- The tulip analogy is now more of a rhetorical punchline than meaningful analysis.  
- Seventeen years of survival in crypto markets demonstrates real network resilience.  
- Burry’s critique is noteworthy, but ignores major technological and institutional developments.  
- Global demand and adoption trends contradict the narrative of a short-lived bubble.
