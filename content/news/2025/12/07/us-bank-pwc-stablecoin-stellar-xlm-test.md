U.S. Bank — one of the five largest banking institutions in the United States — has launched a pilot project to test its own stablecoin on the **Stellar (XLM) blockchain**, in collaboration with **PwC** and the **Stellar Development Foundation**. This marks one of the first known cases of a major U.S. bank simulating the issuance and transfer of a digital dollar on a public blockchain network.

According to the available information, the testing program includes:

- issuing a dollar-backed stablecoin (1:1 collateralized),  
- sending and receiving digital value over the Stellar network,  
- simulating regulatory requirements,  
- comparing transaction costs and speed with SWIFT,  
- evaluating scalability for future institutional integrations.

Stellar was chosen for its low fees, high throughput and ease of integration. Unlike Ethereum, Stellar is built specifically for **banking-grade payment rails** and stable, high-volume settlement flows.

## Why this isn’t “bullish crypto news” — but a banking strategy

This move does not mean that banks are buying Bitcoin or turning “pro-crypto.” In reality, banks are entering the digital asset space in a **fully centralized** manner:

- the stablecoin belongs to the bank,  
- regulators have full oversight,  
- the value is pegged to fiat,  
- the user has no decentralization or self-custody,  
- blockchain is used as infrastructure, not as an open financial alternative.

It is traditional finance adopting blockchain — but strictly on its own terms.

## What does this mean for the market?

**Short term:** little direct impact on BTC or XLM.  
**Long term:** massive — it confirms that banks will adopt blockchain without embracing volatile cryptoassets.

Banks are effectively building a **digital dollar 2.0**, using infrastructure originally created by crypto developers.
