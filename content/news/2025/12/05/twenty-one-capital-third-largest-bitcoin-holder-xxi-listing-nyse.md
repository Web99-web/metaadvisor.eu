# Twenty One Capital becomes the third-largest corporate Bitcoin holder

Twenty One Capital, a Bitcoin-focused financial firm founded by Jack Mallers, has received shareholder approval to complete its merger with Cantor Equity Partners — clearing the way for the company to list on the **New York Stock Exchange** next Tuesday under the ticker **XXI**.

With **43,514 BTC** on its balance sheet (≈ **$4 billion**), the company instantly becomes the **third-largest corporate holder of Bitcoin**, ranking just behind:

1. **Strategy** (Michael Saylor)  
2. **MARA Holdings** (major Bitcoin miner)  
3. **Twenty One Capital (XXI)** ← now entering the top tier  

This positions the company among the most influential institutional players in the global Bitcoin ecosystem.

---

## A merger that accelerates Bitcoin treasury growth

The approval follows a shareholder vote by Cantor Equity Partners, the SPAC vehicle used to bring Twenty One Capital to the public market.  
Final results of the vote will be included in a forthcoming **Form 8-K** filing with the U.S. SEC.

The firm launched in April with a clear goal:  
**to build one of the largest corporate Bitcoin treasuries in the world.**

Its early backers include:

- **Cantor Fitzgerald**  
- **Tether**  
- **Bitfinex**  
- **SoftBank**  

Such a capital base signals strong institutional conviction in the strategy of holding BTC as a primary reserve asset.

---

## Why the listing matters

Going public on the NYSE gives Twenty One Capital:

### **✔ Access to deeper institutional liquidity**  
Public-market capital can dramatically accelerate BTC accumulation.

### ✔ **Increased transparency**  
Quarterly disclosures under SEC rules strengthen trust among investors.

### ✔ **Competitive positioning against giants**  
Joining Strategy and MARA in the “top 3” raises the visibility of a new heavyweight Bitcoin treasury contender.

## Market context: Bitcoin under pressure

Despite a turbulent Q4, which saw:

- a massive liquidation wave of **$19+ billion** on October 10  
- BTC falling from **$126,000** in early October to below **$80,000**

…Institutional players appear to be **doubling down**, not retreating.

This reinforces a long-term macro thesis:  
**Institutions are accumulating Bitcoin during periods of volatility — not exiting.**
