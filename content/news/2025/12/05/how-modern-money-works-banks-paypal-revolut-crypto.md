# How Modern Money Really Works  
## Banks vs. PayPal vs. Revolut vs. Crypto

## 📌 Introduction: Why almost nobody understands how money actually works

On the surface, the financial system looks simple:

**Money in the bank → card → payment → it works.**

But behind the scenes, banks, PayPal, Revolut and crypto operate **completely differently** — legally, technically and structurally.

The most important truth is:

> **Each system gives you different rights over your money.  
> In some, you own it. In others, you are only a creditor.**

---

# 🏛️ 1) How banks *actually* work

## Bank deposits are not legally your money

There is a well-known line:

> **“Money in the bank is not your money.”**

This is not a slogan — it is a **500-year-old banking principle** dating back to early Italian banks and goldsmiths.

### Why?

When you deposit money, banks record it as:

- **their liability**,  
- not your property.

Meaning:

- **The bank becomes the owner of the money**,  
- and you receive a **claim against the bank**.

Put simply:

> **You do not own the money — you own the right to request it.**

## What does the bank do with your money?

- lending  
- buying government bonds  
- creating new credit  
- interbank settlement  

## What does the customer get?

- deposit insurance  
- regulatory protection  
- stability

# 🏦 2) SWIFT, SEPA and the illusion of “instant transfers”

In the EU, **SEPA Instant** exists — but not globally.

In the U.S., there is **no universal instant bank-to-bank settlement**.

As a result:

- the notification is instant  
- but the **real settlement** often takes 1–5 days

Fintechs like PayPal and Revolut grew because banks were slow to modernize.

---

# 💳 3) How PayPal and Revolut work (EMI model)

PayPal and Revolut are **not banks**.  
They are *Electronic Money Institutions (EMI)*.

An EMI:

- cannot lend out customer deposits  
- must fully safeguard funds  
- keeps user funds in pooled accounts  
- does **not** offer deposit insurance

### What does this mean for the user?

The user has:

- **a claim to electronic money**,  
- but **no legal ownership** of the underlying funds.

EMIs can:

- freeze accounts  
- delay withdrawals  
- change terms

# 🏛️ 5) CEX crypto exchanges — users do *not* control their assets

On centralized exchanges:

- the exchange holds the **private keys**  
- the exchange controls **access**  
- withdrawals can be **paused or frozen**  
- in bankruptcy, users are treated as **creditors**, not owners  

> **Crypto on an exchange is not your crypto.**

Examples:  
FTX,  
[Celsius](https://metaadvisor.eu/news/celsius-paradox-company-mines-btc-but-creditors-get-usd-in-bankruptcy/),  
Mt. Gox.

# 🧱 6) Crypto self-custody & DEX — true ownership for the first time

DEXs are not companies — they are *protocols*.

Self-custody means:

- full control  
- no intermediaries  
- fast global transfers  
- on-chain transparency  

Stablecoins enable:

- instant settlement  
- dollar access without banks  
- low-cost transfers  

> **For the first time in history, private individuals can hold and transfer digital value without third-party control.**
