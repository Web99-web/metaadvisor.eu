# QuadrigaCX scandal escalates: Canada seizes over $1 million in gold and cash from the exchange’s co-founder

A major new development has emerged in one of the largest crypto scandals of the last decade. The Canadian province of **British Columbia** has permanently seized more than **$1 million in gold, cash, luxury watches and jewelry** from **Michael Patryn**, co-founder of the collapsed exchange **QuadrigaCX**.

The seizure was granted through a **default judgment**, after Patryn failed to appear in court to contest the action. This gives the province the authority to liquidate:

- **45 gold bars**,  
- over **$250,000 in cash**,  
- luxury watches and jewelry,  
- additional items seized during a 2024 investigation.

The case represents one of the most consequential uses of Canada’s *Unexplained Wealth Order (UWO)* framework — a civil tool allowing the government to seize assets when the owner cannot prove lawful origin.

---

## Why this seizure matters for the QuadrigaCX case

When QuadrigaCX collapsed in 2019 — following the sudden death of CEO Gerald Cotten — investigators uncovered a shortfall of more than **$215 million** in missing customer funds. It became one of the defining crypto frauds of its era.

Subsequent investigations revealed:

- QuadrigaCX operated much like a **Ponzi scheme**,  
- customer funds were mixed with private accounts,  
- internal accounting barely existed,  
- assets were secretly moved to personal wallets and external exchanges.

Today’s forfeiture marks the first time authorities have formally tied Patryn’s assets to the misconduct inside Quadriga.

---

## Who is Michael Patryn?

Patryn is one of crypto’s most controversial figures:

- previously known as **Omar Dhanani**,  
- convicted in the U.S. for financial crimes,  
- linked to shadow banking and illicit payment networks,  
- long denied having operational involvement in QuadrigaCX.

Court records now confirm he played a key operational role inside the exchange.

---

## Will QuadrigaCX victims get any of this money?

That remains uncertain.

The ruling covers only the **forfeiture**, not the distribution of the seized assets.  
A separate proceeding will determine:

- whether any funds can be returned to victims,  
- or whether the proceeds will go entirely to the provincial government.

With more than **75,000 affected users**, public pressure for restitution is growing.

---

## What this means for the global crypto industry

- Governments are increasingly using civil forfeiture in crypto cases — even without criminal convictions.  
- Proof-of-origin requirements for assets are becoming stricter worldwide.  
- Exchanges without transparent governance face rising global scrutiny.  
- The Canadian UWO model may soon influence regulators in the EU and the United States.

Canada is positioning itself as one of the most proactive jurisdictions in tackling crypto-related crime.
