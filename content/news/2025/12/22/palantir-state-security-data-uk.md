# Palantir, state security and data: Why the UK Parliament is demanding answers

US technology company **Palantir** has once again become the focus of political scrutiny in the United Kingdom after an investigation in Switzerland revealed why the country’s government chose **not to work with the firm** over security concerns.

Although Palantir has held contracts with the UK government for years — including projects linked to the **National Health Service (NHS)** and public administration — the Swiss findings have prompted MPs to reassess whether the risks of such partnerships have been adequately evaluated.

## Why is Palantir controversial?

Palantir develops software designed to **integrate and analyze vast amounts of data** from multiple systems, including healthcare databases, administrative records and security-related information. Its technology is also used in **military and intelligence contexts**, often enhanced by artificial intelligence.

According to the Swiss investigation, an internal assessment by the Swiss army concluded that Palantir’s status as a **US-based company** creates a risk that sensitive state data could, at least in theory, become accessible to **US government agencies or intelligence services**, regardless of local contractual safeguards.

## Reaction in the UK Parliament

Following the publication of these findings, MPs from across the political spectrum called for **greater transparency and due diligence** regarding the UK government’s contracts with Palantir. Particular concern has been raised over the handling of **health and personal data** belonging to citizens.

Lawmakers stress that the issue is not merely technical performance, but the **ethical, legal and geopolitical implications** of entrusting critical state data infrastructure to a private US technology firm.

## The bigger question: Who controls the data?

The Palantir case highlights a broader issue that is becoming increasingly important in the digital age:

- Who ultimately controls public and state data?  
- Where are those data located legally and technically?  
- And how much sovereignty are governments willing to cede to large technology companies?

In the era of artificial intelligence and automated data analysis, **data governance is increasingly a matter of national security**, not just IT infrastructure.

## Technology is no longer neutral

While Palantir maintains that it complies with all local laws and security standards, this case underscores a wider reality: technology is no longer neutral. The ways in which data are collected, connected and analyzed have direct **political and societal consequences**.

The debate unfolding in the UK Parliament is therefore not only about a single company, but about the **relationship between state power, technology and control** in a data-driven world.
