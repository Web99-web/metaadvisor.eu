# When crypto treasuries turn sellers

The strategy of aggressively accumulating cryptocurrencies on corporate balance sheets—so dominant throughout 2024 and early 2025—**is beginning to reverse**. Instead of expanding their crypto reserves, some publicly listed companies are now selling digital assets to **cut debt and shore up liquidity**.

## ETHZilla sells Ether to trim liabilities

A recent example is **ETHZilla (ETHZ)**, a Nasdaq-listed firm focused on building a large Ether (ETH) reserve. The company disclosed that it sold **24,291 ETH** at an average price of **$3,068**, totaling approximately **$74.5 million**. This marks the **second major sale** from its ETH treasury in a short period.

According to a regulatory filing, the proceeds will be used to **redeem senior secured convertible notes** issued under an agreement signed earlier this month. After the transaction, ETHZilla still holds roughly **69,800 ETH**, valued at more than **$200 million**.

## Market response reflects fading confidence

Despite the debt reduction, the market reaction was negative. ETHZ shares fell about **4%**, and the stock is now down roughly **96% from its August highs**. The move highlights the growing disconnect between the value of on-balance-sheet crypto assets and investor confidence in crypto-heavy corporate strategies.

## Is the crypto-treasury narrative breaking down?

ETHZilla’s case underscores a broader issue with **corporate crypto-treasury models**. Many firms raised capital to buy Bitcoin or Ether, expecting higher crypto prices to translate into higher equity valuations. In practice, **share prices have often fallen faster than the underlying crypto assets**.

As a result, several companies now trade **below the net asset value (NAV)** of their crypto holdings. This makes raising new capital for further accumulation difficult, shifting the focus from growth to **liability management and balance-sheet preservation**.

## What comes next?

Earlier in the fourth quarter, ETHZilla sold an additional **$40 million in ETH**, using the proceeds for share buybacks—moves that failed to halt the stock’s decline. The company has indicated it may pursue **further ETH sales or equity offerings** to meet its obligations.

The episode signals a **turning point in the cycle**: away from aggressive crypto accumulation and toward more conservative risk management. While Ethereum’s long-term fundamentals remain intact, markets are becoming far less forgiving of companies whose business models are heavily exposed to crypto price volatility.
