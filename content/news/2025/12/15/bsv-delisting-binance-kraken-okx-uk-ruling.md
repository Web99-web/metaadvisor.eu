# BSV vs. Crypto Exchanges: Why Binance, Kraken and OKX Delisted Bitcoin Satoshi Vision — and What the Court Ruled

With the UK Supreme Court's decision on December 8, one of the largest and most controversial crypto lawsuits in Europe has effectively come to an end.  
The judges refused to hear a further appeal in the **$13 billion case** brought by BSV investors against major exchanges over the 2019 delisting of Bitcoin Satoshi Vision.

The Supreme Court stated clearly:  
➡️ Exchanges are **not** liable for hypothetical future gains.  
➡️ The performance of a token is **a market outcome**, not a legal issue.

But why was BSV delisted in the first place — and why did this turn into a multi-year legal battle?

---

## 🔍 Why was BSV delisted in 2019?

BSV (Bitcoin Satoshi Vision) launched in 2018 as a hard fork of Bitcoin Cash, led by the controversial entrepreneur **Craig Wright**, who claims to be Satoshi Nakamoto.

In April 2019, several major exchanges — Binance, Kraken, OKX — delisted the token. The reasons:

### 1️⃣ Legal threats and aggressive behaviour by Craig Wright  
Wright issued legal notices against developers, critics and community members.  
Exchanges called his actions *harmful, hostile and contrary to open-source culture*.

### 2️⃣ Concerns about centralization  
BSV’s node distribution and governance model raised red flags.

### 3️⃣ Reputational risk tied to Wright’s “Satoshi” claims  
Many industry leaders viewed the claims as unverified or misleading.

### 4️⃣ Low trading activity relative to the operational risk  
BSV liquidity was declining, making it less attractive to maintain.

This led to a coordinated delisting across multiple global exchanges — one of the most dramatic in crypto history.

---

## ⚖️ What was the $13 billion lawsuit about?

BSV Claims Ltd. argued that:

- the delisting caused significant damage to the token’s price,  
- liquidity collapsed,  
- and that BSV would have grown more if it had remained listed.

They used a *“lost chance”* argument — seeking compensation for imagined future gains.

---

## 🏛️ What did the Supreme Court decide?

The UK Supreme Court dismissed the appeal outright.

### ✔ No legal question of general public importance  
The judges stated the case raised no significant point of law.

### ✔ Exchanges are not responsible for speculative token trajectories  
The law cannot enforce imagined market outcomes.

### ✔ Market, not courts, determines success  
The court emphasised:

> “Courts cannot revive projects that the market has already rejected.”

### ✔ Delisting is a commercial decision  
Not every price drop creates a legal claim.

The lawsuit is now effectively over — a major precedent for the entire European crypto industry.
