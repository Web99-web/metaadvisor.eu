Silver prices in recent days have moved in a way that crypto investors know all too well. Sharp rallies, equally fast pullbacks, and intense intraday swings have turned the traditionally “stable” precious metal into an asset whose behavior increasingly resembles Bitcoin.

During the weekend of December 28–29, silver reached a **new all-time high close to $84 per ounce**, fueled by a strong rally in precious metals and growing expectations of a shift in US monetary policy. At the same time, gold continued climbing toward fresh record levels, while the crypto market showed signs of hesitation and weakness.

## Extreme volatility within a single hour

What truly caught market attention was the **extraordinary short-term volatility**. According to market analyses, silver first surged by roughly **6%**, only to fall more than **10%** in less than an hour and a half, wiping out most of its gains in a very short time. Such price behavior is far more typical of cryptocurrencies than of precious metals.

Although silver has long been considered more volatile than gold, these price swings went well beyond usual expectations. By **December 31, 2025 at 06:00 CET**, silver had pulled back and stabilized in the **$72–73 per ounce** range, signaling strong **year-end profit-taking** after an exceptionally powerful rally throughout 2025.

## A strong year with a sobering finish

Despite the late pullback, silver closed 2025 as one of the **best-performing commodities**, posting gains of more than **140%** over the year. However, the final trading days highlighted just how quickly market sentiment can shift, especially as investors close positions ahead of year-end.

The macroeconomic backdrop remains crucial. Expectations that a new leadership at the US Federal Reserve could pursue **significant interest rate cuts from 2026 onward** have reduced the appeal of bonds and pushed investors toward hard assets such as gold and silver. Lower rates generally increase demand for assets perceived as hedges against inflation.

## Why silver is behaving “crypto-like”

Unlike gold, silver benefits from strong **industrial demand**, ranging from electronics and solar panels to broader energy transition technologies. This combination of investment and industrial use makes silver prices more sensitive to changes in economic expectations and market sentiment.

Silver is also part of the so-called **“debasement trade”** — a strategy investors use to protect capital against long-term erosion of confidence in the US dollar due to inflation and expansive monetary policy. At this point, the parallel with Bitcoin becomes especially clear.

Both silver and Bitcoin react strongly to inflation expectations, interest rates, and monetary policy signals. The key difference lies in perception: while Bitcoin is still widely viewed as highly speculative, recent market action shows that even precious metals are no longer immune to **crypto-style volatility**.
