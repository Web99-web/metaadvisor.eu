# Cancer, Chemicals and Lawns: Why Startup Naware Is Developing Weed-Killing Technology Without Pesticides

For decades, pesticides and herbicides have been treated as a normal part of modern agriculture and lawn care. But mounting scientific evidence suggests these chemicals may carry **serious long-term risks** for human health and the environment.

The US-based startup **Naware** is betting on a radically different approach: **destroying weeds with heat and steam — not chemicals**.

What makes this story stand out is its deeply **personal motivation** and its connection to **cancer research and chemical exposure**.

## Why agricultural chemicals have become a global health concern

Modern herbicides and pesticides:
- seep into **soil**
- contaminate **groundwater**
- enter the **food chain**
- accumulate in the human body over time

A growing body of research links prolonged exposure to these substances with:
- cancer
- hormonal disruption
- neurological disorders
- food contamination
- ecosystem collapse

This is the context in which Naware’s technology is gaining attention.

## The personal story behind Naware

Naware founder **Mark Boysen** grew up in North Dakota. Three members of his family died from cancer, and there is strong suspicion that long-term exposure to agricultural chemicals in water and soil may have played a role.

Instead of launching a software startup, Boysen asked a fundamental question:  
👉 *Can weeds be eliminated without poisoning the ground we grow food on?*

## From lasers to steam: why heat won

Early experiments involved:
- high-powered lasers
- cryogenic techniques
- mechanical destruction

But each approach came with major drawbacks — including fire risk, cost, and scalability issues.

The breakthrough came with **high-temperature steam**.

### How the technology works

Naware’s system:
- uses **computer vision** to identify weeds
- targets them precisely with **superheated steam**
- destroys plant cells through thermal shock
- leaves **no chemical residue** in soil or water

The system can be mounted on:
- lawn mowers
- small tractors
- agricultural equipment
- maintenance vehicles for parks and golf courses

## Where Naware is already being tested

Naware is currently focusing on:
- lawns
- golf courses
- public green spaces
- regulated agricultural environments

Early interest comes from:
- municipalities
- schools
- hospitals
- organic and sustainable food producers

In these environments, chemical-free weed control is not just preferable — it is often essential.

## A bigger picture: health, food and the future of soil

Naware is not just a weed-control startup.  
It represents a broader shift toward:
- reduced pesticide use
- safer food production
- healthier soil ecosystems
- long-term public health protection

If such technologies scale successfully, they could:
- reduce toxic exposure
- lower cancer risks
- fundamentally change how we manage land and food systems
