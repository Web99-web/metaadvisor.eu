# Polymarket and prediction markets: what they are, how they work, why they’re not gambling — and why the platform was fined

Prediction markets are exploding in popularity.  
On them, people trade not stocks, not crypto — but **probabilities of real-world events**:

- will inflation fall?  
- will the Fed cut rates?  
- will Bitcoin reach $100,000?  
- will a law be passed?  

At first glance it sounds like gambling.  
But it isn’t — and here’s the explanation.

---

# 🔵 What are prediction markets?

Prediction markets trade **event contracts** — binary “YES/NO” options.

Example:

**“Will US inflation in January exceed expectations?”**

If the market thinks the probability is 32%, the price is 0.32 USD.

If the event happens → the contract settles at $1.00  
If it does not → it settles at $0.00

👉 This is not gambling.  
👉 This is a financial market that trades *probability*.

---

# 🔵 Why prediction markets are NOT gambling

Gambling involves:

- many possible outcomes  
- outcomes with no economic consequence  
- artificial odds set by bookmakers  

Prediction markets involve:

✔ binary outcomes (YES/NO)  
✔ measurable economic impact  
✔ outcomes tied to politics, markets, macroeconomics  
✔ signals used by analysts, hedge funds and economists  

When inflation goes up or down, it affects:

- interest rates  
- stock markets  
- employment  
- crypto markets  

This is **market information**, not entertainment.

---

# 🔵 How pricing works

If a contract costs 0.32 USD:

- the market estimates the probability ≈ 32%  
- for $1 you buy ~3 contracts  
- if the event happens, each pays $1  

Example:

- invest $10 → get ~31 contracts  
- if correct → receive $31  
- profit = difference between purchase price and $1

Simple. Transparent. Mathematical.

---

# 🔵 What is a market-maker?

A **market-maker** is a trader or company that:

- is always ready to buy when others sell  
- is always ready to sell when others buy  
- ensures liquidity  
- narrows spreads  
- prevents the market from freezing  

Without market-makers, markets become:

- empty  
- unstable  
- unusable  

Market-makers exist on:

- Nasdaq  
- NYSE  
- Binance  
- Coinbase  
- Forex  
- derivatives markets  

Prediction markets function the same — only the traded asset is different (events instead of stocks).

---

# 🔵 What is an internal trading desk?

Some platforms maintain their own internal trading team that:

- provides liquidity  
- stabilizes prices  
- reduces volatility  
- keeps markets active 24/7  

They **are not allowed to**:

✘ use private user data  
✘ manipulate outcomes  
✘ trade against users for profit  

Their job is technical — not speculative.

---

# 🔵 Why was Polymarket fined?

In 2022, the CFTC fined Polymarket **$1.4 million** because:

- it offered unregistered binary options in the US  
- some markets did not meet US regulatory structure  
- it lacked the proper license to serve US residents  

Important:

✔ Polymarket was NOT fined for “cheating users”  
✔ NOT for manipulating outcomes  
✔ NOT for having an internal desk  

It was purely a **regulatory compliance fine**.

Polymarket then redesigned the platform and removed access for US traders.

---

# 🔵 What is Polymarket doing DIFFERENT today?

After the fine, Polymarket:

✔ blocks US users from unregulated markets  
✔ moved operations outside the US regulatory perimeter  
✔ no longer uses an aggressive internal trading desk  
✔ is recruiting **external market-makers** (sports bettors, HFT traders, quants)

Why external?

To:

- increase liquidity  
- tighten spreads  
- stabilize pricing  
- allow institutions to use the platform  

It is no longer a Wild West experiment — it is evolving into a semi-professional market.

---

# 🔵 Why does Polymarket want sports bettors & professional traders?

Because they:

- react fast to new information  
- understand binary probability  
- already run HFT strategies  
- model probabilities for a living  
- provide *continuous* liquidity  

And yes — they profit by:

- tight spreads  
- statistical edges  
- arbitrage  
- high-volume micro-profit trading  

It’s like grid trading — but for events.

Prediction markets often outperform:

✔ polls  
✔ analysts  
✔ media  
✔ experts  

Why?

People put **money**, not opinions, on the line.

# 🔵 Polymarket vs Kalshi — two opposite philosophies

Prediction markets look similar from outside — but Polymarket and Kalshi are completely different systems.

---

## 🟣 1. Regulatory status

**Kalshi (USA)**  
✔ fully regulated by CFTC  
✔ its contracts are classified as *commodity futures*  
✔ every market requires explicit approval  
✔ extremely strict compliance  

**Polymarket (global)**  
✔ unregulated in the US (hence the $1.4M fine)  
✔ operates from non-US jurisdictions  
✔ can list markets Kalshi never could  
✔ faster, more flexible, more Web3-native  

---

## 🟣 2. Who can use it?

**Kalshi**  
- US citizens and residents only  
- SSN required  
- closed ecosystem  

**Polymarket**  
- global users  
- crypto-native onboarding  
- excludes Americans  
- permissionless access  

---

## 🟣 3. Market types

**Kalshi** (strict rules)

- inflation  
- economic indicators  
- interest rates  
- elections  
- weather (agriculture)  
- macro data  

Everything must be **measurable** and **economically justified**.

**Polymarket** (Web3 freedom)

- elections  
- BTC price  
- geopolitical events  
- entertainment  
- sports  
- social trends  

Kalshi = “Wall Street probability market”  
Polymarket = “internet-native probability market”

---

## 🟣 4. Liquidity: who provides it?

**Kalshi**  
- internal Kalshi Trading desk  
- institutional market-makers (e.g., Susquehanna)  
- deep liquidity  
- ultra-tight spreads  

**Polymarket**  
- formerly internal desk (no longer used)  
- now external market-makers  
- liquidity varies with crypto capital flows  

---

## 🟣 5. Why Polymarket was fined but Kalshi wasn’t

Polymarket fine ($1.4M):

- no license in the US  
- offered binary options to US users  
- markets not compliant with CFTC rules  

Kalshi avoids fines because:

- it *has* a US license  
- every contract is reviewed  
- operates under commodity futures law  

Simple:

👉 Kalshi plays by Washington’s rules.  
👉 Polymarket plays by the internet’s rules.

---

## 🟣 6. Why both models are valuable

- Kalshi = institutional, regulated, conservative  
- Polymarket = open, fast, innovative  

Neither can replace the other — they fill different niches.
