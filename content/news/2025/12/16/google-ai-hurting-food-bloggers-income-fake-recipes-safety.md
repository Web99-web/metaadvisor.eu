# Google AI threatens food bloggers: distorted recipes, collapsing incomes, and new safety risks

Since the rollout of **Google’s AI Mode**, food bloggers worldwide have raised concerns that the feature is undermining both their livelihoods and the quality of online recipes.

The issue?  
Google’s AI frequently produces **incorrect, merged, or outright dangerous recipes** by combining content from multiple creators without attribution — sometimes with absurd or risky outcomes.

In one widely shared example, Google AI confused *The Onion* satire site with real cooking resources and advised users to cook with **“non-toxic glue.”**

## Income collapse: Food bloggers lose their main revenue stream

Most food bloggers rely on website traffic and display ads for income.  
But with AI summaries appearing directly in Google Search, many report:

- sudden drops in traffic  
- significant declines in ad revenue  
- AI-generated “Frankenstein recipes” replacing their original work  
- misattributed or unattributed use of their text, steps, images or concepts  

Because recipe instructions themselves are **not copyright-protected**, creators have almost no legal tools to defend their work.

Some describe this moment as **an extinction-level event for independent food bloggers**.

## Safety and health concerns: AI-generated recipes can be dangerous

Beyond economic damage, many experts warn of **food safety risks**.

Users have reported AI recipes that:

- instruct unsafe cooking temperatures  
- mix incompatible or raw ingredients  
- omit critical preparation steps  
- suggest hazardous substitutions  

Inaccurate recipes can lead to spoiled food, allergic reactions, or in extreme cases, **serious health consequences**.

## A wider industry problem: trust in online recipes is eroding

The explosion of low-quality AI-generated cooking sites, ebooks and Pinterest posts is also diluting trust in genuine food creators.

This trend threatens:

- SEO visibility  
- reader loyalty  
- the business model of free content supported by ads  
- the distinction between real expertise and AI content farms  

For many creators, the question is no longer *“How do I grow?”*  
but *“How do I survive?”*

## Our view (MetaAdvisor)

Google’s AI Mode raises a fundamental challenge for the creator economy:  
when AI absorbs and repackages human expertise without compensation, the original authors — and the users — suffer.

Food bloggers are among the first to feel the impact, but they will not be the last.

If platforms do not introduce attribution, revenue-sharing, or protective mechanisms, AI-driven search may reshape entire digital industries — not always for the better.
