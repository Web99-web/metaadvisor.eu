Google has confirmed that the **Dark Web Report** — a tool that alerted users when their personal information appeared on the dark web — will be discontinued in February 2026.

The feature existed for just over a year. It scanned dark-web data dumps for leaked details such as email addresses, names, phone numbers and personal identifiers.  
However, Google said the tool **“didn’t provide helpful next steps”** for users facing potential risks.

Many users agreed. On Reddit, people described the same frustration:

> “I got a warning, but didn’t know which account was affected or what exactly to do.”

### 🔍 What could Google’s Dark Web Report detect?
- Email addresses  
- Phone numbers  
- Names  
- Addresses  
- Some personal identifiers  

But the tool did *not* tell users in which breach their data appeared — making it difficult to take meaningful action.

### 😬 Why are users disappointed?
For many, the feature felt incomplete.  
A warning without context or follow-up left users guessing.

### 🔐 What should users rely on now?
Google recommends its other tools:
- **Security Checkup**
- **Password Manager**
- **Password Checkup**

These improve account hygiene, but none fully replace the dark-web monitoring function.

### 🕸 How can you check if your data has leaked now?
The most trusted free option remains:

👉 **HaveIBeenPwned.com**

It shows:
- whether your email is in a breach  
- which service leaked it  
- when it happened  

### ⚠️ Beware of fake “dark web scanners”
Many websites are:
✘ unreliable  
✘ collecting user data  
✘ pushing expensive subscriptions  

Stick to reputable tools only.
