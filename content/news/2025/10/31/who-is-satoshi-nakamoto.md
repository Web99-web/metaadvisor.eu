### Introduction
**Satoshi Nakamoto** — a name that appeared on a nine-page PDF in 2008 — sparked a revolution in finance, technology and how we think about money.  
Despite millions engaging with Bitcoin today, **no one knows** who Satoshi Nakamoto is.

A single person? A team? An institution? Or a collective pseudonym?  
The answer remains one of the great puzzles of the digital age.

### What we know for sure
- **Satoshi** published *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System* on 31 Oct 2008 — the *whitepaper* describing a decentralised payment network without intermediaries.  
- In Jan 2009 he mined the **Genesis block** and executed the first transactions — including 10 BTC sent to Hal Finney.  
- He communicated via forums and email until 2010.  
- After that — **he vanished.**

### How much Bitcoin does Satoshi own?
Blockchain analysis (notably the **Patoshi pattern** by Sergio Demian Lerner) suggests Satoshi controls around **1.1 million BTC**, mined in Bitcoin’s earliest months.  
Those BTC have **never moved**.  
Apart from a few early test transactions, **no “Satoshi block”** has been spent since 2010.

> If Satoshi moved just 1 BTC, it would be global breaking news — proof he is still present.

### Theories and possible identities
- **Hal Finney** — early developer, first BTC recipient; passed away in 2014 — denied being Satoshi.  
- **Nick Szabo** — “Bit Gold” author; linguistic similarities — denies.  
- **Adam Back** — cryptographer, Blockstream CEO; cited in the whitepaper — denies.  
- **Wei Dai** — author of “b-money,” referenced by Satoshi.  
- **Dorian Nakamoto** — media confusion; unrelated to Bitcoin.  
- **Craig Wright** — self-proclaimed “Satoshi”; widely rejected by the crypto community.  
- **CIA/NSA** theories — popular but unproven.

None of these theories is confirmed.  
**Private keys of the “Satoshi addresses”** remain the only definitive proof of identity.

### Why has Satoshi never spent his BTC?
Three main hypotheses:
1. **Deliberate choice** — to keep Bitcoin leaderless and decentralised.  
2. **Security reason** — keys deleted or physically lost.  
3. **Death / disappearance** — access permanently gone.

Whatever the reason, this decision strengthened the **myth** and Bitcoin’s ethos: **“Don’t trust, verify.”**

### What if Satoshi reappears?
- Short term: dramatic market reaction — any movement of his coins would have huge psychological impact.  
- Long term: could weaken the “no-leader” narrative.  
- Many therefore believe **Satoshi will never speak again.**

### Satoshi as an idea
Today, “Satoshi” is more than a person.  
It is **an idea** — a symbol of freedom, censorship-resistant tech and an experiment that changed the history of money.  
His anonymity allowed Bitcoin to outgrow its creator.

### Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **The idea outlives the author.**  
- Anonymity preserved Bitcoin’s neutral, global character.  
- **1.1 million BTC** remain an untouched symbol of discipline and trust.  
- In a world chasing fame, Satoshi chose silence — and became timeless.
