## What happened
A common frustration: the market dumps, your short is in profit — and the exchange **closes it earlier than you intended**. That’s what many traders experienced during the Oct. 10 flash crash, when **Auto-Deleveraging (ADL)** forcibly closed even winning shorts.

## What is ADL?
**Auto-Deleveraging (ADL)** is an exchange safety mechanism.  
When mass liquidations hit and liquidity is insufficient, the system can:
- take over **profitable offsetting positions**, and
- close them to ensure payouts to the losing side.

In short: even if you’re in profit, the exchange may close you to preserve market balance.

## Why does it happen?
- **Liquidation cascades**: too many leveraged longs unwind at once  
- **Shallow order books**: not enough bids to absorb the flow  
- **ADL rank**: exchanges track who’s most likely to be ADL’d (based on profit and leverage)

## Real-world examples
- Traders saw shorts on alts like **AAVE** and **PEPE** closed early while the drop continued.  
- Platform logs show **Auto-Deleveraging** instead of “Closed by user” or “Stop hit.”  
- Result: missed extra profit, but improved system stability.

## How to reduce ADL risk
- Trade **more liquid pairs** (BTC, ETH)  
- Use **lower leverage**  
- Monitor **funding & open interest** (extremes → higher ADL risk)  
- Consider **hedging** rather than all-in shorts

## Our take (Metaadvisor)
- ⚖️ ADL is unpleasant, but it helps prevent systemic failure.  
- 🚨 **Profitable positions aren’t “safe”** if ADL is triggered.  
- 📊 More transparency (rank, book data) would ease fairness concerns.  
- 🧭 In extreme episodes, **risk management** beats squeezing the last few percent.
