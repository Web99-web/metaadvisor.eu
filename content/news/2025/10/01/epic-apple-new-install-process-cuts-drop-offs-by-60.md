**TL;DR:** **Epic Games** says **Apple** streamlined the **install flow** for alternative marketplaces in the EU with **iOS 18.6**, producing **far fewer user drop-offs**. The **Epic Games Store** went from **~65% abandoning** mid-flow to **~25%**, closer to **Windows/macOS** behavior.

## Context
- Under the **DMA**, Apple must allow **alternative app stores** in the EU. The initial rollout included several **“scare screens”** and extra steps that discouraged users.  
- With **iOS 18.6**, Apple now shows **one** concise screen (e.g., notes on subscriptions and data handling by the third-party store), then proceeds with installation.

## What changed in practice
- **Fewer steps, less friction** → higher **conversion** through the install funnel.  
- **Clearer messaging**: one succinct notice instead of multiple warning dialogs.  
- **Closer to desktop norms** (Windows/macOS), where EGS typically sees **lower drop-off**.

## Still contentious (per Epic)
- The **core technology fee** and other charges.  
- **Notarization/approval** policies that, in Epic’s view, still **hamper distribution** outside the App Store.  
- The broader question of whether Apple’s “new rules” truly align with the **DMA’s spirit** or are just a **minimal adjustment**.

### Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **Policy → UX → Conversion:** DMA pressure visibly improved the **first-run experience** — conversion is the metric that matters.  
- **Material lift:** Cutting drop-offs from **65% → 25%** is **significant**, making third-party stores on iPhone a more viable path.  
- **What to watch:** the **fee model**, **notarization** requirements, and any **further flow tweaks** in upcoming iOS releases — these will determine whether an **off-App-Store ecosystem** can be sustainable.
