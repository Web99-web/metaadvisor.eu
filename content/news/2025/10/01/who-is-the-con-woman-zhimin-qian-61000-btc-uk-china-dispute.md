## Who she is and how the fraud worked
**Zhimin Qian**, known as **Yadi Zhang**, ran a **large-scale investment fraud** in China (2014–2017). Lured by outsized “guaranteed” returns, victims’ funds were **converted into Bitcoin** and moved offshore via layers of intermediaries.  
In 2017 Qian **fled to the UK** under a **false identity**; in 2018 London’s Metropolitan Police **seized access to ~61,000 BTC** — one of the UK’s largest crypto seizures.  
In September 2025 Qian **pleaded guilty** at Southwark Crown Court to **possessing/transferring criminal property (Bitcoin)**. Sentencing and asset decisions proceed in separate confiscation hearings.

## The network and related cases
- **Jian Wen**, a London-based associate, was **convicted in 2024** of laundering part of the funds; **confiscations** followed.  
- **Seng Hok Ling**, another associate, **pleaded guilty** in a separate track.  
- Investigators traced attempts to **launder BTC** via **real estate and luxury goods** across multiple entities.

## Why 61,000 BTC is now a UK–China dispute
With the plea entered, the fight shifts to **who gets the coins**:
- **China** demands **restitution** to **victims** (place of crime; scale of harm).  
- The **UK** is pursuing **confiscation**, as the assets were **found and seized** on UK soil and fall under **“proceeds of crime”** rules.  
- In parallel: **mutual legal assistance**, **civil claims**, potential **sharing agreements** or **escrow** until litigation ends.

Expect a **multi-year** path; rulings must balance criminal law, victim relief and international cooperation.

## Timeline (brief)
- **2014–2017 (China):** investor fraud; funds shifted into BTC.  
- **2017:** flight to the UK under an alias.  
- **2018:** seizure of **61k BTC** in London.  
- **2024:** conviction of associate **Jian Wen** and related confiscations.  
- **2025:** **Qian’s guilty plea**; custody dispute over **61k BTC** intensifies.

### Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **Precedent for mega-seizures:** How do courts treat **out-of-jurisdiction victims** at this scale? The outcome (confiscation vs restitution) may set **future playbooks**.  
- **“Market value” vs “cost basis”:** Whether victims (if prioritized) are paid at **today’s BTC price** or **at time-of-crime** remains open — with huge financial implications.  
- **Market impact:** **61k BTC** is structurally large; any monetization would likely be **staggered** or kept in **long custody** to avoid market shock.  
- **Extradition UK ↔ China (reader question):** There is **no bilateral UK–China extradition treaty**. That **complicates** extraditions **from the UK to China**, but it **doesn’t grant immunity** to **UK criminals in China**. In practice, cases proceed **ad hoc** via **mutual legal assistance**, **Interpol notices**, **immigration violations/deportations**, or **domestic prosecution** where possible. In short: **no automatic extradition either way**; outcomes hinge on policy and case specifics.
