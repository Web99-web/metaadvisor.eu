We came across the claim that Wi-Fi can “see” us. With so much fake news around, we decided to dig deeper — and found that research on this has been ongoing for years, with multiple teams across different countries publishing results.  

---

## What does “Wi-Fi can see” mean?
No cameras, no magic. **Radio waves** from a router bounce off people and objects. Tiny changes in reflection patterns appear in parameters like **Channel State Information (CSI)**. With machine learning models, systems can **detect presence, movement, and even posture**.

---

## What studies show
- **WiSee (University of Washington, 2013):** gesture recognition through walls with Wi-Fi.  
- **RF-Pose (MIT CSAIL, 2018):** human poses reconstructed through walls using radio waves.  
- **Study with 197 participants (Germany, 2025):** researchers identified individuals with near 100% accuracy — under controlled lab conditions.  
- **IEEE 802.11bf (2025):** the new Wi-Fi standard formally introduces “Wi-Fi sensing”.

---

## What it can – and can’t – do
**Can:**  
- detect presence  
- recognize motion  
- detect gestures or falls  
- track breathing in controlled environments  

**Can’t (in real-world use):**  
- reliably identify *who* without extra data  
- remain accurate in cluttered spaces or with multiple people  
- mass-spy without access to router hardware/data

---

## Why standardize?
Industry sees value in:  
- **Smart homes:** lights/heating based on presence.  
- **Elder care:** fall detection without wearables.  
- **Security:** alerts on unexpected movement.  
- **Healthcare:** passive breathing monitoring.

---

## Privacy & protection
- Keep firmware updated, use WPA3.  
- Disable remote management.  
- Segment IoT devices onto separate networks.  
- Check whether router sensing features exist and can be disabled.  

---

## Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **The tech is real and maturing.** No longer just lab demos.  
- **No panic, but awareness is key.** Wi-Fi sensing raises new privacy questions.  
- **Manufacturers must be transparent.** Users need on/off options and clarity.  
- **Worth watching.** As 802.11bf rolls out, privacy issues will become central.
