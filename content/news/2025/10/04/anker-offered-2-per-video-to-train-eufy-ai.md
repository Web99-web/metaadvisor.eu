Earlier this year **Anker**, maker of **Eufy** security cameras, offered users **$2 per video** showing package thefts or car break-ins to help train its AI systems to detect such incidents more accurately.

The campaign asked for both **real and staged** events: “You can even create events by pretending to be a thief and donate those events,” the site read. Users were encouraged to record the same act with multiple outdoor cameras to maximize submissions and rewards.

Anker stated that data collected from staged events would be used **solely for training AI algorithms**, but initiatives like this raise privacy and security concerns — especially when they involve mass uploads of footage from private spaces.

A cautionary comparison: the app **Neon**, which paid users for call recordings and transcripts, suffered a **security flaw** that allowed access to other users’ data and was taken offline after the issue was disclosed.

**Timeline:** Eufy’s program ran from **Dec 18, 2024 to Feb 25, 2025**. More than **120 users** commented on the announcement page saying they participated. The campaign reportedly aimed to collect roughly **20,000 videos** for model training.

---

### Why it matters
- **Data as currency:** device makers will pay for valuable user footage to improve AI.  
- **Staged vs. real data:** staged events increase volume but risk introducing biases.  
- **Privacy & security:** mass uploads from private spaces raise leakage and misuse risks.  
- **Regulatory exposure:** consent handling, storage and anonymization are critical.

---

### Our take (Metaadvisor):
- **$2 per video** is low for potentially sensitive footage — users deserve clear terms and revocation options.  
- Encouraging **staged events** can reduce dataset quality and model generalizability.  
- Strong security and retention policies are necessary before large-scale collection.  
- Expect more such programs as edge devices become primary data sources in the AI race.
