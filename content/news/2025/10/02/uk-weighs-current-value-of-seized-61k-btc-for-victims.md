The UK is considering how to **compensate victims of a China-based fraud scheme** in which police seized roughly **61,000 BTC**. The core question: **pay victims the current market value** of the coins (which have appreciated) or **compensate based on fiat loss** calculated at the time of the offense.

The legal framework points to **Proceeds of Crime** (POCA) and **victim restoration** mechanisms. Crypto adds complexity: price volatility, **who bears the risk of gains/losses** while assets are state-held, how to treat **“returns”/appreciation**, and possible **fiscal implications** if appreciation remains with the public purse.

### What’s on the table
- **Option A – current BTC value to victims:** maximizes restitution but sets a precedent that the state must pass on **all appreciation** of seized crypto.  
- **Option B – fiat loss at t=crime:** simpler for accounting/legal treatment; **appreciation remains with the state** or is handled by specific rules.  
- **Hybrid/limits:** caps on compensation, a **snapshot** at the seizure date, or **proportional allocation** where victim mapping is incomplete.

### Why it matters
- **Precedent for crypto restitution:** This case may set **guidance** on handling gains/losses in crypto criminal cases.  
- **Operational execution:** Safekeeping, liquidation, and distribution of **61k BTC** require disciplined **market-risk management** and **transaction-cost control**.  
- **Perception of fairness:** Victims expect **maximum restitution**, while the state must balance **budgetary** and **legal** duties.

### Celsius parallel: which date counts?
The **Celsius** bankruptcy showed how pivotal the **valuation date** is: courts tied asset values to the **petition date** and measured in **USD**, meaning many users received **fewer coins** than they would have at later, higher prices.  
In the UK case with **61,000 BTC**, choosing **current value** vs. a **snapshot (e.g., on seizure date)** carries the same tension:  
- **Current value** maximizes victim restitution but shifts **market risk** to the state.  
- **Snapshot/fiat loss** is cleaner legally but deprives victims of **appreciation** accrued under state custody.

### Metaadvisor’s Take

- **Volatility dilemma:** Paying at current value feels fair yet transfers **market risk** entirely to the state.  
- **Precedent & signaling:** The UK decision will shape practice across other jurisdictions.  
- **Likely outcome:** Expect a **seizure-date snapshot** with limited treatment of appreciation and a clear **calculation methodology** for future cases.  
- **Watch next:** final valuation method, BTC **liquidation/custody** plan, and potential **legal challenges** from claimants.  
- **Celsius lesson:** Set a clear **snapshot date** and method (USD vs. coins) *before* liquidation — otherwise, prolonged disputes and reputational damage follow.
