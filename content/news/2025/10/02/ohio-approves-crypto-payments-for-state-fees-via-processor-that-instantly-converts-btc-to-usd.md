Ohio has taken a step toward **accepting crypto for state fees**: the State Board of Deposit approved a **payment processor (vendor)** that will accept crypto and **convert it to USD in real time**. This lets citizens and businesses choose **Bitcoin** (and potentially other supported assets) while the **state treasury still receives dollars**.

> **What is the “vendor/processor”?**  
> A company that sits between the payer and the state — it **accepts BTC, converts to USD immediately**, and **remits dollars** to the state. Think of it like paying by card: the processor handles the rails and the merchant ultimately receives local currency.

### Why this matters
- **Institutional design**: The state **doesn’t hold crypto** and avoids volatility on its balance sheet.  
- **Payer choice**: Firms and citizens get an **additional payment rail** without changing USD reporting.  
- **Phased rollout**: After approval, implementation will be **gradual** by fee/service category.

### Historical context
Ohio piloted a similar approach in **2018**, later **suspended in 2019**. The reboot comes with a **cleaner legal setup** (vendor + USD conversion).

### What could be paid
The first phase targets **state fees and services** (e.g., administrative charges). **Taxes** could follow in later steps, depending on each office’s legal and technical readiness.

### Metaadvisor’s Take

- **Pragmatic compromise**: **crypto in, USD out** — minimizing balance-sheet and volatility risk.  
- **Signaling effect**: With a **vendor model**, regulatory/accounting friction drops; pilots become practical.  
- **Key success factors**: UX (clear instructions, fees, deadlines) + **transparent FX/fee schedule** from the processor.  
- **Watch next**: which **payment types** open first, **volumes**, and whether **municipalities** follow suit.  
- **Risks**: reputational (if UX falters) and **edge cases** around refunds/reallocations of crypto-origin payments.
