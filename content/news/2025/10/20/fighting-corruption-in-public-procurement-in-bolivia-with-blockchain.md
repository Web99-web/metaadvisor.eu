Newly elected president **Rodrigo Paz** has placed **blockchain** at the center of a procurement overhaul. The plan is to record tender documents, awards, contracts and payments **on-chain**, and to trigger **smart-contract** payouts only after verified delivery, making every step **auditable and tamper-resistant**.

### Why this matters now
With inflation pressures, fuel shortages and declining gas revenues, the administration seeks **trust-building** reforms that also attract **investment**—notably into lithium. The package includes **disclosure of digital assets** by officials and an exploration of a **stabilization fund** model based on digital assets.

### How it would work
- **On-chain tenders:** every stage—docs, bids, decisions, contracts, invoices, payments—timestamped on a blockchain.  
- **Smart-contract settlement:** automatic payment once delivery/acceptance is confirmed.  
- **Asset disclosure:** transparent reporting of crypto holdings by public officials.

### Benefits vs. hurdles
**Benefits:** immutable audit trails, transparent money flows, reduced discretion.  
**Hurdles:** legal updates for evidentiary status and archiving, selecting a suitable (public vs. permissioned) chain, ERP integration, capacity building across agencies, and cybersecurity around keys and oracles.

### Glossary
- **Blockchain:** distributed ledger with cryptographically linked blocks; difficult to alter, easy to audit.  
- **Smart contract:** code that automatically executes agreed rules (e.g., release payment after acceptance).  
- **AML/KYC:** *Anti-Money Laundering / Know Your Customer*—compliance frameworks for identification and transaction monitoring.

### Big picture
Latin America has piloted blockchain registries and procurement projects before. If Bolivia standardizes processes and opens machine-readable data, it could become a **regional transparency leader**—but technology won’t replace **political will** or strong institutions.
