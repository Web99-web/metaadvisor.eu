In 2025, charts are simpler than ever — yet the forest of indicators and “smart” tools can be confusing. Here’s a practical guide to help you read charts like a pro: from candles and trends to volume, RSI and liquidity. No jargon, with examples and the most common beginner traps.

---

## 1) Chart basics: candles, trend, levels

**Candlesticks:** Each candle shows **Open–High–Low–Close (O/H/L/C)** for the chosen period (e.g., 1h, 4h, 1D).  
- **Open:** price at the start of the period.  
- **High:** highest price during the period.  
- **Low:** lowest price during the period.  
- **Close:** price at the end of the period.  

Long **green** bodies = strong buying; long **red** = strong selling; **wicks/shadows** show rejections.

**Trend:**
- **Uptrend:** sequence of **higher highs (HH)** and **higher lows (HL)**.  
- **Downtrend:** **lower highs (LH)** and **lower lows (LL)**.  
- **Sideways (range):** price zigzags between **support** and **resistance**.

**Levels (support/resistance):** horizontal **zones** where price often reacts. Draw them using **body clusters** and **volume areas**, not a pixel-perfect single price.

**Rule:** trade **with the trend** until a **clear structure shift** appears.

---

## 2) Volume: the fuel of every move

Volume measures **how much actually traded**. **Breakouts without volume** are suspect; **breakouts with rising volume** are more credible.

**Volume by price (Volume Profile):** shows **where** the market traded most — often strong **support/resistance** zones.

## 3) Momentum indicators (and how not to misuse them)

**RSI – Relative Strength Index:** measures speed/changes on **0–100**.  
- **>70** often **overbought**, **<30** **oversold**.  
- **Bullish/Bearish divergence:** price makes a new low/high, RSI **doesn’t** → momentum **weakens**.  
- **Note:** RSI **doesn’t predict**; use it **with trend & levels**.

**MACD – Moving Average Convergence Divergence:** line crossovers and moves above/below zero **hint at momentum shifts**.

**EMA/SMA – Exponential/Simple Moving Average:**  
- **EMA 20/50/200** are classics: price **above EMA 200** = long-term **bullish** context; **below** = **bearish**.

---

## 4) Liquidity, orders and “stop hunting”

**Liquidity** = how quickly you can **buy/sell** without moving price much. **Thin books** = bigger jumps, higher risk.

**Stop-loss clusters** often build **below obvious supports** and **above obvious resistances**. Markets love to “collect” that liquidity with a quick **flush/squeeze** before the real move.

---

## 5) Timeframes: multi-timeframe reading

- **Daily/3D/Weekly:** give **context** (primary trend, key zones).  
- **4h/1h:** for **setup** (entry/exit).  
- **15m/5m:** **fine-tuning** and position management.  

**Rule:** align **with the higher timeframe**, time entries **on the lower**.

---

## 6) Breakouts & pullbacks: don’t chase every candle

A **breakout** is more reliable with: **volume** + **candle close** beyond the level + a **retest/pullback** confirming the new role.  
A **fakeout** often happens on **thin volume** and snaps back into the range.

---

## 7) Derivatives, leverage & funding

**Funding rate:** when **longs** are crowded, **funding turns positive/expensive** — often a sign of overheating; inverse for **shorts**.  
**Open interest (OI):** OI↑ + price↑ = **fresh capital**; OI↓ with price↓ = **liquidations/exits**.  
**Leverage** amplifies wins and losses — beginners should stick to **no leverage** until the basics are mastered.

---

## 8) Common beginner mistakes (and fixes)

- **Trading against the higher trend** because “RSI is low/high.”  
- **Entering after a big candle** without volume & confirmation (**FOMO**).  
- **No exit plan:** no **stop-loss**/**take-profit** set in advance.  
- **Overloaded chart:** 10 indicators = **zero decisions**. Use **2–3 tools** you understand.

---

## 9) A simple 3-step framework for 2025

1) **Context:** trend + key levels (daily/weekly)  
2) **Confirmation:** volume + momentum (RSI/MACD) + candle structure  
3) **Management:** position size, stop-loss, take-profit, what-if scenarios

---

## Where is volume measured (in crypto)?

- **Candlestick volume:** sum of real executed trades on the **exchange/pair** during the period (e.g., 1h, 4h, 1D).  
  - Shown as **bars below the price chart**.  
  - Can be expressed in the **base asset** (e.g., BTC) or **quote currency** (e.g., USD).  
- **Aggregated volume** (indices/TradingView “COMBINE”): sum **across multiple exchanges** — helpful for “broad” liquidity, but always know **the source**.  
- **Volume Profile (by price, not time):** how much traded on **price levels** (horizontal histogram along the y-axis) → helps find **areas of interest** (support/resistance).  
- **On-chain ≠ exchange volume:** on-chain shows **token flows on the network**, **not** the same as exchange trading volume.

### How to read volume bars
- **Breakout without volume = suspect.**  
- **Breakout with rising volume = higher odds** it holds.  
- In **ranges**, look for **confluent volume pickup** at the edges (rejection) or on the exit (confirmation).  
- Always consider the **higher-timeframe context (D/3D/W)** → **key zones + volume** around them.
