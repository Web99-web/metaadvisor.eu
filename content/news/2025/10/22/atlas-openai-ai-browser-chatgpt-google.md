### Atlas: a new AI browser

OpenAI introduced **ChatGPT Atlas** in a livestream — its own **AI browser**. Competing efforts exist (The Browser Company’s **Dia**, **Opera Neon**, **Perplexity Comet**, **Strawberry**), but OpenAI’s edge is reach: potentially **800 million weekly ChatGPT users**. TechCrunch’s take: Atlas isn’t mainly about “better browsing,” but about **cementing ChatGPT as the primary interaction layer**.

### Availability and platforms

Atlas is currently **Mac-only**, with **Windows, iOS, and Android** versions in development — the same surfaces where ChatGPT already lives. Unlike some rivals, OpenAI **skipped invite-only access** and opened Atlas **to everyone**.

### How it works

Instead of a traditional Google search, you **type into the address bar** and get **an immediate AI answer** — no link lists, fewer tabs. The aim is to shift the habit from “Google → links → many tabs” to **“address bar → AI answer.”**

### Altman’s vision

Sam Altman framed AI as a **once-a-decade chance** to rethink the browser: what it is, how we use it, and how to be **more productive** on the web. “Tabs were great, but there hasn’t been much innovation since,” he said.

### Distribution strategy

Even as leaders like **Sundar Pichai** and **Satya Nadella** call AI a **platform shift**, for consumers **phones and desktop OS** remain the main gateways. OpenAI wants to **own more of ChatGPT’s distribution pipes**, and Atlas is a clear move in that direction.
