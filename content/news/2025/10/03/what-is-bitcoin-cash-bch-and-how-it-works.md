**Bitcoin Cash (BCH)** was born from a **hard fork of Bitcoin in August 2017** after years of debate over **scalability**. Instead of relying primarily on off-chain solutions, BCH chose **larger blocks** (practically up to *32 MB*) to **increase throughput** and **keep fees low** directly on-chain.

### BCH vs. BTC at a glance
- **Block size:** BCH uses **larger blocks** → higher on-chain TPS; BTC favors smaller blocks + L2.  
- **Fees & latency:** BCH optimizes for **cheap, fast** on-chain payments.  
- **Security model:** both are **Proof-of-Work**; BTC still enjoys much **higher hashrate** and network effects.  
- **Positioning:** BCH is more **payments-oriented**, while BTC is broadly seen as **store-of-value**.
- The BCH community promotes it as “digital cash for everyday payments,” while Bitcoin is more often viewed as “digital gold” and a long-term store of value.

### Where it’s used
- **Point-of-sale payments** where supported.  
- **Remittances and micro-payments** due to low fees.  
- **Exchange liquidity**: BCH is widely listed and easy to swap.

### Risks & caveats
- **Smaller ecosystem** and developer base versus BTC/EVM networks.  
- **Competition from BTC L2s** and low-fee PoS chains.  
- **Volatility/regulation** may affect merchants and payment rails.

### Metaadvisor’s Take
- **Value prop:** BCH shines when you want **fast, low-fee on-chain settlement** without L2 complexity.  
- **Market reality:** **BTC** dominates the **store-of-value** narrative; BCH remains a **payments-focused niche**.  
- **Watch:** **POS adoption**, **average fees** at peak load, and **liquidity/volatility** across exchanges.
