The U.S. **Internal Revenue Service (IRS)** issued **interim guidance** that softens application of the **Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT)** to **unrealized gains** at **large C-corporations**. In practice, companies above the “large taxpayer” threshold (C-corp, revenue > **$1B** based on book income) **won’t owe** the minimum tax on **paper gains** from certain assets — **including crypto**.

That’s pivotal for crypto-heavy balance sheets. **Bitcoin is highly volatile**: sharp run-ups create large paper gains that can evaporate the next quarter. If such unrealized gains were taxed under CAMT, firms would need to **liquidate assets** just to pay taxes — **without** realizing any proceeds. The new guidance **materially reduces** that risk.

**Who benefits?**  
- **MicroStrategy (MSTR)** and **Marathon Digital Holdings (MARA)** are among the most visible **crypto treasury** players, holding substantial Bitcoin as part of their capital allocation. The rule enables **more stable treasury management** (liquidity remains in the asset instead of tax outflows).  
- **DAT (Digital Asset Treasury) companies** and other large corporates with crypto on the balance sheet gain **more predictable tax treatment** and **fewer distortionary period-end sales**.

**What it doesn’t solve (yet):**  
- **Selectivity**: It mainly covers **the largest taxpayers**; **SMBs** and early crypto startups outside the threshold remain under standard corporate/CAMT rules.  
- **Interim & scope**: This is **interim guidance** — expect further clarifications, scoping and possibly **permanent rules**.  
- **Accounting ≠ taxes**: Even with FASB’s **fair-value** for crypto (through P&L), the **tax** impact is now mitigated, but the **asymmetry** between financial reporting and tax rules still requires CFO coordination.

**Market implications**  
- **Less “forced selling”** at quarter/year-end lowers liquidity stress and may **reduce volatility** linked to tax-driven selling by large treasury holders.  
- **Regulatory signal**: Narrowly targeted yet a **positive signal** that U.S. institutions recognize crypto’s specifics.  
- **Treasury policies**: Expect more large corporates to **formalize crypto treasury policies** (allocation bands, hedging, rebalancing) without CAMT on unrealized gains.

### Our take (Metaadvisor):
- ✅ Positive signal: the U.S. is increasingly adapting the tax framework to crypto.
- ⚠️ **Inequality widens**: Selective relief cements the advantage of the largest; smaller players struggle without the same tax treatment.
- 🧱 **Entry/scale barrier**: Startups and SMBs find it harder to build crypto treasuries if paper gains remain taxable.
- ✅ **Liquidity and stability for large holders**: MSTR/MARA and peers gain real protection from forced selling.
- 🧾 **Accounting vs. tax**: The gap narrows for large firms but persists; for smaller firms it’s largely unchanged.
- 🌍 Global impact: Other jurisdictions may follow the U.S. lead and adjust their tax rules.
