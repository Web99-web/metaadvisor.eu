**Overview.** **Babylon** proposes a way to use **native Bitcoin (BTC)** as **collateral** in DeFi — **without** “wrapped” variants like **wBTC** and **without** relying on **custodians**. The idea: keep BTC locked on **Bitcoin L1** while DeFi apps on other chains accept a **cryptographic proof** of that lock as collateral.

### Who is Babylon?
**Babylon** is a protocol effort aiming to unlock idle **BTC capital** as a **security and collateral layer** for other chains and DeFi apps — prioritizing **security without centralized intermediaries**.

### BTC vs. wBTC — the difference
- **BTC (native):** real bitcoin on **Bitcoin L1**.  
- **wBTC (“wrapped BTC”):** a token **on another chain** (e.g., Ethereum) that **represents** BTC. It requires a **custodian** to hold your real BTC and mint wBTC.

### What are custodians?
**Custodians** are **intermediaries** (company/consortium/protocol) that **hold your BTC** and issue **replacement tokens** on another chain (e.g., wBTC).  
Pros: BTC liquidity beyond Bitcoin.  
Risks: **trust dependence** (operations, hacks, governance, freezes, KYC issues).

### What does Babylon change?
Instead of wrapping BTC via a custodian:  
1) **BTC remains locked on Bitcoin L1.**  
2) A **cryptographic proof** of that lock is made **available cross-chain.**  
3) DeFi protocols **accept the proof** as **collateral** — **without** wBTC and **without** custodians.

**Goal:** tap **BTC value** for DeFi **without** extra intermediary/bridge risk.

### Why it matters (if it works)
- **Less intermediary risk:** reduced reliance on custodians and bridges.  
- **More DeFi liquidity:** even a slice of BTC becoming productive can move the needle.  
- **Better for BTC holders:** capital **works** while staying on **Bitcoin L1**.

### Challenges
- **Crypto/protocol design:** proofs must be **indisputable** and **attack-resistant**.  
- **UX/cost:** lock/unlock must be **simple & affordable**.  
- **Adoption:** without major DeFi integrations it remains a **tech demo**.
