## What happened?
In the middle of the **federal shutdown**, the US Senate confirmed a **senior Treasury official**.  
This ensures that core Treasury functions — **FinCEN** (AML/KYC), **OFAC** sanctions, and international coordination — continue without a leadership gap.

---

## Why it matters for crypto
- **AML/CFT continuity:** FinCEN remains focused on **illicit finance** in crypto (mixers, unlicensed exchanges, risky stablecoin flows).  
- **OFAC sanctions:** Targeted measures remain on the table for sanction evasion.  
- **Travel Rule & marketing rules:** Ongoing pressure on **data transfer between VASPs** and clearer risk disclosures.  
- **No blanket ban:** The signal is **stricter compliance**, not prohibition.  

---

## Shutdown: what’s on, what’s not
- **SEC/CFTC limited operations:** new approvals may slow, **existing enforcement continues**.  
- **Macro impact:** longer shutdown = more **political uncertainty**, raising volatility in USD/bonds. Crypto may act as a **hedge**, but not always consistently.

---

## What to watch
1. **New FinCEN/OFAC guidance** for VASPs.  
2. **Pace of SEC/CFTC cases** during shutdown.  
3. **Banking partnerships** for on-/off-ramps.  
4. **International coordination** (EU, UK, VARA).  

---

## Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **Positive:** less risk of a regulatory vacuum at Treasury.  
- **Neutral:** short-term market impact is minimal — more a background factor.  
- **Risks:** stricter compliance raises costs for crypto firms, but could bring **long-term stability**.
