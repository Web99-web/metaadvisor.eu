**TL;DR (what happened):**  
Ledger launched a new **multisig wallet** interface. Instead of applause, it met **community pushback**: many argue it turns a core security feature into a **“cash cow,”** adding fees beyond standard network costs.

**Why it matters:**  
Multisig is a pillar of crypto security: a transaction executes only when **multiple distinct keys** (e.g., 2-of-3) sign. Because this is **baseline security**, **pricing and openness** around implementation are especially sensitive.

### Where the backlash comes from
- **Additional commercial fees** on top of network fees fuel claims that basic security is being **monetized**.  
- Some worry the solution works **best inside Ledger’s ecosystem**, implying **vendor lock-in** and less interoperability.  
- Users call for a **clear separation** between on-chain costs and provider fees, and for **transparent pricing**.

### What Ledger says
In short, fees cover **infrastructure, development and support**, plus integrations that **simplify multisig** for non-experts. Part of the community still wants an **advanced mode** with **no extra provider fees**.

### What it means for users
- If you use multisig, check **total cost**, not just network fees.  
- Consider your **trust model**: single-vendor vs. open, mix-and-match setups.  
- Multisig greatly **improves security**, but **costs and UX** must fit your use case (personal custody, team treasury, DAO reserves, etc.).

---

### For newcomers: What is **Ledger**?
**Ledger** makes **hardware wallets** — physical devices (USB-like) that keep **private keys offline**. The key never leaves the device; transactions are **signed on-device**, reducing the risk from compromised computers.  
**Multisig** adds another layer: instead of one key, **multiple signatures** are required (e.g., 2/3 or 3/5), lowering theft risk and removing a single point of failure.

**Our take (Metaadvisor):**
- Multisig is **foundational security** and should be usable without hidden hurdles; **full fee transparency** is crucial.  
- Charging for support/infra is fine, but ideally with a **clear rate card** and an **expert option** without extra provider fees.  
- Long term, winners will pair **strong security** with **openness and interoperability** — at **fair prices**.  
- Users: define your **custody model**, choose **signature thresholds** (2/3, 3/5 …), and weigh **fees versus protected value**.
