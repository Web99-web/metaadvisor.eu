On October 25, Solana (SOL) traded around $191.95 at 15:45 UTC, after momentum toward $195 faded. Traders are now watching whether the $192–195 range can form a stable base and if the upper-$180s support can hold.  

Earlier that day, crypto analyst Ali Martinez highlighted $188 as Solana’s most critical support, sharing a Glassnode “realized price distribution” chart – a histogram showing where large amounts of SOL were last transacted.  

Because a large supply cluster sits near $188, many holders are close to breakeven there. Such zones often act like floors: staying above reduces selling pressure, while a breakdown below can trigger further supply.  

On October 23, Fidelity added SOL for U.S. brokerage clients, expanding access alongside bitcoin (BTC), ether (ETH), and litecoin (LTC). These access changes don’t determine short-term price action but broaden the pool of potential buyers.  

On October 20, Gemini introduced a Solana edition of its Gemini Credit Card, originally launched in 2023.  

The Solana-branded card provides up to 4% cashback in SOL for gas, EV charging, and rideshare (up to a monthly cap), 3% on dining, 2% on groceries, and 1% on other purchases, with select merchant offers reaching up to 10%.  

The Gemini Card has no annual fee, no fee for receiving crypto rewards, and no foreign transaction fees. It also offers the option to auto-stake Solana rewards, though staking APRs vary and are not guaranteed.  

According to CoinDesk Research’s technical analysis model, SOL gained about $5.24 (around 2.7%) over the last 24 hours, with buyers defending $189.25 and sellers showing up near $195. The model highlights primary support at $189.25, secondary at $186, and resistance clustered around $195.49, with a nearer intraday level around $192.50.  

The strongest pressure on buyers was recorded around these key levels, signaling that the tug-of-war between bulls and bears in the coming days will decide whether Solana remains above critical support or drifts lower.
