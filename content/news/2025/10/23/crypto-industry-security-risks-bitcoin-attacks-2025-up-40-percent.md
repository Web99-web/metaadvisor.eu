### The promise vs. reality: “be your own bank”

The early crypto ethos — **no intermediaries** — promised freedom, but carried a **hidden assumption**: that **all security rests on the user**. That may have worked when crypto was experimental. Today, with **millions of users** and a **trillion-dollar market**, this approach **doesn’t scale**. For mainstream adoption, security must be **baked in** — not left solely to end-users.

### The ecosystem expanded — and so did attack surfaces

Crypto now encompasses **exchanges, protocols, dApps, stablecoins, custody providers**, and dozens of **token standards** interacting. With that complexity come new risks:

- **Phishing 2.0**: fake domains, **bogus QR codes**, “support” scams, airdrop lures  
- **Malware**: keyloggers, clipboard hijacks (swapping addresses), info-stealers  
- **Social engineering**: urgent calls, deepfake **voices/videos** of managers or friends  
- **Physical coercion**: forced unlocks, “$5 wrench” scenarios  
- **On-chain exploits**: rug-pulls, unlimited approvals, malicious smart contracts  

Industry data show **~40% more phishing in 2025**, with user losses of **~$410M** already in the first months. With **AI-powered deepfakes**, attackers adapt **faster** than defenses.

### What must change (without abandoning core values)

**1) Security-by-design in apps and protocols**  
- **Transaction simulation** pre-signature: show exactly **what** is approved, **who** the recipients are, and **which** risks exist.  
- **Default-limited approvals** instead of “unlimited forever.”  
- **Automatic revoke reminders** for stale approvals.  

**2) UX that reduces human error**  
- **Clear warnings** for phishing, new domains, suspicious contracts.  
- **Two-man rule** for large transfers: **second signature** or time-delay.  
- **Allowlists** of trusted addresses plus **time-locks** for withdrawals outside the list.  

**3) Stronger authentication & key protection**  
- **MFA/FIDO2 security keys** instead of SMS 2FA.  
- **MPC wallets** and **social recovery** mechanisms without compromising privacy.  
- **Hardware wallets** with long-press confirmation and clear on-device signing messages.  

**4) Custody standards & exchange defaults**  
- **Fund segregation**, cold storage for most reserves, **withdrawal policies** with delay and multisig.  
- **Daily limits**, geo/device fingerprinting, behavioral anomaly detection.  
- **Incident-response teams** with public reporting and playbooks.  

**5) Privacy vs. protection: finding the balance**  
- Avoid blanket **over-KYC**; use **granular permissions** and **local processing** where possible.  
- Preserve **anonymity/pseudonymity** while adopting **permission-based** AI/data access.

### What users can do now (quick checklist)

- **Never share seed/private keys.**  
- Use **FIDO2 security keys**; disable **SMS 2FA**.  
- Store larger funds on a **hardware wallet**; use a separate **spending wallet** for dApps.  
- Regularly **review and revoke** token approvals.  
- Apply **allowlists + time-locks** for exchange withdrawals.  
- Don’t scan random **QR codes**; always verify URLs.  
- **Pressure or threats = red flag** — pause and confirm with a third party.

### Glossary

- **AI** = Artificial Intelligence.  
- **MFA/2FA** = Multi-factor / Two-factor authentication.  
- **FIDO2** = Standard for hardware security keys (e.g., YubiKey).  
- **MPC wallet** = Keys are split; multiple parties co-sign transactions.  
- **Allowlist (whitelist)** = Pre-approved recipient list.  
- **Rug-pull** = Exit scam by a project/contract.  
- **Self-custody** = User controls private keys; **custodial** = keys held by a third party.
