Aave Labs, the team behind one of DeFi’s leading protocols, announced the acquisition of **Stable Finance** to strengthen its **consumer DeFi** push — everyday products like stablecoin savings, payments and predictable yields, with a UX that feels more like fintech than a crypto terminal.

The deal opens **broader markets**, as Stable Finance brings tech to ship stablecoin-based products faster and to bridge fiat and blockchain via regulated on/off-ramps.

### How borrowing & collateral work on Aave
On Aave, users can **borrow** by depositing other assets as **collateral**.  
- Example: deposit 1 ETH (worth $4,000). With an LTV of 70%, you may borrow up to $2,800 of another asset.  
- If ETH falls, your **Health Factor (HF)** drops. Once **HF < 1**, the position is **liquidated** — part of the collateral is sold to repay the loan, including a **liquidation bonus** cost.  
- The **survival kit** is buffer, conservative LTV and active HF monitoring.

### What happened on Oct 10?
During the sharp **Oct 10, 2025** sell-off:  
- The market saw **$19B+** in liquidations across crypto within ~24h.  
- On Aave, roughly **$180–200M** of collateral was forcibly unwound.  
- The protocol stayed **solvent**, but high-LTV users lost portions of their collateral.  
- Some users reported **over-liquidation** tied to an extreme oracle wick — a reminder that sudden price spikes can amplify risk.

### Why this matters for consumer DeFi
The Stable Finance acquisition brings DeFi closer to the mainstream. But Oct 10 shows that while DeFi is powerful, it also carries risk. Consumer-facing products must pair **simple UX** with **protective mechanisms** to shield users from sudden shocks.
