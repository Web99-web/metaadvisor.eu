Imagine someone’s **first day in crypto**. They hear the promises of owning money, accessing global markets, joining a new economy. They download a wallet, buy some ETH, find an app — and then hit a **wall of confusion**.

What next? Panic-Googling, YouTube tutorials, maybe success, maybe not. Most quit; studies say **80% of users** leave blockchains within **90 days**.

The decade’s greatest innovation — powerful new blockchains — has unintentionally created Web3’s biggest weakness: **a fragmented, clumsy UX** that drives away everyone but the most determined.

The clearest symbol? The humble **“Network Switch”**, now a shorthand for what holds us back.

Back at **ConsenSys** ten years ago, the mission was simple: onboard the world to Ethereum through **MetaMask**. At first there was only **one chain**. Users focused on apps, not infrastructure. Millions of users, billions in volume. Huge success.

But its evolution revealed the fundamental flaw: the **“Networks” dropdown** wasn’t a feature — it was an **admission of failure**. Expansion was prioritized over comprehension.

**The brutal truth:** If users have to think about chains, we’ve already lost.

Want to use **Ethereum assets in a Solana app today**? Brace yourself: find a **bridge** (hope it’s secure & cheap). Connect wallet. Approve tokens. Pay gas. Wait for confirmations. Switch networks. Connect again. Hope nothing breaks…

---

### Our take (Metaadvisor)
- **UX > infrastructure**: newcomers need **one smooth flow**, not network choices or RPC headaches.  
- **Bridges & network switches** must become the **invisible layer**.  
- **Standards & aggregation**: unified token standards, chain-agnostic routers, smarter identities.  
- **Measure success**: not just TVL, but **D1/D7/D30 retention** and successful completion of first user actions.
