The United Kingdom recently began enforcing the Online Safety Act’s age-check rules, and The Washington Post reports that it’s already having a significant effect on web traffic.  

Under the law, pornography websites must verify users’ ages through methods such as face scans or driver’s licenses; platforms must also prevent children from being exposed to adult content (which is why sites like Bluesky and Reddit have started checking some users’ ages).  

To study the law’s impact, the Post analyzed the top 90 porn sites based on UK visitor data from Similarweb, finding 14 that still do not perform age checks. All 14 of those sites have seen dramatic increases in traffic — one of them even doubled year-over-year.  

Meanwhile, many websites that ostensibly complied with the law also criticized it, linked to petitions for repeal, or even offered instructions on how to bypass it.  

John Scott-Railton, a researcher at the University of Toronto’s Citizen Lab, told the Post this is “a textbook illustration of the law of unintended consequences,” noting that the law “suppresses traffic to compliant platforms while driving users to sites without age verification.”  

**Our comment:** This law may end up backfiring — reducing safe options while rewarding non-compliant platforms.
