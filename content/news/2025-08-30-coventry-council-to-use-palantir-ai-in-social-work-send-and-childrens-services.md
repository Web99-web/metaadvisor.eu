Public sector workers have raised “deep concern” after Coventry city council signed a £500,000 annual contract with US data firm **Palantir**.  

The deal is the first of its kind between a UK local authority and Palantir, which supplies technology to the Israeli military and was involved in Trump’s deportation efforts.  

A pilot project in the council’s children’s services used Palantir AI for transcription and summarising case notes, and the system will now expand to children with special educational needs (SEND).  

Council chief executive Julie Nugent said the goal was to “improve internal data integration and service delivery” and to “explore the transformative opportunities of AI.”  

Palantir already holds multiple public sector contracts in the UK, from crime prevention in Leicestershire to the new **NHS federated data platform**. Labour leader Keir Starmer toured Palantir’s Washington office in February with CEO Alex Karp.  

Unions and independent councillor Grace Lewis criticised the deal, calling for its immediate cancellation and for the money to be redirected into the community.
