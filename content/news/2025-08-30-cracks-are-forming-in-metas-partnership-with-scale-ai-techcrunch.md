It’s only been since June that Meta invested $14.3 billion in the data-labeling vendor **Scale AI**, bringing on CEO Alexandr Wang and several of the startup’s top executives to run **Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL)**. But the relationship is already showing signs of strain.

One of those executives, **Ruben Mayer** — Scale AI’s former SVP of GenAI Product and Operations — left Meta after just two months, two people familiar with the matter told TechCrunch. Mayer had spent roughly five years with Scale AI across two stints. At Meta he oversaw AI data operations teams but reportedly wasn’t part of TBD Labs — the unit tasked with building AI superintelligence.  

Mayer disputes some of those details, saying his role was “to help set up the lab with whatever was needed,” and that he was “part of TBD Labs from day one.” He also clarified he did not report directly to Wang and was “very happy” with his Meta experience.

Beyond personnel changes, Meta’s relationship with Scale AI appears to be shifting. TBD Labs is working with other third-party data-labeling vendors like **Mercor** and **Surge** — two of Scale AI’s largest competitors — to train upcoming AI models.

While it’s common for AI labs to work with multiple vendors, it’s unusual for a core unit to distance itself so visibly from a strategic partner.
