**Bitcoin (BTC) treasury companies** are facing a harsh reality: can they outperform BTC itself, or should investors simply buy a **Bitcoin ETF** instead?  

At **BTC Asia in Hong Kong**, Matt Cole, CEO of **Strive Asset Management**, stated:  
“If you aren’t doing that, there’s no reason for the strategies — just buy a Bitcoin ETF.”  

Cole is known for advocating BTC on company balance sheets, echoing the playbook of **MicroStrategy** led by Michael Saylor.  

### Key challenges
- **Scale**: Reaching at least **$1 billion** in capital to unlock cheaper financing, IPO access, and larger teams.  
- **Financing**: Shifting from convertibles to **perpetual preferred equity** to secure stable leverage.  
- **Asset choice**: Cole argues that **Ethereum (ETH)** and other tokens behave like equities with variable monetary policies, making them unsuitable for treasuries.  

He emphasized that only Bitcoin’s **fixed supply** and resistance to fiat debasement can support a levered treasury strategy over time.  

### Market context
The **BTC treasury model** gained fame through MicroStrategy, but smaller companies lack the scale and credibility to replicate it. Meanwhile, **Bitcoin ETFs** offer investors simpler and cheaper exposure to BTC without additional corporate risk.  

---

## Our take
For most companies, the **Bitcoin treasury play** is more of a marketing tactic than a sustainable model. **ETFs** are becoming the dominant gateway for Bitcoin exposure.  
Still, if more large-cap firms follow **MicroStrategy’s** path, the treasury model could revive — but for now, it’s reserved for **billion-dollar giants** only.
