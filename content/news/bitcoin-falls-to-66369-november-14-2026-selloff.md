**Bitcoin extended its decline overnight into Saturday, November 14, 2026, touching $66,369.12 on Coinbase, according to published one-minute price data. The low was recorded at approximately 04:14 UTC (05:14 Central European Time), after the largest cryptocurrency lost the psychologically important $70,000 level. Prices subsequently recovered toward $67,000, but continued selling pressure highlights the market's sensitivity to expectations for US interest rates, outflows from exchange-traded funds and forced closures of leveraged positions. The quoted low is specific to Coinbase; prices can differ across exchanges.**

## Why Is Bitcoin Falling Again?

One major source of pressure on cryptocurrencies is US monetary policy. On November 13, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that producer prices had risen 0.3% month over month and 6.5% year over year in October. Although the monthly increase was slightly below the 0.4% forecast, annual producer-price inflation remains elevated. Markets have therefore increasingly priced in another interest-rate increase in December. According to a November 13 update from CME Group, futures markets implied a probability of approximately 91.7% for a 25-basis-point increase. That is a market expectation, not a decision already taken by the Federal Reserve.

Higher interest rates can strengthen the dollar and increase yields on interest-bearing US assets, reducing the appeal of some riskier investments. Bitcoin is not immune: when investors reduce risk exposure, cryptocurrencies can experience sharper moves than traditional financial markets.

Still, a single inflation reading does not explain the entire sell-off. The Producer Price Index, or PPI, measures changes in prices received by producers for goods and services and differs from the Consumer Price Index, or CPI. An increase can influence expectations for future monetary policy, but it does not mean the Fed has already decided to raise rates. For Bitcoin, the interaction between rate expectations, market liquidity and investors' willingness to take risk matters most.

## Liquidations Accelerate the Sell-Off

The decline was amplified by forced closures of positions held by traders betting on higher prices. According to CoinGlass figures cited by The Block on November 13, approximately $1.47 billion in crypto positions were liquidated over the preceding 24 hours. Around $1.31 billion of that total involved LONG positions. These are estimates for a particular reporting window and should not be added to later liquidation figures covering overlapping periods.

As prices fall, traders using leverage may no longer have enough collateral to keep positions open. Exchanges then close those positions automatically, and the resulting sales can put additional pressure on prices. This creates a chain reaction: falling prices trigger liquidations, while liquidations can drive prices lower still. The break below $70,000 was therefore important not only psychologically but also as a trigger for some positions in derivatives markets.

A trader holding a LONG position expects prices to rise, but leverage means that even a relatively modest market decline can cause a large loss relative to the initial margin. If the liquidation level is close to the current market price, an exchange may close the entire position before any potential rebound occurs. During sharp sell-offs, it is therefore important to look beyond Bitcoin's percentage decline and consider how many open positions depend on borrowed capital.

## Bitcoin ETFs Record a Seventh Consecutive Day of Outflows

According to Farside Investors, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded **$185.4 million in net outflows** on November 13. BlackRock's **IBIT** accounted for $143.5 million, while Fidelity's **FBTC** saw $41.9 million leave the fund. The previous day's aggregate net outflow was $284.1 million, meaning the daily negative flow eased by roughly 35%.

Meanwhile, spot Ethereum ETFs recorded a modest net inflow of $2.3 million on November 13, following a $70.5 million outflow in the preceding session. Slower outflows may signal easing selling pressure, but one improved trading day does not confirm a sustained return of institutional demand.

It is important to distinguish **net ETF flows** from total Bitcoin trading activity. A negative net flow means more money was withdrawn from the funds being tracked than invested in them that day. It does not mean every institutional investor was selling, nor do ETF flows alone determine Bitcoin's price. Nevertheless, seven consecutive sessions of outflows are an unfavorable sign when the market is already under pressure.

## Why Are Bitcoin and Wall Street Moving Differently?

It is notable that the main US equity indexes recovered on a weekly basis while Bitcoin continued to weaken. This suggests the crypto market is responding to more than general Wall Street sentiment. Fund withdrawals, reduced liquidity and forced position closures create additional pressures specific to cryptocurrencies. The divergence does not prove Bitcoin has permanently decoupled from technology stocks. Instead, it illustrates how different forces can dominate over shorter periods.

Equity indexes include companies across a range of sectors, and a weekly gain for an index does not mean every technology stock has risen. Crypto trading also has a different structure: it operates around the clock, features substantial derivatives activity and reacts to its own capital flows. A weekly rise in equity indexes alongside a decline in Bitcoin is therefore not a contradiction. It reflects different assessments of risk across asset classes.

## Which Bitcoin Price Levels Matter Now?

Following the overnight low of $66,369, attention has shifted to the area around **$66,000**. Another break below that level could increase the risk of further selling. A recovery above **$68,000** would offer an initial sign of stabilization, while reclaiming **$70,000** would be more meaningful when evaluating the strength of a rebound. These are not guaranteed reversal points and do not constitute trading signals by themselves.

Earlier Glassnode analysis identified an area between roughly $62,000 and $66,000 where significant amounts of Bitcoin had previously changed hands. Such a zone may attract buyers but does not guarantee a price floor. Traditional US stock markets are closed over the weekend, while cryptocurrencies continue trading. Liquidity can be less evenly distributed, potentially making prices more sensitive to large orders and forced liquidations.

There is a meaningful difference between Bitcoin briefly moving above $68,000 for a few minutes and holding that level alongside stronger buying activity. Equally, a short-lived move below $66,000 would not automatically mean a drop to $62,000. The duration of the move, trading volume, changes in open positions and news affecting investor expectations all matter.

## Is the Decline Ending, or Is a Recovery Beginning?

Current data do not yet confirm a lasting reversal. Smaller daily outflows from Bitcoin ETFs and the rebound from the overnight low may be early indications of weakening selling pressure. However, expectations of higher rates, a strong dollar and repeated liquidations continue to constrain risk appetite. A short-term rally after a sharp decline may be a technical bounce rather than the start of a new uptrend.

Upcoming US inflation releases, remarks from Federal Reserve officials and fresh ETF flow figures could affect sentiment after the weekend. But markets do not need to wait for an official announcement to move sharply. A rebound after widespread liquidations can also occur when traders close SHORT positions, not only when fresh long-term demand emerges.
