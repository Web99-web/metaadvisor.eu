The Trump administration appears determined to **limit Intel’s ability to make independent decisions** about its struggling foundry business.

According to the *Financial Times*, Intel CFO **David Zinsner** told a Deutsche Bank conference that the U.S. government secured a **10% equity stake in Intel** as part of the latest agreement.

The deal is structured to **penalize Intel** if it spins off or sells its foundry division—responsible for making custom chips for external clients—within the next few years.

It also includes a five-year warrant allowing the U.S. to acquire an **additional 5% of Intel at $20 per share** if the company’s ownership in its foundry falls below 51%. Zinsner said he expects this warrant to expire unused.

> “From the government’s perspective, they didn’t want us to spin off or sell the business,” Zinsner said.

Zinsner further revealed that Intel received **$5.7 billion in cash** on Wednesday from previously approved, but unpaid, CHIPS and Science Act grants.

White House Press Secretary **Karoline Leavitt** told reporters the agreement was still being finalized. Intel declined to comment further.

The deal underscores the Trump administration’s ambition to **expand U.S.-based chip manufacturing**, countering the industry’s heavy reliance on **TSMC’s offshore production**. At the same time, the warrant forces Intel to hold onto a unit that continues to lose money — **Intel Foundry** reported an operating loss.
