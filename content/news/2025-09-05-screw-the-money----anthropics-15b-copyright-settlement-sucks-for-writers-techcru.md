About **half a million writers** will be eligible for payouts of at least $3,000 thanks to a historic **$1.5 billion settlement** in a class action lawsuit against **Anthropic**.  

This settlement represents the largest payout in U.S. copyright history, but it’s not a win for authors — it’s another victory for tech companies.  

Tech giants are scrambling to amass as much written material as possible to train their **LLMs**, which power products like **ChatGPT and Claude** — the same tools that are undermining creative industries, even if their results are mediocre. These systems grow more powerful the more data they consume, but after scraping nearly the entire internet, companies are literally running out of new sources.  

That’s why **Anthropic**, the company behind Claude, pirated millions of books from so-called “shadow libraries” and used them to train its AI. This lawsuit, **Bartz v. Anthropic**, is just one of dozens filed against companies like **Meta, Google, OpenAI, and Midjourney**.  

But writers aren’t benefiting directly from this settlement — it’s simply an **expensive reprimand** for Anthropic, a company that recently raised another $13 billion, for illegally downloading books instead of purchasing them.  

In June, federal judge **William Alsup** sided with Anthropic, ruling that it is legal to train AI on copyrighted works. He argued that this use case is “transformative” enough to qualify under the **fair use doctrine**, a carve-out in U.S. copyright law that hasn’t been updated since 1976.

## Our take
This settlement raises a crucial question: **how can writers be protected in the age of AI?** While tech companies pay billions in settlements, their models continue to exploit copyrighted works without real compensation for authors. The **fair use framework**, unchanged for nearly 50 years, is no longer adequate for the digital era. Without legal reform, writers will remain the losers while tech giants keep extracting value from their work.
