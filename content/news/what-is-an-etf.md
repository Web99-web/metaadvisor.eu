## What is an ETF?

An **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)** is an investment fund **traded on an exchange like a stock**. Instead of buying each asset yourself, you buy **ETF shares** that track a **target asset/index** (e.g., S&P 500, gold, **bitcoin**).

### How it works (30-second version)
- **Purpose:** **Track the price** of the target asset as closely as possible.
- **Trading:** Intraday like a stock (market/limit/stop).
- **Basket:** The fund holds assets (stocks, bonds, gold… or **actual BTC** with a custodian for spot bitcoin ETFs).
- **AP mechanism:** Authorized Participants create/redeem share blocks for assets, keeping the ETF price **near NAV**.
- **Costs:** Annual **expense ratio (TER)**, exchange **spreads**, potential **tracking error**.

### Types of crypto ETFs
- **Spot bitcoin ETF:** The fund **buys and holds real BTC** with a custodian; aims to mirror spot price (minus fee).
- **Futures bitcoin ETF:** Holds **futures contracts**, not BTC; can have **roll costs** and larger deviations from spot.

### Why popular?
- **Easy access:** Via broker/retirement accounts.
- **Regulated wrapper:** No self-custody/seed-phrase headaches.
- **Liquidity & transparency:** Exchange-traded, daily NAV/holdings.

### Risks & trade-offs
- **Expense ratio** drags returns.
- **Custody concentration** (BTC with a few large custodians).
- **Exchange hours** (vs. 24/7 crypto markets).
- For **futures ETFs**: **tracking error** and **roll costs**.

## Why “ETFs kill transaction fees” on Bitcoin?

- **Spot BTC ETFs buy BTC and hold it for long periods.** After initial creation, trading occurs **in ETF shares on stock exchanges** — **off-chain**.
- Result: **Fewer on-chain transactions** → **lower network fees**.
- **BTC price** can still rise on **ETF inflows**, without pushing **on-chain fees** higher.
- **Miners** earn: **block subsidy** + **fees**. As the subsidy **halves** every cycle, fees matter more; if they stay low, **miner profitability** is pressured.
