Welcome to Asia Morning Briefing, a daily digest of key stories during U.S. hours and an overview of market moves. Today’s spotlight: China’s accelerating focus on stablecoins.

According to Dr. Vera Yuen of Hong Kong University’s Business School, Beijing’s interest is not about embracing crypto but defending the yuan from U.S. dollar dominance. With dollar-pegged stablecoins embedding themselves into global finance, China sees the need to respond. Yet, tight capital controls limit the effort mostly to Hong Kong’s offshore renminbi market, where liquidity is shallow.

This pivot comes after Washington moved first, establishing a regulatory framework for stablecoins. Reuters recently reported that China’s State Council is reviewing a yuan-backed stablecoin roadmap later this month, with Hong Kong and Shanghai expected to fast-track adoption.

Animoca Group president Evan Auyang told CoinDesk that the U.S. GENIUS Act was the trigger, embedding stablecoins as part of mainstream finance. “The law is pressuring China to act much faster,” he said, noting Beijing now frames stablecoins as infrastructure rather than speculation.

Yuen explained that the government initially prioritized the e-CNY, China’s central bank digital currency, because of the control, traceability, and seigniorage revenues it offered regulators. Stablecoins, however, hold a clear edge internationally: “Many CBDCs are designed for domestic use. For cross-border payments, interoperability is a major problem. Stablecoins, by design, work internationally,” she told CoinDesk.

In essence, e-CNY remains a domestic tool, while stablecoins may give Beijing what it needs most — a way to stay competitive in international payments and settlement flows.
