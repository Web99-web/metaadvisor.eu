A recent MIT NANDA report found that **95% of generative AI pilots in enterprises are failing**. Rather than abandoning the technology, leading organizations are now exploring **agentic AI systems** that can learn and remain supervised.

That’s where **Maisa AI** steps in. The year-old startup bases its entire philosophy on the idea that enterprise automation requires accountable AI agents—not opaque black boxes. With a fresh **$25 million seed round led by Creandum**, Maisa has launched **Maisa Studio**, a model-agnostic self-serve platform that enables users to deploy digital workers trainable via natural language.

Unlike “vibe coding” platforms like Cursor or Creandum-backed Lovable, Maisa insists its approach is fundamentally different:  
> “Instead of using AI to build the responses, we use AI to build the process that needs to be executed to get to the response — what we call ‘chain-of-work,’” CEO David Villalón told TechCrunch.

The system’s principal architect is co-founder and Chief Scientific Officer Manuel Romero, who previously worked with Villalón at Spanish AI startup Clibrain. In 2024, the duo joined forces to tackle AI hallucinations after concluding that models could not be blindly trusted.

Their method, called **HALP (Human-Augmented LLM Processing)**, works like a classroom exercise: digital workers sketch out each step, while humans provide oversight.  
Villalón explained: “Humans cannot realistically review three months of work done in five minutes. HALP makes the process transparent, allowing users to supervise effectively.”

Maisa believes this hybrid approach could dramatically reduce AI project failure rates and finally make enterprise AI automation truly operational.
