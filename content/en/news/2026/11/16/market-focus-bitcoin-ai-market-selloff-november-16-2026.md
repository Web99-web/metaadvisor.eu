**Cryptocurrencies and artificial intelligence stocks are coming under heavy pressure at the start of the week. Bitcoin fell toward $62,000 over the weekend before recovering some ground. Asian markets recorded sharp losses on November 16, particularly in Taiwan, South Korea and Japan, with semiconductor manufacturers among the hardest hit. Meanwhile, US spot Bitcoin ETFs continue to experience net withdrawals. The common theme is a retreat from risk, amplified by uncertainty over US interest rates, Nvidia's leadership and Chinese AI companies' access to advanced chips.**

## Bitcoin remains under pressure after the weekend

Bitcoin reached roughly $62,112 on Sunday, November 15, according to market reporting, before staging a partial rebound. The subsequent recovery has not been strong enough to establish that the multiday downtrend is over. Beyond the wider decline in risk appetite, the crypto market faces its own pressures: withdrawals from investment funds, forced liquidations of leveraged positions and hesitant buying after a series of negative sessions.

A short-lived rebound should not be confused with a lasting trend reversal. When traders close SHORT positions or buy after an intense selloff, prices can rise quickly without a corresponding increase in long-term investment demand. A more durable recovery would require greater stability across several sessions, stronger buying and reduced pressure in derivatives markets. A bounce from the weekend low alone does not prove that Bitcoin has reached its final bottom.

## Bitcoin ETFs continue to lose capital

The last completed US trading session before this report was Friday, November 13. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded **net outflows of $185.4 million**, extending a multiday withdrawal streak. Spot Ethereum ETFs registered a small **net inflow of approximately $2.3 million** on the same day. The difference illustrates how institutional flows can diverge even between the two largest cryptocurrencies.

Negative ETF flows do not mean every institutional investor has sold Bitcoin, nor do they establish that the entire amount was immediately sold on spot exchanges. Combined with falling prices, however, they remain an unfavorable short-term demand signal. The US ETF flows for November 16 had not been finalized when this article was prepared, so the November 13 figures should not be presented as today's totals.

## AI and semiconductor selling spreads across Asia

The equity selloff was particularly pronounced in Asia. Taiwan's **TAIEX fell 6.03%**, South Korea's **KOSPI lost approximately 3.59%**, and Japan's **Nikkei 225 declined about 2.42%**. Companies associated with chipmaking and AI infrastructure suffered some of the largest losses. That concentration matters because the sector has attracted substantial investment and optimism in recent years, making shares highly sensitive to changes in expectations for future demand.

Nvidia is at the center of attention following its sudden leadership transition, with quarterly earnings scheduled for November 17. Investors are also assessing whether tougher US policies could restrict Chinese AI developers' access to the most powerful computing resources. On November 16, the US administration issued a rule intended to prevent advanced chip export restrictions from being circumvented through remote access to data centers in third countries. The regulatory development is confirmed, although the precise consequences for individual companies remain to be seen.

Not every Asian market followed the same pattern. Hong Kong's broader benchmark resisted part of the regional decline even as certain Chinese AI developers suffered heavy losses. The selling was therefore especially concentrated in businesses exposed to AI infrastructure spending and future access to US technology.

## Nvidia adds uncertainty to the AI outlook

Nvidia announced on November 12 that Jensen Huang had resigned as chief executive, with finance chief Colette Kress taking over on an interim basis. The Financial Times subsequently reported that Huang had discussed a possible return with allies, some of whom are lobbying for his reinstatement. **No return has been confirmed**, and Nvidia's board has not announced a decision to restore him to the position. Investors must now evaluate leadership uncertainty alongside the effects of new export rules.

US premarket indications also pointed to weakness in AI-related businesses, including AMD, Micron and CoreWeave. Premarket moves are not the same as official Wall Street closing prices, but they showed that the negative mood in Asia was carrying into US trading. Whether further selling follows will depend on new information and the response from buyers.

## Have markets reached a bottom?

Available evidence does not yet confirm an end to the broader decline. Bitcoin's bounce from its weekend low has not eliminated ETF outflows, and isolated gains in technology shares do not prove that sentiment across the AI industry has reversed. Key developments to watch include Nvidia's results, clarification of US chip-access restrictions and the next confirmed ETF flow data.

Sharp declines can create conditions for temporary rebounds as selling pressure eases. Such a move is not automatically the beginning of a new long-term uptrend. Distinguishing genuine new investment demand from the short-term covering of bearish positions will be especially important.
