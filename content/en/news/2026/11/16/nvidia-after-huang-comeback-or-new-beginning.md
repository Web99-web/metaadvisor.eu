**Just days after Nvidia confirmed the departure of longtime chief executive Jensen Huang, influential figures in Silicon Valley began campaigning for his return. The Financial Times reported that Huang had discussed a possible comeback with confidants, but Nvidia has neither announced his reinstatement nor appointed a permanent successor. Meanwhile, Washington has introduced new restrictions designed to curb remote access to the most advanced AI chips through overseas data centers. Nvidia's leadership crisis is therefore unfolding at a crucial moment for the global artificial intelligence industry and US-China technology policy.**

## What has Nvidia officially confirmed?

On November 12, Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive officer and a member of its board of directors. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim CEO, while the board began searching for a permanent successor from both inside and outside the company. The departure was also disclosed in a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission.

That confirms a leadership change, but not who will ultimately run the company. Nvidia has said that its product development and commitments to customers will continue. Investors are also awaiting quarterly earnings scheduled for November 17. Delivering those results amid such significant management uncertainty will test investor confidence in both the business and its interim leadership.

## Where did the reports of Huang's comeback originate?

The Financial Times reported on November 15 and 16, citing people familiar with the discussions, that Huang was considering a return and that a group of technology entrepreneurs and investors was seeking to persuade Nvidia's board to reinstate him. Reuters subsequently relayed that reporting while making clear that it was based on media sources. **Nvidia has not publicly confirmed formal talks to reinstate Huang.**

An open letter and public campaign have also attracted support from prominent technology figures. Such backing may increase pressure on the board, but it cannot replace a corporate decision. Some market analysts consider a comeback highly unlikely given the circumstances surrounding Huang's resignation, the independent investigation and political scrutiny. For now, the accurate description is a **campaign for a possible comeback**, not an agreed return.

## What is known about the allegations and investigation?

Media reports have linked Huang's departure to disclosures about personal relationships and suspicions raised by US intelligence officials concerning one person's possible connections to Chinese intelligence. The reports have prompted questions about corporate governance and potential access to sensitive information. **Allegations are not proof of espionage**, and the publicly available evidence does not establish that Nvidia knowingly enabled illegal transfers of chips or confidential information.

The findings of the independent investigation therefore matter. It is especially important to distinguish documented management decisions from unproven allegations when the company involved plays a strategic role in US-China technology relations.

## The US introduces new AI chip access restrictions

On November 16, the US Bureau of Industry and Security issued a rule designed to prevent existing export controls from being circumvented through remote access to advanced chips hosted in data centers in third countries. According to Reuters, the restrictions include additional licensing requirements relating to the most advanced chips in certain countries, including Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Brazil.

This is a confirmed regulatory development, not merely a market rumor. It does not, however, mean that all Nvidia chips are now banned from sale or use overseas. The practical impact will depend on the exact terms of the rule, the licenses granted, existing business arrangements and enforcement.

## Why does this matter beyond Nvidia?

Nvidia supplies more than processors. Its chips, software and computing infrastructure underpin many large-scale AI systems, linking its business to manufacturers, cloud providers and customers around the world. Uncertainty over corporate leadership and export rules can therefore spread quickly through the technology supply chain and capital markets.

On November 16, Taiwan's TAIEX dropped 6.03% and South Korea's KOSPI lost approximately 3.59%. Those declines cannot be attributed to a single headline, but they highlight how sensitive investors have become to AI infrastructure risk. Nvidia's next earnings report and management comments will be important for determining whether confidence in the company's operational stability can be restored.
