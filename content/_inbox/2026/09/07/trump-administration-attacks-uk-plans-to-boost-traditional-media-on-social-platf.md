US embassy says Downing Street’s plan to give BBC, ITV and other established media more online visibility carries risk of ‘censorship’

The Trump administration has raised “serious concerns” over Downing Street’s plan to force YouTube and other digital platforms to hand more prominence to established broadcasters, warning the move risks “facilitating censorship”.

In a pointed intervention, the US government said the proposals to boost traditional media outlets in social media algorithms could “establish a template that authoritarian regimes could invoke to justify more control over online content”.

It also warned it could undermine “independent journalists” and amount to “extraterritorial censorship of protected speech by Americans online”.

The statement, made via the US embassy in London, raises the stakes in the plans to give the likes of the BBC, ITV and other established media more visibility on increasingly dominant digital platforms.

Ministers, regulators and public service broadcasters have suggested new rules are needed to counter growing misinformation online as people increasingly turn to less regulated online platforms such as X, YouTube and TikTok.

In a consultation this year, the UK government said it would consider legislating should the platforms not act to prioritise trustworthy content. However, Washington claimed such a plan risked effectively removing the content of “independent journalists” over established outlets.

“Preserving a free, open internet and protecting freedom of expression online are top priorities for the Trump administration,” it said. “A framework that empowers governments to influence which sources receive preferential visibility risks becoming a mechanism for viewpoint-based censorship.
