Today in crypto, Solana validators approved a proposal to accelerate SOL disinflation, California lawmakers passed a bill restricting public officials’ involvement in memecoin issuance and an Abu Dhabi royal’s group reportedly backed a 49% stake in the holding company behind World Liberty Financial’s proposed US trust bank.

Solana validators have approved a major change to the network’s issuance schedule, doubling its annual disinflation rate from 15% to 30% to reduce the pace of new SOL entering circulation.

The proposal, SGP-0002, passed with 67% support, while 25.16% voted against and 7.84% abstained. Nearly 61% of eligible stake participated.

The change keeps Solana’s long-term inflation target at 1.5% but is expected to reach that level in roughly 2.8 years instead of 5.7 years. An estimated 18.9 million fewer SOL could be issued over the next six years, reducing holder dilution while also lowering staking rewards for validators and delegators.

Major validators were divided. Figment opposed the proposal, while Helius and Jupiter largely supported it. Kraken initially voted against the measure before more than 90% of its voting stake ultimately backed it.

The vote comes as US-listed Solana ETFs gain traction, with Bitwise’s Solana ETF recently surpassing $1 billion in assets.

California lawmakers passed a bill restricting public officials’ involvement with memecoins, citing concerns around conflicts of interest and “pay-to-play arrangements.”
